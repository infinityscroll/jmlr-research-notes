PROVED DECLARATIONS

1. gaussian_singleton_null
   A scalar Gaussian with nonzero variance assigns zero mass to every
   singleton, by its absolute continuity with respect to Lebesgue measure.

2. gaussian_noAtoms
   Packages that statement as the NoAtoms property.

3. product_linear_hyperplane_null
   A proper affine linear equation has zero mass under a finite product
   of sigma-finite scalar measures whenever one coordinate with nonzero
   coefficient has an atomless marginal. The proof uses the measurable
   equivalence separating that coordinate, the symmetric product-integral
   formula, and its singleton fibers. Other marginals need not be atomless.

4. gaussian_product_linear_hyperplane_null
   Specializes the actual product measure to scalar Gaussians. Only one
   nonzero coefficient at a positive-variance coordinate is required;
   degeneracy at other coordinates is permitted.

5. gaussian_product_score_tie_null
   Distinct coefficient rows have zero probability of equal linear scores
   under a finite product Gaussian with all variances nonzero.

6. standard_gaussian_score_tie_null
   Specializes to the standard independent Gaussian measure.

7. ae_gaussian_scores_injective
   A finite family of distinct coefficient rows has pairwise distinct
   scores almost everywhere, by the finite intersection of the null-tie
   complements.

8. ae_gaussian_unique_max
   A nonempty finite family of distinct linear scores has an almost
   everywhere unique winning label. Existence comes from finite maximum
   attainment; strictness comes from the proved no-tie result.

9. ae_gaussian_routing_rigidity
   For actual Gaussian linear scores from distinct rows, an a.e.
   simplex-valued rule with zero expected routing loss is a.e. the
   maximizing label's indicator. This theorem assumes a.e. maximizing
   behavior of the supplied winner function, not uniqueness. Uniqueness
   is derived from the Gaussian measure. It also retains an integrability
   premise for the routing loss; the separate Gaussian-moment development
   can discharge that premise under measurable simplex weights.

PARAMETERS AND LIMITS

Finite coordinate spaces are Fin (n+1) -> Real, so the statements cover
every positive finite dimension. Labels are an arbitrary finite type.
Means may be nonzero; variances may vary by coordinate. The standard
Gaussian specialization takes every mean zero and variance one.

These results prove the null-tie and a.e.-purity step needed by the
manuscript's final rigidity argument once the analytic estimates have
forced the actual centroid rows to be regular and the routing integral
to vanish. They do not prove that centroid/deficit conclusion, the
noise-stability inequality, covariance concavity, or a quantitative
Gaussian margin estimate. They do not construct a measurable argmax
selector in this file. No full formalization is claimed.
