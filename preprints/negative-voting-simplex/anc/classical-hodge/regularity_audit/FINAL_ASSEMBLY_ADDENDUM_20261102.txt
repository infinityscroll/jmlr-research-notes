2. FIRST DERIVATIVE: SIGN, NORMALIZATION, AND COAREA

The added identity

  DF_nu(C)[H]=(1/2) sum_(i<j) w_ij(C) a_ij^T H a_ij

is correct at the stated full-simplex, positive-mass, C>0 scope.

For fixed heights h define l_h(x)=max_i(y_i dot x+h_i). The dual value is
E_(N(0,C)) l_h - sum_i p_i h_i, minimized over heights modulo constants.
The already established smooth optimizer permits the envelope theorem;
its height derivative is zero because its cell masses equal p_i. Thus
the derivative of the optimized heights contributes nothing.

The directional Gaussian heat identity is

  D_C E l_h[H]=(1/2) integral gamma_C tr(H dD^2 l_h).

It is legitimate distributionally: l_h has linear growth, its Hessian is
a locally finite facet measure, and Gaussian density derivatives have
integrable polynomial tails. Its i,j facet contribution is

  D^2l_h=(a_ij a_ij^T/|a_ij|) dH^(d-1).

To verify the exact whitening coefficient, let x=C^(1/2)z, a=a_ij, and
let F_x,F_z be corresponding facets. Their normals are
n_x=a/|a| and n_z=C^(1/2)a/|C^(1/2)a|. The surface change of variables gives

  gamma_C(x) dH_x^(d-1)
    = [|a|/|C^(1/2)a|] gamma_I(z) dH_z^(d-1).

Consequently

  (1/|a|) integral_(F_x) gamma_C dH^(d-1)
    = area_gamma(F_z)/|C^(1/2)a|=w_ij(C).

This proves the displayed derivative with one copy of every unoriented
facet and the factor 1/2. It uses no derivative of a Brenier map and no
chain-complex identity. For score sites P e_i the matrix sum
sum_(i<j) w_ij(e_i-e_j)(e_i-e_j)^T is exactly the offset Laplacian,
restricted to 1-perp. Therefore the derivative matrix is that Laplacian/2,
as needed by the prior calibration input.
