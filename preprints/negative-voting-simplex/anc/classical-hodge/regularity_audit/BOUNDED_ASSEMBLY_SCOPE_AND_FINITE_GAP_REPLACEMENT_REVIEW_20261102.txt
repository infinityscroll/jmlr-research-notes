2. THEOREM 2 AND STRICTNESS

The smoothness argument applies to d+1 affinely independent sites in R^d
with all masses positive. Such offset partitions are translations of a
simplex fan. Every pair has a genuine codimension-one facet with positive
Gaussian surface area. The offset Laplacian is positive modulo constants,
so the claimed smooth optimized value is supported by the implicit-function
argument, rather than assumed for a generic finite target.

The coefficient is exactly
  w_ij (a_ij^T H a_ij)^2/(a_ij^T C a_ij),
  w_ij=area_gamma(F_ij)/|C^(1/2)a_ij|.
The Hessian jump is |C^(1/2)a_ij| n_ij n_ij^T. Combining that coefficient
with n_ij^TC^(-1/2)HC^(-1/2)n_ij gives the displayed expression, with
one copy of each unoriented facet. Lower touching converts the Peano
coefficient -1/8 to the classical second-derivative coefficient -1/4.

Strictness follows without an additional geometric hypothesis. In the
basis v_i=y_i-y_0, vanishing of every edge quadratic first gives
v_i^THv_i=0 and then, using v_i-v_j, gives v_i^THv_j=0. Thus H=0.
All weights are positive, so nonzero H gives strictly negative curvature.

The intrinsic clause is valid for the SAME affinely independent simplex
embedded in a higher-dimensional ambient space. Its exact value depends
only on Q^TCQ and its curvature only on Q^THQ. It is not a claim about
arbitrary affinely dependent families with more than rank+1 sites.
The component audit states that restriction explicitly; this is the
intended reading of the assembly's shorter intrinsic clause.
