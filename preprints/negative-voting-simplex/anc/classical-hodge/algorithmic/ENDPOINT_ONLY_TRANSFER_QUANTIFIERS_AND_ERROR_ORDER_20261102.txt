ENDPOINT-ONLY TRANSFER: QUANTIFIERS, NORMALIZATION, AND ERROR ORDER
2 November 2026. Successor audit of the algorithmic consequence.

VERDICT

No substantive obstruction was found in the passage from the stated Gaussian
theorem to the exact fixed-alphabet Frieze--Jerrum/UGC threshold. In particular,
there is no hidden equal-mass hypothesis, two-rule Gaussian inequality,
correlation rescaling, or unjustified interchange of extrema in that passage.
This is conditional verification of the transfer, not validation of the
Gaussian theorem. The frozen manuscript remains an internal proof candidate.

There is a useful stronger formulation of the dependency: once the previously
credited endpoint theorem is used, matching hardness needs Gaussian optimality
at ONE correlation, rho_m=-1/(m-1). It does not need the entire negative voting
interval, a minimizing-correlation search, or any uniform invariance estimate
as m grows. Below, the error choices and decoder are made explicit. This gives
a concrete endpoint-only certificate and a nearly satisfiable instance gap.

One wording refinement is warranted: the cited FJ algorithm directly supplies
a RANDOMIZED polynomial-time guarantee IN EXPECTATION. An arbitrary small
additional loss gives high probability by repetition. The manuscript's bare
"polynomial-time algorithm" should not be read as a separately proved
deterministic derandomization. The NP-hardness promise itself is deterministic.

1. EXACT INPUT THAT THIS AUDIT USES

Fix an integer m>=max(m0,3). Let r_m=-1/(m-1), let g_m be a regular Gaussian
simplex assignment, and put

  b_m=S_(r_m)(g_m),              a_m=1-b_m.

The sufficient Gaussian hypothesis is only

  (E_m) For every finite N>=m-1 and every measurable PURE m-part assignment h
        on Gaussian R^N, with arbitrary cell masses,
        S_(r_m)(h)>=b_m.

The stated manuscript theorem is stronger: it permits fractional assignments,
all negative voting correlations, and gives equality classification. None of
those additional conclusions is required for the endpoint hardness proof.
Fractional DISCRETE rules remain essential in the verifier calculation, but
IM's transfer maps them to pure Gaussian rules. Thus Gaussian purity alone
would suffice, provided unrestricted masses and all finite dimensions remain.

The standard simplex exists in every N>=m-1. Cylindrical extension preserves
stability. Therefore (E_m) implies that the infimum of Gaussian stability is
exactly b_m in EACH such finite dimension, not merely in a dimension limit.
For N<m-1, embedding a competitor into R^(m-1) gives the same lower bound.
No existence theorem for a minimizing arbitrary partition is needed.

The sources actually checked are the manuscript main.tex, statement/input
section, discrete-consequence section, its frozen transfer companion, and the
already credited endpoint successor. Source pins and primary locators appear
in Section 8. No frozen source was changed.

2. CORRELATION AND FJ NORMALIZATION

For an SDP edge with unit endpoint vectors u,v and rho=<u,v>, independent
Gaussian rounding directions Z_1,...,Z_m produce score pairs

  X_i=<Z_i,u>, Y_i=<Z_i,v>,       Cov(X_i,Y_j)=rho*1_(i=j).

The two winning labels agree with probability P_m(rho). Subtracting each score
vector's coordinate mean does not change its winning index. On 1-perp the
centered scores have identity covariance and cross-covariance rho times the
identity, in orthonormal coordinates. Consequently

  P_m(rho)=S_rho(g_m)=s_m(rho),

with exactly the SAME rho. The simplex-vertex inner product -1/(m-1) is the
edge feasibility bound, not an extra multiplier in the Gaussian correlation.
The SDP edge contribution is c_m(rho)=(m-1)(1-rho)/m. At rho=r_m it equals 1.

The original FJ algorithm and its edge ratio were checked in the primary
Frieze--Jerrum preprint, Section 2 and Appendix. Its analysis is an expected
rounding guarantee. Endpoint minimization is NOT credited to that 1994
preprint or to this project. The relevant already credited later input is
Garcia-Soriano--Miyauchi, arXiv:2609.35453v1, Theorem 4.8:

  min_(r_m<=rho<1) [1-s_m(rho)]/c_m(rho) = 1-s_m(r_m)=a_m.

Their Theorem 4.9 concerns tightness for the specified rounding algorithm;
it is not an all-algorithm hardness theorem. The Gaussian input plus UGC
provides the latter type of complexity consequence. Those claims must stay
separate. The existing endpoint successor already makes this distinction.

3. THE SINGLE-RULE SOUNDNESS IDENTITY AND THE MASS CHECK

Write F_w(x)=e_(f_w(x)) for an arbitrary proof table at a right-hand Unique
Games vertex. Let P_(v,w) permute the coordinates according to its constraint.
Conditional on a left-hand vertex v, sample w,w' independently with the same
neighbor law, independently of a correlated pair (X,Y), and define

  G_v(x)=E_w F_w(P_(v,w)x).

Expansion of the finite sums, before any inequality, gives

  Pr[rejection | v]
    =E_(X,Y),w,w' <F_w(P_(v,w)X), F_w'(P_(v,w')Y)>
    =E_(X,Y) <G_v(X),G_v(Y)>
    =S_(r_m)(G_v).

The two tables need not agree. Their independent neighbor averages do agree,
which is precisely why one-rule stability is enough. A verifier with two
different conditional neighbor laws would instead yield a bilinear expression;
that is not the IM verifier being invoked here.

Each G_v is simplex-valued. Its mean is the neighbor average of the proof-table
means, and can be any probability vector. For example, all tables constantly
equal to label 1 yield mean e_1 and zero influences. The reduction does not
balance such a mean. IM Theorem 7.1 preserves it; it does not convert it into
the uniform vector. A Gaussian theorem only for equal masses would therefore
leave a real soundness gap unless a separate balancing reduction were proved.
Hypothesis (E_m), and the manuscript theorem, have exactly the needed
unrestricted-mass scope. There is no common-mass requirement across vertices.

4. ENDPOINT CHANNEL AND THE ORDER OF THE QUANTIFIERS

At r_m the discrete channel is particularly simple:

  Pr[Y_j=a | X_j=b] = 0 if a=b, and 1/(m-1) otherwise.

All probabilities are rational. On the zero-mean coordinate subspace the
channel operator is multiplication by r_m; its maximal correlation is
|r_m|=1/(m-1)<1 for m>=3. Thus the zero diagonal probabilities at the endpoint
do not cause a maximal-correlation-one obstruction to invariance. The m=2,
rho=-1 degeneracy is outside this audit and need not be imported.

For every FIXED m and every zeta>0, IM Theorem 7.1 gives integers d>=1 and
tau>0, independent of the voter count k, such that every F:[m]^k->Delta_m with

  Inf_j^(<=d)(F_i)<=tau for every output i and input coordinate j

has a pure Gaussian transfer h in dimension k(m-1) satisfying

  |S_(r_m)(F)-S_(r_m)(h)|<=zeta.

Applying (E_m) gives S_(r_m)(F)>=b_m-zeta. The same masses are preserved by
the cited theorem, but they need not be estimated because (E_m) is uniform
over them. The endpoint plurality limit is identified by IM Lemma 7.3.

The dependency order is

  fixed m -> desired hardness slack -> zeta -> d,tau -> Unique Games
  soundness gamma -> Unique Games label size M -> input graph size.

The exponential long-code size m^M is a constant in the final input-size
complexity statement. Nothing requires d,tau to be uniform in m, a computable
numerical m0, an explicit rate in tau, or a relation between the number of
input vertices and m. A theorem confined to one fixed Gaussian dimension
would not justify this step: M can be large and the transfer uses M(m-1)
dimensions. The candidate explicitly has the required all-finite-dimensional
quantifier.

5. EXPLICIT DECODING AND RATIO ERRORS

Here is an independent finite-parameter reconstruction of the endpoint
soundness, so that an asymptotic residual cannot hide in the notation.

Take a desired nontrivial hardness slack 0<epsilon<1-a_m=b_m, and set

  delta=epsilon/16, eta=epsilon/16, zeta=delta/4.

Use d,tau for the preceding transfer at error zeta. For a vector-valued rule
H define I_j(H)=sum_i Inf_j^(<=d)(H_i). Parseval gives

  sum_j I_j(F_w)
    =sum_sigma |supp sigma| 1_(|supp sigma|<=d) ||Fhat_w(sigma)||^2
    <=d E||F_w||^2=d.

Suppose verifier acceptance were greater than a_m+delta. Then
E_v S(G_v)<b_m-delta. Put B={v:S(G_v)<b_m-delta/2}. Since S(G_v)>=0 and
b_m<=1, the fraction p of vertices in B satisfies p>delta/2. Indeed,

  E_v S(G_v)>=(1-p)(b_m-delta/2),

and b_m-delta/2>0. For each v in B, the transfer with error delta/4 implies
that some output/input influence exceeds tau; hence max_j I_j(G_v)>tau.

Choose l(v) to maximize I_j(G_v). At w, choose l(w)=j with probability
I_j(F_w)/(sum_t I_t(F_w)); if that denominator is zero, use any label.
The zero-denominator case causes no difficulty, since its influences vanish.
For every w,j this random label has probability at least I_j(F_w)/d.
Squared Fourier coefficients are convex under averaging, so

  I_(l(v))(G_v)
    <=E_w I_(sigma_(v,w)^(-1)(l(v)))(F_w).

It follows that the conditional probability of satisfying the Unique Games
constraint out of v in B exceeds tau/d. The resulting labeling satisfies
more than delta*tau/(2d) of the constraints in expectation, and therefore
some deterministic labeling does. Choosing

  gamma=delta*tau/(4d)

contradicts Unique Games soundness VAL<=gamma. Hence every cut in the
soundness case has value at most a_m+delta. This proof uses vector influence
only as a sum of the manuscript's scalar influences; no silent conversion
of conventions is needed. The conservative m*d denominator used in IM would
also suffice and changes no conclusion.

In the completeness case, dictator tables succeed whenever both sampled
constraints are satisfied. At the endpoint the queried coordinates are always
different, so completeness is at least 1-2eta. Therefore the gap ratio is

  (a_m+delta)/(1-2eta),

and its difference from a_m is

  (delta+2eta*a_m)/(1-2eta)
     <=(3epsilon/16)/(1-epsilon/8)<epsilon.

Here 0<a_m<1 and epsilon<1; the strict inequalities follow from the positive
Gaussian joint density at |r_m|<1 and the nontrivial simplex cells. Larger
requested epsilon is covered by proving the result for a smaller positive
slack. This completes the actual fixed-m quantifier proof.

In particular, for each fixed m satisfying (E_m) and arbitrary positive
eta_0,delta_0, UGC gives hardness of distinguishing normalized weighted
MAX-m-CUT instances with OPT>=1-eta_0 from those with OPT<=a_m+delta_0.
This is the endpoint's nearly satisfiable instance formulation, not a claim
of perfect completeness or hardness on exactly m-colorable graphs.

If a substitute Gaussian estimate has a nonzero error e_m,
S_(r_m)(h)>=b_m-e_m, the same proof gives a soundness value no better than
a_m+e_m+delta. Arbitrarily small invariance and UGC errors do NOT remove e_m.
In particular an O(log log m/m^2) or o(m^-2) loss as m->infinity does not
prove exact fixed-m hardness. This is precisely the distinction from KKMO
Proposition 11.4 and Theorem 11. Those are asymptotic bounds; their low-degree
replacement appears separately in Proposition 11.5. The current transfer
uses the exact candidate Gaussian lower bound and then lets only the freely
chosen transfer/reduction errors tend to zero, after m has been fixed.

6. WHY THERE IS NO INFIMUM/SUPREMUM OR DIMENSION-LIMIT GAP

For any pure N-dimensional partition h, define
R_h(rho)=[m/(m-1)]*[1-S_rho(h)]/(1-rho), rho<1.
For N>=m-1, the credited endpoint FJ identity gives inf_rho R_g(rho)=a_m,
while (E_m) gives R_h(r_m)<=a_m for EVERY h. Hence, already in each finite
N>=m-1,

  sup_h inf_rho R_h(rho) <= a_m,

with equality by testing g. Also, for every rho, sup_h R_h(rho)>=R_g(rho)
>=a_m, while at r_m that supremum equals a_m. Thus

  inf_rho sup_h R_h(rho)=a_m.

These two extrema are evaluated separately. They are not interchanged by a
minimax theorem. Both equal a_m before the outer dimension limit in IM's
constants is taken. At rho=1 the edge contribution vanishes; the ratio is
understood on rho<1 or by a limiting convention and cannot lower the infimum.

Even without the later endpoint theorem, the manuscript's full negative-range
input justifies its original compact-minimum route. Hermite weights of the
pure balanced regular partition show R_g(rho)>=1 for rho>=0; R_g(0)=1.
The same regular rule maximizes disagreement at every negative admissible
rho. These facts evaluate both extrema without assuming positive-noise
simplex optimality. The later endpoint input simply removes an unnecessary
moving parameter and rational-approximation step: r_m itself is rational.

7. PRECISE SCOPE AND RECOMMENDED SUCCESSOR WORDING

The following is the appropriate compact application statement:

  For every fixed m>=max(m0,3), put
  a_m=1-s_m(-1/(m-1)), using the credited FJ endpoint theorem.
  Frieze--Jerrum rounding gives, unconditionally, a randomized polynomial-
  time (a_m-epsilon)-approximation in expectation for every epsilon>0.
  Conditional on the candidate Gaussian endpoint inequality and UGC,
  approximation within a_m+epsilon is NP-hard for every epsilon>0.
  The algorithmic constants and reduction may depend on fixed m,epsilon.

The rounding guarantee actually exists for every fixed m>=3 independently
of the Gaussian candidate. Its new conditional significance in this project
is matching hardness for all fixed m beyond an existential threshold. No
improved rounding algorithm, numerical m0, particular small alphabet, exact
finite-precision attainment of a_m, or uniform variable-alphabet running time
has been obtained. Equality classification and quantitative distance-to-
simplex estimates are not needed for this consequence.

To turn the expected algorithm guarantee into high probability, run independent
roundings and retain the best. Normalizing by OPT gives a random variable in
[0,1]. If its expectation is at least a_m-epsilon/2, its probability of being
at least a_m-epsilon is at least epsilon/2. Thus O(epsilon^(-1) log(1/q))
repetitions give failure probability at most q. This adds no Gaussian proof
assumption. It does not by itself furnish a deterministic derandomization.

The direct audit concerns nonnegative rational weighted graphs, exactly the
primary transfer formulation. The manuscript's optional simple-unweighted
extension remains a separate mixing-graph reduction and was not silently
used here. No new external publication, priority claim, or numerical test is
part of this note.
