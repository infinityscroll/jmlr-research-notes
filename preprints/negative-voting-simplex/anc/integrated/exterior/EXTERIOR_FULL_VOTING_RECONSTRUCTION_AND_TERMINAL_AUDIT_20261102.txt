1. BALANCED OFFSETS AND THE CORRECT DEFICIT SCALE

Since delta in [0,2D], |r Delta|=|D-delta|<=D. Thus
 |W-w|<=D/r<=Cr/m and W/w=1+O(r/L).
In particular s=sqrt(W/w) is bounded above and away from zero. The entropy
centroid bound gives |B_i|<=p_i sqrt(2log(1/p_i))<=C alpha. Centering follows
from sum_i f_i=1, and Cauchy gives B^T B<=p_max I. All these hold for
fractional f; no optimizer or affine partition is assumed.

For fixed mass p let F_p(B) be the optimal linear assignment value. The actual
f is feasible, so F_p(B)>=W. Testing its dual with the balanced offsets h_U
shows F_p(B)<=F_U(B)-h_U.(p-U). Put R=(w+W)/2-F_U(B).
Covariance concavity and permutation averaging imply F_U(B)<=sqrt(wW), hence
R>=0. The bounded-row offset certificate gives ||h_U||^2<=C L^2 R and thus
 R<=Delta/2+C L sqrt(R delta)<=Delta/2+R/2+C L^2 delta/2.
Consequently, once C r L^2<=1,
             0<=R<=Delta+C L^2 delta<=Delta+delta/r=D/r=:J.       (1)
The division by r is required. The calculation remains valid if W>w.

I also checked the proof of the offset certificate used here. For distinct
bounded sites, Gaussian coarea plus isoperimetry yields a physical graph gap
at least c/L. The row-norm error in its Cheeger calculation is retained as

Inverting the offset Laplacian gives mass Lipschitz constant CL. Coincident

2. RECONSTRUCTION OF THE HAZARD COEFFICIENT LEMMA

Let sum xi_i=0, ||xi||_infinity<=A fixed, v_i=1+t xi_i,
0<=t<=c_A/L^2, and choose mean-zero balancing offsets h_i(t). With
 F_i=Phi((x-h_i)/sqrt(v_i)), a_i=(partial_x F_i)/F_i,
 W_0=product F_i, rho_ij=W_0 a_i a_j,
 omega_ij=int rho_ij, ell_i=sum_j omega_ij,
the analytic diagonal omega_ii is included only in ell_i. It is not a facet.

The whole-threshold moment proof gives
 omega_ij comparable to alpha/m,
 E_ij[(m max_k a_k)^p(1+|x|)^q]<=C_pq L^((p+q)/2),            (2)
for every one of the finitely many fixed moment orders used in the proof.
For positive thresholds, the change s=1-Phi(z), followed by y=ms, leaves
m^p int s^(p+1-O(1/L))(1-s)^(m-p-2)log(e/s)^((p+q+1)/2) ds.
Its normalization is alpha/m comparable to sqrt(L)/m^2. Thus the m powers
cancel and the logarithmic power in (2) is correct. The negative and bounded
threshold intervals are exponentially small in m. The bounds apply equally
to i=j; the extra reciprocal F_i on positive thresholds is bounded.

The complete-graph Laplacian has weights between c alpha/m and C alpha/m.
For mean-zero b its inverse satisfies
 |(Laplacian^-1 b)_i|<=C alpha^-1(|b_i|+mean_j |b_j|).
This component bound follows from L1 contraction after subtracting a complete
graph, followed by the individual row equation; L1 contraction alone would
not suffice.

Define b_i(x)=h_i'+xi_i(x-h_i)/(2v_i). Differentiating the mass exactly gives
 0=sum_(j!=i) int rho_ij(b_i-b_j).
The moment and inverse estimates yield
 |h_i'|<=C sqrt(L)(|xi_i|+M1),  M1=mean|xi|.
This closes the coordinate box by continuation: h_i=O(t sqrt L), while
v_i-1=O(t). No Frobenius-small perturbation is assumed.

For clarity, the terms whose omission would break this calculation are
 P_ij=partial_t log rho_ij
     =L_i+L_j+a_i b_i+a_j b_j-sum_k a_k b_k,
 L_i=-xi_i/(2v_i)+xi_i(x-h_i)^2/(2v_i^2)+h_i'(x-h_i)/v_i,
 b_i'=h_i''-xi_i^2(x-h_i)/(2v_i^2)-xi_i h_i'/(2v_i).
All other labels remain in the sum. Differentiating the mass equation gives
the same Laplacian applied to h'', with source int rho_ij times
P_ij(b_i-b_j) plus the displayed quadratic b_i' remainder. Holder and (2)
give source_i<=C alpha L^(3/2)(xi_i^2+M2), M2=mean xi^2. The component inverse
therefore gives |h_i''|<=C L^(3/2)(xi_i^2+M2).

Differentiating rho_ij again gives rho_ij(P_ij^2+P_ij'). The identities
 a_i'=a_i(L_i+a_i b_i) and
 (sum_k a_k b_k)'=sum_k a_k[(L_k+a_k b_k)b_k+b_k']
retain both offset acceleration and all labels. For the sums one uses
sum a_k xi_k^2<=m max a_k M2 and
sum a_k(|xi_k|+M1)^2<=C m max a_k M2.
Equation (2), not a crude sum of m separate estimates, then yields
 E_ij(P_ij^2+|P_ij'|)<=C L^2(xi_i^2+xi_j^2+M2).
Summing against omega_ij<=C alpha/m proves
                |ell_i''|<=C alpha L^2(xi_i^2+M2).           (3)
The offset derivatives and normalizations above also justify differentiation
under the integrals; the Gaussian tail domination is uniform on the box.

At the regular point, the tie density is proportional to
phi(x)^2 Phi(x)^(m-2). Write M=E T, V=Var T. Balancing gives h_i'=-M xi_i/2.
The log-hazard derivative is [(x+a(x))(x-M)-1]xi_i/2. The derivatives of
W_0 and sum a_i vanish because sum xi_i=0. Integration by parts in the tie
law gives -2V+(m-2)Cov(T,a(T))=-1, hence
 ell_i(0)=alpha, ell_i'(0)=-alpha eta_m xi_i,
 eta_m=[m-1-mV]/[2(m-2)].
The tie law has potential Hessian at least 2, so V<=1/2. Conditioning regular
scores on the tie and being maximal does not constrain their independent
common mean; therefore V>=1/m. These bounds give eta_m in [1/4,1/2].
This is an exact coefficient, not just the correct sign asymptotically.

3. GLOBAL SUMMED ROW PAYMENT, WITH A SINGULAR TARGET ALLOWED

Normalize Bhat=B/s, so ||Bhat||^2=w, and put C=Bhat Bhat^T/alpha^2.
Then C>=0, C1=0, tr C=m-1, and C_ii is uniformly bounded. Define
 z_i=C_ii-(m-1)/m, kappa=1-2/m, xi_i=z_i/kappa,
 D_t=P diag(1+t xi)P, t=b_A/L^2.
Here (D_t)_ii=(m-1)/m+t z_i and ||xi||_infinity is bounded independently of m.
Only D_t, not C, must be positive definite on 1-perp.

At D_t the exact hazard identity is
 Laplacian=P diag(ell_i)P-H_t,
 H_t=int W_0(Pa)(Pa)^T>=0.
The covariance derivative is Laplacian/2. Euler homogeneity and concavity give
 Gamma(C)<=tr[Laplacian(C+D_t)]/2
          <=sum_i ell_i(C_ii+(D_t)_ii)/2.                    (4)
The discarded term is -tr[H_t(C+D_t)]/2<=0. This is its exact sign; no
operator bound on C or estimate of off-diagonal errors is needed.

Taylor with (3) gives ell_i=alpha-alpha eta_m t xi_i+e_i,
 |e_i|<=C alpha L^2 t^2(xi_i^2+M2).
The constant contribution in (4) is mu. Its linear contribution is controlled
by the exact identity sum_i xi_i(C_ii+(D_t)_ii)=kappa(1+t)||xi||^2.
The full remainder sums to C alpha L^2 t^2||xi||^2. Choose b_A small enough
once and for all. The positive linear term dominates and gives
 R_U(Bhat)>=c alpha^2 L^-2 sum_i z_i^2.
Equivalently,
 sum_i(|Bhat_i|^2-beta^2)^2<=C L^3 R_U(Bhat)/m^2,
 E_rad:=sum_i(|Bhat_i|-beta)^2<=C L^2 R_U(Bhat)<=C L^2 R.     (5)
The last step uses the exact radial identity
 R=w(s-1)^2/2+s R_U(Bhat).
No factor m is lost in (5), and no rank restriction is introduced.

4. ROW LOWER BOUNDS AND THE EXACT CENTERED EQUAL-ROW PROXY

The one-row theorem can be derived using the new normal-strain inequality.
Symmetrize the target covariance over permutations fixing label i. At fixed
trace this gives K_a=a uu^T+b(P-uu^T), b=(m-1-a)/(m-2), where
 a=|Bhat_i|^2/beta^2. For edges incident to i, the squared edge length is a+1
and its derivative in a is exactly 1. The normal-strain inequality plus
Gaussian isoperimetry of the balanced i-th cell gives
 -4 d^2 Gamma(K_a)/da^2>=I(1/m)/(a+1)^(3/2).
Integration from a=1, including its zero tangent, yields
 R_U(Bhat)>=[alpha I(1/m)/(2sqrt2)](sqrt(1+a)-sqrt2)^2.
Continuity includes a=0,m-1. On the fixed row-cap interval this implies
 max_i ||Bhat_i|^2/beta^2-1|<=C sqrt(m^2 R/L)
                           <=C sqrt(mr/L)=o(1).             (6)
Thus the normalized rows are uniformly bounded away from zero.

Cauchy gives ||Bhat||_op^2<=C/m. For u_i=Bhat_i/|Bhat_i|,
||mean_i u_i u_i^T||_op<=C/L. Let Phi(c)=mean_i|Bhat_i-c| on the row span.
For |c|<=c0 beta with c0 fixed sufficiently small, its Hessian is >=c/beta.
Using sum Bhat_i=0,
 |grad Phi(0)|<=C ||Bhat||_op sqrt(E_rad)/(m beta^2)
               <=C sqrt(m E_rad)/L<=C sqrt(r)=o(1).
Its global minimizer therefore lies inside that ball, at |c|<=C beta|grad Phi(0)|.
The exact definition Z_i=beta(Bhat_i-c)/|Bhat_i-c| then satisfies
 sum Z_i=0, |Z_i|=beta,
 ||Z-Bhat||^2<=C E_rad,  ||Z-B||^2=:E_Z<=C L^2 R<=C L^2 J.   (7)
The minimizer is ordinary and differentiable because no row lies in the small
ball. This is only a test matrix. It need not be a centroid matrix, have
separated rows, or preserve the actual routing. It stays in the original row
span; no unused coordinate in the original problem is assumed.

5. THE EXTERIOR FLOOR AND DIMENSION-FREE GLOBAL PAYMENT

Write d=dist_F(B,regular centroid orbit), q=d/alpha,
e=||BB^T/alpha^2-P||_F. Procrustes gives e>=q, also for singular B.
The finite covariance-gap argument gives
 R>=c alpha^2 e^2/m *min(1,c/(L e)).                         (8)
Its propagation to arbitrary PSD endpoints is legitimate: along
P+t(C-P), integrate the local normal-strain curvature only for
0<=t<=min(1/2,c/(L e)), where the balanced facet weights are controlled.
Concavity propagates this loss to t=1. The integration weight is 1-t, so
the payment is proportional to the interval length, not its square.
The regular tangent accounts for tr(C-P) even without trace normalization.

I checked the local facet bootstrap: the reference facet is a convex Gaussian
cone, hence has Poincare constant <=1. Its mean is O(sqrt L) and covariance
differs from the identity on 1-perp by bounded Frobenius norm. The latter also
follows directly from the tie law used above: on differences of the m-2
nonwinning coordinates the variance is E[1-T a(T)-a(T)^2], whose deficit is
O(L/m) by (2); the remaining distinguished variance is <=1 by Brascamp--Lieb,
and the normal variance is zero. Under a small covariance/mean tilt, the
potential remains uniformly convex. The mean/covariance derivative bounds
then control the facet weight ratio by exp(C[epsilon L+sqrt(L)|h|+|h|^2]).
The balancing ODE and the complete-graph gap give |h'|<=C sqrt(L) epsilon,
closing the bootstrap at epsilon<=c/L. All trace terms cancel before any
estimate; no ambient determinant or dimension factor survives.

In the exterior d>1/m, q>=c/sqrt L and q<=C sqrt m. Thus L e is large,
and (8) yields
          J>=R>=c q/m^3>=c/(m^3 sqrt L),

The strict positive floor is essential below. It is not a bound for all

6. THE MAXIMUM COMPARISON AND ITS EXACT EXTERNAL SCOPE

Let Y_i=Z_i.X/beta, M=max_i Y_i>=0, and let M_* be the regular unit-row maximum.
Let a0=(m-1)/m and add an independent standard G only for the probabilistic
comparison: nu=sqrt(a0)M+G/sqrt m. The covariance R_c of its underlying scores
has unit diagonal and R_c-J_label/m=a0 ZZ^T/beta^2>=0.
Mulgund, arXiv:2607.14087v4, Theorem 2.1, gives
 H(t)=P(nu<=t)-P(max_i Z_i^iid<=t)>=0 for every common threshold t.
I reopened the primary theorem on 2 November 2026:
https://arxiv.org/html/2607.14087v4
Its statement and Appendix B include singular covariance matrices. No
unequal-threshold or unequal-variance extension is used. The paper's own
covariance transformation is the same one above. Its full external proof
or claimed Lean formalization is not reverified by this audit.

Integration gives int H=sqrt(a0)(E M_*-E M) and
E M_*^2-E M^2=(2/a0)int t H(t)dt. The right and left tails are bounded by
m barPhi(t) and Phi(t), respectively. Consequently, for T>=1,
 (E M_*^2-E M^2)_+<=CT(E M_*-E M)+Cm exp(-T^2/2).             (10)
This is an absolute tail error; it is not silently called deficit-relative.
With V=beta M, V_*=beta M_*, E V_*=w, the bound is
 (E V_*^2-E V^2)_+<=C beta T(w-E V)+C beta^2m exp(-T^2/2).
In particular E V<=w. Adding the common independent Gaussian here is a
comparison identity and does not require an extra coordinate in f's space.

7. ROUTING AND THE DIRECT QUADRATIC CERTIFICATE

Let z_i=Z_i.X and c_f=V-sum_i f_i z_i>=0. Both E c_f and w-E V are
nonnegative and their sum is exactly
             w-<Z,B>=(Delta+E_Z)/2<=C L^2 J.                (11)
Since V>=0 and z_i<=V,
 V^2-sum f_i z_i^2<=2V c_f.
For A_X=max_i|Y_i|, c_f<=2beta A_X and V<=beta A_X. The unit marginal
Gaussian union bound gives E[A_X^2 1_(A_X>T)]<=Cm(T^2+1)e^(-T^2/2).
Splitting the preceding routing inequality and combining with (10), at
T=sqrt(32L), gives
 E sum_i f_i z_i^2>=E V_*^2-C L^3 J/m-C beta^2mL e^(-16L).   (12)
The actual masses can be unequal and the actual f fractional throughout.

Define Q_i=E[f_i(XX^T-I)] and T_i=Z_i Z_i^T-Z^TZ/m. Then sum Q_i=0 and
 T_f:=sum<Q_i,T_i>=E sum_i f_i z_i^2-beta^2,
 sum||T_i||_F^2=m beta^4-tr((ZZ^T)^2)/m
                  <=D_*:=alpha^4(m-1)(m-2)/m.              (13)
The denominator inequality is trace-squared/rank, using rank<=m-1; no inverse
Gram matrix appears. The Hermite convention is A2(f)=sum||Q_i||_F^2/2.
Thus A2(f)>=(T_f)_+^2/(2D_*), even if the test tensors vanish.

For the regular reference, conical homogeneity makes each Q_i trace-free on
the score span; outside it Q_i=0. Permutations fixing i leave only the radial
and transverse invariant tensor components, so trace zero makes Q_i a scalar
multiple of the regular T_i. This proves equality in the regular Cauchy bound.
Direct centering of independent scores gives
 T_*=alpha^2(E(max_i Z_i^iid)^2-1)>0,
 A2(g)=T_*^2/(2D_*), and T_*/D_*<=Cm for large m.
Using (T_*-e)_+^2>=T_*^2-2T_*e in (12)-(13) yields
 A2(f)-A2(g)>=-C L^3 J-C L^2 e^(-16L).
The exterior floor (9) pays the latter since its ratio to J is at most
C m^-13 L^(5/2). Therefore
                    A2(f)-A2(g)>=-C L^3 J.                 (14)
This is an exterior estimate. It does not assert the false unrestricted
near-simplex relative-A2 inequality ruled out in earlier work.

8. INDEPENDENT REGULAR-MARGIN DERIVATION

The regular centroid scores are alpha(Z_i-Zbar). Their top-two gap is
alpha times the independent Gaussian order-statistic gap. If F(s) is the
probability that that independent gap is <=s, differentiating its exact
order-statistic integral for 0<=s<=1 gives
 F'(s)=m(m-1)e^(-s^2/2) int phi(x)^2 e^(-s x)Phi(x)^(m-2)dx.
On x>=0, the extra exponential is <=1. On x<0, the integral is at most
C 2^(-(m-2)), uniformly for s<=1, by completing the Gaussian square.
Since int phi^2 Phi^(m-2)=alpha/m, this proves F'(s)<=C mu for large m.
For centroid gap c(X), P(c<=a)<=C m a when a<=alpha. When a>alpha,
C m a>=C m alpha>=1 for large m, so the same bound holds for every a>0.

Let B0 be a closest regular centroid matrix and g0 its assignment. Its routing
loss is R0=w-<B0,B>=(Delta+d^2)/2>=0. The mismatch weight of fractional f is
1-f_(regular winner)(X), between zero and one. It pays at least that weight
times c(X). Thus for epsilon=||f-g0||_L1/2,
 epsilon<=P(c<=a)+R0/a<=Cma+R0/a,
                  epsilon<=min(1,C sqrt(m R0)).             (15)
No actual affine geometry, stationarity, purity or interface regularity is
needed for (15).

9. ALL SIGNED HIGHER DEGREES AND THE TWO EXTERIOR REGIMES

Set tau^2=1/(4L). Hypercontractivity, 0<=f_i<=1, and p_i<=3/(2m) give
 ||T_tau f||_2^2+||T_tau g0||_2^2<=C/m.
Writing e_i=E|f_i-g0_i|<=C/m and using sum e_i=2epsilon gives
 ||T_tau(f-g0)||_2^2<=sum e_i^(2/(1+tau^2))<=C epsilon/m.
The factor m^(2tau^2/(1+tau^2)) is bounded by e^(1/2). The chosen smoothing
time depends on m, not on mismatch amplitude. On Hermite degrees >=3 the
residual multiplier after factoring through T_tau has absolute value at most
(r/tau^2)^3. For large m, r<=tau^2 throughout the voting interval. Cauchy gives
 |H_r(f)-H_r(g0)|<=C r^3 L^3 sqrt(epsilon)/m,                (16)
where H_r includes every signed Hermite degree >=3. Rotation leaves the
regular reference's Hermite weights unchanged.

If J>=d^2, then R0<=J, and (15)-(16) imply
 |Hdiff|/J<=C r^3 L^3/(m^(3/4)J^(3/4))
           <=C r^3 m^(3/2)L^(27/8),
using the floor in (9).
If J<d^2, then R0<=d^2. With d=alpha q and J>=c q/m^3, they imply
 |Hdiff|/J<=C r^3 m^(7/4)L^(13/4)q^-1/2
           <=C r^3 m^(7/4)L^(7/2).
The latter bound dominates both. Hence
             |H_r(f)-H_r(g)|<=C r^3 m^(7/4)L^(7/2)J.        (17)
These powers were recomputed explicitly; no signed tail is dropped.

10. STRICT EXTERIOR CONTRADICTION AND QUANTIFIERS

Hermite orthogonality gives exactly
 S_-r(f)-S_-r(g)=D+r^2[A2(f)-A2(g)]+H_r(f)-H_r(g).
Substitute (14),(17), J=D/r:
 S_-r(f)-S_-r(g)
   >=D[1-CrL^3-Cr^2m^(7/4)L^(7/2)].                         (18)
For every 0<r<=1/(m-1), the two losses are bounded by
C L^3/m and C L^(7/2)/m^(1/4), both tending to zero. There is therefore one
absolute eventual m threshold for which the bracket is >=1/2. By (9),
D=rJ>0 in the exterior. Equation (18) contradicts competitiveness strictly.

All constants used for this branch depend only on fixed absolute mass/row
boxes and the fixed finite moment orders. None depends on ambient N or on
positive r in the stated range. All singular score limits use values or
continuous covariances; no endpoint facet derivatives are taken. No property
of a minimizing assignment is used, and no attainment theorem is needed.
