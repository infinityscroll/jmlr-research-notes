ACTUAL GAUSSIAN MAXIMUM MEAN / QUANTILE SEPARATION

GaussianMaximumMeanBounds.lean is a checked theorem about the actual maximum
of n+2 independent standard Gaussian coordinates. Put m=n+2,
b=upperQuantile n, mu=maximumMean n, Z=b(M-b).

The proof derives the signed tail-integral identity E Z, integrates the
finite positive-tail bound on [0,8], and bounds all three negative-tail
regions. For n>0 and b^2>=1000000 it gives the explicit finite inequality

 b(mu-b) >= 187/250 - 23/99
             - b^2 exp(-exp((99/100)b^2/50)) - 3 b 2^(-m).

Both displayed remainders are proved to tend to zero from the actual
quantile's divergence and b<=m. Consequently, eventually b(mu-b)>=103/200.
No Gumbel limit, Euler-constant identity, unproved asymptotic notation, or
mean/quantile separation hypothesis is used for this eventual conclusion.

Mills inequalities and the mean bound give eventually

These formulas supply the strict first-moment coefficient margin needed
in competitive mass localization. This module does not identify w with
