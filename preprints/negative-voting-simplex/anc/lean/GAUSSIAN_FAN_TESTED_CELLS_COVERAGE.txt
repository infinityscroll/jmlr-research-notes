Actual tested winning cells for Gaussian fan differentiation
Source: GaussianFanTestedCells.lean
Namespace: NegativeVotingFanTestedCells

The source extracts and generalizes the actual Gaussian tie-nullness argument
from the frozen mass_eq_winningCell proof without editing that predecessor.
For injective rows B, each uniform-on-maximizers weight agrees almost everywhere
with the indicator of the literal closed winningCell B h i.

Verified endpoints
* weights_eq_winningCell_indicator_ae is an actual Gaussian AE statement.
* weights_smul_eq_winningCell_indicator_ae permits any normed real vector-space
  valued test F. No regularity or integrability premise is needed for AE equality.
* integral_weights_smul_eq_cell proves the corresponding Bochner integral
  identity; integral_weights_mul_eq_cell is its scalar specialization.
  These equalities respect Lean's usual integral convention, including the
  nonintegrable case. They do not claim that every arbitrary test is integrable.
* centroid_eq_cell_integral identifies the centroid already used in the joint
  expected-maximum derivative with the actual unnormalized cell first moment.
* affine_test_integrable and weighted_affine_test_integrable discharge actual
  Gaussian integrability for affine tests, rather than assume it.
* integral_weighted_affine_test evaluates an affine test as
    <D, centroid_i> + v * mass_i.
  It holds even for tied rows with their fractional weights.
* integral_cell_affine_test gives the same formula on the literal winning cell
  for injective rows.
* jointDerivative_eq_cell_affine_tests expresses the previously proved actual
  joint expected-maximum derivative as a sum of integrals over actual cells.

No positive-mass, affine-independence, centered-singular, moving-boundary
formula, differentiable-cell, or Hessian-identification premise is used in
this source. Distinct rows are enough for the AE weight/cell identification.
The source does not itself differentiate a moving cell or assert the joint
score Hessian or the main negative-voting theorem.
