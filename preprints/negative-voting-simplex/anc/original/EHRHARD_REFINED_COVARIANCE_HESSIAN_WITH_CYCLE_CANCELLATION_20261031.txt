1. Classical input and its differentiated consequence
We use Ehrhard's inequality for convex Gaussian sets. The precise statement
was checked on the publisher page for R. Latala, A note on the Ehrhard inequality,
Studia Mathematica118(1996),169--174, DOI10.4064/sm-118-2-169-174:
 https://www.impan.pl/en/publishing-house/journals-and-series/studia-mathematica/all/118/2/109424/a-note-on-the-ehrhard-inequality
That statement permits two Borel sets when one is convex; only the simpler
case of two translates of the same convex set is used here. The publisher's
full-PDF link returned403 during this check; the exact inequality is displayed
on its primary landing page. We do not claim a new classical inequality or an
independent proof of Ehrhard's theorem.

Let K be convex in R^k with 0<p=gamma_k(K)<1. Put
 mu=E[X|K], Sigma=Cov(X|K), q=Phi^{-1}(p),
 a(p)=1+q p/phi(q).
Convexity gives lambda(K+x)+(1-lambda)(K+y)=K+lambda x+(1-lambda)y.
Thus x -> Phi^{-1}(gamma_k(K+x)) is concave. At x=0,
 grad gamma(K+x)=-p mu,
 Hess gamma(K+x)=p(Sigma+mu mu^T-I).
The chain rule, using (Phi^{-1})''(p)=q/phi(q)^2, yields

 I-Sigma >= a(p) mu mu^T.                               (1)

Gaussian domination justifies differentiating translations even for unbounded
convex K. No boundary smoothness is needed.

The coefficient is strictly positive. For q>=0 it is >=1. For q=-t<0,
write R(t)=barPhi(t)/phi(t)=integral_0^infty exp(-tu-u^2/2)du. Integration
by parts gives
 a(p)=1-tR(t)=integral_0^infty u exp(-tu-u^2/2)du>0.
Restricting to 0<=u<=1/(1+t) gives
 a(p)>=exp(-3/2)/[2(1+t)^2].
Since t^2<=2log(1/p), this proves, for every 0<p<1,

 a(p)^{-1} <= 8 exp(3/2)[1+log(1/p)].                    (2)

Repeated tail integration also gives a(p)^{-1}=t^2+O(1) as p->0,
but that sharper asymptotic is unnecessary below.

2. Simplex notation and exact intrinsic probability
Take m=d+1>=3 affinely independent sites y_i in R^d, fixed positive masses,
and source covariance C=I. Let h_i be the optimizing score offsets for
 F(C)=inf_h {E max_i(y_i dot X_C+h_i)-sum_i p_i h_i}.
For an oriented edge e=(i,j), define
 d_e=y_i-y_j, ell_e=|d_e|, n_e=d_e/ell_e,
 h_e=h_i-h_j, P_e=I-n_e n_e^T,
 Gamma_e={x: i,j co-win},
 mu_e=integral_Gamma_e phi_d dH^(d-1), w_e=mu_e/ell_e,
 m_e=conditional mean of X on Gamma_e,
 Sigma_e=conditional covariance on Gamma_e,
 E_e=w_e(P_e-Sigma_e).
The facet lies in n_e dot x=-h_e/ell_e. Its tangential section is a convex
set in R^(d-1), and its standard intrinsic Gaussian probability is exactly

 pi_e=mu_e/phi(h_e/ell_e),  0<pi_e<1.                    (3)

The conditioned tangential mean is P_e m_e, and its covariance is Sigma_e
restricted to the tangent space. Applying (1) intrinsically gives

 E_e >= w_e a(pi_e)(P_e m_e)(P_e m_e)^T.                 (4)

The pi_e in (3) is NOT a cell mass. Controlling cell masses alone has not
been used to assert a lower bound for pi_e.

Set j_e=w_e(c_e+b_i-b_j). Its graph divergence is zero, so it is a cycle.
Use the accepted weighted gauge: partial_2 lambda=j and

 sum_e eta_e^T B_e eta_e=j^T W^{-1}j=CYC,

The second equality in the last expression uses orthogonality of j to the

Expansion using (5)--(6) now gives the exact, nonnegative identity

 K := sum_e z_e^T E_e z_e-CYC
    = sum_e r_e^T E_e r_e.                             (7)

This is not a bound on the total covariance deficit before subtracting CYC.
It identifies the remainder after subtracting the entire cycle mean energy.

Moreover (5) gives N_e^{-1}P_e m_e=1/w_e. Since sum_g eta_(e,g)=j_e,

Applying (4) to the residual vectors, not to the original z_e, proves

 K >= sum_e w_e a(pi_e)

All cycle coordinates and signs in (6)--(9) are the same as in the accepted

4. Refined covariance Hessian, with every factor retained
Let C(t)=I+tH, H symmetric, and let h_i(t) be the optimizing offsets at the
fixed target masses. In the accepted exact Hessian formula take z_i=H y_i.
The minimizing b in Section3 is b_i=2 h_i'(0) modulo a common constant:
the square-root score derivative is (H y_i dot X)/2+h_i'(0).
Since (I-P_e)m_e=-(h_e/ell_e)n_e, put
 q_e=d_e^T H d_e/ell_e^2.
Then (9) becomes
 K>=sum_e w_e a(pi_e)[2h_e'-h_e q_e]^2.
The remaining normal term of the exact Hessian is
 N=sum_e w_e |(I-P_e)H d_e|^2=sum_e w_e ell_e^2 q_e^2.
Consequently the refined bound is

 -4 D^2F(I)[H,H]
   = N+K
   >=sum_e w_e {ell_e^2 q_e^2
                   +a(pi_e)[2h_e'-h_e q_e]^2}.          (10)

Equivalently let ell_e(t)=|C(t)^(1/2)d_e|. Its derivative at0 is
ell_e q_e/2, so the offset bracket is exactly

 2h_e'-h_e q_e =2ell_e [h_e(t)/ell_e(t)]' at t=0.         (11)

Thus (10) controls both changing edge lengths and changing normalized facet
offsets. At any other positive definite base covariance, whitening applies
the same formulas with transformed sites; no conclusion at a singular base
is asserted by differentiating through it.

5. What the estimate leaves open
The new term in (10) is valid even when the raw cycle energy is large.
This resolves the specific logical failure of replacing K by the total
facet deficit. It does not yet yield a uniform O(log m) offset certificate:
that requires control of the actual intrinsic probabilities pi_e, graph
weights, and/or a justified integrated path. Formula(2) is logarithmic in
1/pi_e, not automatically logarithmic in m. Neither rank persistence of the
optimized centroid Gram nor a singular-covariance passage is proved here.
