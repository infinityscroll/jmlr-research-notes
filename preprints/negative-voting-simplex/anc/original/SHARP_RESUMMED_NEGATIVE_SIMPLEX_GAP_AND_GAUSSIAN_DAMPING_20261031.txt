SHARP RESUMMED NEGATIVE SIMPLEX GAP WITH MILD GAUSSIAN DAMPING
31 October 2026. Branch derivation; independent full audit pending.

This is a pointwise bound on the EXACT negative-noise reference potential.
It retains all chaoses. It replaces an exponentially-small-in-m gap
constant by the correct first-moment scale, and is asymptotically sharp
on typical Gaussian balls at voting correlations. It is not an arbitrary-
speed quadratic-operator bound or a full Standard Simplex theorem.

1. Gaussian utility weights, including all constants
Let Z_1,...,Z_m be independent standard normals, and define
  P_i(a)=P(Z_i+a_i=max_k(Z_k+a_k)), a in R^m.
For i!=j the derivative weight is
  w_ij(a)=int_R phi(t-a_i)phi(t-a_j)
                           product_(k!=i,j)Phi(t-a_k) dt. (1)
The Jacobian of P is the weighted Laplacian L(a): its diagonal is the
row sum of w_ij and its off-diagonal entry is -w_ij. Differentiation under
the one-dimensional Gaussian integral proves this directly; ties have
probability zero and all weights are positive.

At a=0 put
  w0=int phi(t)^2 Phi(t)^(m-2) dt
     =mu_m/[m(m-1)],  mu_m=E max_i Z_i.                 (2)
The equality follows by integration by parts of
m int t phi(t)Phi(t)^(m-1)dt.
Let T have probability density phi(t)^2Phi(t)^(m-2)/w0 and let M=E T.
For m>=2, M>=0; m=2 gives zero by symmetry, and for m>2 the density at t
is larger than at -t for t>0. Its potential has second derivative at
least2 because log Phi is concave. All exponential tilts have the same
curvature bound, so the one-dimensional Brascamp--Lieb variance bound
gives Var(T under tilt lambda)<=1/2. Integrating the second derivative
of the log moment-generating function gives, for every real lambda,

  E exp(lambda T)<=exp(lambda M+lambda^2/4).             (3)

All such moments exist by Gaussian domination. This uses the same
classical BL variance input as the covariance proof, not an unverified
concentration assumption. One also has the exact formula
  M=[E(max_i Z_i)^2-1]/mu_m,                            (4)
by the two integrations by parts in the exact second-chaos computation.
Consequently M^2=O(log m).

2. Uniform pair-weight comparison under bounded utility means
Assume max_i|a_i|<=U, U>=0. For a lower bound in (1), set t=u+U and
delta_i=U-a_i, delta_j=U-a_j, both in [0,2U]. The other CDF factors are
at least Phi(u). Thus
  w_ij(a)>=w0 E exp[-(delta_i+delta_j)T
                              -(delta_i^2+delta_j^2)/2]
           >=w0 exp[-4UM-4U^2],                        (5)
where the last step is Jensen, M>=0 and delta_i+delta_j<=4U.
For the upper bound set t=u-U and delta_i=U+a_i, delta_j=U+a_j.
The CDF factors are now at most Phi(u), so (3) yields
  w_ij(a)<=w0 E exp[(delta_i+delta_j)T
                              -(delta_i^2+delta_j^2)/2]
           <=w0 exp[4UM+4U^2].                         (6)
Common shifts of all means leave the weights unchanged. Thus U may always
be chosen as half the range of the mean vector, after centering its two
extremes, regardless of its original common offset. In particular

 e^(-E(U))w0<=w_ij(a)<=e^(E(U))w0,
 E(U)=4UM+4U^2.                                       (7)

No factor2^(-m), union bound over all cells, or tail truncation occurs.
The upper estimate may discard the favorable negative square term; this
does not affect its exact limiting value1 at U=0.

3. Exact integration of a two-label mean swap
Suppose a_j-a_i=delta>=0, with max|a_k|<=U. Hold all other means fixed,
and interpolate a_i,a_j symmetrically about their common midpoint, from
equal means at delta'=0 to their actual separation delta. Every point
on this interpolation still has coordinate absolute value at most U.
At its initial point P_i=P_j. Along it,
  d(P_j-P_i)/d delta'
    =2w_ij+(1/2)sum_(k!=i,j)(w_ik+w_jk).
The coefficients sum to m. Therefore (7) gives the exact comparison

  m w0 delta e^(-E(U))
      <=P_j(a)-P_i(a)
      <=m w0 delta e^(E(U)).                           (8)

This is a linear gap times a global damping factor, with no unjustified
large-gap saturation. At large U the lower damping is sufficient for
compatibility with probabilities bounded by1.

4. Application to the reference simplex at negative correlation
Work in H=1-perp subset R^m. The regular cells are g_i(x)=1{x_i=max_k x_k}.
Their first-moment vectors are
  b_i=alpha P e_i, alpha=mu_m/(m-1).
Let 0<r<1 and sigma=sqrt(1-r^2), and write G_i=T_-r g_i.
Adding a common independent normal coordinate does not affect the maximum,
so the utility representation uses a_i=-r x_i/sigma. At a reference
winning point x in g_i, a_j-a_i=r(x_i-x_j)/sigma>=0. Apply (8) with
  U_x=r[max_k x_k-min_k x_k]/(2sigma),
using the common-shift invariance and m w0=alpha to obtain

 (r/sigma)(b_i-b_j).x e^(-E(U_x))
       <=G_j(x)-G_i(x)
       <=(r/sigma)(b_i-b_j).x e^(E(U_x)).                 (9)

The comparison is exact for every x, including arbitrarily remote
winning facets. It also proves the correct strict ordering whenever
x_i>x_j. At a tie both sides and the potential difference vanish.

For a simpler Euclidean radial corollary, U_x<=r|x|/sigma and Young's
inequality gives 4UM<=1+4M^2U^2. Thus

 G_j(x)-G_i(x)
  >=e^-1(r/sigma)(b_i-b_j).x exp(-kappa_r |x|^2),
 kappa_r=4(M^2+1)r^2/(1-r^2).                         (10)

This keeps the r^2 log(m) dependence in the exponent instead of replacing
it by an m-independent positive radial exponent.

5. Actual assignment loss and the size of the damping moments
For any relaxed assignment f (balanced or not), define
  J_f(x)=max_i b_i.x-sum_i f_i(x)b_i.x>=0,
  D_-(f)=sum_i E[(f_i-g_i)G_i].
At each reference-winning label i, sum the inequalities (9) against
f_j(x). This gives

 (r/sigma)E[J_f(X)e^(-E(U_X))]
       <=D_-(f)
       <=(r/sigma)E[J_f(X)e^(E(U_X))],            (11)

whenever the upper expression is finite; the pointwise statement remains
valid without an integrability assertion. In particular the lower bound
is always well defined and
  D_-(f)>=e^-1(r/sigma)E[J_f(X)e^(-kappa_r|X|^2)].       (12)

Now restrict to the full voting interval 0<r<=1/(m-1). Since M^2=O(log m),
  kappa_r=O(log m/m^2).
For each fixed q>0, for all sufficiently large m,
  E exp(q kappa_r|X|^2)
       =(1-2q kappa_r)^(-(m-1)/2)
       =exp(O_q(log m/m)),                             (13)
uniformly in this entire interval. Thus these damping compensation moments
remain bounded and tend1, unlike the earlier fixed radial exponent whose
Gaussian moments grew exponentially with dimension.

More sharply, on the set range(x_i)<=R sqrt(log m),
  E(U_x)=O_R(r log m+r^2 log m)=O_R(log m/m),
  1/sigma=1+O(1/m^2).
Hence (9) has relative error O_R(log m/m) against the first-chaos gap
r(b_i-b_j).x, uniformly in r and all labels on this set. This includes
points arbitrarily close to a facet; no lower distance to a tie is imposed.
For X in H, write X=PZ with independent standard Gaussian coordinates Z.
Their ranges are identical, and the union bound gives
  P(range(X_i)>R sqrt(log m))<=2m^(1-R^2/8).
Thus any fixed R>sqrt(8) gives a set of probability tending1. The range
comparison is the primary pointwise estimate; the Euclidean damping in
(10)-(13) is a convenient integrable corollary.

6. Remaining operator issue
Equations (9)-(13) control the reference potential and the actual linear
assignment loss. They do not bound the quadratic term
<f-g,T_-r(f-g)> by that loss with a usable relative constant, and do not
give a weighted-L2 interface operator inequality for arbitrary normal
speeds. In particular they do not justify a separate global per-chaos
comparison: remote tangential deformations can make such ratios unbounded.
The next required step is a full weighted, resummed quadratic comparison,
not removal of the Gaussian channel or an additive chaos truncation.
