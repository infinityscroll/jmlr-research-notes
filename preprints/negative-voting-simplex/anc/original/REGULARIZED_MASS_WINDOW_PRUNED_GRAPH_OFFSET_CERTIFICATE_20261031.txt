1. Setup and conclusion

  W_m={p:sum p_i=1, c/m<=p_i<=C0/m}.
Assume m is sufficiently large depending only on c,C0; in particular m>=3.
Work on H=1-perp, dimension d=m-1, with P=I-11^T/m. For positive masses,
  F_p(C)=sup E[Z_I], Z~N(0,C), P(I=i)=p_i,
  V(p)=max_{C>=0,C1=0} {2F_p(C)-tr C},
  V_eps(p)=max_{C>=eps P,C1=0} {2F_p(C)-tr C}.
Here and below the covariance inequalities and trace are on H. There is
no added trace constraint or trace multiplier.

Let C_eps(p) denote the unique constrained optimizer. There is an
eps0(m,c,C0)>0 such that, for every 0<eps<=eps0 and p in W_m, the
mean-zero balanced-to-p offset h(C,p) satisfies, for every symmetric

  ||D_C h(C_eps,p)[A]||^2
       <= K(c,C0) L_m^2 [-D_C^2 F_p(C_eps)[A,A]],        (1)

where L_m=1+log(m/c), and one valid constant is

  K(c,C0)=128 C0^2 pi [56 exp(3/2)+2]/c^2.              (2)

The same conclusion, with a fixed-factor enlargement of K, holds in a

envelope argument with a constant independent of eps.

2. Primal interpretation, compactness and the covariance floor
Factor C=B B^T, using m centered score rows b_i in R^d. The linear
assignment principle gives
  V(p)=max_f sum_i |int x f_i dgamma|^2,
where f has masses p. Indeed optimize B in
2 sum_i b_i.int x f_i-||B||_F^2. At a maximizing C, choose any maximizing
pure assignment f and write M_i=int x f_i. Then
  2F_p(C)-tr C=||M||_F^2-||B-M||_F^2<=V(p),
so equality forces B=M. This does not presume full rank.

Coercivity F_p(C)<=sqrt(tr C) implies existence and a common finite trace
bound for optimizers when eps d<=1. This bound is only a compactness
argument: the optimization itself has no trace cap. Strict covariance
concavity at positive C gives uniqueness of C_eps(p).
Adding independent centered Gaussian noise to an optimal coupling shows
F_p(C+epsP)>=F_p(C), so

  0<=V(p)-V_eps(p)<=eps d.                              (3)

The assignment support is continuous jointly in C,p when p stays in a
compact positive mass window. For example, the offset dual has uniformly
bounded minimizers on bounded covariance sets, using p_min>0 and score
integrability. Therefore any sequence eps_n->0, p_n->p and constrained
optimizers has covariance accumulation points that maximize V(p).
This also proves uniform nearness to the true optimal set on W_m, for
each fixed m. No continuity or uniqueness of singular true optimizers is
needed for this compactness statement.

3. Uniform pair separation at every true optimizer
For a pure maximizing assignment, fix i!=j and keep A_i union A_j fixed.
Choose a unit direction e perpendicular to b_i+b_j; this is possible
because d>=2. The projected, unnormalized measure of the union has total
mass p_i+p_j, total first moment0, and density at most phi(0).
Re-split it into an upper p_i tail and a lower p_j tail. If t is the split
threshold, the density cap implies
  int_upper (x-t)>=p_i^2/[2phi(0)],
  int_lower (t-x)>=p_j^2/[2phi(0)].
Thus the two conditional means differ by at least
(p_i+p_j)/[2phi(0)]. Their total projected moment is zero, so the new
projected first-moment DIFFERENCE is at least p_i p_j/phi(0).
The common vector sum b_i+b_j is unchanged. Maximality of sum|b_i|^2
therefore implies

  |b_i-b_j|>=sqrt(2pi) p_i p_j.                        (4)

For the upper bound, Gaussian exponential Jensen on any event of mass p
gives |int_A x|<=p sqrt(2log(1/p)). Hence on W_m,
  |b_i-b_j|<=2C0 sqrt(2L_m)/m.
The uniform optimizer compactness from Section2 transfers these bounds,
with fixed slack, to constrained optimizers for sufficiently small eps:

  ell_min=sqrt(2pi)c^2/(2m^2)<=ell_ij
       <=4C0 sqrt(2L_m)/m=ell_max.                     (5)

This argument does not apply the re-splitting optimality assertion to a
regularized assignment. It applies it to every true accumulation point.
All constrained score rows are affinely independent because C_eps>0.

4. KKT and the actual facet graph
Let mu_ij be the intrinsic Gaussian surface mass of the winning facet,
ell_ij the score-row separation, and w_ij=mu_ij/ell_ij. Let L be the
corresponding weighted graph Laplacian. Price differentiation of the
offset-minimized support gives grad_C F_p=L/2 on H. Equivalently this is
the Gaussian second-derivative formula for the affine maximum, with its
optimizing offset held by the envelope theorem. The weights here are the
ACTUAL facet weights, not a prescribed complete-graph surrogate.

KKT for maximization of the concave objective on C>=epsP yields

  0<=L<=P,   (P-L)(C-epsP)=0.                          (6)

In particular the maximum weighted degree is at most1. For reference,
Gaussian boundary IBP gives M=LB, so with the symmetric factor B=C^(1/2)
the complementarity identity gives B-M=sqrt(eps)(P-L). Thus the residual
also tends0, without a rank assumption. This residual is not needed to
replace the compactness argument in Section3.

For any label set S, its union of cells has Gaussian perimeter equal to
sum_{i in S,j notin S}mu_ij; codimension-two ties contribute no perimeter.
Gaussian isoperimetry and concavity/symmetry of I(p)=phi(Phi^-1(p)) give
  sum_cut mu_ij>=I(p(S))>=2phi(0)min(p(S),1-p(S)).
If k=min(|S|,m-|S|), the mass window and (5) imply

  sum_cut w_ij>= h0 k,
  h0=c/[4C0 sqrt(pi L_m)].                             (7)

Retain only edges with w_ij>=m^-4, and call their Laplacian L_str.
A cut loses at most k m^-3. For sufficiently large m, m^-3<=h0/2, so
every retained cut has conductance at least h0/2. Retained degrees remain
at most1. The elementary weighted Cheeger inequality then gives

  z^T L_str z>=lambda_*||z||^2 for sum z_i=0,
  lambda_*=c^2/[128 C0^2 pi L_m].                      (8)

For clarity, the Cheeger inequality used here is lambda_2>=h^2/(2dmax)
with COUNTING measure. Its proof applies coarea to v_i^2 for a nonnegative
vector supported on at most half the vertices and then Cauchy:
h sum v_i^2<=sum_edges w|v_i^2-v_j^2|
 <=sqrt[(sum w(v_i-v_j)^2)(2dmax sum v_i^2)].
Split an arbitrary vector at a median, and use that its squared distance
to a median is at least its squared distance to its mean. This proves (8)
with the stated unordered-edge convention.

5. Intrinsic probabilities and normalized offsets on retained edges
Let q_ij=h_ij/ell_ij. Each cell i has mass at least c/m and is contained
in its winning halfspace against j, and conversely. Therefore

  |q_ij|<=Phi^-1(1-c/m),   q_ij^2<=2L_m.               (9)

On an affine facet its intrinsic STANDARD tangential probability is
  pi_ij=mu_ij/phi(q_ij).
For a retained edge, (5), w_ij>=m^-4 and phi(q)<=phi(0) give

  pi_ij>=pi c^2/m^6.                                  (10)

No such bound is asserted for the deleted edges. For the Ehrhard
coefficient a(p)=1+Phi^-1(p)p/phi(Phi^-1(p)), the accepted elementary
bound a(p)^-1<=8 exp(3/2)[1+log(1/p)] gives from (10)

  a(pi_ij)^-1<=56 exp(3/2)L_m                         (11)

on every retained edge. In particular small individual weights outside
the retained graph cause no unproved uniformity assumption.

7. Local persistence and scope for the envelope
For each fixed m,eps and positive mass vector, offsets and facet data are
smooth locally because all sites are affinely independent and all cell
masses are positive. The covariance Hessian is strictly negative definite.
The particular finite retained graph at an optimizer can be kept fixed
in a sufficiently small neighborhood. Its weights, gap and facet bounds

that inequality was used to establish the optimizer's graph bounds, which
then persist by continuity.
