GLOBAL RANK-FREE BALANCED AFFINE SIGNED HIGHER-TAIL COMPARISON
31 October 2026. strong_signal_prior_art. Full independent audit requested.

THEOREM
Put L=1+log m, alpha=E max_(i<=m) Z_i/(m-1), and
w=alpha^2(m-1). Let N>=m-1 and let B be a centered m-by-N score
matrix normalized by ||B||_F^2=w. There is NO lower rank, singular-
value, pair-distance, equal-row, or facet-mass hypothesis. Let f be
a measurable simplex-valued balanced assignment that maximizes the
linear score at these prescribed masses. Equivalently, it is supported
on the maximizers of B_i.X+h_i for optimal offsets h. Arbitrary
measurable or fractional splitting of coincident optimal scores is
allowed. Define
 R=R_U(B)=w-F_U(B),
 H_r(f)=<f,T_-r Pi_(>=3)f>=sum_(k>=3)(-r)^k A_k(f),
 C_m=m L^11+m^(7/4)L^(7/2).
For all sufficiently large m, with an absolute constant C, uniformly
in 0<r<=1/(m-1),

                 |H_r(f)-H_r(g)|<=C r^3 C_m R.               (1)

Here g is a regular-simplex partition, and constants do not depend
on the ambient dimension or on the splitting of coincident scores.
In particular r^2 C_m tends to zero uniformly on the voting interval.
The ENTIRE higher tail and its signs are retained. No convergence of
individual label tensors under singular score regularization is used.

Combining(1) with[A] gives, for its rank-free balanced equal-row affine
class (scores scaled to the normalization above),

 S_-r(f)-S_-r(g)
 >=r[w-W(f)]-[C r^2 L+C r^3 C_m]R.                           (2)

Equivalently the right side is r[w-W(f)]-eta_m rR, where eta_m->0
uniformly for voting r. This does NOT replace w-W(f) by R, or assert
global full-noise positivity. Their exact distinction is retained in
Section7. Unequal rows are included in(1), but not in the A2 input
needed for(2).

1. A global covariance remainder from a short reference bootstrap
Let B0 be a closest regular centroid matrix in Frobenius norm. Write
 P=I-J/m, K=BB^T/alpha^2, E=K-P,
 e=||E||_F, q=||K^(1/2)-P||_F, d=||B-B0||_F=alpha q.
The Procrustes identity is valid for singular B as well: extend its
partial polar isometry into R^N, using N>=m-1. Since ||B||^2=w,
tr K=m-1 and tr E=0. By spectral calculus, e>=q.

The INITIAL bootstrap of[F, Sections1-4] gives absolute constants
c1>0 and m0 such that, whenever ||K'-P||_F<=c1/b^2, with
b=Phi^(-1)(1-1/m), the balanced covariance functional satisfies
 -d^2[alpha mathcal F(K'+tH)]/dt^2|_(t=0)
                          >=alpha^2||H||_F^2/(12m).           (3)
Here mathcal F(K')=F_U((K')^(1/2)). This is the retained normal-strain
term and the balanced facet-density bootstrap, not an endpoint
conditioning claim.

We extend its finite remainder to ANY positive semidefinite endpoint
K. Along K_t=P+tE, take
 s=min(1/2,c1/[b^2 e]),
with s=1/2 if e=0. On 0<=t<=s, equation(3) applies with direction E.
Every K_t for t<1 is positive definite on1-perp, and the covariance
functional is concave all the way to the possibly singular endpoint
by[C]. Its tangent derivative at P is (alpha^2/2)tr E=0. Hence the
nonnegative distributional curvature measure, or direct concavity
after the short segment, yields

 R>=c alpha^2 e^2/m *min(1,c1/[b^2 e]).                       (4)

For clarity, the factor is s rather than s^2: the tangent remainder
integrates -phi''(t) with weight1-t, which is at least1/2 on this
initial interval. Continuity of F_U at singular covariances follows
from Gaussian square-root coupling, so no derivative at t=1 is used.
This extension of the initial bootstrap requires neither a bound
e<=1/4 nor regularity of the endpoint facets. The small-neighborhood
restriction in[F]'s final score statement is not imported here.

Since alpha^2 is comparable to L/m^2 and b^2 to L, for all sufficiently
large m the following consequence holds:

                  d>1/m  implies  R>=c q/m^3.                (5)

Indeed q=d/alpha>1/(m alpha)>=c/sqrt(L), e>=q, and consequently
b^2 e is large. The second branch of(4) gives R>=c e/m^3>=c q/m^3.

2. Optimality controls routing to the regular reference
Let M_i=E[X f_i]. The accepted balanced first-moment endpoint[C] gives
||M||_F^2=W(f)<=w; it applies to simplex-valued assignments as well.
Let g be the regular partition whose centroid matrix is B0. Its
fixed-reference routing cost is

 R0=w-<B0,M>
   =E sum_i f_i(X)[max_j B0_j.X-B0_i.X]>=0.                   (6)

Because f is a prescribed-mass linear optimum for B,
<B,M-B0> >=0. With delta B=B-B0 this implies
 R0<=<delta B,M-B0> <=d||M-B0||_F.
Also
 ||M-B0||_F^2=W(f)-w+2R0<=2R0.
If R0=0 nothing needs division. Otherwise these inequalities give

                              R0<=2d^2.                     (7)

Thus score optimality couples routing to score distance globally.
No inverse score matrix or own-centroid fixed-point assumption is
present. In particular B is not assumed to equal M.

3. The regular margin and a dimension-free mismatch estimate
Let c(X) be the difference between the largest and second largest
regular centroid scores B0_i.X. The elementary one-label exit-margin
bound[M, Sections1-2], used ONLY at the regular reference, gives
 P(c<=a)<=C m a for 0<a<=a_*, where a_* is comparable to1/m.
Indeed each of the m disjoint winning cells contributes at most C a:
lowering its own offset by a changes its mass by the integral of its
offset-Laplacian degree, uniformly bounded on this short interval.
For a>=a_*, the same bound follows from probability<=1 after enlarging
the absolute constant. Hence it holds for every a>0.

Write
 epsilon=E[1-sum_i f_i g_i]=(1/2)sum_i||f_i-g_i||_1.
Pointwise the integrand in(6) is at least c(X) times the mismatch
weight1-f_(old winner)(X). Consequently, for every a>0,
 epsilon<=P(c<=a)+R0/a<=Cma+R0/a.
Optimize a, and also use epsilon<=1. With(7),

                 epsilon<=min(1,C sqrt(m)d).                 (8)

This remains true for fractional f and for arbitrary splitting of
coincident scores. It is a ROUTING estimate in terms of score distance,
not the unproved sharp global partition-coercivity bound in terms of
w-W(f).

4. A full signed-tail continuity estimate with a rare-label factor
This step applies to ANY two balanced simplex assignments f,g; no
affine hypothesis is used. Let t^2=1/(4L) and p=2/(1+t^2)>1.
Gaussian hypercontractivity gives
 sum_i||T_t f_i||_2^2<=sum_i||f_i||_(1+t^2)^2
                         <=m*m^(-p)<=C/m,
and the same for g. Here 0<=f_i<=1 and int f_i=1/m.

Set h=f-g and e_i=||h_i||_1. Then |h_i|<=1, e_i<=2/m, and
sum_i e_i=2epsilon. Therefore
 sum_i||T_t h_i||_2^2<=sum_i e_i^p
       <=(2/m)^(p-1)sum_i e_i<=C epsilon/m.                  (9)
The constants are absolute, because
m^(2t^2/(1+t^2))<=exp(1/2). The exponent t is fixed by m and never
depends on epsilon. Thus(9) retains arbitrarily tiny mismatch amplitude.

For large m, sqrt(r)<=t throughout the voting interval. Hermite
orthogonality and the semigroup property give, for any v,
 ||T_(sqrt(r)) Pi_(>=3)v||_2
                   <=(sqrt(r)/t)^3||T_t v||_2.
Reflection Jv(X)=v(-X) is a unitary operator and
T_-r=T_(sqrt(r)) J T_(sqrt(r)). The exact difference identity is

 H_r(f)-H_r(g)
 =<T_(sqrt(r))Pi_(>=3)(f-g),
                          J T_(sqrt(r))Pi_(>=3)(f+g)>.

Cauchy, (9), and the preceding rare-label bounds prove

             |H_r(f)-H_r(g)|<=C r^3 L^3 sqrt(epsilon)/m.      (10)

Every Hermite degree>=3 is included in this identity. Reflection
retains its sign before Cauchy; no assertion of positivity or a
coefficientwise comparison has been made. The series converge
absolutely in the usual Hermite quadratic norm for r<1.

5. The global relative bound outside the score ball
Suppose d>1/m. From(8), d=alpha q, and alpha<=C sqrt(L)/m,
 sqrt(epsilon)<=C L^(1/4)m^(-1/4)q^(1/2).
The right side may exceed1; it is still a valid upper bound. Inserting
it into(10) gives
 |H_r(f)-H_r(g)|<=C r^3 L^(13/4)m^(-5/4)q^(1/2).
Equation(5) gives R>=c q/m^3, and q>=c/sqrt(L). Consequently

 |H_r(f)-H_r(g)|
 <=C r^3 m^(7/4)L^(13/4)q^(-1/2) R
 <=C r^3 m^(7/4)L^(7/2)R.                                   (11)

This is genuinely relative to R. It covers singular endpoints and
coincident-score splitting directly, rather than by convergence of
individual label partitions under regularization.

6. The inside ball and the common statement
If d<=1/m, then for large m the score matrix is full rank on1-perp,
because alpha is comparable to sqrt(L)/m. The unique balanced affine
assignment is covered by[T] with its fixed A=1. It gives

                   |H_r(f)-H_r(g)|<=C r^3 m L^11 R.          (12)

Fractional splitting can differ only on Gaussian-null tie sets in
this full-rank case. Equations(11)-(12) prove(1) with an absolute
constant. They overlap at the boundary; no qualitative neighborhood
or unquantified amplitude limit is required. The comparison with the
chosen closest regular g is sufficient because its noise stability
and every chaos weight are invariant under ambient rotations.

7. Combining the A2 input without changing the first-chaos term
For the balanced equal-row affine class of[A], scale its unit rows
by beta=mu_m/sqrt(m(m-1)). This produces ||B||^2=w without changing
the assignment; multiply its balancing offsets by the same beta.
Its exact R_U is the R used here. Hermite orthogonality gives
 S_-r(f)-S_-r(g)=r[w-W(f)]+r^2[A2(f)-A2(g)]
                                      +H_r(f)-H_r(g).
Insert[A] and(1) to obtain(2). Uniformly for voting r, the coefficient
of rR lost here is at most
 C[L/m+L^11/m+L^(7/2)/m^(1/4)],
which tends to zero.

The exact identity for any normalized balanced affine optimum is
 2R=w-W(f)+||M-B||_F^2.
Thus w-W(f)<=2R, not a proved lower bound by a fixed multiple of R.
Equation(2) is the requested GLOBAL signed-tail/reference reduction;
it does not by itself establish a full-noise sign. Nor does it imply
that arbitrary actual minimizers are affine, balanced, or equal-row.
