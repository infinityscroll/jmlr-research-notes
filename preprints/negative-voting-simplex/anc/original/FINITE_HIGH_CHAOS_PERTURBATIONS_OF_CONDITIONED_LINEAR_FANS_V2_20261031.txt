FINITE HIGH-CHAOS PERTURBATIONS OF CONDITIONED LINEAR FANS
31 October 2026. Branch derivation. Independent hostile audit pending.

THEOREM
Fix c0,C0,Cp>0. Let m be sufficiently large, alpha=E max_i Z_i/(m-1),
b=Phi^(-1)(1-1/m), and N>=m-1. Suppose a centered score matrix B has
rank exactly m-1 and its m-1 nonzero singular values in[c0 alpha,C0 alpha].
Its affine winning cells
A_i, with offsets h_i, have masses0<p_i<=Cp/m. Put
 l_i(x)=B_i.x+h_i, w(x)=argmax_i l_i(x).
For a vector perturbation psi in L^q, q>=2, define its exact max-Bregman
remainder
 J_tau(psi)=E[max_i(l_i+tau psi_i)-max_i l_i-tau psi_w].
Let V_q^2=sum_i||psi_i||_q^2 and A=|tau|V_q. There are constants C,c>0,
depending only on c0,C0,Cp, such that with a0=c alpha/b,

 0<=J_tau(psi)
 <=C[m^(1/(q+1)) A^(2q/(q+1))+a0(A/a0)^q].                     (1)

In particular, for fixed beta>1+delta>1, Q>0, q=Q log m, and
 m^(-beta)<=A<=m^(-1-delta),
                         J_tau(psi)<=C_(beta,delta,Q) A^2.       (2)
The constants do not depend on N or offset magnitudes. For
psi=T_s Pi_(>=k)u and s^2(q-1)<=1, one may replace A in(1) by

 A=|tau|[s^2(q-1)]^(k/2) ||u||_(L2(vector)).                    (3)

This is a finite nonlinear result, not merely an interface Hessian. The
baseline must be a conditioned AFFINE Gaussian score fan. No assertion
identifies the full resummed reference potential with these linear scores.

1. Small offset shifts preserve the rare-cell upper bound
For any original cell A of Gaussian mass p<=Cp/m, and a translation v,
Gaussian Cameron--Martin and Holder with q0=1+log m give
 gamma(A-v)<=p^(1-1/q0) exp[(q0-1)|v|^2/2].                      (4)
Raising or lowering ONE label offset by a is, modulo a common score
shift, exactly the translation v=B^+(a P e_i). Thus
 |v|<=|a|/(c0 alpha).
Choose c so that for |a|<=a_*:=4a0=4c alpha/b, the right side of(4)
is at most C'/m, since
b^2~2log m. This holds simultaneously for all labels, including those
whose original mass is much smaller than1/m. There is no lower-mass
assumption or change of the score matrix B.

For every such shifted fan, Gaussian integration by parts gives M=L B.
The centroid bound ||M_i||<=p_i sqrt(2log(1/p_i))<=C b/m and the
pseudoinverse bound ||B^+||<=1/(c0 alpha) imply
                   deg_i(L)=L_ii<=||L_i||<=C.                   (5)
These constants are uniform along all the offset intervals just described.
The exact derivative of a cell mass in its own offset is deg_i(L).

2. Both one-label margins have linear small-sublevel bounds
Define the target-entry margin on the complement of A_j by
 c_j^+(x)=max_i l_i(x)-l_j(x)>0,
and the source-exit margin on A_i by
 c_i^-(x)=l_i(x)-max_(j!=i) l_j(x)>0.
Ties have Gaussian measure zero. The two exact set identities are
 {x outside A_j: c_j^+(x)<=a}=A_j(h_j+a)\A_j(h_j),
 {x in A_i: c_i^-(x)<=a}=A_i(h_i)\A_i(h_i-a).
Consequently, by integrating(5), for 0<a<=a_*,
 gamma(c_j^+<=a, x outside A_j)<=C a,
 gamma(c_i^-<=a, x in A_i)<=C a.                               (6)
This uses the MAX margin, not a sum over ordered pair margins. In
particular, no uncontrolled multiplicity of nearly tied labels appears.

3. Exact separation of the finite maximum remainder
At a point with old winner i, write c_ij=l_i-l_j>=0. For real numbers
a,b and c>=0, convexity of the positive part gives
 (a+b-c)_+ <=[(2a-c)_++(2b-c)_+]/2.
Apply this with a=tau psi_j, b=-tau psi_i, then take the largest new
winning-label improvement. The target terms can be summed, while the
source term uses only the smallest old gap. After integration,
 J_tau(psi)
 <=(1/2)sum_j int_(outside A_j)(2tau psi_j-c_j^+)_+ dgamma
   +(1/2)sum_i int_(A_i)(-2tau psi_i-c_i^-)_+ dgamma.             (7)
The identity defining J_tau is nonnegative pointwise. Equation(7)
remains valid for arbitrary measurable perturbations with finite moments;
no differentiability of perturbed interfaces is required.

4. A finite Lq bathtub estimate
Suppose a nonnegative cost c on a measure space satisfies
 measure{c<=a}<=C a for0<a<=a_*. Let v have Lq norm W, q>=2. On
D={v>c, c<=a_*}, with theta=measure(D), the cost distribution bound gives
 int_D c>=theta^2/(2C),
while Holder gives int_D v<=W theta^(1-1/q). Optimizing the difference
over theta>=0 proves
 int_(c<=a_*)(v-c)_+<=C' W^(2q/(q+1)),                          (8)
with C' uniformly bounded for q>=2 when C is fixed. To justify the
cost bound, theta<=C a_*, and integration of(theta-Ct)_+ from0 to a_*
gives exactly theta^2/(2C).
On c>a_*,
 (v-c)_+<=|v| 1_(|v|>a_*)<=|v|^q/a_*^(q-1).
Therefore
                 int(v-c)_+<=C W^(2q/(q+1))+W^q/a_*^(q-1).      (9)

Use(9) for all the terms in(7). Their Lq norms are at most
2|tau| ||psi_i||_q. The direct conclusion is
 J_tau<=C m^(1/(q+1))A^(2q/(q+1))
              +(2A)^q/a_*^(q-1).                              (10)
The choice a_*=4a0 made in Section1 absorbs the factors2 without an
exponential-in-q loss in the theorem's constant, because
 (2A)^q/(4a0)^(q-1)<=4 a0(A/a0)^q
for q>=2. The near part retains the same absolute constant. This proves(1).
The label sums used here are, with p=2q/(q+1),
 sum_i||psi_i||_q^p<=m^(1-p/2)V_q^p=m^(1/(q+1))V_q^p,
 sum_i||psi_i||_q^q<=V_q^q.
There is no ambient-dimensional norm or a factor m from summing ordered
pair costs.

5. Polynomial amplitudes and Gaussian high degrees
Divide the first term of(1) by A^2. For q=Q log m and A>=m^(-beta),
 m^(1/(q+1))A^(-2/(q+1))<=exp((1+2beta)/Q).
For A<=m^(-1-delta) and a0 comparable to1/m,
 [a0(A/a0)^q]/A^2=(A/a0)^(q-2)/a0 tends0 faster than any fixed
power of1/m. This proves(2), uniformly over the stated amplitude interval.

Finally hypercontractivity and the spectral gap in Hermite degree give
 ||T_s Pi_(>=k)u_i||_q
 <=||T_(s sqrt(q-1)) Pi_(>=k)u_i||_2
 <=[s^2(q-1)]^(k/2)||u_i||_2
whenever s^2(q-1)<=1. Summing squares proves(3). Since the right side
of(1) is increasing in A, that upper value of A may safely be substituted.
When invoking the simplified conclusion(2) with this substituted upper
amplitude, its lower polynomial bound is imposed on that upper amplitude,
not claimed automatically for the actual Lq norm.

6. Prescribed masses and the exact remaining application gate
Let the original offsets h be optimal for prescribed masses p. Define
the perturbed fixed-mass score value
 F_p(B;tau psi)=sup_(E f=p) E sum_i f_i(B_i.X+tau psi_i).
In the offset dual, using the original h remains admissible, so
 F_p(B;tau psi)-F_p(B)-tau E sum_i1_(A_i)psi_i
                                      <=J_tau(psi).             (11)
Thus(1)-(3) give a genuine finite higher-degree remainder around every
conditioned affine fan, with no assumed topology of the perturbed winner
sets. The lower bound0 in(1) concerns J_tau; the left side of(11) is also
nonnegative by testing the old assignment.

For voting-scale high odd degrees, the relevant effective amplitude can
be polynomially small (for example order m^-2), so(2) supplies a constant
quadratic bound even though the raw first-chaos cap has the saturation
obstruction already recorded elsewhere. A complete negative-noise proof
must still connect these affine score values and perturbations to the
actual full potential, retain all even terms, and justify any required
score-radius or amplitude hypotheses. None of those steps is asserted here.
