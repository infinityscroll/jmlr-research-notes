RANK-FREE BOUNDED-ROW AFFINE OFFSET GAP AND DEFICIT-RELATIVE OFFSETS
31 October 2026. New analytic candidate; independent audit pending.

No lower singular value, pair-separation, equal-row or radial-exposure
assumption appears. The physical graph statement is for DISTINCT sites
with positive cell masses. Duplicate sites are treated separately at the
value/offset level, not by pretending their 1/ell edge weights are finite.

Notation: L=1+log m, alpha=E max_(i<=m) Z_i/(m-1),
w=alpha^2(m-1), U=(1/m,...,1/m). Thus alpha is comparable to sqrt(L)/m.
Constants may depend on fixed0<cp<Cp and C0, but not m or ambient N.
All statements below hold for sufficiently large m.

THEOREM A. Physical affine graph, with no rank requirement
Let B_i be distinct sites in a finite Gaussian R^N, centered by
sum_i B_i=0 and satisfying max_i|B_i|<=C0 alpha. Let the affine
maximizer cells of B_i.X+h_i have masses cp/m<=p_i<=Cp/m. Write
 ell_ij=|B_i-B_j|, mu_ij=int_(Sigma_ij) gamma_N dH^(N-1),
 w_ij=mu_ij/ell_ij, and mathcal L for the label Laplacian with weights w.
Zero-area/nonadjacent facets have weight0. Then

             z^T mathcal L z>=c L^-1 |z|^2 for z perpendicular1. (1)

The sites may be collinear, nonvertices, or have deficient affine span.
Every cell has positive Gaussian measure by hypothesis.

THEOREM B. Mass values and offsets, including duplicates
Let N>=m-1, B centered and max_i|B_i|<=C0 alpha, now allowing coincident
rows and any rank. On a fixed positive mass box cp/m<=p_i<=Cp/m,
let F_p(B) be the prescribed-mass linear-score value and let h(p) be the
mean-zero optimal offset vector, using maximizers of B_i.X+h_i. Then

 |h(p)-h(q)|<=C L |p-q|,                                    (2)
 -C L |p-q|^2<=F_p(B)-F_q(B)+h(q).(p-q)<=0.                 (3)

The vector h is common on each duplicate group; fractional allocation
inside that group is allowed. No convergence of individual allocations
is asserted or needed.

COROLLARY C. Global deficit-relative balanced offsets
Under Theorem B, additionally normalize ||B||F^2=w and put
R_U(B)=w-F_U(B)>=0. Then

                       |h(U)|^2<=C L^2 R_U(B).              (4)

This is global in the score covariance, subject only to the row cap.
For p in a fixed positive box one also obtains
 |F_p(B)-F_U(B)|<=C L sqrt(R_U(B))|p-U|+C L|p-U|^2.          (5)
These are offset/value conclusions. They do not prove a full-noise sign,
centroid rank, equal row norms, or proximity to the regular score orbit.

1. Exact physical row identity
For distinct sites, the cells are convex polyhedra and have finite
Gaussian perimeter. Let M_i=int_(cell i) x dgamma. Gaussian integration
by parts, with outward normal (B_j-B_i)/ell_ij, gives

                 M_i=sum_j w_ij(B_i-B_j)=(mathcal L B)_i.   (6)

Let v_i=|B_i|^2 and k_i=sum_j w_ij ell_ij^2. The exact identity is

                    k_i=2B_i.M_i-(mathcal L v)_i.           (7)

It is important to retain the second term when row norms differ.
Gaussian one-dimensional rearrangement gives |M_i|<=I(p_i), where
I(p)=phi(Phi^-1(p)); the elementary entropy bound also suffices.
The mass upper bound therefore implies

                       max_i |M_i|<=C sqrt(L)/m.             (8)

No relation between M_i and B_i beyond(6) is assumed.

2. A weighted Cheeger estimate with its row error retained
For a real label vector z define
 E(z)=sum_(i<j) w_ij(z_i-z_j)^2,
 K(z)=sum_(i<j) w_ij ell_ij^2(z_i+z_j)^2,
 S(z)=sum_(i<j) mu_ij |z_i^2-z_j^2|.
By Cauchy,

                            S(z)<=sqrt(E(z)K(z)).            (9)

Equations(7)-(8) give, with Z=|z| and Bmax=max|B_i|,
 K(z)<=2sum_i z_i^2 k_i
      =4sum_i z_i^2 B_i.M_i
           -2sum_(i<j)w_ij(v_i-v_j)(z_i^2-z_j^2)
      <=A Z^2+4Bmax sqrt(E(z)K(z)),                          (10)
where A=4Bmax max_i|M_i|. The last step uses exactly
 |v_i-v_j|<=2Bmax ell_ij.
Consequently

               sqrt(K(z))<=sqrt(A)Z+4Bmax sqrt(E(z)).        (11)

Suppose z>=0 and the union of its positive labels has Gaussian mass at
most1/2. Gaussian isoperimetry, in its elementary consequence
P_gamma(E)>=2phi(0)gamma(E) for gamma(E)<=1/2, and discrete coarea give
 S(z)=int_0^infinity P_gamma(union_{z_i^2>t} cell_i)dt
     >=2phi(0)sum_i p_i z_i^2>=h Z^2,
where h=2phi(0)cp/m. Thus, writing u=sqrt(E(z))/Z when Z>0,

                  h<=sqrt(A)u+4Bmax u^2.                    (12)

At least one term on the right is at least h/2. It follows that
 E(z)/Z^2>=min[h^2/(4A),h/(8Bmax)]
          >=c/[m Bmax sqrt(L)]>=c/L.                        (13)
The last inequality uses the row cap and alpha comparable to sqrt(L)/m.
This proof does not require an upper bound on the physical graph degrees.
Tiny distances make w large; they do not spoil the argument.

For arbitrary z perpendicular1, choose a median a for the probability
weights p_i. Both z_+=(z-a)_+ and z_-=(a-z)_+ have positive support mass
at most1/2. Edgewise,
 E(z)>=E(z_+)+E(z_-),
while |z_+|^2+|z_-|^2=|z-a1|^2>=|z|^2.
Apply(13) twice to prove(1). This also proves connectedness/coercivity of
the physical offset graph quantitatively.

3. Physical degeneracies and the offset derivative
For distinct sites and positive masses, cells have nonempty interior.
A whole facet cannot have three distinct affine scores identically tied
while all three cells have positive volume: the three affine functions
would be collinear combinations, and the middle one could never win
strictly. Thus genuine facets are unsplit apart from lower-dimensional
sets. This statement allows arbitrary rank and bounded cells.

Ordinary Gaussian coarea gives the derivative of the mass vector with
respect to offsets as mathcal L. These first derivatives are continuous
on the positive-cell region. One can see this on each fixed pair plane:
all other restrictions have boundary of surface measure zero unless a
whole-facet triple degeneracy occurs, which was just excluded. Gaussian
domination then proves continuity of the facet integrals. Existence of
offsets for positive target masses is the standard finite-target dual
fact already used in the pinned covariance proof.

The mean-zero offsets consequently have derivative
                   dh/dp=(mathcal L|1-perp)^-1.
The gap(1), uniformly along the segment of masses inside the fixed box,
proves(2) for distinct sites. Since the concave mass value has gradient
-h, integration gives(3). No second derivative in score sites is claimed.

4. Exact passage to singular and duplicate rows
Rank loss with distinct sites is already covered by Sections1-3. For a
uniform treatment including duplicates, approximate B by centered
full-rank matrices B_epsilon in the SAME R^N, using an extended partial
polar isometry; preserve the row cap up to a fixed factor. If ||B||^2=w
is imposed, normalize each approximant to that same norm. The finite
dimension N>=m-1 makes the extension possible.

For any positive mass vector in the box, the halfspace constraint for
cell i against j gives
 |h_i-h_j|<=ell_ij Phi^-1(1-cp/m).
Thus all mean-zero approximating offsets are uniformly bounded. By(2)
they are also uniformly Lipschitz as functions of p. Subsequential limits
on the compact mass box exist and satisfy(2).

The score-value estimate
 |F_p(B_epsilon)-F_p(B)|<=E max_i|(B_epsilon-B)_i.X|
                       <=||B_epsilon-B||F
is uniform in p. Passing in the dual objective identifies every limit
as an optimal offset. For duplicate rows, positive prescribed masses
force their offsets equal: a strictly smaller offset would give that
label zero mass. Grouping duplicates leaves distinct sites with positive
group masses. Their whole-facet adjacency is connected (a generic line
segment between cell interiors crosses only facets), and equality in the
convex dual along two optima forces equal offset differences on every
adjacent pair. Hence optimal offsets are unique up to a common constant.
The mean-zero convention identifies the limit uniquely. Alternatively
this is precisely the standard uniqueness argument for the finite
Gaussian semi-discrete dual, applied to duplicate groups.

Consequently all approximating offsets converge to the same h(p), and
(2)-(3) pass to the endpoint. This uses values and offsets only; arbitrary
fractional splitting of a duplicate group need not converge.

5. Deficit-relative offsets without a covariance lower bound
For this section use a fixed auxiliary mass box containing, for example,
1/(2m)<=p_i<=3/(2m). Let h=h(U), sum h_i=0. The balanced halfspace bounds
from Section4 give

                         ||h||infty<=C L/m.                  (14)

The accepted global mass-window theorem gives
 V(p)<=w+C L^2|p-U|^2,
including singular covariance optimizers. For any B with ||B||^2=w,
Young's inequality against each assignment's centroid matrix gives
 F_p(B)<=1/2(||B||^2+V(p))<=w+C L^2|p-U|^2.                  (15)

Let z=p-U. The lower Taylor estimate(3) gives
 F_p(B)>=w-R_U(B)-h.z-C L|z|^2.
Combining with(15),

                    -h.z<=R_U(B)+C1 L^2|z|^2.               (16)

Choose z=-h/(2C1 L^2). Equation(14) ensures this choice lies in the
auxiliary mass box for all sufficiently large m; it is centered.
Equation(16) yields |h|^2<=4C1 L^2 R_U(B), proving(4).
Finally(3) and(4) give(5), using an auxiliary box containing both U and
the target fixed positive box if necessary.

6. Exact scope and next use
The physical spectral gap applies to any finite ambient dimension with
distinct sites and positive cells. The duplicate extension in Theorem B
uses N>=m-1; it does not assign physical graph weights to duplicate sites.
All constants are uniform in the rank, pair separation and offsets.

The row cap is satisfied by actual competitive centroid matrices from
the mass window and the Gaussian centroid bound, after harmless common
normalization when their total moment is comparable to w. This makes
the lemma applicable without asserting that such minimizers are affine.
An affine score comparison and an actual partition remain different
objects. No actual geometry, second-chaos sign, or full-noise theorem
is inferred here.
