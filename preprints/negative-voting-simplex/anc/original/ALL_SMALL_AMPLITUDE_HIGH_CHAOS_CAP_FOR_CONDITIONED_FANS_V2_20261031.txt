THEOREM
Fix positive c0,C0,cp,Cp and delta>0. For sufficiently large m, let B be
a centered m-by-N score matrix of rank EXACTLY m-1, N>=m-1, with all
m-1 singular values in[c0 alpha,C0 alpha], alpha=E max_i Z_i/(m-1).
Its affine winning fan has masses cp/m<=p_i<=Cp/m. Let0<s^2<=1/(m-1),
fix k in{2,3}, let u have only total Hermite degrees at least k, and set
 q=2+log m,
 theta=|tau| s^k ||u||_(L2(vector)),
 A=theta(q-1)^(k/2).
If A<=m^(-1-delta), then the exact maximum remainder defined in[V] obeys

 J_tau(T_su)<=C_delta tau^2(s^2 log m)^k||u||_2^2.                (1)

There is NO lower amplitude cutoff. Constants are independent of N.
The same upper remainder holds for the prescribed-mass score value by
retaining its original offsets in the dual. This still concerns an
AFFINE baseline, not the full resummed reference potential.

1. Geometry and dimension-free polynomial bounds
Write l_i(x)=B_i.x+h_i and ell_ij=|B_i-B_j|. Then
 c alpha<=ell_ij<=C alpha.
Both winning masses are at least cp/m, and each cell is contained in its
comparison halfspace against the other. Consequently the signed normal
offset a_ij of every pair hyperplane satisfies
 |a_ij|=|h_i-h_j|/ell_ij<=Phi^(-1)(1-cp/m)<=C sqrt(log m).         (2)
For every distinct i,j,k, the projection of B_i-B_k tangent to the ij
hyperplane has norm at least c alpha. Indeed minimizing over multiples
of B_i-B_j and using the smallest singular value reduces this assertion
to the fixed angle between e_i-e_k and e_i-e_j. Thus near-tie slabs in
the ij plane have Gaussian measure at most C times their physical width.

For u!=0 put
                         v=T_su/(s^k||u||_2).
For any fixed p>=2 and derivative order j<=2, Gaussian hypercontractivity
gives dimension-free bounds of the following form on every pair plane:
 ||partial_n^j v_i(a+z,.)||_(L^p(gamma_(N-1)))<=m^C,
                       |z|<=1,                                (3)
where C depends only on the fixed data,p,j, not on N,s,u or the pair.
Here and below m^C can absorb fixed multiplicative constants.

To verify(3), choose a fixed t>0 with t^2<=1/(p-1), and for large m s<t.
Write T_su=T_t w, w=T_(s/t)u. Since u starts in degree k,
||w||_2<= (s/t)^k||u||_2. The normal Mehler row and its first two
derivatives have L2 norms bounded by
 C_j(1+|a+z|^j) exp(C_t(a+z)^2).
This follows by differentiating the one-dimensional Mehler kernel; its
derivatives are polynomials of degrees at most2 times that kernel, and
their squared integrals are Gaussian polynomial moments. Tangential
L2-to-Lp hypercontractivity supplies(3) after division by s^k||u||_2,
because(2) turns the displayed exponential into a fixed power of m.
No full-N point-evaluation bound is used.

The fundamental theorem of calculus in the normal variable, together
with the j=0,1,2 bounds, implies for each pair
 W_ij(y)=sup_(|z|<=1)(|v_i-v_j|(y+zn)+|partial_n(v_i-v_j)|(y+zn))
                         ||W_ij||_32<=m^C.                     (4)
Also ||v_i||_32<=C by ordinary full-space hypercontractivity with a fixed
time and the same degree k factorization. These facts hold uniformly in
all extra coordinates, which have been integrated rather than evaluated.

2. A quantitative local upper cap with polynomial constants
The sign of tau can be absorbed into v. Assume theta>0 is small. The
maximum remainder is bounded by the sum of its ordered-pair positive
rewards. On source cell i use normal coordinates x=y+zn, z>=0, about
the ij comparison plane, oriented so that l_i-l_j=ell_ij z. The pair
reward is
                       (theta(v_j-v_i)-ell_ij z)_+.

Choose the normal-sup cutoff M=theta^(-1/8). On a good projected point
W_ij(y)<=M, positive rewards with0<=z<=1 have
 z<=eta_ij:=theta M/ell_ij.
For theta<=m^(-B0), with an absolute sufficiently large B0, all eta_ij
are at most1/[2(1+|a_ij|)]. Taylor in z and the Gaussian density ratio
then compare this fiber integral with
             theta^2 (v_j-v_i)(y)_+^2/(2ell_ij)
times Gaussian surface density. The absolute error per fiber is bounded
by a fixed polynomial in m times theta^3 M^3. In detail the score error
is at most theta zM; its integral up to eta is O(theta M eta^2).
The Gaussian relative error is O((1+|a_ij|)eta), multiplying a fiber
integral at most theta M eta. Both are O(m^C theta^3 M^3).

The old source-cell restriction causes no extra main term. If y belongs
to the actual ij facet, extending its normal fiber can only increase the
upper bound. If y does NOT belong to that facet but y+zn belongs to cell
i for some0<=z<=eta, then some third comparison at y is negative by at
most C alpha eta. By the angle bound in Section1, y lies in a slab of
width C eta about an affine codimension-one plane inside the ij plane.
There are at most m-2 such slabs. Their conditional Gaussian measure is
at most C m eta. Multiplying by the good-fiber bound theta M eta gives
another O(m^C theta^3 M^3) error. This explicitly accounts for all triple
junction neighborhoods; no smooth single-interface representation is
assumed there.

On bad projected points W_ij>M and0<=z<=1, discard the cost and bound
the reward by theta W_ij. The normal Gaussian density ratio over that
unit interval is at most exp(C sqrt(log m))<=m^C. Equation(4) gives a
total bad contribution bounded by
 m^C theta M^(-31)=m^C theta^(39/8).                            (5)
For z>1 the cost is at least c alpha, so full-space L32 bounds give
 a total contribution <=C theta^32 alpha^(-31)<=m^C theta^32.    (6)
These estimates also apply to the reverse ordered side. There are only
m(m-1) ordered pairs, so their number is absorbed into a fixed polynomial.

Summing the two signed main terms at each actual facet yields exactly
theta^2/2 times its full squared value, rather than twice that value.
Since M^3=theta^(-3/8), the preceding bounds prove, for some fixed K,

 J_tau(T_su)
 <=(theta^2/2) sum_e ell_e^-1 int (v_i-v_j)^2 d lambda_e
                 +C m^K theta^(21/8),  theta<=m^(-B0).          (7)

No lower bound on any individual facet area is used. All error estimates
are upper bounds, so deleting bad projected points from the main term
and then restoring the full nonnegative main term is legitimate.

3. The exact linear-fan trace pays the main term
Apply[T] with the chosen k in{2,3}. Since s^2 O(log m)<=1 for large m, and v has the
normalization used above,
 sum_e ell_e^-1 int (v_i-v_j)^2 d lambda_e<=C(1+log m)^k.         (8)
Choose a fixed B larger than B0 and8K/5, increasing it if needed to absorb
the fixed constants. For every0<theta<=m^(-B),
 C m^K theta^(21/8)<=C theta^2,
and(7)-(8) imply
                 J_tau(T_su)<=C theta^2(1+log m)^k.             (9)
Thus arbitrarily small amplitudes are controlled with a quadratic bound
and no ambient-dimensional prefactor.

4. Join the small and moderate amplitude regimes
For theta>=m^(-B), put q=2+log m and
A=theta(q-1)^(k/2). Then A>=m^(-B). If also A<=m^(-1-delta), the
polynomial-amplitude part of[V] applies, with beta=B and a harmless
adjustment of fixed constants, giving
 J_tau(T_su)<=C_delta A^2<=C_delta theta^2(1+log m)^k.             (10)
Together(9),(10) cover every amplitude in the theorem, including theta=0
by direct evaluation. This proves(1). The prescribed-mass comparison is
exactly[V](11), which uses the original offsets as a dual competitor.

5. Remaining full-noise issue
The result removes the amplitude floor from the finite higher-degree
comparison around conditioned affine Gaussian scores. It can be paired
with the finite first-chaos remainder and their mixed boundary trace.
It does not replace the true noise reference's even/high-degree LINEAR
potential by a linear Gaussian score. That cancellation, and any actual
conditioning/localization hypothesis needed for application, remain
separate requirements for the named conjecture.
