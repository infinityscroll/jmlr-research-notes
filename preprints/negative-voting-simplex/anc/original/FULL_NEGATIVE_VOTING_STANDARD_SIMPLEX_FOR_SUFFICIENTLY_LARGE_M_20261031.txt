THEOREM
There exists an absolute integer m0 such that for every m>=m0, every
finite ambient dimension N>=m-1, every rho in[-1/(m-1),0), and every
measurable f:R^N -> {u in[0,1]^m:sum_i u_i=1},

 sum_i <f_i,T_rho f_i> >= sum_i <g_i,T_rho g_i>,

where g is the indicator assignment of a regular m-cell Gaussian simplex
partition, extended cylindrically to R^N. Equality holds only for these
regular partitions, up to orthogonal transformations, relabeling and
Gaussian null sets. T_rho is the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck noise operator.
No mass constraint is imposed on f. The threshold m0 is independent
of rho and finite N; this proof does not give a numerical value for it.

SCOPE
This settles the stated sufficiently-large-m, single-rule, negative
voting-range case of the Gaussian Standard Simplex problem if the proof
is correct. It does not settle every m, all negative Gaussian
correlations, the positive-noise problem, or a two-rule bilinear problem.
Discrete Plurality and computational-hardness consequences require their
own transfer argument and are not asserted by this file.

1. Actual minimizer, notation, and existing localization
Choose an actual global minimizer f using[E]. Let
  p_i=E f_i, B_i=E[X f_i], W=||B||^2,
  delta=|p-U|^2, Delta=w-W, D=delta+r Delta,
  R=R_U(B), d=dist_F(B,regular centroid orbit).
The accepted localization[A] gives
  p_i in[1/(2m),3/(2m)],
  0<=D<=Cr^2/m, delta<=2D,
  |W-w|<=D/r, W/w=1+O(r/L).                              (1)
The mass-window theorem[V] gives
  D>=(1-CrL^2)delta+r[V(p)-W]>=delta/2.                    (2)
Write B0 for a closest regular centroid matrix, ||B0||^2=w. The
distance is realized in the same finite N because N>=m-1.

If d<=1/m, the accepted own-centroid local theorem[L] applies to f
and gives S(f)-S(g)>=delta/2+rR. Competitiveness forces delta=R=0,
and its equality argument forces regularity. Thus it remains to exclude
  d>1/m.                                                   (3)
All estimates below are used only in this exterior regime.

2. The own-score remainder is paid by D/r
The actual f is feasible for F_p(B), hence F_p(B)>=W. Balanced optimal
offsets h_U are a dual test at p, giving
  R<=(w-W)/2-h_U.(p-U).
By[O] and bounded normalization scale from(1),
  ||h_U||^2<=CL^2R.
Young therefore gives
  R<=Delta+CL^2delta.
For sufficiently large m, CrL^2<=1; consequently
  0<=R<=Delta+delta/r=D/r=:J.                              (4)
This argument allows W>w and never identifies f with an affine fan.

The global covariance remainder bound[G, and its unnormalized extension]
applies to B. With q=d/alpha and e=||BB^T/alpha^2-P||_F,
  R>=c alpha^2 e^2/m min(1,c/(Le)), e>=q.
Since(1),(3) imply c/sqrt(L)<=q<=C sqrt(m), this yields
  R>=c q/m^3>=c/(m^3sqrt(L)), J>=R,                        (5)
  d^2<=C m^(3/2)L R.
The lower bound in(5) is indispensable for paying absolute tails below.
In particular J>0 in this exterior regime.

3. Build the equal-row test matrix, not an alleged optimizer
Put s=sqrt(W/w), Bhat=B/s. Equation(1) bounds s above and below by
absolute constants. The exact radial identity gives
  R=w(s-1)^2/2+sR_U(Bhat), R_U(Bhat)<=CR.
The actual entropy cap and(1) give a fixed normalized row cap for Bhat.
Apply(RC) to Bhat. With beta=sqrt(w/m),
  sum_i (|Bhat_i|-beta)^2
  <=beta^(-2)sum_i(|Bhat_i|^2-beta^2)^2<=CL^2R.             (6)
The actual uniform row theorem[U] gives |Bhat_i|/beta=1+O(L^(-1/2)).
Conditional Cauchy gives B^TB<=p_max I, hence ||Bhat||op^2<=C/m.

Apply the geometric-median construction proved in[U, Section6] to
Bhat. It provides a matrix Z with
  sum_i Z_i=0, |Z_i|=beta, ||Z||^2=w,
  ||Z-Bhat||^2<=CL^2R.
The radial identity also gives ||B-Bhat||^2=w(s-1)^2<=2R. Therefore
  E_proxy:=||Z-B||^2<=CL^2R<=CL^2J.                        (7)
The construction stays in the actual centroid span; it needs no new
coordinate. We do NOT claim that Z is a realizable centroid matrix,
has separated rows, defines an optimizer, or decreases noise. It will
only be a quadratic test matrix. Duplicate rows and rank loss are allowed.

4. A free-maximum second-moment comparison with an explicit tail
Let Y_i=Z_i.X/beta, M=max_i Y_i, and let M_* be the regular unit-row
maximum. Since the rows of Z sum to zero, M>=0 pointwise. Write
  a0=(m-1)/m, nu=sqrt(a0)M+G/sqrt(m),
with G an independent standard Gaussian. The covariance of the Gaussian
scores defining nu is a0 C+J_label/m, C=(ZZ^T)/beta^2, and has unit
diagonal and dominates J_label/m. The primary equal-threshold theorem[W]
therefore gives H(t)=P(nu<=t)-P(M_m<=t)>=0. The regular unit-row
maximum with the same independent common Gaussian has distribution M_m.
Thus
  epsilon_free=E M_*-E M>=0,
  int H=sqrt(a0)epsilon_free,
  E M_*^2-E M^2=(2/a0)int t H(t)dt.

For t>=T, H(t)<=m P(G>t); for t<=-T, H(t)<=P(G<=t).
These use only unit marginal variances. For every T>=1,
  (E M_*^2-E M^2)_+
     <=C T epsilon_free+C m exp(-T^2/2).                  (8)
This is an absolute Gaussian tail, not an unsupported deficit-relative
CDF bound. No centering or facet convergence issue arises under singular
covariance: the primary theorem applies by its PSD continuity statement.

Let V=max_i Z_i.X=beta M and V_*=beta M_*. The regular mean is
E V_*=w, so(8) becomes
  (E V_*^2-E V^2)_+
     <=C beta T(w-E V)+C beta^2 m exp(-T^2/2).             (9)
In particular E V<=w, the free weak-simplex bound at this scale.

5. The actual assignment's routing loss pays its quadratic test error
Set Z_i.X=z_i(X) and
  c_f(X)=V(X)-sum_i f_i(X)z_i(X)>=0.
Its expectation is E V-<Z,B>. Both this quantity and w-E V are
nonnegative. Their sum is exactly
  w-<Z,B>=(Delta+E_proxy)/2<=CL^2J.                       (10)
The last estimate uses Delta<=J and(7); Delta itself may be negative.
The nonnegativity of the left side follows from the free maximum bound,
not from assuming balance of f.

Pointwise, since each z_i<=V and V>=0,
  V^2-sum_i f_i z_i^2
   =sum_i f_i(V-z_i)(V+z_i)<=2V c_f.
Let A=max_i |Y_i|. Then c_f<=2beta A, V<=beta A, and the unit marginal
Gaussian union bound gives
  E[A^2 1_(A>T)]<=C m(T^2+1)exp(-T^2/2).
Splitting at V<=beta T therefore yields
  E V^2-E sum_i f_i z_i^2
   <=2beta T E c_f+C beta^2 m(T^2+1)exp(-T^2/2).           (11)
No property of the actual interfaces or routing by Z is assumed.

Combine(9)-(11), choose T=sqrt(32L), and use beta T<=CL/m:
  E sum_i f_i z_i^2
   >=E V_*^2-C L^3J/m-C beta^2 m L exp(-16L).             (12)
The last error is explicitly paid after the next certificate step.

6. Direct second-chaos certificate for f
Let Q_i=E[f_i(XX^T-I)] and
  T_i=Z_i Z_i^T-(Z^T Z)/m.
Since sum_i Q_i=0 and all |Z_i|^2=beta^2,
  T_f:=sum_i <Q_i,T_i>=E sum_i f_i z_i^2-beta^2.           (13)
This formula is valid for the UNEQUAL masses p because sum_i p_i=1.
The denominator satisfies
  sum_i||T_i||^2=m beta^4-tr((ZZ^T)^2)/m<=D_*,
  D_*=alpha^4(m-1)(m-2)/m,
by trace w and rank at most m-1. No inverse Gram matrix is used.

For the regular reference, T_*=alpha^2(E M_m^2-1)>0,
A2(g)=T_*^2/(2D_*), and T_*/D_*<=Cm. Cauchy and the squared
positive-part inequality thus convert(12)-(13) into
  A2(f)-A2(g)>=-CL^3J-C L^2exp(-16L).
By(5), the last term is at most CJ for sufficiently large m. Hence
  A2(f)-A2(g)>=-CL^3J.                                   (14)

This is an EXTERIOR estimate for the localized actual minimizer using
(RC) and the actual-row proxy construction. It is not the refuted
global nonaffine relative-A2 inequality: the fixed A/m neighborhood
is handled separately by the full-noise theorem[L].

7. Entire signed higher tail for the actual unequal-mass assignment
Let g0 be the regular assignment associated with the closest B0 and
put epsilon=(1/2)||f-g0||_1. Its regular linear routing loss is
  R0=w-<B0,B>=(Delta+d^2)/2>=0.
The regular top-two gap estimate in[H] gives
  epsilon<=min(1,C sqrt(m R0)).                            (15)
It holds for fractional assignments as well: at each point the mass
sent to nonwinning labels pays at least the top-two gap.

At tau^2=1/(4L), rare-label hypercontractivity, using p_i<=3/(2m), gives
  ||T_tau f||^2+||T_tau g0||^2<=C/m,
  ||T_tau(f-g0)||^2<=C epsilon/m.
Factoring all Hermite degrees>=3 through T_tau proves
  |H_r(f)-H_r(g0)|<=Cr^3L^3 sqrt(epsilon)/m.               (16)
This retains the ENTIRE signed tail; no finite truncation is substituted.

If J>=d^2, then R0<=J by Delta<=J. Equations(5),(15)-(16) give
  |Hdiff|/J<=Cr^3 L^3/(m^(3/4)J^(3/4))
           <=Cr^3m^(3/2)L^(27/8).
If J<d^2, then R0<=d^2, so epsilon<=C sqrt(m)d. Using d=alpha q,
J>=R>=c q/m^3 and q>=c/sqrt(L), one obtains
  |Hdiff|/J<=Cr^3 m^(7/4)L^(13/4)q^(-1/2)
           <=Cr^3m^(7/4)L^(7/2).
The latter also dominates the first case. Thus throughout the exterior,
  |H_r(f)-H_r(g)|<=Cr^3m^(7/4)L^(7/2)J.                  (17)
Rotation does not change the regular reference's noise energy.

8. Full signed comparison and conclusion
The exact decomposition into degrees0,1,2 and the entire higher tail is
  S(f)-S(g)=D+r^2[A2(f)-A2(g)]+H_r(f)-H_r(g).
Equations(14),(17), and J=D/r give
  S(f)-S(g)
   >=D[1-CrL^3-Cr^2m^(7/4)L^(7/2)]>=D/2>0              (18)
for sufficiently large m, uniformly over0<r<=1/(m-1). The endpoint
errors are O(L^3/m) and O(L^(7/2)/m^(1/4)), respectively.
The strict inequality uses the exterior floor(5). This contradicts
global minimality because g is an admissible competitor.

Thus every global minimizer is inside d<=1/m, where[L] gives regularity
