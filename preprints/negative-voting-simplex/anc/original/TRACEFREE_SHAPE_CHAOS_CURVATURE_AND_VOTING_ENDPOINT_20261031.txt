TRACE-FREE SYMMETRIC SIMPLEX SHAPE: EXACT CHAOS CURVATURES AND THE NEGATIVE ENDPOINT
31 October 2026. gaussian_cleanroom_end_to_end. New proof for independent audit.

RESULT AND SCOPE
Let m>=4 and let T be a real symmetric m-by-m matrix with zero diagonal
and T1=0. Define the actual Gaussian partition
 A_i(epsilon)={x: [(I+epsilon T)x]_i=max_j[(I+epsilon T)x]_j}.
It depends only on H=1-perp and is a genuine smooth linear deformation
of the regular-simplex partition for sufficiently small epsilon.

We compute the exact second derivatives of its first and second Hermite
weights. For sufficiently large m the noise-stability curvature at the
negative voting endpoint rho=-1/(m-1) is strictly POSITIVE for every
nonzero such T. More precisely, uniformly over this whole matrix space,

 S_(-1/(m-1))''(0)
      ~16 (log m)^2 m^(-4) ||T||_F^2.                                  (A)

Thus this explicit potentially soft shape representation is not a
negative-correlation instability for all sufficiently large m. This
is a local statement for the specified actual deformations. It does
not prove the full negative Standard Simplex Conjecture, a finite-m
threshold, or stability for all interface speeds. The proof includes
a legitimate bound for the entire higher-chaos remainder.

The concrete matrix suggested for the attack has
 T13=T31=T24=T42=1, T14=T41=T23=T32=-1,
all other entries zero. Its squared Frobenius norm is8. No abstract
covariance coupling replaces its actual score partition in this proof.

1. NOTATION AND THE TIE LAW
Write phi,Phi for the standard normal density and CDF, and let
 h(z)=phi(z)/Phi(z),
 v(z)=Var(N | N<=z)=1-z h(z)-h(z)^2,
 k=m-2.
Let Z have probability density proportional to
                         phi(z)^2 Phi(z)^k.
All expectations below refer to this tie law. Define
 t=EZ, V=Ev(Z), a=E[Z v(Z)], b=-E[h(Z)v(Z)],
 c=E kappa_3(N | N<=Z).
Here a,b,c are moment parameters, NOT interaction energies of a lens.
Let s be the Gaussian surface measure of a single simplex interface.
If mu_m=E max_(1<=i<=m) X_i for independent standard normals, then

 s=sqrt2 int phi^2 Phi^(m-2)
   =sqrt2 mu_m/[m(m-1)].                                                (1)

The last identity is one integration by parts using x phi=-phi'.
The tie coordinates are X_i=X_j=Z; conditional on Z, the other m-2
coordinates are independent normals truncated above at Z.

The central third moment of the truncated normal is
 kappa_3(z)=(1-z^2)h(z)-3z h(z)^2-2h(z)^3=-v'(z).
Since the tie density has logarithmic derivative -2z+k h(z),
integration by parts gives

                         c=-2a-k b.                                    (2)

All boundary terms vanish by Gaussian decay, including at negative
infinity where h has only polynomial growth.

2. ACTUAL SPEEDS, MASS TANGENCY, AND THE SIGNED HESSIAN
For e=(i,j), let b_e=e_i-e_j and n_e=-b_e/sqrt2 be the outward normal
from cell i. The score deformation has velocity -Tx, so its interface
speed is
 f_ij(x)=(T_i-T_j).x/sqrt2=c_ij.x,
where on the interface we may delete the i,j components and put

 (c_ij)_l=(T_il-T_jl)/sqrt2 for l outside{i,j},
 (c_ij)_i=(c_ij)_j=0.                                                   (3)

The deleted contributions cancel because X_i=X_j and T is symmetric
with zero diagonal. Moreover sum_l(c_ij)_l=0. Therefore the conditional
mean of f_ij at every tie value Z is zero. Each interface has zero
integrated speed separately, so every Gaussian cell mass has zero
first derivative. In particular the zeroth Hermite weight sum_i p_i^2
has zero second derivative at the equal-mass point.

Let r_ef=b_e.b_f and p_rho be the joint Gaussian density. The exact
simplex Hessian, including negative rho, is

 S_rho''/2=A_rho-rho B_rho,
 A_rho=sum_ef r_ef int_(Sigma_e x Sigma_f)p_rho f_e(x)f_f(y),
 B_rho=(1/2)sum_ef r_ef^2 int_(Sigma_e x Sigma_f)p_rho f_e(x)^2.           (4)

This follows from distributional differentiation of the indicator
gradients. The diagonal is signed: its density is rho/2 times the
positive sum of squared-incidence kernel rows. The regular scores tie
on every interface for every rho, so their common volume multiplier is
zero. Acceleration of (I+epsilon T)^(-1) and second-order mass changes
therefore do not introduce an omitted Hessian term.

Write the noise expansion S_rho=sum_(l>=0)rho^l W_l, where
 W_1=sum_i||int x1_Ai gamma||^2,
 W_2=(1/2)sum_i||int (xx^T-I)1_Ai gamma||_F^2.
Expansion of(4) gives
 W_1''/2=A_1-B_0,  W_2''/2=A_2-B_1.                                    (5)
The symbols A_l,B_l here mean Hermite coefficients of the boundary
pairings in(4). Their higher-coefficient convergence is justified in
Section7 rather than assumed from formal differentiation.

3. THE EXACT FIRST-CHAOS CURVATURE
Conditional independence and sum c_ij=0 give
 int_(Sigma_e) gamma f_e^2=s V ||c_e||^2,
 int_(Sigma_e) gamma x f_e=s V c_e.
Two elementary matrix identities are

 sum_(i<j)||c_ij||^2=(m-1)||T||_F^2/2,
 sum_(j!=i)c_ij=(m-1)T_i/sqrt2.                                        (6)

For the second identity orient c_ij consistently with the row i.
For the first, sum all row differences and subtract their two deleted
entries. Since T1=0, the full row-difference sum is m||T||_F^2/2 and
the deleted sum is||T||_F^2/2.

Consequently
 A_1=s^2 V^2 (m-1)^2 ||T||_F^2/2.
For each edge sum_f r_ef^2=2m, so
 B_0=m s sum_e int gamma f_e^2
    =m(m-1)s^2 V ||T||_F^2/2.
Thus the exact answer is

 W_1''=-s^2(m-1)V[m-(m-1)V]||T||_F^2.                                 (7)

It is strictly negative for nonzero T because0<V<1. Its actual
coercivity contains m-(m-1)V=1+(m-1)(1-V); replacing this quantity by1
would lose the decisive order-log m factor in its asymptotic value.

For m>3 the variance loss also has the exact formula

 1-V=m(2EZ^2-1)/[(m-2)(m-3)].                                          (8)

Indeed integration of(z times the tie density)' gives
k E[Z h]=2EZ^2-1. Integration of(h times the density)' gives
(k-1)E h^2=3E[Z h]. Add these two contributions to1-V.

4. THE EXACT SECOND-CHAOS CURVATURE
Put d=a-b, z_i=e_i-1/m, t_i=T_i as a column vector, and P=I-11^T/m.
On a face e=(i,j), let d_e=e_i+e_j and u_e=1-d_e. Direct conditional
third moments give

 Q_e:=int_(Sigma_e)gamma f_e(xx^T-I)
   =s[a(d_e c_e^T+c_e d_e^T)
       +b(u_e c_e^T+c_e u_e^T)+c diag(c_e)].                            (9)

The subtraction of I has no effect because int gamma f_e=0. For
distinct remaining coordinates the conditional third moment is
-h v(c_l+c_q); on a repeated coordinate it is
2(-h)v c_l+kappa_3 c_l; a tied/remaining pair gives Z v c_l.
These are all entries of(9). Equation(2) also verifies Q_e 1=0,
as required by independence of the irrelevant common coordinate.

Summing the consistently oriented face derivatives at cell i yields

 M_i':=sum_(j!=i) Q_ij
  =(s/sqrt2)[m d(z_i t_i^T+t_i z_i^T)-2d T
              -m h_* P diag(t_i)P],
 h_*=2a+(m-3)b.                                                        (10)

For transparency, the invariant contractions used to square this are
 sum_i||z_i t_i^T+t_i z_i^T||_F^2=2(m-1)||T||_F^2/m,
 sum_i||P diag(t_i)P||_F^2=(m-2)||T||_F^2/m,
 sum_i<z_i t_i^T+t_i z_i^T,T>=2||T||_F^2,
 sum_i<z_i t_i^T+t_i z_i^T,P diag(t_i)P>=-2||T||_F^2/m,
 <T,P diag(t_i)P>=0.
The last identity uses the zero diagonal of T. Therefore

 A_2=(1/2)sum_i||M_i'||_F^2
 =m(m-1)s^2/4
   *[6a^2+4(m-4)ab+(m-3)(m-4)b^2]||T||_F^2.                           (11)

For the diagonal correction, the unnormalized face centroid is s mu_e,
 mu_e=[m t/(m-2)](e_i+e_j-2/m),
and sum_f r_ef^2 mu_f=m mu_e. The common coordinate is independent of
the face event and of f_e^2, so int gamma f_e^2 sum_l x_l=0. It follows
that

 B_1=m^2(m-1)s^2 t a/[2(m-2)] ||T||_F^2.                              (12)

Combining(5),(11),(12), the exact second-chaos answer is

 W_2''=m(m-1)s^2/2
 *[6a^2+4(m-4)ab+(m-3)(m-4)b^2-(2m/(m-2))t a]||T||_F^2.              (13)

The terms of apparent order m^2 s^2 log m in(11),(12) cancel. They
must not be bounded separately when assessing the endpoint.

5. TIE-LAW ASYMPTOTICS, WITH THE CANCELLATION RETAINED
Let L=log m. Elementary Gaussian maximum estimates give
 mu_m~sqrt(2L). For completeness, the union bound above
(1+delta)sqrt(2L) and independence below(1-delta)sqrt(2L), followed by
the Gaussian tail integral, give upper and lower expectations with this
ratio. By(1), the tie normalizing integral is therefore of order
sqrt L/m^2.

For every fixed positive p,

             E|Z|^p~(2L)^(p/2),  EZ~sqrt(2L).                         (14)

Here concentration follows directly from the tie density: below
(1-delta)sqrt(2L), Phi(Z)^(m-2) is exponentially small in a positive
power of m; above(1+delta)sqrt(2L), its phi(Z)^2 tail is at most
m^(-2(1+delta)^2) times a polynomial. Division by the stated normalizer
proves concentration. The same tail bounds with |Z|^p give uniform
integrability, including negligible negative tails.

Equation(8) now gives
 V=1-(4+o(1))L/m,
 m-(m-1)V~4L,
 s^2~4L/m^4.
Hence(7) gives

                 W_1''~ -16 L^2/m^3 ||T||_F^2.                         (15)

To expand(13) without a false large remainder, define
 I1=E[Z^2 h], I2=E[Z h^2], I3=E h^3.
For m>4, integration by parts gives exactly
 I1=2(EZ^3-t)/k,
 I2=(3I1-2t/k)/(k-1),
 I3=4I2/(k-2).
Consequently
 a=t-I1-I2,
 a-t~ -2(2L)^(3/2)/m,
 c=2t/k-I1-3I2-2I3=O(L^(3/2)/m).                                      (16)

Substitute b=-2a/k-c/k from(2) into the bracket in(13). It becomes

 (2m/k)a(a-t)
  +[8a^2-4(k-2)a c+(k-1)(k-2)c^2]/k^2.                                (17)

Its first term is asymptotic to-16L^2/m. The rest is
O(L^3/m^2)=o(L^2/m). Therefore

                 W_2''~ -32 L^3/m^3 ||T||_F^2.                         (18)

At r=1/(m-1), the second-chaos contribution r^2 W_2'' divided by
the positive first-chaos contribution-r W_1'' is asymptotic to
-2 log(m)/m. It is too small to reverse the sign. This statement about
the ratio does not assume a sharper error term in(15) than was proved.

6. A FIXED-CORRELATION SURFACE BOUND
We next control ALL higher chaoses, not just the first two. Fix
 eta=1/16 and beta=56/17. For a face Gaussian measure
 lambda_e=gamma_H dH^(m-2)|_(Sigma_e), let
 I_eta(mu,nu) be its Mehler pairing. There is an absolute constant C
and a sufficiently large absolute m_0 such that

                   I_eta(lambda_e,lambda_e)<=C m^(-beta).               (19)

Proof: work in R^m, integrating out the independent common coordinate.
Conditioning on the two equalities for this same face gives tie values
U,V of variance1/2 and correlation eta. The remaining m-2 independent
coordinate pairs must each lie below U,V. Their joint probability is
at most min(Phi(U),Phi(V)) and hence at most sqrt(Phi(U)Phi(V)). The
bivariate tie density is at most
 C exp(-(U^2+V^2)/(1+eta)).
The equality-constraint density is a fixed bounded constant. Thus the
surface integral is at most
 C [int exp(-z^2/(1+eta)) Phi(z)^((m-2)/2) dz]^2.

Split at z_0=sqrt((7/4)log m). Below z_0, the lower Mills bound gives
 Phi(z)^((m-2)/2)<=exp(-c m^(1/8)/sqrt(log m)).
Above z_0, the widened Gaussian tail is at most
 C m^(-7/[4(1+eta)])=C m^(-28/17).
The first contribution is smaller than this power for all sufficiently
large m. Squaring proves(19).

For the linear face function f_e=c_e.x, Gaussian conditioning on the
two normal equalities leaves every fixed linear Gaussian moment bounded
by a constant times the corresponding power of||c_e||. Holder with
exponent16 on the winning event therefore gives, with
                       beta'=15 beta/16=105/34>3,

 I_eta(f_e lambda_e,f_e lambda_e)
       <=C||c_e||^2 m^(-beta'),
 I_eta(f_e^2 lambda_e,f_e^2 lambda_e)
       <=C||c_e||^4 m^(-beta').                                        (20)

Absolute values may be put inside the two weighted integrals to obtain
these upper bounds. The high but fixed Gaussian moments only affect C,
not its dependence on m or T. This avoids assuming that a conditional
winning-event moment is bounded without proof.

7. A LEGITIMATE BOUND FOR THE FULL HIGHER-CHAOS HESSIAN
Expand these Gaussian surface distributions in normalized Hermites.
Their Mehler self-pairings at eta are finite by(19)-(20). Thus their
eta-weighted square coefficient sums converge. This can equivalently
be justified by first applying a small OU smoothing to each surface
distribution and passing to the limit in these finite positive sums.

For A_rho in(4), the degree coefficients A_l are nonnegative: group
the signed edge measures by their label incidence to write A_rho as
sum_i I_rho(sum_e b_e,i f_e lambda_e, sum_e b_e,i f_e lambda_e).
For the scalar coefficients B_l in(4), Cauchy--Schwarz over Hermite
coefficients gives
 sum_l eta^l |B_l|
 <=(1/2)sum_ef r_ef^2
       sqrt[I_eta(f_e^2 lambda_e,f_e^2 lambda_e)
                    I_eta(lambda_f,lambda_f)].

The line-graph absolute-incidence row sum is2m-2, its squared-incidence
row sum is2m, and(6) supplies sum_e||c_e||^2=O(m)||T||_F^2. Applying
(19)-(20), and Cauchy--Schwarz to the signed cross terms of A_eta,
therefore yields

 A_eta + sum_l eta^l |B_l|
                   <=C m^(2-beta')||T||_F^2.                            (21)

For example the A_eta bound uses
 sum_ef |r_ef| ||c_e|| ||c_f||
 <=(2m-2)sum_e||c_e||^2;
the B bound uses sum_f r_ef^2/2=m. No count of all edge pairs of
order m^4 is introduced, since disjoint edges have zero incidence.

For |rho|=r<=eta, subtracting the coefficients in(5) gives

 |S_rho''-rho W_1''-rho^2 W_2''|
    <=C r^3 m^(2-beta')||T||_F^2.                                     (22)

Indeed the A remainder starts at degree3 and is bounded by
(r/eta)^3 A_eta; the rho B remainder starts at degree3 and is bounded
by r(r/eta)^2 sum eta^l|B_l|. Constants absorb the fixed powers of eta
and the factor2 in(4). A_0=0 by the exact mass tangency in Section2.

At r=1/(m-1), equation(22) is

 |remainder|<=C m^(-1-beta')||T||_F^2
             =C m^(-4-3/34)||T||_F^2.                                 (23)

This is o((log m)^2/m^4)||T||_F^2. Together with(15),(18), it proves(A)
and strict positive curvature for sufficiently large m. The constants
are not optimized and this proof does not state an explicit threshold.

8. WHAT THIS ATTACK DOES AND DOES NOT ESTABLISH
The first-chaos reserve was computed, not identified with a lossy
covariance-coercivity lower bound. The second-chaos leading cancellation
and every higher-chaos term have been retained. The four-label matrix
and the full symmetric zero-diagonal row-sum representation are thus
excluded as local counterexamples at the negative voting endpoint for
all sufficiently large m.

This does not classify finite perturbations, give a global optimizer
theorem, cover arbitrary shape representations, or assert anything
outside the named single-partition problem. The first-order masses of
this actual family vanish; if positive equal-mass variants are later
tested, a standard second-order exact mass repair has no Hessian cost
at the regular partition because its multiplier is zero.
