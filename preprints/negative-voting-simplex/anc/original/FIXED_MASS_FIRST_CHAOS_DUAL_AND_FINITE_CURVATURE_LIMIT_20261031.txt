FIXED-MASS FIRST-CHAOS DUAL AND A FINITE-CURVATURE LIMIT
31 October 2026. Branch derivation. Independent full audit pending.

This note has two distinct conclusions. The entire linear Gaussian score
block admits an exact fixed-mass dual with no ambient-dimensional factor.
However, a constant fraction of its FULL local shape curvature cannot be
assumed throughout a score ball of radius order1/m. The second conclusion
is proved on an exactly mass-calibrated Gaussian covariance path, not on
the uncalibrated cap example. A weaker finite gap of order1/m times the
squared score displacement is NOT ruled out. No noise-stability theorem
or counterexample is claimed.

1. Exact constrained score dual
Let U=(1/m,...,1/m), P=I-11^T/m, mu=E max_(i<=m) Z_i,
 alpha=mu/(m-1), B0=alpha P, w=alpha^2(m-1).
The rows of B0 are the regular partition's unnormalized first moments.
For a centered score matrix B, in any finite ambient dimension at least
m-1, define
 F_p(B)=sup{E sum_i f_i(X) B_i.X: E f_i=p_i, f_i>=0, sum_i f_i=1}.
The same notation permits smaller rank. Gaussian coupling/projection
identifies F_p(B) with the covariance functional of[C] at C=BB^T for
the fixed target law supported on the coordinate simplex with masses p.

For balanced p=U, covariance concavity and the exact gradient at
C0=alpha^2 P give
 F_U(B)<= (w+||B||_F^2)/2.
One can also obtain this inequality directly from the balanced endpoint
theorem: for every admissible centroid matrix M,
 2<B,M>-||B||^2=||M||^2-||B-M||^2<=w.
Define the exact nonnegative tangent remainder
 R_U(B)=(w+||B||^2)/2-F_U(B).                                  (1)

If J0(f)=w-<B0,M_f>, then, for every centered score test Z,
 sup_(E f=U){<M_f-B0,Z>-J0(f)}
  =F_U(B0+Z)-w-<B0,Z>
  =||Z||^2/2-R_U(B0+Z).                                       (2)
This is an exact nonlinear supremum over balanced assignments. It does
not replace a constrained assignment by an arbitrary coupling between
two labels or by an unrestricted noise coupling.

For prescribed p in the accepted positive mass box, the same calculation
and the definition V(p)=sup||M_f||^2 give
 sup_(E f=p){<M_f-B0,Z>-J0(f)}
 <=||Z||^2/2+[V(p)-w]/2
 <=||Z||^2/2+(A_m/2)||p-U||^2, A_m=O(log^2 m),                 (3)
using[M,G]. Thus the mass block can be retained exactly instead of
entering an unconstrained pair-cap Taylor estimate. Equations(2)-(3)
concern first moments. The full reference potential T_-r g contains
higher degrees, whose comparison is not supplied by this identity.

2. An exactly calibrated covariance perturbation
Let A have entries A_12=A_21=1 and every other entry zero. On H=1-perp
put
 E=-PAP,
 C_t=P(I-tA)P|_H,
 B_t=alpha C_t^(1/2),   0<=t<1.
All covariances are positive definite on H. These score vectors have
exactly the law alpha P Y where Y_1,Y_2 are standard normals with
correlation -t, and Y_3,...,Y_m are independent standard normals,
independent of the first pair. The common projection does not affect
winner comparisons, and its expectation is zero in the objective.

We prove, uniformly for 0<=t<=1/2 and large m,
                         F_U(B_t)>=w-O(m^-4).                    (4)
This is enough for the finite-curvature comparison; no asymptotic
expansion of the calibrated maximizing partition is needed.

Write M=max(Y_3,...,Y_m). At zero offsets the exceptional group wins with
probability
 p_t(0)=2/(m-1)-Q_t,
 Q_t=P(Y_1>M,Y_2>M).
For nonpositive pair correlation, the bivariate Gaussian upper-tail
probability at equal thresholds is no greater than at correlation0.
This follows, for example, by differentiating the bivariate CDF in its
correlation, whose derivative is the positive bivariate density. Hence
 0<=p_t(0)-2/m<=Q_0=2/[m(m-1)].                                (5)
The value of Q_0 follows by conditioning on M: Phi(M) is Beta(m-2,1),
so E[1-Phi(M)]^2=2/[m(m-1)].

Put a common offset a on labels1,2, and zero on all other labels. Then
 p_t'(a)=2 E[phi(M-a) Phi((M-a)sqrt((1+t)/(1-t)))].                (6)
Let b=Phi^(-1)(1-1/m). The event b<=M<=b+1/b has probability bounded
below by an absolute positive constant. On that event, for
-K/(mb)<=a<=0 with any fixed K, the integrand in(6) is bounded below
by c phi(b)>=c' b/m. Therefore, after fixing a sufficiently large K,
there is a_t in [-K/(mb),0] such that p_t(a_t)=2/m. Symmetry within
the exceptional pair and among the other labels then makes EVERY mass
exactly1/m. Strict positivity of(6) also gives uniqueness of this offset.

The convex offset dual is
 Psi_t(a)=E max(Y_1+a,Y_2+a,M)-2a/m.
At a_t all individual mass derivatives vanish, so this one-parameter
critical point is a full optimal offset vector, by convexity and the
stated two-group symmetry. Its calibration cost satisfies
 0<=Psi_t(0)-Psi_t(a_t)
    =int_(a_t)^0 [p_t(a)-2/m] da
    <=[K/(mb)]*[2/(m(m-1))]=O(1/(m^3 b)).                      (7)

Moreover E max(Y_1,Y_2,M)>=mu. Indeed its CDF is
Phi_2(z,z;-t) Phi(z)^(m-2)<=Phi(z)^m for every real z, again by the
positive correlation derivative. Both maxima are integrable, so this
stochastic ordering implies the expectation inequality. Multiplying
Psi_t(a_t)>=mu-O(1/(m^3 b)) by alpha~b/m proves(4).

3. The finite tangent remainder is smaller than its full local model
The exact trace change is
 tr(C_t)-tr(P)=2t/m.
It follows from(1),(4) that
 0<=R_U(B_t)<=alpha^2 t/m+O(m^-4).                              (8)
Choose t_m=1/b. Then
 R_U(B_(t_m))=O(b/m^3).                                       (9)
Meanwhile the score displacement is
 ||B_(t_m)-B0||^2
  =(alpha^2 t_m^2/4)||E||^2(1+o(1))~1/(2m^2),                 (10)
because ||E||^2=2-4/m+4/m^2 and b~mu. Thus this example lies at exactly
the score-displacement scale order1/m under consideration.

For completeness, the fixed-m second derivative of the SAME remainder
at t=0 has size at least c b^4/m^3. At B0, R_U has zero gradient.
The symmetric score space decomposes orthogonally into its scalar,
standard and diagonal-zero shape parts, and its Hessian has no mixed
terms between these parts by the permutation-invariant contractions in[H].
It is positive semidefinite on the whole score space by(1).

For a shape matrix T=T^T,T1=0,T_ii=0, the first-order optimizing offsets
vanish because every conditional facet mean is zero. The exact facet
second variation therefore gives
 F_U''(B0)[T,T]=(m-1)V_m||T||^2/m,
 R_U''(B0)[T,T]=kappa_m||T||^2,
 kappa_m=1-(m-1)V_m/m~4log m/m~2b^2/m,                          (11)
as in[E](3)-(4). These are score derivatives, not partition speeds;
the displayed facet normalization accounts for that distinction.

The shape projection E_sh of E has ||E_sh||^2->2. Explicitly the scalar
coefficient is2/[m(m-1)], and the standard matrix is P diag(u)P with
 u_1=u_2=2/m, u_j=-4/[m(m-2)] for j>=3.
The standard squared norm is8/m^2, and the scalar squared norm is
4/[m^2(m-1)]; subtracting these from ||E||^2 proves the claim.
Since B_t'(0)=alpha E/2 and the gradient of R_U at B0 vanishes,
 (d^2/dt^2)R_U(B_t)|_(t=0)
 >=(alpha^2/4) kappa_m ||E_sh||^2
                  ~alpha^2 b^2/m~b^4/m^3.                     (12)
Combining(9),(12),
 R_U(B_(t_m)) /
 [ (t_m^2/2) (d^2/dt^2)R_U(B_t)|_(t=0) ] ->0.                 (13)
Thus no absolute positive fraction of the FULL local shape-curvature
prediction remains valid on every such score displacement of order1/m,
even with exact mass calibration and positive-definite covariances.

4. The remaining quantitative target
This example does not refute a finite bound
 R_U(B0+Z)>=c ||Z_shape||^2/m
under a suitable gauge and neighborhood: its upper bound in(9) is larger
than the proposed scale m^-3 by a factor of order sqrt(log m). Nor does it
show that this weaker bound holds. A proof must control a genuine finite
covariance remainder, rather than identify it with the full local Hessian.

The two exact reductions useful for continuation are(2)-(3) and the
calibration estimate(7). Neither supplies the higher-chaos coupling or a
global noise comparison. No master-state files or prior sources are changed.
