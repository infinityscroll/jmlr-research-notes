GLOBAL MASS LOCALIZATION THROUGH THE FULL ADMISSIBLE NEGATIVE INTERVAL
31 October 2026. Proof candidate; independent audit pending.

This is a new implication for the SINGLE-assignment problem. It does not
prove Standard Simplex/Plurality is Stablest, nor the stronger two-rule
negative statement. It does overcome the O(m^-2) positivity restriction
in the previously frozen bilinear transfer. No publication claim.

1. Statement and exact finite-m certificate
Let f=(f_1,...,f_m) be a Gaussian partition in any finite dimension, or a
simplex-valued measurable assignment. Put p_i=E f_i, U=(1/m,...,1/m),
delta=|p-U|^2, W=sum_i |E[X f_i]|^2. Let g be the balanced regular
m-cone partition, allowed in a larger dimension when necessary. Write
M_m=max of m independent standard normals, mu_m=E M_m, and
  w_m=mu_m^2/(m-1),
  I(p)=phi(Phi^-1(p)), I(0)=I(1)=0,
  F_m=m I(1/m)^2,
  B_m=max(2 log m+1,6),
  A2_m=m(E M_m^2-1)^2/[2(m-1)(m-2)]       (m>=3).

Suppose 0<r<=1/(m-1) and S_{-r}(f)<=S_{-r}(g). If m-1>B_m, then

  delta <= [r(F_m-w_m)+r^2 A2_m+r^3+r^4]/(1-r B_m).       (1)

In particular define the explicit, one-dimensional-integral quantity

  L_m = m^2/[1-B_m/(m-1)] *
        [(F_m-w_m)/(m-1)+A2_m/(m-1)^2
                            +1/(m-1)^3+1/(m-1)^4].       (2)

Then m^2 delta<=L_m UNIFORMLY throughout the full interval. Moreover

  L_m -> 2-2 gamma <1,                                  (3)

where gamma is Euler's constant. Consequently for every eta>0 with
2-2 gamma+eta<1, all sufficiently large m and every competitive f satisfy

  [1-sqrt(2-2 gamma+eta)]/m <= p_i
       <= [1+sqrt(2-2 gamma+eta)]/m       for every i.     (4)

Thus no label can disappear in a competitive assignment anywhere in the
full negative interval for all sufficiently large m. This does not force
p=U, closeness of the partition to g, or equality of noise stability.
The theorem gives an asymptotic threshold, not a claimed computed m_0.

2. The scalar Bregman bound is global, including p=0 and p=1
Set H(p)=I(p)^2 and a=1/m. On (0,1), with z=Phi^-1(p),
  H'(p)=-2z I(p),   H''(p)=2z^2-2.
We prove for all p in [0,1]

  H(p)<=H(a)+H'(a)(p-a)+B_m(p-a)^2.                     (5)

For p<a, the integral Taylor remainder is
  int_p^a (t-p) H''(t) dt.
For t<=1/2 the elementary Gaussian tail bound implies
|Phi^-1(t)|^2<=2 log(1/t). With t=p+(a-p)s>=as, the remainder is at most
  4(a-p)^2 int_0^1 s[log m+log(1/s)] ds
  =(2 log m+1)(a-p)^2.
This also proves the endpoint p=0 by monotone limiting or integrability.
For a<=p<=1/2, H''(t)<=2|Phi^-1(a)|^2<=4 log m, proving (5).
For p>=1/2, H'(a)>=0 and H(a)>=0, while H(p)<=1/(2pi).
Since p-a>=1/6 for m>=3, the remainder divided by (p-a)^2 is at most
18/pi<6. This proves (5) on the entire interval.
Summing and using sum_i(p_i-a)=0 gives

  sum_i I(p_i)^2 <= F_m+B_m delta.                      (6)

Gaussian one-dimensional rearrangement gives |E X f_i|<=I(p_i): project
onto the centroid direction and place the allowed mass on a half-line.
This holds also for 0<=f_i<=1. Therefore W<=F_m+B_m delta.

3. Retaining the single-rule parity
Let a_k(f) be the summed squared Hermite coefficients at degree k.
All a_k>=0, a_0=|p|^2, a_1=W, and sum a_k<=1 (equality for a partition).
For negative correlation,
  S_{-r}(f)>=|p|^2-r W-r^3(1-|p|^2-W)
            >=|p|^2-r W-r^3.                           (7)
Even degrees were kept nonnegative; the odd degrees >=3 were bounded
by r^3. This is unavailable for an arbitrary bilinear pair.
For the reference, discard its negative odd degrees and bound even degrees
>=4 by r^4 to get
  S_{-r}(g)<=1/m-r w_m+r^2 A2_m+r^4.                    (8)
Competitive comparison and (6) now give (1). Since F_m>=w_m (apply the
individual centroid bound to g), every numerator coefficient is nonnegative.
The right side of (1) increases with r while rB_m<1. This proves (2).

4. Exact second-chaos coefficient of the regular partition
Root derived the formula for A2_m independently; the derivation here
checks it directly. Work in H=1-perp subset R^m with projected standard
Gaussian X and unit regular axes t_i=sqrt(m/(m-1))(e_i-1/m).
For cell i, the matrix E[(XX^T-I_H)1_{g_i}] has one eigenvalue along t_i
and a repeated eigenvalue on its orthogonal complement, by permutation
symmetry. Its trace is zero because g_i is conical and Gaussian radius
is independent of direction. Conditional on cell i, the axis second moment is
  (m E M_m^2-1)/(m-1).
Indeed, the common Gaussian mean is independent of H and has variance1/m,
and subtracting it from M_m leaves the winning projected coordinate.
Multiplying the conditional deficit by the cell mass1/m gives the axis
eigenvalue q=(E M_m^2-1)/(m-1); the other eigenvalue is -q/(m-2).
Degree-two Hermite weight is one half the squared Frobenius norm of this
matrix. Summing over m cells gives exactly A2_m above.
For direct evaluation, integration by parts twice gives
  E M_m^2-1
    =m(m-1)(m-2)/2 * int_R phi(t)^3 Phi(t)^(m-3) dt.     (9)
Boundary terms vanish by Gaussian decay. In the first step use
(t phi(t))'=(1-t^2)phi(t); in the second use (phi^2)'=-2t phi^2.

5. Asymptotics needed for (3), with tail control
Put b_m=Phi^-1(1-1/m). Mills expansion, obtained by repeated integration
by parts of the Gaussian tail, gives
  phi(b_m)/(1/m)=b_m+1/b_m+O(b_m^-3),
  F_m=[b_m^2+2+o(1)]/m,   b_m^2=O(log m).              (10)
For Z_m=b_m(M_m-b_m), the same tail ratio gives
  P(Z_m<=z)->exp(-exp(-z)) for every fixed real z.
Moment convergence is justified as follows. For z>=0 the union bound and
Mills bounds give P(Z_m>z)<=C exp(-z). For 0<=z<=b_m^2/2, the lower
Mills bound at b_m-z/b_m gives P(Z_m<-z)<=exp(-c exp(z/2)).
Between b_m^2/2 and b_m^2 this probability is at most
exp(-m barPhi(b_m/2)), whose product with any fixed power of b_m tends0.
For z=b_m^2+b_m s, s>=0, use
  P(M_m<-s)=barPhi(s)^m<=2^-m exp(-m s^2/2).
These bounds give uniform integrability of Z_m and Z_m^2 and hence
  E M_m^2=b_m^2+2 gamma+o(1),
  (E M_m)^2=b_m^2+2 gamma+o(1).                         (11)
Here gamma is the mean of the displayed Gumbel law. Its equality with
Euler's constant follows, for example, by the substitution u=exp(-z)
in its density integral, or the usual harmonic-number limit.
Equations (10)-(11) imply
  m(F_m-w_m)->2-2 gamma,
  A2_m=O(log^2 m/m),   B_m=O(log m).
In (2), the second, third and fourth terms after multiplication by m^2
are respectively O(log^2 m/m), O(1/m), O(1/m^2); the denominator tends1.
This proves (3). The strict gamma>1/2 can be seen without a decimal
approximation: the increasing trapezoidal error for 1/x has limit
gamma-1/2 and already has positive value3/4-log2 on [1,2].

6. Scope and the genuine remaining bridge
This theorem excludes zero or arbitrarily underweight cells in large-m
competitive SINGLE-rule assignments throughout [-1/(m-1),0). It does
not convert the old bilinear transfer into a full-range theorem. Nor does
it control all higher-chaos differences relative to the endpoint deficit.
The full named conjecture still requires a sign proving that a competitive
assignment with the mass window (4) is exactly the regular partition.
No endpoint coercivity constant, compactness-only delta_*, or numerical
threshold is silently used in (1)-(4).
