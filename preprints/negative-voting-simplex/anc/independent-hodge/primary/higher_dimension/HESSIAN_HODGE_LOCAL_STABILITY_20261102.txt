HESSIAN-WEIGHTED GAUSSIAN HODGE ESTIMATE AND LOCAL NUCLEAR-TV STABILITY
Date: 2026-11-02
Status: derived argument; independent algebra audit requested and partly received.
No claim of originality, literature recognition, global rearrangement, or major-result status.

1. CLAIM AND SCOPE

Let gamma be the standard Gaussian measure on R^d. Let phi be smooth and convex,
M=D^2 phi positive definite, and v be a smooth compactly supported vector field
with div_gamma v=div v-x.v=0. Let S_t be its Gaussian-preserving flow and T=grad phi.
Provided V(t)=integral ||D(T o S_t)||_* dgamma is finite (or one takes the finite
local change V(t)-V(0)), the calculation below gives V'(0)=0 and V''(0)>=0 in ALL
dimensions. In particular the proposed higher-dimensional local negative-mode
attack fails in this smooth compact-flow regime.

The core estimate is first proved when m I <= M <= L I globally, m>0. In that
regime it holds for any distributionally Gaussian-solenoidal v in L^2(gamma)
for which the right side below is defined. Moreau regularization then extends
the local inequality to arbitrary smooth positive-definite convex Hessians and
compact v. This is a local second-variation result; it does not prove global N.

Use the Jacobian convention Dv_ij=partial_j v_i. Define
  K = M Dv - Dv^T M,
  I(v) = integral v^T M v dgamma,
  E(v) = integral sum_{i<j} K_ij^2/(lambda_i+lambda_j) dgamma,
with components taken in any pointwise orthonormal eigenbasis of M.
The conclusion is I(v)<=E(v).

2. OPERATORS AND THE EXACT INTEGRATION-BY-PARTS IDENTITY

Write partial_j^*=x_j-partial_j. A skew matrix A is identified with a 2-form,
using the norm sum_{i<j} A_ij^2. Set
  (delta_gamma A)_i = sum_j partial_j^* A_ij.
This convention differs by a harmless sign from some exterior-form conventions.
Let S_p(M) be the additive action of M on p-forms: its eigenvalue at the index
set i_1<...<i_p is lambda_{i_1}+...+lambda_{i_p}. In particular
  S_2(M) A = M A + A M,
  J(A) = integral <A,S_2(M)A> dgamma
       = integral tr(A^T M A) dgamma.

For compact smooth skew A, put v=delta_gamma A. Two integrations by parts give
  I(v) = J(A) + R(A),
  R(A) = integral sum_{i,k,j,l} M_ik (partial_l A_ij)(partial_j A_kl) dgamma. (2.1)

Detailed derivation:
  I = integral A_ij partial_j(M_ik v_k).
The term A_ij (partial_j M_ik) v_k vanishes by the full symmetry of D^3 phi and
the skew symmetry in i,j. Since
  partial_j partial_l^* = partial_l^* partial_j + delta_jl,
the remaining term is
  integral A_ij M_ik partial_l^* partial_j A_kl
  + integral A_ij M_ik A_kj.
Move partial_l^* to the other factor. The new term
  A_ij (partial_l M_ik) partial_j A_kl
vanishes by symmetry in k,l. The two surviving terms are R and J.

The same first integration gives the pairing identity
  I(v) = integral sum_{i<j} A_ij K_ij dgamma.                         (2.2)
Both identities use the Hessian property, not merely M positive definite.

3. THE POINTWISE THREE-INDEX ALGEBRA

At a fixed point choose a CONSTANT orthogonal coordinate rotation diagonalizing
M at that point; do not differentiate an eigenvector frame. In these coordinates
the density of R splits as
  sum_{i != j} lambda_i (partial_j A_ij)^2 + sum_{i<j<k} q_ijk.
For a triple i<j<k set
  alpha=lambda_i, beta=lambda_j, gamma=lambda_k,
  a=partial_k A_ij, b=partial_i A_jk, c=partial_j A_ki.
Then
  q = -2(beta*a*b + gamma*b*c + alpha*c*a).                           (3.1)

Let B=S_2(M)A. Full symmetry of D^3 phi cancels every derivative-of-M term in
the exterior derivative dB. Thus its triple component, up to the global sign
chosen for d, is
  k=(alpha+beta)a+(beta+gamma)b+(gamma+alpha)c.                        (3.2)

The elementary inequality needed is
  q + k^2/[2(alpha+beta+gamma)] >= 0.                                (3.3)
Indeed, the symmetric matrix of this quadratic form has null vector (1,1,1),
positive diagonal entries, and every principal 2-by-2 minor equals
alpha*beta*gamma/(alpha+beta+gamma)>0. Hence it is positive semidefinite.

Equivalent direct check on k=0: solve for c to obtain
  q = 2/(alpha+gamma) * [alpha(alpha+beta)a^2
                        +2 alpha gamma a b
                        +gamma(beta+gamma)b^2] >= 0.
The determinant of the bracket's 2-by-2 matrix is
alpha*beta*gamma*(alpha+beta+gamma)>0.

Consequently (2.1) gives, for every compact smooth skew A, the augmented estimate

 integral |delta_gamma A|_M^2 dgamma
 + (1/2) integral <d(S_2(M)A), S_3(M)^(-1)d(S_2(M)A)> dgamma
 >= J(A).                                                          (3.4)

The second term is absent in dimensions below 3. If M>=mI, it is bounded above
by (6m)^(-1) ||d(S_2(M)A)||_{L^2(gamma)}^2.

4. CHOOSING THE CORRECT POTENTIAL

First assume mI<=M<=LI globally. Minimize J(A) over the closed affine subset of
skew L^2(gamma) fields satisfying delta_gamma A=v in distributions. The norm J
is equivalent to the ordinary L^2 norm, so a unique minimizer exists once the
constraint is nonempty.

Nonemptiness follows from the elementary Gaussian Hodge construction. With
L_OU=Delta-x.grad, set w=(-L_OU+1)^(-1)v, componentwise, and
  A^0_ij=partial_j w_i-partial_i w_j.
The commutation relation for delta_gamma and L_OU gives delta_gamma w=0,
and direct calculation yields delta_gamma A^0=(-L_OU+1)w=v. Gaussian Hermite
expansion makes A^0 in L^2 for v in L^2. Equivalently, this can be proved
degree-by-degree in the orthogonal Hermite decomposition.

For a compact smooth 3-form C, the variation A+t delta_gamma C preserves the
constraint because delta_gamma^2=0. The Euler equation is therefore
  d(S_2(M)A)=0 in distributions.                                   (4.1)
This weighted closure condition is essential. Imposing dA=0 instead does not
make R nonnegative, even for some constant anisotropic M.

No unproved smoothness of the minimizing A is needed. Consider the graph of the
first-order differential operator
  A -> (delta_gamma A, d(S_2(M)A)).
Compact smooth fields are dense in this graph for smooth bounded M:
  (i) multiply by cutoffs chi_R with |grad chi_R|<=C/R. The commutator terms
      are -(grad chi_R) contracted with A and dchi_R wedge S_2(M)A, which
      vanish in L^2 because M is bounded and A is in L^2;
  (ii) on a fixed compact support, standard Friedrichs mollification for
      first-order operators with smooth coefficients converges in graph norm.
The Gaussian density is smooth, positive, and locally equivalent to Lebesgue
measure, so the local mollification statement applies directly.

Approximate the minimizer by compact smooth A_n in this graph. Then
  delta_gamma A_n -> v,
  d(S_2(M)A_n) -> 0,
  J(A_n) -> J(A).
Apply (3.4), using the bound (6m)^(-1) on the correction term, and pass to the
limit. This proves
  J(A) <= I(v).                                                     (4.2)

5. LOCAL STABILITY

For smooth v with the indicated integrability, (2.2) extends to the minimizing
potential by distributional integration by parts (compact support of v suffices).
Weighted Cauchy-Schwarz gives
  I(v)^2 <= J(A) E(v) <= I(v) E(v).
For nonzero v, I(v)>0, so I(v)<=E(v). The zero field is immediate.

For the flow calculation, put F_t=T o S_t and H=partial_t DF_t|_0=D(Mv).
The D^3 phi contribution to H is symmetric, so H-H^T=K. The nuclear norm Hessian
at positive-definite M is
  D^2 ||.||_*(M)[H,H] = sum_{i<j}(H_ij-H_ji)^2/(lambda_i+lambda_j).
Stationarity follows from
  integral tr D(Mv) = integral x.Mv
                   = integral grad(x.grad phi-phi).v = 0.
Moreover,
  partial_t^2 F_t|_0 = (v.grad)(Mv),
and Gaussian-solenoidal integration by parts yields
  integral tr D[(v.grad)(Mv)] = integral x.(v.grad)(Mv) = -I(v).
Thus
  V''(0)=E(v)-I(v)>=0.                                             (5.1)

6. REMOVING GLOBAL HESSIAN BOUNDS FOR COMPACT v

For a smooth globally convex phi, take its Moreau envelope
  phi_epsilon(x)=inf_y [phi(y)+|x-y|^2/(2 epsilon)],
and add eta |x|^2/2. Its Hessian is smooth, bounded above by (epsilon^-1+eta)I,
and bounded below by eta I. On compact sets these Hessians converge to D^2 phi
as epsilon,eta decrease to zero. Since v and Dv have compact support and M is
positive definite there, I and E converge by continuity and uniform local
eigenvalue bounds. Hence (5.1) holds for the stated general compact-flow regime.

7. LITERATURE DISTINCTION

Dorian Le Peutrec, 'On Witten Laplacians and Brascamp-Lieb's inequality on
manifolds with boundary', arXiv:1607.08714v2, revised 22 February 2017:
  https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.08714
  https://arxiv.org/pdf/1607.08714
gives differential-form Brascamp-Lieb inequalities associated with Witten
Laplacians. Its standard Hessian term is tied to the logarithmic density V in
e^{-V}. Here the integration measure is fixed Gaussian and M=D^2 phi is an
independent Hessian. The source is contextual background, not a citation that
proves (3.4) or (5.1). No exact prior formulation was located in the bounded
search. That absence is not a novelty certificate.

8. NUMERICAL SANITY CHECK AND QUADRATURE TRAP

second_variation_search.py builds a 23-dimensional Gaussian-solenoidal Hermite
basis of polynomial degrees 2 and 3, excluding linear rotation zero modes, and
forms the Q=E-I Galerkin matrix. Six small smoke trials used softmax, exponential,
and quartic convex potentials. This is diagnostic evidence only.

A sharp anisotropic softmax gave a FALSE apparent negative mode on coarse
tensor Gauss-Hermite quadrature:
  order 12^3: minimum eigenvalue -0.1941929972;
  order 28^3: minimum eigenvalue -0.1085369396.
Resolving its sharp second coordinate by 256-point Gauss-Legendre integration
on [-9,9], times Gauss-Hermite order 16 in the other two coordinates, gave
  minimum eigenvalue +0.01585450011.
A refinement to Gauss-Legendre 384 times Gauss-Hermite 20 squared gave
  minimum eigenvalue +0.0158544905553,
agreeing to approximately 1e-8.
The refinement results are saved beside the script. Coarse negatives must not
be represented as counterexamples. No numerical check replaces the argument.
