\subsection{How to read the supplied proof package}
The article gives the statement, the principal analytic reductions, and
the complete final comparison. The longer weak Hodge, finite-fan cycle,
moving-boundary, and all-amplitude cap calculations remain in the supplied
ancillary notes. The PDF alone is therefore not a self-contained replacement
for those notes. They are mathematical parts of this submission, not
external theorems inferred from successful tests or audit verdicts.

\subsection{External mathematical inputs}
The modern Gaussian maximum comparison is Mulgund's
Theorem 2.1 \cite{Mulgund}: common thresholds, unit variances, and
$K\succeq\one\one^T/m$, including singular covariances. The transformation
in Section~\ref{sec:assembly} verifies these exact hypotheses.
Neither a general rectangular comparison nor unequal marginal variances
are assumed.

The Hodge successor uses Colombo--Figalli--Jhaveri regularity \cite{CFJ}
through the reciprocal-coordinate reduction. It replaces the earlier
recent-preprint regularity input in that branch. It does not remove the
cycle-refined Ehrhard argument from the mass envelope. The classical
Gaussian isoperimetric, intrinsic Ehrhard, Brascamp--Lieb/Poincare, and
hypercontractive inputs are identified in Section~\ref{sec:inputs}.

The discrete transfer is credited to Isaksson--Mossel \cite{IM}.
The Frieze--Jerrum endpoint characterization is credited to
Garc\'ia-Soriano--Miyauchi \cite{GSM}. Their tightness analysis for that
rounding scheme is distinct from the matching UGC-hardness consequence
claimed here. Heilman's three-label result \cite{Heilman} is prior work;
this manuscript neither reproves it nor claims a new result for $m=3$.

\subsection{What the Lean development establishes}
The accompanying Lean project is a partial formalization. Its individual
theorems and explicit hypotheses, pinned toolchain, and coverage ledger
determine its precise scope. Its concrete Gaussian components prove
hyperplane nullness for product Gaussians, almost-everywhere unique winning
linear scores, exponential and first-moment integrability, and integrability
of finite linear scores and their maximum. These facts support fractional
routing rigidity from a zero expected maximum gap. Finite centroid-pairing
and covariance edge-strain identities are also formalized. The scalar
analysis includes shifted-logarithm decay, the exact endpoint power
comparison, and a single threshold uniform over the voting interval for
fixed supplied constants. A conditional local/exterior assembly consumes
the analytic estimates explicitly. Gaussian transport, hypercontractivity,
facet regularity, and the source papers' transfer theorems are not supplied
by these component proofs. A checked implication from analytic estimates
does not prove those estimates.

In particular, this manuscript does not claim a Lean proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, its analytic interfaces, the uniformity of their
constants in dimension and noise, or the UGC reduction. Scalar eventual
threshold statements concern fixed supplied constants; they do not
establish the Gaussian estimates or the uniformity of their constants.
The formal development is a foundation for further work, with its
unformalized obligations stated explicitly. Source checksums certify file
identity. Compiling this PDF certifies typesetting. Neither operation
certifies the analysis.
