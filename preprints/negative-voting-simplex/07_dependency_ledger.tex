the complete final comparison. The longer weak Hodge, finite-fan cycle,
moving-boundary, and all-amplitude cap calculations remain in the supplied
ancillary notes. The PDF alone is therefore not a self-contained replacement
for those notes. They are mathematical parts of this submission, not
external theorems inferred from successful tests or audit verdicts.

\subsection{External mathematical inputs}
The modern Gaussian maximum comparison is Mulgund's
Theorem 2.1 \cite{Mulgund}: common thresholds, unit variances, and
$K\succeq\one\one^T/m$, including singular covariances. The transformation
in Section~\ref{sec:assembly} verifies these exact hypotheses.
Neither a general rectangular comparison nor unequal marginal variances
are assumed.

The Hodge successor uses Colombo--Figalli--Jhaveri regularity \cite{CFJ}
through the reciprocal-coordinate reduction. It replaces the earlier
recent-preprint regularity input in that branch. It does not remove the
cycle-refined Ehrhard argument from the mass envelope. The classical
Gaussian isoperimetric, intrinsic Ehrhard, Brascamp--Lieb/Poincare, and
hypercontractive inputs are identified in Section~\ref{sec:inputs}.

The discrete transfer is credited to Isaksson--Mossel \cite{IM}.
The Frieze--Jerrum endpoint characterization is credited to
Garc\'ia-Soriano--Miyauchi \cite{GSM}. Their tightness analysis for that
rounding scheme is distinct from the matching UGC-hardness consequence
claimed here. Heilman's three-label result \cite{Heilman} is prior work;
this manuscript neither reproves it nor claims a new result for $m=3$.

\subsection{What the Lean development establishes}
The accompanying Lean project is a partial formalization. Its individual
theorems, explicit hypotheses, pinned dependencies, and coverage ledger
determine its precise scope. It proves the actual Gaussian noise integral's
infinite signed Hermite expansion for almost-everywhere measurable simplex
assignments. The first three levels are identified with label masses,
centroids, and the full quadratic moment tensor. The regular reference is
constructed in the exact dimensions used by the theorem; its winning
probabilities, full centroid vectors, and centroid energy are also checked.

The actual-to-affine comparison retains the positive centroid square and
the complete odd residual. The affine-routing identity derives offset
cancellation from equal masses. Local one-dimensional Gaussian cap lemmas
prove the short-fibre integral, its explicit cubic error, and the
large-perturbation contribution under their stated normal regularity and
moment hypotheses. The exact Gaussian kernel row norm and actual $L^{32}$ normal
trace bound are proved for arbitrary square-integrable inputs; normal
derivative and supremum estimates remain separate obligations. Actual
Gaussian slab and third-label tangent-strip bounds are also checked.
Finite covariance algebra, weighted gauge selection,
scalar asymptotics, and conditional assembly are also supplied.

The development imports attributed, checked proofs of the Gaussian Hermite
basis and noise action, Gaussian hypercontractivity, and Mulgund's
common-threshold comparison. These are prior mathematical results, not
contributions of this manuscript. The vendored sources, exact revisions,
licenses, compatibility changes, and independent statement and axiom audits
are included in the source package. Every first-party theorem is audited by
its compiled source module, including generated helpers; only the standard
Lean axioms are permitted.

The full statement of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is recorded as an unproved
proposition. The Gaussian covariance/offset geometry, weighted facet-energy
bounds, complete facet/cap assembly, dimension-and-noise-uniform localization,
and discrete transfer still require formal proofs. A conditional assembly
or a fixed-constant eventual threshold does not establish these inputs.
Neither source checksums nor PDF compilation certify the analytic argument.
