\begin{abstract}
We present an argument for the negative Gaussian simplex inequality for
every sufficiently large number of parts, throughout the negative voting
interval. The comparison permits arbitrary measurable simplex-valued
assignments, without prescribed masses, in every finite ambient dimension
at least one less than the number of parts. The argument combines
competitive mass localization, a deficit-relative local comparison, and
an exterior estimate obtained from calibrated covariance derivatives and
an exactly centered equal-row test matrix. The local calculation retains
a positive centroid square and controls perturbations at all amplitudes;
the exterior calculation controls the entire signed Hermite tail.
Equality is characterized by regular simplex indicator partitions.
Using the Isaksson--Mossel transfer and the credited Frieze--Jerrum
endpoint characterization, the conclusion gives negative Plurality Is
Stablest and the exact fixed-alphabet MAX-$m$-CUT approximation threshold,
with Unique Games assumed only for hardness. The alphabet threshold is
existential. We state all analytic dependencies and provide the supporting
proof notes with the source package. The accompanying Lean development
formalizes Gaussian routing and integrability, finite covariance identities,
scalar asymptotics, and conditional assembly. The comparison estimates
themselves remain analytic proof obligations.
\end{abstract}
