\section{Discrete plurality and the fixed-alphabet cut threshold}
\label{sec:discrete}
This section applies the primary transfer results of Isaksson--Mossel
\cite{IM}, with the precise negative-range and single-rule bookkeeping
in the endpoint-only transfer note \cite{endpointtransfer}. Its conclusions inherit the
proof-candidate status of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
Write $s_m(\rho)=S_\rho(g)$.

On $[m]^k$ use uniform product measure. The coordinate noise channel is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:discrete-channel}
 \Prob\{Y_j=a\mid X_j=b\}
       =\rho\one_{\{a=b\}}+(1-\rho)/m,
\end{equation}
independently for $j\le k$. Its allowed range is
$-1/(m-1)\le\rho\le1$. The stability of a simplex-valued rule is
$\E\ip{F(X)}{F(Y)}$. With any orthonormal product Fourier basis, put
\[
 \operatorname{Inf}^{\le d_0}_j(F_i)
  =\sum_{\substack{\sigma_j\ne0\\|\operatorname{supp}\sigma|\le d_0}}
                   \widehat F_i(\sigma)^2.
\]
The ordinary influence is the same sum without the degree restriction.

\begin{corollary}[Negative Plurality is Stablest]\label{cor:plurality}
For every fixed $m\ge m_0$, every $\rho\in[-1/(m-1),0)$ and every
$\varepsilon>0$, there are $d_0\ge1$ and $\tau>0$, depending only on
$(m,\rho,\varepsilon)$, with the following property. For every voter
count $k\ge1$ and every $F:[m]^k\to\Delta_m$ satisfying
$\operatorname{Inf}^{\le d_0}_j(F_i)\le\tau$ for every $i,j$,
\[
 S_\rho(F)\ge s_m(\rho)-\varepsilon
   =\lim_{\ell\to\infty}S_\rho(\operatorname{Plurality}_{\ell,m})
       -\varepsilon.
\]
No output-balance, label-equivariance, unanimity, or purity assumption
is imposed. The influence parameters are independent of $k$; no
explicit or uniform-in-$m$ influence threshold is claimed.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Theorem~7.1 of \cite{IM}, at the fixed parameters in the statement,
transfers $F$ to a pure Gaussian assignment $h$ in dimension
$k(m-1)$ with the same masses and
$|S_\rho(h)-S_\rho(F)|\le\varepsilon$.
This finite dimension is at least $m-1$, so Theorem~\ref{thm:main}
applies. Its absence of a mass constraint is essential here.
Lemma~7.3 of \cite{IM} identifies the Gaussian regular value with the
plurality limit by the joint central limit theorem; Gaussian ties have
measure zero, so the finite plurality tie convention does not affect
the limit. These two statements give the displayed inequality.
\end{proof}

The stronger ordinary-influence assumption also suffices. So does a
bound by $\tau$ on the sum of the ordinary influences of all output
labels at each coordinate. The negative endpoint of
\eqref{eq:discrete-channel} is included: a coordinate then changes
uniformly to a different label. At $\rho=0$, the conclusion follows
without any influence restriction from $S_0(F)=\norm{\E F}^2\ge1/m$.
There is no implication here for positive noise or for a channel below
the negative endpoint.

\subsection{The exact Frieze--Jerrum constant}
Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:FJ-constant}
 \mathfrak a_m=\min_{-1/(m-1)\le\rho\le0}
       \frac{m}{m-1}\frac{1-s_m(\rho)}{1-\rho}.
\end{equation}
This is an exact fixed-$m$ constant. The endpoint characterization of
Garc\'ia-Soriano--Miyauchi \cite[Theorems 4.8--4.9]{GSM} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:FJ-endpoint}
       \mathfrak a_m=1-s_m(-1/(m-1)).
\end{equation}
That endpoint analysis and rounding-specific tightness are credited prior
results. The new Gaussian inequality supplies the separate ingredient for
matching hardness against all algorithms through the UGC reduction. The
value of the ratio at zero is $1$.

For $0\le\rho<1$, purity of $g$ and its nonnegative Hermite weights give
\[
 s_m(\rho)=\frac1m+\sum_{j\ge1}\rho^j A_j(g)
      \le\frac1m+\rho\left(1-\frac1m\right).
\]
Thus the regular ratio is at least $1$ throughout the positive interval.
Its usual Frieze--Jerrum infimum over the full channel interval is
therefore exactly \eqref{eq:FJ-constant}. This is the negative-range
reduction of \cite[Lemma~A.4]{IM}; no positive-noise simplex optimality
is needed.

\begin{corollary}[Sharp fixed-$m$ MAX-$m$-CUT threshold under UGC]
\label{cor:cut}
For every fixed integer $m\ge m_0$ and every $\varepsilon>0$, there is
a randomized polynomial-time algorithm for nonnegative rational edge-weighted
MAX-$m$-CUT with approximation ratio $\mathfrak a_m-\varepsilon$ in expectation,
and with high probability after repetition and suitable internal slack.
Assuming the Unique Games Conjecture, approximating this problem
within $\mathfrak a_m+\varepsilon$ is NP-hard.
The algorithm and reduction may depend on the fixed $m,\varepsilon$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
An edge of the Frieze--Jerrum semidefinite relaxation has vector
correlation $\rho\ge-1/(m-1)$ and SDP contribution
$(m-1)(1-\rho)/m$. Regular-simplex rounding cuts it with probability
$1-s_m(\rho)$. Equation~\eqref{eq:FJ-constant} and the positive-range
estimate above give the edgewise ratio $\mathfrak a_m$.
Polynomial-precision solution and rounding give arbitrary small slack,
as in \cite[Appendix~A.3, Theorem~A.6]{IM}.

For hardness, the UGC reduction in \cite[Appendix~A.4]{IM}, in its
negative-correlation form, has completeness arbitrarily close to
$(m-1)(1-\rho)/m$ and soundness arbitrarily close to
$1-\Lambda_m^-(\rho)$, where $\Lambda_m^-$ is the unrestricted
Gaussian minimum. Theorem~\ref{thm:main} identifies it with
$s_m(\rho)$ for every negative channel correlation. Dimensions below
$m-1$ are covered by cylindrical embedding. Use the rational endpoint $\rho=-1/(m-1)$, where the completeness
parameter is one, and choose the invariance and decoding errors sufficiently
small in the order given in \cite{endpointtransfer}. The resulting hardness ratio is arbitrarily
close to $\mathfrak a_m$ from above.

The soundness calculation is genuinely single-rule. Conditional on a
verifier vertex $v$, its independently sampled neighbors define the
averaged simplex-valued function
$G_v(x)=\E_w[\one_{\{F_w(\sigma_{vw}x)=i\}}]_{i=1}^m$.
The rejection probability is exactly $\E_v S_\rho(G_v)$.
Corollary~\ref{cor:plurality} supplies the low-degree soundness and
decoding step for this same function in both slots. No inequality for
two independently chosen Gaussian rules is used.
\end{proof}

The approximation statement is unconditional; UGC is needed only for
the hardness assertion. We state the primary weighted formulation. The algorithm and hardness
reduction may depend on the fixed $m$ and approximation slack. No
deterministic derandomization, explicit numerical $m_0$, or running-time
claim uniform in a growing input alphabet is made. The older
asymptotic endpoint result \cite[Proposition~11.4]{KKMO} does not by
itself identify the exact constant \eqref{eq:FJ-constant} for every
fixed sufficiently large $m$.
