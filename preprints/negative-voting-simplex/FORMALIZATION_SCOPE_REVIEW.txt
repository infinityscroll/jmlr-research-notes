CONCRETE GAUSSIAN CONTENT

1. GaussianRouting (nine declarations) uses Mathlib's actual scalar Gaussian
   measures and the actual finite product measure. Scalar singleton nullness
   follows from absolute continuity at nonzero variance. The product hyperplane
   proof separates a coordinate with nonzero coefficient, checks its fibers are
   singletons, and uses the product-integral formula. This is a real Fubini
   argument: the caller does not supply the desired hyperplane-null assertion.
   Distinct coefficient rows then yield pairwise distinct scores almost
   everywhere; finite maximum attainment supplies a strict winner.

   The general affine-hyperplane theorem permits degenerate Gaussian coordinates
   provided a coordinate with nonzero coefficient has nonzero variance. The
   family-of-scores statements instead require every coordinate variance nonzero.
   These are substantive, satisfiable hypotheses. Fin (n+1) means every positive
   finite dimension; standard independent Gaussian measure is an explicit case.

2. GaussianMoments (ten declarations) derives exponential and first-moment
   integrability, coordinate marginal laws, and integrability of finite linear
   scores and their maximum. The exponential proof uses Mathlib's exact Gaussian
   moment-generating function, whose value is nonzero. The use of
   Integrable.of_integral_ne_zero is legitimate: integrability is derived from
   that proved value and is not an unstated premise. The later zero-integral
   rigidity proofs explicitly establish integrability, so Lean's convention of
   assigning integral zero to nonintegrable functions cannot produce vacuous
   purity conclusions here.

   The strongest statement, ae_gaussian_indicator_of_zero_maximum_gap, assumes
   measurable simplex weights, distinct score rows, nondegenerate product
   Gaussian coordinates, and zero integral of the actual finite maximum minus
   the actual weighted score. It internally constructs a maximizing label and
   proves almost-everywhere unique indicator routing. Neither no-tie behavior,
   selector measurability, nor routing integrability is a premise. A separate
   measurable selector is unnecessary: its selected score equals the measurable
   finite maximum, and the conclusion is an almost-everywhere existence/uniqueness
   statement. The remaining zero-gap equality is indispensable and plainly
   visible. It is not derived from full-noise competitiveness by these modules.

3. Integrability (six declarations) supplies a.e. [0,1] bounds for the simplex
   weights and domination for weighted scores. Finite maxima are handled directly
   by finite induction. Routing integrability follows from its equality to a
   maximum minus an integrable mixture. No probability-one assumption is silently
   used in the generic arbitrary-measure versions.

4. GaussianCentroid (three declarations) defines actual coordinate integrals.
   Expected fractional score equals the score/centroid pairing; substituting the
   centroid score gives its squared Frobenius norm; subtracting yields the exact
   expected self-centroid maximum-gap identity. Integrability is proved before
   exchanging finite sums and integrals. These are unconditional identities for
   measurable simplex weights under finite product Gaussians, including zero
   variance. They do not prove a sharp bound for the expected maximum, regularity
   of the centroid arrangement, or noise-stability optimality.

FINITE ALGEBRA AND SCALAR THRESHOLD

Routing (six declarations), Centroid (three), and EdgeEnergy (three) prove their
literal finite-sum statements. In particular, the EdgeEnergy identity is for a
symmetric matrix with every row sum zero. Its left side is the ORDERED-edge sum;
the unordered-edge sum is half of it. The coefficient of Frobenius energy is four
in that ordered convention. This gives coercivity and zero-strain rigidity, but
not a Gaussian facet-weight estimate or covariance Hessian bound.

Threshold (two declarations) proves actual real-power/logarithm decay for fixed
p >= 0 and q > 0, then a natural-number eventual bound for fixed supplied real
coefficients. VotingRange (six declarations) proves endpoint scaling, the
quadratic r^2 m^(7/4) bound, and one threshold with quantifiers

  for every fixed A,B >= 0, exists M >= 2, for every m >= M,
  for every 0 < r <= 1/(m-1), the scalar remainder is <= 1/2.

Thus its scalar uniformity in r is genuine; a separate threshold for each noise
value is not being substituted. The exact endpoint exponent is m^(-1/4). The
analytic constants' independence from ambient dimension/noise is not proved by
this statement and is correctly left outside its claim.

Assembly (six declarations) and the last VotingRange theorem are conditional
real inequalities. Local comparison, exterior deficit, and signed-Hermite bounds
remain explicit premises. Their proofs derive scalar nonnegativity and equality
consequences once those premises hold. The code does not define a full correlated
Gaussian noise functional or identify the scalar variables with all quantities
of the analytic theorem. It therefore does not establish Gaussian simplex
optimality, negative Plurality Is Stablest, or UGC hardness.

AUDIT DESIGN AND SOURCE ESCAPES

No sorry, admit, custom axiom, unsafe declaration, native_decide, implemented_by,
extern, or alternate evaluation escape was found in the proof modules. Ordinary
ring, linarith, norm_num, simp, and decide uses produce proof terms; no native
oracle is substituted for the kernel proof. Noncomputable choice supplies finite
maximizers and uses the stated standard classical foundation.

Inventory.lean reads the compiled environment, lists imported NegativeVoting
modules, rejects custom axioms in that namespace, and lists its theorem constants.
AxiomAudit.lean contains direct #print axioms commands. verify.py rebuilds, compares
that compiled inventory with the audit list, verifies that every source module is
imported, and rejects transitive axioms outside propext, Classical.choice, and
Quot.sound. This addresses hidden admitted proof dependencies; source scanning
alone would not suffice. The lexical comment-removal scan is only a secondary
check, not a complete Lean parser. The actual simple source and audit metaprograms
were also inspected. No apparent gap in coverage affects this snapshot.

PUBLICATION BOUNDARY

The inspected preprint calls the development partial, names its genuine Gaussian,
finite algebra, and scalar content, and states that the comparison estimates,
Gaussian analytic uniformity, main theorem, and discrete/UGC transfer remain
unformalized. The formalization README now states the same boundary and makes
the zero-gap premise explicit. No claim that a source build certifies the full
analysis is supported or made in these inspected scope passages.

The final public package should include the completed build transcript/receipt
and preserve this boundary. A future edit expanding the theorem claims requires
fresh semantic review; a successful build alone cannot decide whether a new
statement encodes the intended mathematics.
