# Bounded independent review of the structured native runs

27 October 2026. This is a source/read-only diagnostic, not a new experiment. Producer files were not changed. The inspected driver `run_structured_release.py` has SHA256 `a1b8bd30125762c5bbf1c0794ba103a9a652092b7745718231b3b75b111d8bea`.

**Finding: no obvious optimizer-interface mismatch was found. The observed float64 convergence materially limits the practical failure claim, but does not contradict the exact expected-error theorem.**

The driver invokes the unchanged released ASkotchV2, Nyström and ten-loop power routines. Its structured model adapter has the correct equicorrelation block operator, trace, response `(lambda+delta)*u`, stale/repaired gradient distinction and zero initialization. The recorded A-energy is the exact structured expression `(lambda+delta)||e||^2+(1-delta)(sum e)^2`, evaluated with floating arithmetic. The global sum uses a different floating summation route from the FullKRR KeOps kernel operator, as the protocol discloses. This audit does not establish untouched-FullKRR equivalence in finite precision.

For the two fixed float64 seeds, the actual rounded delta is approximately `1.000000082740371e-10`, within the theorem's interval. The recorded native stepsize ranges and near-delta reconstructed smallest eigenvalues are consistent with the exact model; they reveal no order-of-magnitude stepsize or damping substitution. All six paired float64 cases complete 20000 steps, with identical random-draw digests within each seed. The released variants end at relative A-errors about `2.71e-8` and `2.41e-6`; the repaired variants also converge over this budget. Therefore these runs do not demonstrate a practically diverging float64 trajectory.

The mathematical theorem lower-bounds an **expectation over all block and power randomness**, not a quantile or every sample path. Its certified lower bound at t=20000 is only about `0.00102`; two samples can be far below that bound without contradiction. A separate deterministic-eta covariance calculation around `eta=1.0098` is also an expectation calculation, not a trajectory prediction. Neither assertion implies that two observed trajectories should resemble the expected value.

A plausible, explicitly unproved explanation is rare-event domination. With block fraction 0.01, an individual coordinate waits roughly 100 iterations between selections. An unselected coordinate integrates its momentum; a later update can amplify or cancel that accumulated error depending on its random waiting time. A rough scalar approximation has a multiplier resembling `1-G/100`, where G is a geometric gap. Such random multiplicative systems can have negative typical logarithmic growth and a noncontracting second moment. This heuristic is not a proof for the coupled ASkotch process. The correct next evidence would be a derived tail/typical-growth result or a predeclared unbiased expectation experiment, not selecting an unlucky seed after seeing outcomes.

The two float32 cases follow a different mechanism. Their RBF off-diagonal rounds to one, so actual delta is zero, outside the exact theorem's positive-delta interval. The spectral floor sets the smallest retained eigenvalue to approximately `1e-4`. Both terminate with a Cholesky positive-definiteness error, at iterations 368 and 1434; one recorded power estimate also produces a negative reciprocal stepsize. These are retained numerical failures of the structured source test, but they are **not** a floating-point confirmation of the rank-100 exact mean-square theorem. Their analysis must keep the rank-one rounded kernel, spectral floor, and finite-precision factorization separate.

Verdict for reporting: retain the exact-arithmetic result, report the convergent fixed float64 runs and failed float32 cases plainly, and do not promote the package to a demonstrated practical divergence or benchmark failure. No theorem repair is indicated by this bounded driver review. The expected-versus-typical distinction remains a substantive unresolved explanatory issue.
