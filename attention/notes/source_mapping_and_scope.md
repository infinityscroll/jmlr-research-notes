# What the non-Euclidean result adds

28 October 2026. This note binds `p_three_halves_dynamics.md`, SHA256 `262392416101c3332a636c66ddc3df899ff1ca3bba9e303b5e0e7b2f1571e23d`. It is a scope and implementation check, not historical novelty clearance.

The new proof removes the p=2 restriction from the selected-path counterexample by establishing a concrete p=3/2 orbit under the exact entrywise mirror update. The earlier Euclidean proof cannot simply be relabeled: dual offsets are conserved while primal rank-one structure generally is not; the local mobility changes with growing weights; and the physical clock acquires a `(1/2)loglog n` correction. The proof handles those changes using an exact signed-square row formula and a discrete stopped-region argument. Initial offsets may be nonzero, and the basin is open in all eight direct-matrix entries.

The true lower selected-query margin fraction is `(8/9)^(2/3)=0.9244816991...`. The selected lower token is correct and the upper selected-token SVM is recovered; first-layer memories approach two different original tokens. The deficit therefore concerns a persistent wrong lower direction, not classification error, token consensus, a factorization mismatch, or an all-query comparator.

The [target paper](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume27/25-0549/25-0549.pdf) uses the entrywise potential `psi(W)=||W||_p^p/p`. Its released `synthetic-data/tools/optimizers.py` performs the exact signed-power dual update followed by the inverse signed power when `normalize=False`. At p=3/2 this inverse is the signed square, exactly the map analyzed in the source. The optimizer SHA256 is `d4ce054aca1fe0001fc9567cfcae1cd72dde60c57910f04198a04172e1f3983b`.

The released `synthetic-data/correlation_calculation.py`, line 22, calls `bregman_correlation(att_svm, Ws[it], p)`: SVM first, trained matrix second. Its helper normalizes both matrices in entrywise p norm. Accordingly, the official discrepancy tends to `1-(8/9)^(1/3)=0.03850028646...`; reversing the arguments gives the different value `1-(8/9)^(2/3)`. The proof and certificate preserve the official orientation. The paper's key-first matrix convention transposes ours without changing the entrywise potential or either metric.

`check_three_halves_interface.py` separately verifies the exact square-completion formula, actual finite signed-square increments, the rational Lyapunov identity and bounds, and the slow-field fixed point. It also compares all eight full autograd derivatives and simultaneous released-SMD updates to independently coded formulas at 24 fixed nonzero-offset states. The maximum gradient discrepancy was `5.628364527915375e-17`, prediction discrepancy `1.1102230246251565e-16`, and update discrepancy zero. No trajectory or tuning sweep was run. These bounded checks support formula/implementation fidelity; the invariant basin and infinite-time claims require the separate analytic audit.

The closest prior comparison remains `../selected_query_coupling_gate_20261028/primary_scope_comparison.md`. In particular, generalized SVM interpretations and gradient competition are established ideas; fixed-data power-mirror max-margin convergence is also established in [Sun et al., JMLR 2023](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume24/23-0836/23-0836.pdf). The attention-specific candidate contribution is an explicit noncollapsed lower selected-path failure with simultaneous moving memories and an exact non-Euclidean iterate proof. The generic idea that the optimizer's geometry changes a margin objective is not new.

Remaining restrictions are two tokens, a paired-sign dataset, fixed identity values and head, no residual/normalization, and a large finite initialization basin with an existence threshold. No p=3/2 compatibility with the paper's printed sufficient one-layer cone has been established here. The published one-layer theorem is not refuted. Nor does this example establish worse test error, a broad realistic-transformer phenomenon, or publication priority.
