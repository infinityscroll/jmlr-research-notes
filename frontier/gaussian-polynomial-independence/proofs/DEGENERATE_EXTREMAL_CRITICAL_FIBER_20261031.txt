DEGENERATE EXTREMAL CRITICAL-FIBER RIGIDITY
31 October2026. New proof candidate; independent audit requested.
No claim of historical novelty or resolution of the full U-conjecture.

THEOREM
Let f,g be real polynomials on R^n and let X be standard Gaussian. Suppose
that for some real c the critical set at value c,
  K={x:f(x)=c, grad f(x)=0},
is nonempty and compact, and every point of K is a local minimum of f.
If f(X) and g(X) are independent, then g is constant. The same conclusion
holds when every point of K is a local maximum.

The Hessians may be completely degenerate. K may be positive-dimensional.
The full level set f^{-1}(c) need NOT be compact. Other critical values may
have noncompact or indefinite critical sets. The hypothesis concerns the
entire critical set at ONE value, not just a chosen isolated component.

1. A sign-definite polynomial witness on the compact critical set
Assume g is nonconstant. Then |g| is unbounded on R^n, and Gaussian full
support implies Pr(|g(X)|>M)>0 for every finite M. Let M0=max_K|g|.
For sufficiently large integer k, with q(x)=g(x)^(2k) and Q=E q(X),
  Q>2(M0+1)^(2k).
Indeed, Pr(|g(X)|>M0+2)(M0+2)^(2k) is a lower bound for Q. Gaussian
polynomial moments are finite, so Q is finite and positive.

By compactness and continuity there is an open neighborhood U of K on
which q-Q<=-Q/2. The local-minimum assumption supplies another open
neighborhood of K on which f>=c: take the union of the individual local
minimum neighborhoods. Shrink U inside this union and inside a bounded
open set. Choose chi in C_c^infinity(U), 0<=chi<=1, equal1 on a neighborhood
of K. In particular chi(q-Q) has one sign and its pushforward under f is
supported in [c,infinity).

2. Value localization and the nonstationary complement at infinity
The real critical values of a polynomial are finite. Its semialgebraic
critical set has finitely many connected components, and f is constant
on each, by piecewise-C1 semialgebraic path connectedness and df=0.
Choose a nonnegative smooth compactly supported function psi on R, equal1
near c, supported in a small value interval containing no other critical
value. Choose a slightly larger CLOSED value band with the same property.

The amplitude
  a_out(x)=(1-chi(x))(q(x)-Q)psi(f(x)) gamma_n(x)
is Schwartz, and vanishes on a neighborhood of every critical point in
that band. To justify uniform integration by parts at infinity, choose
a finite union O of open balls containing K, with closure inside the
region where chi=1. On the closed semialgebraic set
  {x:f(x) is in the larger closed value band} minus O,
the gradient never vanishes. The minimum of |grad f| over its intersection
with a radius-R ball is positive whenever this intersection is nonempty.
This minimum is a positive semialgebraic function of R, so for large R it
is bounded below by C(1+R)^(-N), for some finite C,N>0.

Consequently the vector field grad f/|grad f|^2 and each fixed finite
number of its derivatives have polynomial growth on this region. Insert
smooth cutoffs supported within the larger band and away from K when
defining the vector field globally. Repeated integration by parts against
exp(itf), with the Gaussian amplitude, therefore gives
  integral a_out(x)exp(itf(x)) dx=O(|t|^(-L))
for every L. Boundary terms at infinity vanish by Gaussian decay. This is
the same semialgebraic nonstationary argument used in the already audited
MORSE_FIBER_RIGIDITY proof; no properness of f is assumed.

The Fourier transform of the finite signed pushforward f_*(a_out dx)
thus decays faster than every power. Fourier inversion gives a C^infinity
density. Smoothness alone is enough below; no analyticity claim is needed.

3. Independence forces a signed one-sided measure to be smooth
Since q=g^(2k) and f,g are independent, for every bounded test function H,
  E[(q(X)-Q)H(f(X))]=0.
In particular the entire pushforward of
  (q-Q)psi(f)gamma_n dx
is the zero signed measure. It follows that
  nu=f_*[chi(q-Q)psi(f)gamma_n dx]
equals minus the smooth pushforward from section2. Therefore nu has a
C^infinity density near c.

On the other hand nu is nonpositive and supported in [c,infinity).
A smooth density that vanishes to the left of c has every derivative
zero at c. Consequently, for EVERY integer L,
  |nu([c,c+epsilon])|=O(epsilon^L) as epsilon decreases to0.       (1)

4. A polynomial lower bound contradicts this flatness
Fix x0 in K. On a sufficiently small ball around x0, chi=1, psi(f)=1,
q-Q<=-Q/2, and f>=c. Taylor's theorem with grad f(x0)=0 and a bounded
local Hessian gives
  0<=f(x)-c<=C|x-x0|^2.
This estimate remains valid when the Hessian vanishes or the actual
order of vanishing is much higher. A ball of radius sqrt(epsilon/C)
therefore contributes to nu([c,c+epsilon]). Gaussian density is bounded
below by a positive constant on a fixed small ball around x0. Because
the entire measure nu is nonpositive, contributions elsewhere cannot
cancel this one. Thus, for sufficiently small epsilon,
  |nu([c,c+epsilon])|>=c0 epsilon^(n/2), c0>0.                 (2)
Equations(1) and(2) contradict each other. Hence g is constant. Replacing
f by -f proves the maximum case.

5. Explicit nonproper test with no nondegenerate critical point
For every n>=3 put
  S(x)=sum_{j=1}^n x_j^6,
  f_n(x)=S(x)^2(1-x_1^3).
Its critical points are exactly
  0 and x_*=( (4/5)^(1/3),0,...,0 ).
For j>=2,
  partial_j f_n=12 x_j^5 S(1-x_1^3),
and
  partial_1 f_n=3x_1^2 S[4x_1^3(1-x_1^3)-S].
If S>0 and x_1^3=1, the latter derivative is nonzero. Otherwise all
x_j for j>=2 must vanish, and the remaining equation gives x_1^3=4/5.

The critical values are0 and256/3125. Near0, f_n>=0, so K at value0 is
the singleton{0} and consists of a local minimum. Hess f_n(0)=0. At x_*,
the Hessian has only one nonzero entry, the x_1 second derivative
  -36 x_{*,1}^{10};
all transverse variables first occur to order6. Thus f_n has NO real
nondegenerate critical point. Its level set at0 is
  {0} union {x_1=1},
which is noncompact, and f_n is unbounded both above and below.
The theorem nevertheless proves that every independent polynomial g
is constant. This checks genuinely degenerate and nonproper geometry;
it is not a historical novelty claim.

The essential linear-variable space of f_n is all R^n. If D_v f_n were
identically zero, evaluation on the x_1 axis gives v_1=0. Evaluation at
points with only x_1 and x_j nonzero then gives v_j=0 for every j>=2.
Thus the conclusion is the expected full unlinking conclusion for this
specific f_n, without any unused orthogonal direction.

6. A stronger constraint on the graph-submersion independence pencil
The same argument, without q or independence, proves the following fact:
if f has such a compact extremal critical fiber, its Gaussian law cannot
have a C^infinity density near c. Decompose the positive Gaussian
pushforward using chi and1-chi. The latter is smooth by section2; if the
whole law were smooth, the former would be smooth, one-sided and positive,
contradicting the identical ball-mass argument.

Now let F=X+h(V) and G=g(X,V) be independent, with X standard Gaussian
independent of V. For every real lambda, the law of P_lambda=F+lambda G
has characteristic function
  exp(-t^2/2) phi_h(t) phi_G(lambda t).
All its derivatives decay faster than every power, by the Gaussian factor
and finite polynomial moments. Hence each P_lambda has a smooth density.
No P_lambda can therefore have a compact critical-value fiber consisting
entirely of local minima or entirely of local maxima. This extends the
earlier unique-Morse-point pencil obstruction to degenerate critical sets.
It does NOT classify all Gaussian-divisible polynomials, and does not
assert that Gaussian divisibility reveals an additive input coordinate.

7. Exact remaining gate
This handles arbitrary degeneracy under a one-sided compact critical-set
condition. It does not handle indefinite degenerate critical fibers,
noncompact critical sets, or pencils all of whose relevant members are
submersions. For mixed or saddle critical germs, the localized measure
need not be one-sided, and the smooth-flatness contradiction is unavailable.
The unrestricted graph-submersion case remains equivalent to full U.
No generic perturbation preserving independence has been assumed.

The next concrete mathematical question is whether a compact critical
fiber can have a C^infinity Gaussian pushforward after localization when
its germs are indefinite or have opposing extremal signs. A universal
negative answer would substantially extend the pencil gate, but has not
been proved here. Its truth must not be inferred from the extremal case.
