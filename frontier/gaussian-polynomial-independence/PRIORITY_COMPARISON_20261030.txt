Targeted primary-source priority comparison, 2026-10-30

Conclusion. The checked later theorems do not directly subsume the exact
one-observable, arbitrary-degree additive statement or the isolated-Morse-
fiber statement. That is not a novelty finding. The 1967 primary source
already uses saddle-point analysis and sphere-maximizing directions to
remove variables. It is a direct ancestor of our tail-peeling mechanism.
The exact nonhomogeneous extensions in KLR 1973 Sections11.4--11.6 remain
an unclosed comparison gate. We should not promote these scoped results
as a new major theorem, and they do not meet the full-conjecture objective.

1. Linnik (1962), Sur certaines questions de statistique analytique,
Annales scientifiques de l'Universite de Clermont-Ferrand2,8(2),53--61.
Primary full text:
https://www.numdam.org/article/ASCFM_1962__8_2_53_0.pdf
Cached: sources/linnik_1962.pdf. Precise locator: Section5, pp.60--61
(PDF pages9--10 including cover). The full orthogonal-unlinking assertion
is stated there, with degree-at-most-two and Zinger's two-variable,
all-degree cases recorded. The proposed method uses polynomial moment
equations, finite generation, and algebraic dimension/rank calculations.
No additive or isolated-Morse-fiber theorem appears in that discussion.
This verifies a pre-1973 formulation; it does not independently verify
the contents of the 1954 mimeograph cited by the next paper.

2. Zinger--Linnik (1967), Polynomial statistics from a normal sample,
Dokl. Akad. Nauk SSSR176(4),766--767.
Institutional primary landing page:
https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/dan33365
Public primary PDF read through web tool:
https://www.mathnet.ru/php/getFT.phtml?jrnid=dan&option_lang=eng&paperid=33365&what=fullt
The PDF's Russian text was available to the web reader; a local urllib
download returned HTTP403, so no local PDF is claimed. Theorem2 on p.767
removes coordinate directions associated with extrema on the unit sphere
for decorrelated homogeneous polynomials. Theorem3 treats symmetric
homogeneous observables with a nonexceptional maximizing point against
an arbitrary polynomial. Theorem4 treats a symmetric homogeneous statistic
of centered coordinates. Section4 mentions unspecified nonhomogeneous,
rational, and holomorphic extensions. The proof description explicitly
uses multidimensional saddle points and algebraic geometry. Page766 and
reference1 attribute the conjecture to Linnik's 1954 Calcutta notes.

Our inference from this source: homogeneous additive tail-peeling cases
overlap the old directional mechanism. We have not established that our
mixed-degree additive theorem is new. A different technical proof is not
by itself a priority claim. The source does not state our precise finite
isolated-critical-fiber criterion, but its saddle-point methods make a
method-level novelty claim premature as well.

3. Harge (2005), Characterization of equality in the correlation
inequality for convex functions, the U-conjecture,
Ann. Inst. H. Poincare Probab. Statist.41,753--765.
https://www.numdam.org/article/AIHPB_2005__41_4_753_0.pdf
Cached: sources/harge_2005.pdf. Theorem1.2, p.754: both observables are
convex L2 functions; one is real analytic and minimized at the origin.
This does not cover arbitrary polynomial g or nonconvex additive/Morse f.
Theorem1.1 describes equality in a convex correlation inequality and its
linear-plus-orthogonal-block decomposition. Our scoped claims do not
follow by simply invoking these statements.

4. Malicet--Nourdin--Peccati--Poly (2015 preprint; JFA2016), Squared
chaotic random variables: new moment inequalities with applications.
https://arxiv.org/html/1503.02154
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1503.02154
Cached: sources/malicet_nourdin_peccati_poly_2015.pdf.
Exact locator: Section1.5, Theorem1.7; proof Section3.6. Each observable
belongs to a class of sums of squares of fixed-order Wiener-chaos
polynomials. Pairwise independence yields orthogonal unlinking, including
multiple observables. Its nonnegative-square structure does not include
an arbitrary signed mixed-degree additive f and unrestricted polynomial g.
We cannot assume independence of separate chaos components of an
arbitrary polynomial pair; that would discard the central cancellation
problem. Their theorem is neither a general additive theorem nor the
isolated-Morse-fiber criterion in our notes.

5. Hong--Hu (2020 preprint; Chinese J. Appl. Probab. Statist.2022),
Unlinking Theorem for Symmetric Quasi-convex Polynomials.
https://arxiv.org/html/2012.14568
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.14568
Cached: sources/hong_hu_2020.pdf. Theorem1.1 requires both polynomials to
be symmetric and quasi-convex. It does not subsume our unrestricted g or
nonconvex f. Introduction cites KLR Sections11.4--11.6 and the n=2 result,
making those sections a concrete priority gate rather than evidence that
no earlier relevant theorem exists.
