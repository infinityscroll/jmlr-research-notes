Verdict: the stated one-sided compact-critical-fiber argument passes root
mathematical review. No full U-conjecture or historical novelty is claimed.

I checked the high-even-moment witness independently. If g is nonconstant,
its absolute value is unbounded and every nonempty open tail event has
positive Gaussian mass. The exponential-in-k lower bound on E g^(2k)
therefore dominates the maximum of g^(2k) on a fixed compact neighborhood
of K. This gives a uniform NEGATIVE sign for q-Eq near K, without requiring
g to vary on K. All moments used in independence are finite.

The entire critical set in the selected value band is K: a polynomial has
finitely many real critical values, since its critical set has finitely
many semialgebraic connected components, and it is constant on each.
After removing finitely many open balls whose union contains K and whose
closures lie in the region where chi=1, the remaining closed value band
has no critical points. On radius-R intersections the positive gradient
minimum is a semialgebraic function. The polynomial-growth dichotomy for
such a function supplies a reciprocal-polynomial bound at infinity.
Thus every fixed derivative of grad f/|grad f|^2 has polynomial growth on
the relevant nonstationary region. Smooth cutoffs can be chosen identically
one on the support of the outer amplitude and its derivatives; all added
transition regions are either separated from K in a compact set or still
satisfy the same bound. Gaussian decay absorbs every polynomial factor.
Repeated integration by parts is legitimate, including the nonproper case.
It proves rapid Fourier decay and hence a smooth outer pushforward density.

Independence sets the complete signed pushforward to zero. Its localized
inner portion therefore has a smooth density. Since all critical points
at this value are local minima, compactness permits one neighborhood where
f>=c. The localized signed measure is nonpositive and supported to the
right of c. Smoothness forces infinite-order flatness at c, including the
absence of an atom. The small Euclidean ball around one critical point has
f-c<=C|x-x0|^2 and contributes at least a constant times epsilon^(n/2).
No other contribution can cancel its sign. This contradicts flatness.
The proof never assumes a nonsingular Hessian or a compact full level set.

I separately checked the example f=(sum x_j^6)^2(1-x_1^3): the only critical
points are 0 and ((4/5)^(1/3),0,...,0), with values 0 and 256/3125. The
second point has x1-Hessian entry -36 x1^10, all transverse Hessian entries
zero, and the Hessian at 0 vanishes. The zero fiber is {0} union {x1=1}.
Near 0 the function is nonnegative, and its essential variable space is
all R^n. Thus it is a valid degenerate, nonproper test of the theorem.

The positive-measure variant shows that any such polynomial law itself
fails to have a smooth density at c. If F=X+h(V) is independent of G, every
pencil F+lambda G has a Gaussian factor in its characteristic function.
All derivatives of the other factors are bounded by finite polynomial
moments, so the product and all derivatives decay rapidly. The pencil law
has a smooth density. It therefore cannot contain the proved kind of
compact extremal critical fiber. This is a necessary obstruction, not a
classification of Gaussian-divisible polynomials or a proof of unlinking
for every graph-submersion example.

Remaining gap: indefinite fibers, mixed extremal signs, noncompact critical
sets, and everywhere-submersive pencils. The one-sided argument cannot be
silently extended to those cases. In particular, a polynomial lower mass
bound around a saddle is compatible with a nonzero smooth density there.
