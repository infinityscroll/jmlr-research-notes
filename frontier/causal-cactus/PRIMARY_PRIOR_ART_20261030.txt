PRIMARY-LITERATURE BOUNDARY: COUPLED CYCLE / CACTUS SUBCLASS
Checked 30 October 2026. This is a bounded comparison, not priority clearance.

Status. The new derivation gives a uniform, graph-only, exact real-fiber
classification for its declared coupled-core class and a recursive extension.
It does not classify arbitrary mixed graphs. Its first-moment/Gaussian sibling
work has no bearing on priority here. Existing simple-cycle degree two is
explicitly inherited, not claimed new.

1. Drton, Foygel, Sullivant (2011), Global identifiability of linear structural
equation models, arXiv:1003.1146v3, Section 7, Lemma 9:
https://arxiv.org/html/1003.1146v3
This primary result already proves the simple directed cycle has two admissible
real parameter points generically, and at most two everywhere. Its global
injectivity characterization concerns all parameter points, unlike generic
whole-fiber uniqueness. Our cactus formulas exploit nonzero articulation
coefficients; they do not contradict failure of everywhere injectivity for
graphs containing directed cycles.

2. Foygel, Draisma, Drton (2012), Half-trek criterion, arXiv:1107.5552,
Definition 2, Problem 1, Theorem 1, Section 8:
https://arxiv.org/html/1107.5552
The general generic-identification graph characterization is the recognized
problem. HTC is sufficient, with an ordering condition; its decomposition
discussion is for acyclic mixed graphs.
Our standalone coherent multicycle cactus is strongly connected, with every
vertex having a parent. At the earliest vertex in any proposed HTC ordering,
every possible nonempty source set lies in its half-trek reachable set and
requires an earlier identified vertex. Thus ordinary HTC cannot start. This
proves non-subsumption for the cactus family, not for every new coupled graph.
Some partial-error-probe cycle cases may already be HTC-identifiable.

3. Drton and Weihs (2016), Ancestor decomposition, arXiv:1504.02992v1,
Theorems 2.6 and 3.3:
https://arxiv.org/html/1504.02992v1
Theorem 3.3 supplies identification through ancestral marginals. Tian's
fiber-product decomposition, quoted as Theorem 2.6, assumes acyclicity.
Our marginal calculation is consistent with the former. The new cross-error
support filtering is not justified by the latter's product assertion. In a
standalone strongly connected cactus there is no proper nonempty ancestral
set, so ancestor restriction alone does not identify the cactus.

4. Weihs et al. (2018), Determinantal generalizations of instrumental
variables, arXiv:1702.03884, Theorems 3.8 and 4.1, Algorithms EID/TSID:
https://arxiv.org/html/1702.03884
These are valid stronger methods with cyclic examples; they must not be
described as purely acyclic. Their descendant and half-trek reachable sets
use nonempty paths, so a cyclic vertex can reach itself.
Our inference from the stated rules: on a standalone strongly connected
coherent cactus, Theorem 3.8 cannot apply because v belongs to des(v), violating
condition (a). Theorem 4.1 cannot start with no known edges: htr(v)=V and every
candidate y has an unidentified incoming edge, violating condition (iii).
Consequently the EID+TSID closure from an empty seed set cannot identify a
single edge of this cactus. This is a concrete separation from these stated
criteria. It is not proof that all later methods fail or that the cactus
family has never been treated elsewhere.

5. Ding and Zhang, Iterative Identification Closure, arXiv:2604.09309:
https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.09309
The CURRENT primary record is withdrawn v2, dated 26 October 2026. The authors'
comment is: "The manuscript contains too many errors and is not ready for
publication yet". The accessible v1 is at:
https://arxiv.org/html/2604.09309v1
It is not a valid established frontier to surpass. Its written Reduced HTC
rule has an elementary four-node obstruction, recorded in the separate
WITHDRAWN_IIC_BOUNDARY.txt. This check receives no novelty/breakthrough credit.

6. Wang et al. (2018), Time-invariant biological networks with feedback
loops: structural equation models and structural identifiability:
https://doi.org/10.1049/iet-syb.2018.5004
This paper does study static SEMs with feedback and disturbance correlations;
it should NOT be dismissed as a time-series-only or Lyapunov model. Figure 3
explicitly concerns overlapping loops. Sections 2.2--2.3 provide a different
equation-generation and binary identifiability-matrix pipeline. I did not
establish equivalence or non-subsumption relative to all claims in that work.
This is an unresolved priority lead, not evidence that our formulas are new.

7. Dörfler et al. (2024), Complexity of Identifiability in Linear Structural
Equation Models, arXiv:2407.12528v1, Sections 6--7:
https://arxiv.org/html/2407.12528v1
Generic identification has a PSPACE decision procedure, with cyclic models
discussed. We claim neither a new general decision procedure nor a solution
of the structural characterization problem.

Other exclusions. Directed-tree classification (Gupta--Bläser, AAAI 2024)
has no directed cycles; homoscedastic cyclic graph identification concerns
equal error variances and distinguishing graphs, not our unrestricted fixed-
graph coefficients. No cactus-specific theorem was located by the bounded
exact-title/topic searches. Search absence is not a novelty certificate.

Honest current claim: a checked subclass extension with exact real admissible
branch counts and explicit rational cactus inversion, beyond ordinary HTC
and the stated unseeded EID+TSID rules on a concrete infinite family. Full
literature priority, especially other cyclic SEM methods, remains unresolved.
