COUPLED CYCLE-CACTUS CORES: A STRUCTURAL REAL-FIBER THEOREM
30 October 2026. New independent derivation, awaiting independent audit.
This is a strict extension of the declared recursive E/C/F subclass.
It is NOT a complete structural characterization for arbitrary mixed graphs.
No novelty or priority claim is made; bounded literature review is separate.

MODEL AND GRAPH CLASS
The model is the original unrestricted-variance real Gaussian SEM:
  Sigma=T^(-T) Omega T^(-1), T=I-Lambda invertible, Omega>0,
with directed support zeros in Lambda and bidirected support zeros in Omega.
No equal variance, known error correlation, stability, or latent-rank
assumption is imposed. Positivity is used only in its original sense.

A coupled cactus core has these graph properties:
(1) Every weak component of its directed graph is either an isolated vertex,
    a coherently oriented simple cycle of length at least three, or a
    coherently oriented cactus with at least two cycles.
(2) In the latter case the underlying undirected graph is a connected cactus:
    every edge is in exactly one simple cycle, all cycles have length>=3,
    their block-cut incidence graph is a tree, and every cycle is oriented
    consistently around itself. There are no directed bridges or chords.
(3) No bidirected edge lies inside a nontrivial directed component.
    Arbitrary bidirected edges are allowed BETWEEN distinct components.
    All their allowed entries are free, subject only to Omega>0.
Isolated vertices can have arbitrary bidirected relationships to each other
and to the cyclic components. The directed components are determined from
G; they are not fitted to a covariance matrix.

For a simple-cycle directed component C, an outside vertex v is a partial
error probe if
  empty != (sib(v) intersect C) != C.
Let u be the number of simple-cycle components with NO partial error probe.
Multi-cycle cactus components are not counted in u.

THEOREM
For every coupled cactus core G, outside a proper algebraic subset of its
admissible real parameter domain, its ENTIRE admissible real fiber has
exactly 2^u elements. In particular G is generically globally identifiable
if and only if every simple-cycle component has a partial error probe.
Every multi-cycle cactus component is already generically rationally
identified from its own observed marginal covariance.

This theorem allows arbitrary unknown cross-component error correlations.
It does not assume that the error blocks are independent. The dimension of
the generic fiber is zero in this class even when u>0; the theorem counts
all competing real positive-definite branches, not just local dimension.

1. MARGINAL BLOCKS REMAIN VALID WITH CORRELATED ERRORS
There are no directed edges between the core's directed components. Hence
T is block diagonal. For each component C, exactly
  Sigma_CC=T_C^(-T) Omega_CC T_C^(-1).
Cross entries of Omega may be nonzero; they do not enter this marginal
identity. This is a marginal statement, NOT a conditional-independence or
Schur-complement factorization.

Each isolated vertex has T_C=1. A simple cycle of length m>=3 with diagonal
Omega_CC generically has exactly two admissible real branches, by the
already proved cycle calculation in RECURSIVE_BLOCK_CLASS.txt, section 5.
Both branches are invertible and have positive diagonal noise variances.
The other branch is a rational function of the TRUE block parameters;
it need not be rational in the marginal covariance alone.

For any proposed choices of component branches put
  M_C=T_C'^T T_C^(-T), M=blockdiag(M_C).
Their full forced error covariance is exactly
  Omega'=T'^T Sigma T'=M Omega M^T.
Thus Omega'>0 automatically, because T' is invertible and Sigma>0.
A tuple of marginal branches is globally admissible if and only if it
satisfies the missing cross-component error entries. There is no further
variance inequality that could silently discard a surviving branch.

2. OVERLAPPING CYCLES IDENTIFY THE WHOLE CACTUS BLOCK
Here and throughout this section K means (Sigma_CC)^(-1), the INVERSE OF
THE MARGINAL covariance. It is not the CC principal block of Sigma^(-1).
Write d_v=1/Omega_vv and a_pv=lambda_pv. Then
  K=T_C diag(d) T_C^T.

In a coherently oriented cactus, for every directed edge p->v,
  K_pv=-a_pv d_v.                                      (2.1)
There is no reverse arrow. Also p and v have no common child: such a child
would create a triangle not coherently oriented, or a second cycle using
an edge of the cactus. This proves (2.1) from the matrix product.

If v is an articulation vertex, choose parents p,q that precede v on two
different incident cycles. They are nonadjacent and their unique common
child is v; any extra adjacency/common child would join distinct cactus
blocks into another cycle. Thus
  K_pq=a_pv a_qv d_v.
Consequently, at a generic nonzero-edge point,
  d_v=K_pv K_qv/K_pq.                                 (2.2)
This rational formula forces the same d_v in EVERY admissible branch.
It simultaneously gives all coefficients on arrows into v by (2.1).

Every cycle in a connected multi-cycle cactus contains an articulation
vertex. Each nonarticulation vertex i has exactly one child j within the
component, and
  K_ii=d_i+a_ij^2 d_j.
Starting at known articulation precisions and walking backward along each
cycle segment gives
  d_i=K_ii-K_ij^2/d_j, a_ij=-K_ij/d_j.                 (2.3)
Every vertex is reached before the next articulation or before returning
to the starting one. All denominators are positive true precisions.
These formulas recover d and every a, not just their infinitesimal values.
They force all alternative branches to coincide. The exclusion K_pq=0
removes only a proper algebraic exceptional set, since all cycle edges
can be chosen nonzero and sufficiently small to keep T invertible.
This proves generic rational identification of every multi-cycle block.

The five-vertex figure-eight example consists of cycles (0,1,2) and
(0,3,4). It is already outside the old E/C/F primitives: its directed graph
is strongly connected, is not a single cycle, and has diagonal errors.
It also cannot satisfy ordinary HTC without external vertices: every
vertex has a parent, and every other vertex is half-trek reachable from
it, so the first vertex in a required total order cannot choose a nonempty
HTC set of earlier vertices. This is a limitation of HTC, not a claim that
the example or the entire family is new in the literature.

3. A SIMPLE CYCLE'S NONTRIVIAL ERROR-BASIS CHANGE IS GENERICALLY DENSE
For a length m>=3 cycle, let M_C be the nonidentity branch transformation
in section 1. Every entry of M_C is a rational function of the true cycle
coefficients and diagonal noise precisions. Every such entry is generically
nonzero. It suffices to give one admissible specialization where all are
nonzero simultaneously.

Choose all forward coefficients a=1/2 and all noise precisions d=1.
The second branch has forward coefficient a'=1/a=2 and precision d'=a^2.
Let P be the cyclic shift appropriate to T^T=I-aP. Then
  M_C=(I-a^(-1)P)(I-aP)^(-1).
Expanding the finite geometric series gives its cyclic coefficients:
  coefficient of P^0 = (1-a^(m-2))/(1-a^m),
  coefficient of P^k = a^(k-2)(a^2-1)/(1-a^m), 1<=k<=m-1.
For m>=3 and a=1/2 all are nonzero. Hence entrywise density holds away
from a proper algebraic exceptional set. The identity branch has exactly
the usual identity support. The identified cactus/isolated blocks also
have M_C=I, even though their actual T_C may not equal I.

4. EXACT SUPPORT-SPACE COMPOSITION LEMMA
For two distinct components C,D let E_CD be their allowed bipartite error
support. The true Omega_CD has independent free entries on E_CD. For any
fixed tuple of the finitely many marginal branches,
  Omega'_CD=M_C Omega_CD M_D^T.                       (4.1)
The coefficient of one true free entry (Omega_CD)_ij in the new entry
(Omega'_CD)_rs is exactly
  (M_C)_ri (M_D)_sj.                                 (4.2)
The branch maps depend only on marginal block parameters, not on the
cross-error entries. Therefore the missing-entry equations hold generically
for this tuple if and only if, for every allowed (i,j) and missing (r,s),
product (4.2) is identically zero.

If at least one such coefficient is nonzero as a rational function, that
missing-entry equation is a nonzero rational function of the full true
parameters. Clearing denominators gives a proper algebraic exception to
its failure. There are only finitely many branch tuples, so all tuples
that fail this support test are absent simultaneously outside a proper
algebraic set. The positive-definite domain is an open nonempty set of the
free real entries and is not contained in any nonzero polynomial's zeros.
Conversely, if all coefficients vanish identically, every missing entry
holds, and positivity of the lifted full error covariance follows from
congruence. This proves both directions globally.

This argument is where the finite branch hypothesis matters. An infinite
union of proper subspaces cannot be replaced by a finite union. We do not
apply this lemma to uninstrumented length-two cycles or arbitrary infinite
base fibers.

5. THE GRAPH CRITERION AND EXACT REAL BRANCH COUNT
Suppose a simple-cycle component C is flipped to its other branch and has
a partial error probe v in another component D. Choose i in C with edge
i<->v and r in C without edge r<->v. The coefficient of Omega_iv in the
forbidden output entry Omega'_rv is
  (M_C)_ri (M_D)_vv.
The first factor is generically nonzero by section 3. The second is 1
if D is not flipped, and is generically nonzero if D is a flipped simple
cycle. Thus EVERY branch tuple flipping C fails a missing-entry equation
generically, regardless of what happens in the other components.

Now suppose C has no partial error probe. For every outside vertex v,
its allowed neighbors in C are either all of C or empty. Between C and
an unflipped component D the allowed pattern is consequently C x S for
some subset S of D. An arbitrary left multiplication M_C preserves that
support. Between TWO unprobed simple cycles C,D the allowed pattern must
be either empty or the full C x D: column-completeness from C and
row-completeness from D force this. Arbitrary transformations on both
sides preserve it. Unflipped components cause no change.

Therefore every subset of the u unprobed simple cycles survives, and no
other tuple survives generically. Each surviving tuple gives a distinct
invertible T' and a positive-definite Omega'=T'^T Sigma T'. Hence there
are exactly 2^u distinct ADMISSIBLE REAL parameter points, as stated.
The formula is stronger than merely counting complex roots or Jacobian
rank. It requires no spectral stability restriction.

6. RECURSIVE COMPOSITION STRICTLY EXTENDS THE PREVIOUS CLASS
Start with any coupled cactus core, including an empty core. Adjoin the
previous E/C/F blocks in order, with arbitrary forward directed arrows
from earlier vertices and no error correlation crossing one of these NEW
block boundaries. The core's own cross-component error correlations stay
arbitrary. Treat the entire core as the first block in the old exact
conditional-fiber product lemma.

That lemma remains applicable because it never required the earlier
block to be identified or to have a special internal graph. Thus:
* The resulting graph is generically globally identified iff u=0, every
  new C block has a partial external directed parent, and every new F
  block passes the old vertex-disjoint linking criterion.
* If no F test fails and no uninstrumented two-cycle is adjoined, the
  generic admissible real fiber has exactly 2^(u+v) points, where v is
  the number of newly adjoined uninstrumented cycles of length>=3.
* The old affine fiber dimensions and uninstrumented two-cycle dimensions
  still add; any such failure gives an infinite fiber.
With empty core this includes every previously declared E/C/F graph.
It also includes confounded mutually probing cycle components and all
multi-cycle cactus cores, which that declared class excludes.

7. GRAPH-ONLY PROCEDURE AND HONEST LIMIT
For the core, connected components and biconnected components of the
underlying directed skeleton detect the cactus structure in linear graph
traversal time. Check each cycle's length and coherent orientation, reject
internal bidirected edges, then scan outside bidirected neighborhoods for
each simple-cycle component. A direct implementation takes O(|V|^2+|D|+|B|)
time; no covariance input or polynomial solving is part of the criterion.
The recursive extension uses the existing supplied block certificate and
max-flow linking tests. All operations terminate with explicit finite
graph data. Their completeness is proved only for this declared class.

The full finish line remains unmet. Directed edges between correlated
core components, partially confounded interiors, general overlapping
cycle graphs, and positive-dimensional primitive fibers are not covered.
The four-vertex obstruction below shows why dropping the length condition
and blindly applying the same partial-probe rule is wrong. The finite
support-composition lemma suggests a route through explicit branch-change
supports, but does not itself supply them for arbitrary mixed graphs.

8. LENGTH-TWO CYCLES: AN EXACT GENERIC BOUNDARY
For a two-cycle write T=[[1,-a],[-b,1]], with ab!=1 and ab!=0.
Let P swap the two coordinates and D=diag(-1/a,-1/b). Then
  T'=T P D=[[1,-1/b],[-1/a,1]], M=T'^T T^(-T)=D P.
This is a nontrivial admissible branch with diagonal errors. Its change
matrix is a scaled permutation, not entrywise dense.

Take two disjoint two-cycles on {0,1} and {2,3}, with only the two cross
bidirected edges 0<->2 and 1<->3. Each cycle has partial outside probes.
Yet simultaneously applying the reciprocal swap in both blocks preserves
the diagonal 2x2 cross-error support. It preserves positive definiteness
by congruence and changes T generically. Thus this four-vertex graph is
NOT generically globally identifiable, despite the tempting partial-probe
condition. The exact fixture is TWO_CYCLE_LENGTH_BOUNDARY.json.
This is an obstruction to extending the NEW theorem beyond its assumptions,
not a refutation of a previously established literature criterion.

VALIDATION
exact_coupling_checks.py uses only Python Fraction arithmetic. It checks
2048 flip/support-space cases covering all 512 cross-error supports between
two three-cycles, five whole positive-definite covariance examples with
one/two/four surviving branches, 24 generated cactus reconstructions, the
five-vertex overlapping-cycle fixture, and the length-two simultaneous-swap
obstruction. These calculations supplement the uniform proof; finite
instances are not being substituted for generic completeness.
