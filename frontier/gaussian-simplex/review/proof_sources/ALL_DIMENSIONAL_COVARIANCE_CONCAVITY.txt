MAIN THEOREM
For every probability law nu in P2(R^n), in every finite dimension n,
  F_nu(C)=sup{E[X dot Y]: X~N(0,C), Y~nu}
is concave on the cone of positive semidefinite covariance matrices C.
Equivalently C -> W2^2(N(0,C),nu) is convex in ordinary covariance
coordinates. This concerns C itself, not its square root or a Bures geodesic.

For a target of d+1 affinely independent sites in R^d, all masses positive,
the covariance Hessian is negative definite at every positive definite C.
Strictness for arbitrary target laws is NOT inferred by lifting or limits.

The proof first treats a simplex target. Gaussian boundary identities give
an exact matrix factorization on its triangle/tetrahedron chain complex.
A weighted gauge choice removes the entire potentially adverse term.
Lifting and approximation then give the stated general theorem.

1. The simplex dual, smoothness, and its translated fan
Let y_0,...,y_d be affinely independent in R^d, p_i>0, sum p_i=1, and
nu=sum_i p_i delta_{y_i}. The case d=1 is a positive constant times the
square root of a scalar variance and is immediate. Assume d>=2.
For C>0,
  F(C)=min_h { E max_i(y_i dot X+h_i)-sum_i p_i h_i }.       (1)
The pointwise maximum bounds every coupling. The minimum exists modulo
common offsets: its objective is at least max_i h_i-sum p_i h_i, which
is at least min_i p_i times the offset range. At a minimizer, differentiation
in h gives winning probabilities p_i. Ties have zero Gaussian mass; the
winning assignment therefore attains the dual bound, proving (1).

Every offset vector has the form h_i=c-y_i dot a, for a unique a and a
common c. Thus the score diagram is the normal fan of the target simplex,
translated by a. Every nonempty subset S of sites is a simplex face and
has a nonempty winner stratum
  Gamma_S={x: all i in S tie for the maximum score}.
It has affine dimension d+1-|S|, and is a simplicial cone based at a in
its affine span. Its codimension-one boundary strata are precisely
Gamma_(S union {k}) for k outside S. In particular every pair is adjacent.

At C=I, write gamma_d for the standard Gaussian density. For every such
stratum define its intrinsic Gaussian mass and conditional mean
  mu_S=int_(Gamma_S) gamma_d dH^(d+1-|S|)>0,
  m_S=mu_S^(-1) int_(Gamma_S) x gamma_d dH^(d+1-|S|).
A zero-dimensional stratum has mass gamma_d(a) and mean a. All masses
are finite; all conditional polynomial moments exist. Let P_S be the
orthogonal projector onto the stratum's tangent space.

The covariance/offset objective is smooth locally: it is the convolution
of the fixed simplex support function max_i y_i dot x with a positive
Gaussian density, evaluated at -a, plus the linear term (sum p_i y_i) dot a.
The support function has linear growth, so derivatives under this Gaussian
integral are locally dominated to every required order. Its offset Hessian
is a complete-graph Laplacian with positive facet weights, hence invertible
modulo common constants. The implicit function theorem gives smooth
minimizing offsets and the optimized Hessian used below. This avoids any
unproved regularity assertion at moving multiple junctions.

2. Gaussian integration by parts on every stratum
Let S be a stratum of positive dimension. For k outside S, let n_(S,k)
be the unit normal WITHIN its affine span, pointing inward from its boundary
Gamma_(S union {k}). If i is any member of S, then
  n_(S,k)=P_S(y_i-y_k)/|P_S(y_i-y_k)|.
Intrinsic Gaussian integration by parts gives
  mu_S P_S m_S=sum_(k outside S) mu_(S+k) n_(S,k).          (2)
For a facet e={i,j}, a second integration gives
  mu_e(P_e-Cov_e X)
    =sum_(k outside e) mu_(e+k) n_(e,k)(m_e-m_(e+k))^T.    (3)
The means differ by tangent vectors, since both strata lie in the same
affine facet. The sign in (3) follows from applying the divergence theorem
to (x-m_e)gamma_d: the outward boundary normal is -n_(e,k).
Gaussian decay eliminates boundary terms at infinity. These statements
can be proved on truncated polyhedra and passed to the limit; their new
sphere-boundary integrals vanish by Gaussian polynomial tail bounds.

We also use the standard Brascamp--Lieb covariance consequence
  Cov_e X <= P_e.                                       (4)
Indeed, in orthonormal coordinates on the facet's affine span, the
conditional law has a quadratic Gaussian potential with Hessian I,
restricted to a convex cone. Approximate the convex indicator potential
by nonnegative smooth convex barriers, retain the quadratic potential,
and apply the variance inequality to linear functions. Gaussian domination
passes second moments to the limit. No strictness or invertibility of the
covariance deficit is required. The underlying classical input is
Brascamp--Lieb, JFA22(1976),366-389, DOI10.1016/0022-1236(76)90004-5.

3. Facet matrices and global barycentric vectors
Orient each edge e=(i,j) with i<j and put
  d_e=y_i-y_j, ell_e=|d_e|, w_e=mu_e/ell_e.
For a triangle g={i,j,k}, let A_g>0 be its Euclidean area, and set
  D_g=2A_g/mu_g.                                        (5)
The symbol D=diag(D_g) below is a positive diagonal matrix indexed by
triangles. It must not be confused with a facet covariance deficit.

Let l_k(y) be the barycentric coordinate of target vertex k and define
  v_k=-grad l_k.
These GLOBAL vectors satisfy
  sum_k v_k=0,
  v_k dot(y_i-y_j)=delta_(k,j)-delta_(k,i).                (6)
For k outside facet e, v_k is tangent to that facet. Within its
(d-1)-dimensional tangent space, the d-1 vectors P_e(y_i-y_k), k outside e,
form a basis, whose dual rows are v_k^T. Indeed their pairings are exactly
delta_kl by (6). The choice of i or j as the first vertex makes no difference
after projecting onto the facet.

Let N_e have columns (mu_g/ell_e)n_(e,k), indexed by g=e+k, and T_e have
columns m_e-m_g. The elementary triangle height formula gives
  |P_e(y_i-y_k)|=2A_g/ell_e,
so
  N_e[:,g]=P_e(y_i-y_k)/D_g,
  row_g(N_e^(-1))=D_g v_k^T.                            (7)
The inverse is taken in any orthonormal coordinates on the facet tangent
space; (7) is its coordinate-independent form on tangent vectors.

Define the positive semidefinite facet deficit
  E_e=w_e(P_e-Cov_e X).
Equations(3)-(4) imply E_e=N_e T_e^T, symmetrically, hence
  T_e=E_e N_e^(-T),
  B_e=N_e^(-1)E_e N_e^(-T)=N_e^(-1)T_e >=0.              (8)
No inverse of E_e is used. Equation(2) on the facet says
w_e P_e m_e=N_e 1, so the local B entries have the exact form
  (B_e)_(g,g')=1/w_e-D_g v_k dot m_(g'),  g=e+k.          (9)

4. Cycle space, its gauge, and the assembled comparison
Use the usual oriented simplicial boundary maps on the complete simplex:
  partial_2: triangle coefficients -> edge coefficients,
  partial_3: tetrahedron coefficients -> triangle coefficients.
All edges, triangles and tetrahedra are ordered by increasing labels.
For example partial_2[i,j,k]=[j,k]-[i,k]+[i,j]. With d_e=y_i-y_j,
these signed edge vectors telescope to zero, as do differences z_i-z_j
of ANY chosen cell vectors z_i.

The cycle space of the complete edge graph is im partial_2, and
ker partial_2=im partial_3. These elementary exactness statements follow
by coning a cycle to a fixed vertex; equivalently partial(cone)+cone(partial)
is the identity in positive chain degree. Redundant tetrahedron boundaries
cause no difficulty. For d=2, partial_3 is absent and its image is zero.

Let W=diag(w_e) and
  A=partial_2^T W^(-1) partial_2.
For a triangle vector lambda, put eta_(e,g)=(partial_2)_(e,g)lambda_g
on the triangles incident to e, and define B by
  lambda^T B lambda=sum_e eta_e^T B_e eta_e.
A and B are positive semidefinite. A may have a kernel, precisely the
cycle-coordinate gauge im partial_3.

For each tetrahedron h of target vertices, let V_h>0 be its Euclidean
THREE-dimensional volume, even when the full ambient dimension is larger.
Set c_h=mu_h/(6V_h). We claim the exact assembled identity
  A-B=-D partial_3 diag(c_h) partial_3^T D.                (10)
This is the central calculation. It is proved entry by entry next.

5. Proof of the factorization, including diagonal entries
Take triangles g!=g' sharing the edge e, let k be the vertex of g outside
e, and put h=g union g'. Since k is outside g', v_k is tangent to Gamma_g'.
In its first-moment formula(2), v_k annihilates every boundary normal except
the one corresponding to adding k: equation(6) pairs it to zero with every
other projected target difference. For the surviving boundary, its pairing
with the normalized projected gap is the reciprocal target height. The
height from vertex k to the affine triangle g' is 3V_h/A_(g'). Therefore
  v_k dot m_(g')=mu_h A_(g')/[3V_h mu_(g')].               (11)
Consequently the off-diagonal entry of A-B is
  (partial_2)_(e,g)(partial_2)_(e,g') D_g D_(g') c_h.
The chain identity partial_2 partial_3=0 gives, on this shared edge of h,
  (partial_2)_(e,g)(partial_2)_(e,g')
     =-(partial_3)_(g,h)(partial_3)_(g',h).
Thus this is exactly the off-diagonal entry of the right side of(10).
Distinct triangles sharing no edge contribute zero on both sides.

For a diagonal entry g, equation(9) summed over its three edges gives
  (A-B)_(g,g)=D_g sum_(k in g) v_k dot m_g
            =-D_g sum_(l outside g) v_l dot m_g,
using sum v_k=0. Apply(2) on Gamma_g as above: each l sees only the
boundary h=g+l, and its reciprocal height is A_g/(3V_h). Hence
  (A-B)_(g,g)=-D_g^2 sum_(h containing g) c_h,
again exactly(10). When d=2, the triangle stratum is the point a and
there are no outside vertices; its diagonal difference is zero directly.
This proves(10) in all dimensions under consideration.

6. The admissible weighted gauge
Fix any edge-cycle flow j. Choose lambda with partial_2 lambda=j.
Its representatives form lambda+im partial_3. Since D is strictly positive,
there is a representative minimizing lambda^T D lambda on this affine
space. Its first-order condition is
  partial_3^T D lambda=0.                               (12)
No injectivity of partial_3 is needed: this is ordinary orthogonal projection
onto its finite-dimensional image in a positive inner product. This change
of lambda does not change j. For this representative, (10) gives
  lambda^T B lambda=lambda^T A lambda=j^T W^(-1)j.        (13)
Only cycle coordinates are changed. The actual offsets, target masses,
Gaussian covariance and coupling are not changed by this gauge choice.

Give the cells arbitrary vectors z_i, put z_e=z_i-z_j and c_e=z_e dot m_e.

8. The exact covariance Hessian and strict concavity

H/2 and second derivative -H^2/4. Moving-interface differentiation of(1)
and elimination of the offset derivative give

   =min_b sum_e w_e E_e[(d_e dot H X+b_i-b_j)^2]
      -sum_e w_e|H d_e|^2,                              (17)
where E_e[...] in this display means CONDITIONAL EXPECTATION on the facet,
not the matrix E_e used above. The interior term follows from
  E[X Y(X)^T]=sum_e w_e d_e d_e^T
by Gaussian integration by parts for the winning assignment. Every
unoriented facet is counted once. The factor4 and the sign of the interior
term follow from the two square-root derivatives. The offset Hessian is
invertible on its quotient, so the minimum is the actual Schur complement.

Set z_i=H y_i in(16). Decomposing each conditional second moment in(17)
into variance and mean, the cycle term is bounded by(16). Therefore
  4 F''(I)[H,H]
    <=-sum_e w_e |(I-P_e)H d_e|^2
     =-sum_e w_e (d_e^T H d_e)^2/ell_e^2.                (18)

All facet weights in(18) are strictly positive.

9. Singular covariances, arbitrary finite targets, and P2 limits
For a fixed P2 target, common-standard-Gaussian coupling and gluing give
  |F_nu(C)-F_nu(C')|
     <=sqrt(E|Y|^2)||C^(1/2)-C'^(1/2)||_F.               (19)
Any coupling with C^(1/2)G can be lifted to a coupling with G, including
when C is singular by conditional randomization in its null coordinates.
Thus square-root continuity extends the simplex-target concavity to all
PSD matrices by adding epsilon I and taking epsilon down to zero.

Now let nu=sum_(i=1)^m p_i delta_(y_i) be ANY finite target in R^n.
Remove zero masses and duplicate sites if desired. Lift its sites to
  y_i'=(y_i,epsilon e_i) in R^(n+m), epsilon>0.
They are affinely independent. With source covariance diag(C,0), its
Gaussian has the form (X,0), and every transport cost is exactly X dot y_i.
Couplings correspond by retaining the site label, so
  F_(nu')(diag(C,0))=F_nu(C).                            (20)
Subtract a common lifted site and project onto the (m-1)-dimensional
span of lifted site differences. The Gaussian covariance on that span
is a fixed LINEAR restriction of diag(C,0), possibly singular. The proved
simplex PSD concavity therefore pulls back to concavity in C. No limit
in epsilon or hidden positive-rank assumption is needed.

Finally, for any two P2 targets, gluing their optimal target coupling
to a Gaussian-source coupling and using Cauchy--Schwarz gives
  |F_nu(C)-F_(nu')(C)|<=sqrt(tr C) W2(nu,nu').             (21)
Finite laws are dense in P2 in every finite dimension. Pass to the limit
