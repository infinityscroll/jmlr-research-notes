Definitions and result

Fix an integer m>=3, put d=m-1 and P=I_m-11^T/m. Let Q be an m by d
matrix with Q^T Q=I_d and QQ^T=P; identify its rows with vectors r_i in R^d.
For C>=0 define
  j(C)=inf_(sum h_i=0) E max_i(r_i dot X+h_i), X~N(0,C).
The full score covariance is K=QCQ^T, and J_m(K)=j(C).
Work on tr C=T=m(m-1), or equivalently tr K=T and K1=0. The regular point
is C_*=mI_d, K_*=mP. Write mu_m=E max_i Z_i for iid standard Gaussians.
Then j(C_*)=sqrt(m) mu_m.

Let v_q=pi^(q/2)/Gamma(q/2+1) be the volume of the q-dimensional unit ball.
Define the following explicit positive constants:

  rho_m = 1/(2 sqrt(3m)),
  R_m = sqrt(2m)(m-1) + 2 sqrt(m-2)/m + rho_m,
  b_m = v_(m-2) rho_m^(m-2) (2pi)^(-(m-1)/2) exp(-R_m^2/2),
  g_m = b_m / [8 (3m)^(3/2) sqrt((m-1)(m-2))],
  K_m = 2 + 2/[m(m-1)g_m].                              (1)

Equivalently,
  b_m = exp(-R_m^2/2)
        /[sqrt(2pi) Gamma(m/2) (2 sqrt(6m))^(m-2)].
These are analytic expressions, not floating-point certified decimals.
For computation, their logarithms should be used to avoid underflow.

The global covariance gap is
  J_m(K_*)-J_m(K) >= g_m ||K-K_*||_F^2                 (2)
for every K>=0 with K1=0 and tr K=m(m-1).

For balanced relaxed assignments f_i:R^N->[0,1], sum f_i=1 and E f_i=1/m,
put b_i=E[X f_i(X)], W=sum_i |b_i|^2, and
  w_m=mu_m^2/(m-1).
When N>=m-1, let b_* be any regular reference centroid configuration with
Gram (w_m/(m-1))P. Then
  W<=w_m,
  min_(O in O(N)) sum_i |b_i-(b_* O)_i|^2
       <= K_m (w_m-W).                                (3)
The row convention includes reflections. Permutations of regular-simplex
labels are already realized by orthogonal transformations.

1. Uniform offset and apex bounds in a specified covariance ball

Assume tr C=T and ||C-mI_d||_F<=m/2. Then every eigenvalue of C is between
m/2 and 3m/2. In whitened coordinates put y_i=C^(1/2)r_i. The score vector
S_i=y_i dot Z, Z~N(0,I_d), has E|S|^2=tr C=T.

The minimized offset objective has a minimizer: for mean-zero h its value
is at least max_i h_i, which tends to infinity with ||h||. At its optimizer
h, the value is at most the value at h=0, hence
  M=max_i h_i <= j(C) <= E max_i S_i <= E|S| <= sqrt(T).
For any mean-zero h with h_i<=M, put k_i=M-h_i>=0. Since sum k_i=mM,
  |h|^2=sum k_i^2-mM^2 <= m(m-1)M^2 <= m(m-1)T.       (4)
In particular range(h)<=m sqrt(T), as in the coercivity bound.

There is a unique translated apex a with h_i=-y_i dot a: indeed h lies
in 1-perp, and
  a=-C^(-1/2)Q^T h.
Equation (4) and lambda_min(C)>=m/2 yield
  |a|^2 <= (2/m)|h|^2 <= 2(m-1)T,
  |a| <= sqrt(2m)(m-1).                               (5)
Thus the optimizer's cells are a plus the cones where y_i dot z is maximal.
The offset stationarity makes their probabilities 1/m. No separate bound
on cell masses is used to establish (5).

2. A positive-measure ball inside every translated facet

For an edge e={i,j}, let d_e=y_i-y_j, ell_e=|d_e|, and put
  z_e=C^(-1/2)(r_i+r_j).
The regular-row identities r_i dot r_j=delta_ij-1/m give
  d_e dot z_e=0,
  (y_i-y_k) dot z_e=(y_j-y_k) dot z_e=1  (k notin {i,j}).
Also
  |r_i+r_j|^2=2(m-2)/m,
  |z_e|<=2 sqrt(m-2)/m,
  |y_i-y_k|<=sqrt(3m), ell_e<=sqrt(3m).                (6)

If v is tangent to d_e-perp and |v|<=rho_m, then all the displayed score
gaps at z_e+v remain at least 1-sqrt(3m)rho_m=1/2. Consequently the entire
(m-2)-dimensional tangent ball
  {a+z_e+v: v dot d_e=0, |v|<=rho_m}
lies in the relative interior of the ij facet. Every point of this ball
has norm at most R_m by (5)--(6).

Let mu_e denote the intrinsic Gaussian mass of that facet: it integrates
the FULL d-dimensional standard Gaussian density against (d-1)-dimensional
Hausdorff measure. Its restriction to this tangent ball gives
  mu_e >= (2pi)^(-d/2) exp(-R_m^2/2) v_(d-1)rho_m^(d-1)
        =b_m.                                         (7)
No conditional-Gaussian normalization is being inserted into this mass.

3. Pull back the strict covariance Hessian correctly

For a symmetric direction H in the reduced covariance coordinates, let
  S=C^(-1/2) H C^(-1/2).
The path C+tH is represented after whitening by source covariance I+tS
and the FIXED target sites y_i=C^(1/2)r_i. Therefore the main theorem's
Hessian estimate at identity, applied to this target, says
  4 D^2j(C)[H,H]
    <= -sum_(i<j) (mu_ij/ell_ij^3)
                    [(r_i-r_j)^T H(r_i-r_j)]^2.         (8)
The numerator follows from d_ij^T S d_ij=(r_i-r_j)^T H(r_i-r_j).
In particular, no extra covariance powers are missing from (8).

Here is the exact edge identity needed to control every direction:
  sum_(i<j) [(r_i-r_j)^T H(r_i-r_j)]^2
    =m sum_i (QHQ^T)_ii^2+(tr H)^2+2||H||_F^2
    >=2||H||_F^2.                                    (9)
To verify it, write B=QHQ^T with B1=0 and d_i=B_ii. The three terms in
the square (d_i+d_j-2B_ij)^2 sum to
  (m-2)sum d_i^2+(sum d_i)^2,
  4 sum d_i^2,
  2||B||_F^2-2sum d_i^2,
respectively. Use ||B||_F=||H||_F and tr B=tr H.

Combining (6)--(9) gives the uniform bound throughout this covariance ball:
  D^2j(C)[H,H] <= -kappa_m ||H||_F^2,
  kappa_m=b_m/[2(3m)^(3/2)].                           (10)
The minimized functional is C^2 at every PD C by the Gaussian convolution
and invertible offset-Laplacian argument in the main proof.

4. Local Taylor estimate and one fixed global contraction

Permutation symmetry at C_*=mI makes the first derivative vanish on
trace-zero directions. For completeness, in score coordinates the full
gradient at K_* commutes with all permutations, hence is a scalar on
1-perp. Thus trace-zero directions pair to zero with it.

For tr H=0 and ||H||_F<=m/2, the whole segment C_*+tH stays in the ball.
Taylor's integral formula and (10) imply
  j(C_*)-j(C_*+H) >= (kappa_m/2)||H||_F^2.             (11)

For any PSD C with trace T, its nonnegative eigenvalues give
  ||C-C_*||_F^2=tr(C^2)-mT
               <=T^2-mT=m^2(m-1)(m-2).                (12)
Take the single constant
  theta_m=1/[2sqrt((m-1)(m-2))],
which lies in (0,1) for m>=3. Then
  C_theta=(1-theta_m)C_*+theta_m C
lies in the ball in (11), including when C is singular. Global covariance
concavity gives
  theta_m[j(C_*)-j(C)] >= j(C_*)-j(C_theta)
     >=(kappa_m/2)theta_m^2||C-C_*||_F^2.
Divide by theta_m. Since Q preserves the Frobenius norm under this
embedding, the resulting coefficient kappa_m theta_m/2 is exactly g_m
in (1), proving (2).

5. Explicit centroid-orbit rescaling

Let s=sqrt(T). If W>0, normalize the centroid rows to
  B_i=s b_i/sqrt(W), K=BB^T.
They sum to zero and K has trace T. The balanced score dual gives
  s sqrt(W)<=J_m(K)<=J_m(K_*)=s sqrt(w_m).
The left inequality follows directly by pairing the scores B_i dot X
with f_i and using E f_i=1/m to cancel every mean-zero offset. Thus W<=w_m.
Write delta=w_m-W. Equation (2) and the square-root identity imply
  ||K-K_*||_F^2 <= [s/(g_m sqrt(w_m))] delta.           (13)

Choose a reference B_* with B_*B_*^T=mP. When N>=m-1, orthogonal Procrustes
and the eigenvalues lambda_j(K) on 1-perp give
  min_O ||B-B_*O||_F^2
    =sum_(j=1)^(m-1)(sqrt(lambda_j(K))-sqrt(m))^2
    <=(1/m)||K-K_*||_F^2.                             (14)
This identity includes zero eigenvalues. Rescale B by sqrt(W)/s and B_*
by sqrt(w_m)/s, use the squared triangle inequality, and note ||B_*||^2=T:
  dist(b,orbit(b_*))^2
    <=[2W/(m s^2)]||K-K_*||_F^2
        +2(sqrt(w_m)-sqrt(W))^2
    <=[2+2sqrt(w_m)/(m s g_m)]delta.                   (15)
Here (13), W<=w_m, and (sqrt(w_m)-sqrt(W))^2<=delta were used.
Finally mu_m<=E|(Z_1,...,Z_m)|<=sqrt(m), so
  sqrt(w_m)/(m s)=mu_m/[m sqrt(m)(m-1)]
                 <=1/[m(m-1)].
This is the stated K_m. If W=0, the left side of (3) is exactly w_m,
while K_m>=2, so (3) also holds in that case.

The coefficient is intentionally conservative. Its purpose is a valid
explicit all-m bound; no sharp quantitative claim or improved noise
threshold is inferred without inserting it into the separate transfer.
