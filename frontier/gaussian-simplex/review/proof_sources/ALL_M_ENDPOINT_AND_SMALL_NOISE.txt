A. Exact balanced first-moment theorem, every m>=3
Let X be standard Gaussian in R^N, let f_i>=0, sum_i f_i=1 and E f_i=1/m.
Write b_i=E[X f_i(X)], W=sum_i|b_i|^2, and
  mu_m=E max_(1<=i<=m) Z_i,  w_m=mu_m^2/(m-1),
where the Z_i are independent standard normals. Then W<=w_m.
For N>=m-1 equality holds precisely when f is the regular-simplex winning
partition, up to an orthogonal map, labels and null sets. For N<m-1 the
benchmark is strict. Relaxed assignments and arbitrary measurable cells
are included; no equal-radius assumption on arbitrary centroid vectors
is imposed.

Proof. If W=0 the claim is immediate. Otherwise normalize B_i by
B_i=sqrt(m(m-1)/W)b_i, and let K be their Gram matrix. Then K1=0 and
tr K=m(m-1). Balanced score duality gives
  sqrt(m(m-1)W)<=J_m(K),
  J_m(K)=inf_(sum h=0)E max_i(X_i+h_i), (X_i)~N(0,K).
This is the maximal covariance functional for the uniform simplex target,
so the main theorem makes J_m concave on its whole PSD domain.
Average K over all label permutations. The result is exactly
K_*=mI-11^T, since zero row sums and the trace fix its diagonal and
its off-diagonal entries. Permutation invariance and concavity give
  J_m(K)<=J_m(K_*)=sqrt(m)mu_m.
This proves the bound.

B. Global quantitative covariance and centroid coercivity
The main Hessian bound, or the separately audited local theorem
ALL_CELL_REGULAR_LOCAL_COERCIVITY_20261031.txt, supplies c,r>0 with
J_m(K_*)-J_m(K)>=c||K-K_*||_F^2 in a neighborhood of K_*.
The trace-normalized PSD domain is compact. Fix theta>0 small enough
that (1-theta)K_*+theta K lies in that neighborhood for EVERY K in the
domain. Concavity gives
  J_m(K_*)-J_m(K)>=g_m||K-K_*||_F^2, g_m=c theta>0.        (1)
In particular equality in the endpoint score bound forces K=K_*.
The pointwise maximum inequality then forces f to choose the unique
regular winning label off its null tie hyperplanes. Rank m-1 excludes
endpoint equality in smaller ambient dimensions.

Let O_m be the regular centroid orbit, allowing orthogonal transformations
and label permutations. For N>=m-1 there is a finite K_m such that
  dist_F(b(f),O_m)^2<=K_m(w_m-W).                         (2)
One valid coefficient from(1) is
  K_m=2+2sqrt(w_m)/(m g_m sqrt(m(m-1))),
or any larger number. To verify it, set L=m(m-1). The score inequality and
difference of square roots give
  ||K-K_*||_F^2<=[sqrt(L)/(g_m sqrt(w_m))](w_m-W).
Orthogonal Procrustes gives
  min_(O in O(N))||B-B_*O||_F^2
    =sum_(j=1)^(m-1)(sqrt(lambda_j(K))-sqrt(m))^2
    <=||K-K_*||_F^2/m.
Rescale B and B_* by sqrt(W/L) and sqrt(w_m/L). The two-square inequality,
W<=w_m and (sqrt(W)-sqrt(w_m))^2<=w_m-W yield the displayed K_m. W=0 is
immediate. This proves hypothesis H_m from the existing transfer notes
for EVERY fixed m, with a constant independent of ambient dimension.
These constants are finite and constructive from the analytic derivatives;
no numerical value or uniform-in-m bound is asserted in this note.

C. The exact small-noise Gaussian conclusions
Combining(2) with the already audited finite-cell transfer gives, for each
fixed integer m>=3, positive numbers epsilon_m^+ and epsilon_m^- depending
only on m such that:

Positive correlation. For 0<rho<=epsilon_m^+, among all balanced relaxed
m-assignments in any finite dimension N>=m-1, the regular simplex uniquely
maximizes
  S_rho(f)=sum_i E[f_i(X)f_i(Y)],
where X,Y are standard Gaussians with cross-covariance rho I.

Negative correlation. For -epsilon_m^-<=rho<0, among all relaxed pairs
f,h with E f_i=E h_i=p_i and a FREE common probability vector p, one common
regular-simplex partition uniquely minimizes
  B_rho(f,h)=sum_i E[f_i(X)h_i(Y)].
At equality both assignments equal that SAME partition, up to one common
orthogonal transform, labels and null sets. In particular the single-
partition minimum S_rho(f) needs no imposed mass constraints.
For N<m-1 the same regular benchmark is strict in both comparisons.
The intervals may be different and shrink with m. Neither is claimed to
extend to the full positive or negative correlation range.

D. Discrete consequence and exact limits
At each fixed admissible rho in the relevant intervals, the usual
low-influence m-candidate Plurality is Stablest conclusion follows by
Isaksson--Mossel arXiv0903.3362v3, Theorem7.1 and Lemma7.3. Positive rho
requires balanced output means. The negative single-rule lower bound
allows arbitrary output means. The conclusion has additive epsilon and a
sufficiently small influence threshold; it is not exact optimization at a
finite electorate size. For a uniform m-symbol input channel, restrict
negative rho also to rho>=-1/(m-1), as required for that channel to exist.
No sharp MAX-m-CUT hardness ratio follows from a merely near-zero interval.

E. Historical scope, not a novelty certificate
Heilman arXiv1211.7138v2(2014), Section8, Conjecture3 explicitly isolates
the balanced four-cell first-moment problem and its needed stability.
The present endpoint and coercivity cover it and every larger cell count.
Heilman arXiv2610.32158v1(23 October2026), introduction after Theorem1.3,
still distinguishes the measure-constrained Standard Simplex problem from
the newly solved unrestricted Propeller problem and says four-and-higher
noise cases remain open. These are relevant primary status statements;
they do not constitute an exhaustive priority search for this theorem.

The separate recent Weak Simplex/mean-width results impose equal score
variances or equal codeword radii. The present balanced theorem imposes
cell masses and allows arbitrary centroid radii. The unrestricted propeller
bound optimizes without masses. None of those results is being relabeled
as the theorem proved here. External expert assessment and broader priority
review remain outstanding.
