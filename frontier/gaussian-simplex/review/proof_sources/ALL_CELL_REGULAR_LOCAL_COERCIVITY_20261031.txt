Let m>=3, P=I-11^T/m and K_*=mP. On symmetric PSD matrices with K1=0 put
  J_m(K)=inf_{sum h_i=0} E max_i(X_i+h_i), X~N(0,K).
The trace normalization is tr K=m(m-1). The two-group endpoint theorem
proved in TWO_GROUP_BALANCED_ENDPOINT_20261031.txt is the only nontrivial
new input below: equation(7) there gives a NEGATIVE DEFINITE Hessian in
the squared amplitudes of every S_a x S_b invariant covariance family,
in its positive interior. Mere strict concavity would not suffice.

THEOREM
For every m>=3, the full covariance Hessian of J_m at K_* is negative
definite on the space
  V={H=H^T: H1=0, tr H=0}.
Consequently there are r_m,c_m>0 such that every trace-normalized K with
||K-K_*||_F<=r_m satisfies
  J_m(K_*)-J_m(K)>=c_m||K-K_*||_F^2.                     (1)
No symmetry assumption is imposed on this local perturbation K.
The constants here are existential, not explicit certified numerics.

1. Regularity and permutation symmetry.
Work on the (m-1)-dimensional zero-sum score space, where K_* is strictly
positive definite. Choose fixed regular score sites s_i in that space.
Every zero-sum offset vector has the unique form h_i=-s_i dot a. Thus
the offset objective is a Gaussian convolution of the FIXED simplex
support function max_i s_i dot x, evaluated at -a, with the score
covariance represented in this fixed coordinate system. The support
function has at most linear growth. Differentiation of the positive
Gaussian density therefore gives continuous derivatives of every needed
order in a and in the positive definite covariance, with locally uniform
Gaussian domination. This directly supplies C^2 regularity without a
moving-facet topology assumption. The offset Hessian is the complete
adjacency Laplacian with strictly positive Gaussian facet weights, so it
is invertible modulo constants. The implicit function theorem and envelope
Schur complement give a continuous covariance Hessian for the minimized
functional near K_*.

Permuting labels preserves J_m and fixes K_*. The gradient there is a
scalar multiple of the identity on the zero-sum space. Thus its first
variation vanishes on V. Its Hessian Q(H)=D^2J_m(K_*)[H,H] is a permutation-
invariant quadratic form on V.

2. An elementary classification of the invariant quadratic form.
Write d_i=H_ii, h_ij=H_ij for i<j, D=sum_i d_i^2 and O=sum_{i<j}h_ij^2.
The constraints give sum_i d_i=0, sum_{j!=i}h_ij=-d_i and sum_{i<j}h_ij=0.
Every entry-pair orbit under label permutations is one of: equal/distinct
diagonals; equal off-diagonal entries; a diagonal incident/nonincident to
an edge; two edges sharing a vertex/disjoint. Their cross sums reduce to

  sum_{i<j} d_i d_j=-D/2,
  sum_{i<j}(d_i+d_j)h_ij=-D,
  sum_{i<j}sum_{k not in {i,j}}d_k h_ij=D,
  sum_i sum_{j<k, j,k!=i}h_ij h_ik=D/2-O,
  sum_{unordered disjoint edge pairs}h_ij h_kl=(O-D)/2.

The fourth line follows by squaring each row sum; the fifth follows by
squaring the total off-diagonal sum. Hence there are real alpha,beta with
  Q(H)=alpha D+beta O.                                  (2)
For m=3 the absent disjoint-edge orbit gives O=D, so only one coefficient
combination is needed. No irreducible-representation theorem is invoked.

3. Orthogonal splitting and the two signs.
For any trace-zero diagonal d define
  S(d)_ii=d_i, S(d)_ij=-(d_i+d_j)/(m-2), i!=j.
It has zero row sums and trace, with
  O(S(d))=D/(m-2).
For H in V, T=H-S(d) has zero diagonal and zero row sums. Moreover
sum_{i<j}S(d)_ij T_ij=0, by the zero row sums of T. Therefore
  Q(H)=[alpha+beta/(m-2)]D+beta O(T).                    (3)

For m>=4 use the split a=2,b=m-2. Take a nonzero d constant on each
of these groups, with a d_A+b d_B=0. Then S(d) belongs to the invariant
two-group trace tangent space, and the previously proved strict Hessian
there gives Q(S(d))<0. Thus alpha+beta/(m-2)<0.

There is also a nonzero invariant T with diagonal zero: choose every
cross-group entry equal to t!=0, every within-A off-diagonal entry equal
to -b t/(a-1), and every within-B off-diagonal entry equal to -a t/(b-1).
Its row sums vanish. The same two-group Hessian theorem gives
  beta O(T)=Q(T)<0,
so beta<0. Equation(3) proves Q(H)<0 for every nonzero H in V.

When m=3, the zero-diagonal zero-row-sum space is zero. The split2+1 gives
a nonzero invariant S(d); the singleton version of the two-group theorem
makes alpha+beta<0. Since every H=S(d), this proves the m=3 case as well.
All paths used lie in the positive interior near K_*; the two-group
squared-amplitude coordinates are linear in K, so no chain-rule term is
being omitted when importing their Hessian signs.

4. Local coercivity.
Negative definiteness on the finite-dimensional unit sphere in V gives
a uniform negative Hessian bound at K_*. Continuity gives such a bound
through a sufficiently small neighborhood. The first derivative on V
vanishes, so Taylor's integral formula proves(1). This covers every
local covariance direction, not just the two-group invariant directions.

5. What global covariance concavity WOULD now suffice to prove.
Suppose, additionally, J_m were concave on the ENTIRE trace-normalized
PSD covariance domain. This is NOT proved for general m here. The stationary
regular point would then be a global maximum. Let D_m bound the diameter
of the compact domain and choose a fixed theta in (0,1) with theta D_m<r_m.
For any K, put K_theta=(1-theta)K_*+theta K. Concavity and(1) give
  theta[J_m(K_*)-J_m(K)]
    >=J_m(K_*)-J_m(K_theta)
    >=c_m theta^2||K-K_*||_F^2.
Thus the single additional global concavity statement would imply the
UNIFORM global quadratic gap
  J_m(K_*)-J_m(K)>=c_m theta||K-K_*||_F^2,               (4)
and equality only at K_*. One would not need a separate global uniqueness
assumption or a fresh exhaustive covariance cover.

For completeness, (4) would transfer to centroid-orbit coercivity H_m.
For balanced assignments let W=sum|b_i|^2 and w=mu_m^2/(m-1). When W>0,
normalize B_i=sqrt(m(m-1)/W)b_i, with Gram K. The score dual gives
  sqrt(m(m-1)W)<=J_m(K)<=sqrt(m)mu_m.
Hence W<=w and J_m(K_*)-J_m(K) is at most a finite m-dependent constant
times w-W, by the difference-of-square-roots identity. Equation(4) bounds
||K-K_*||_F^2 by another such constant times w-W.

In dimension at least m-1, the normalized Gram comparison gives
  min_{O in O(N)} ||B-B_* O||_F^2
     =sum_{j=1}^{m-1}(sqrt(lambda_j(K))-sqrt(m))^2
     <=(1/m)||K-K_*||_F^2,
where B_* is any regular reference with Gram K_*. The orthogonal group
includes reflections; equivalently the regular orbit permits label
permutations as well as orientation-preserving rotations. Rescaling back to b
adds only a multiple of (sqrt(w)-sqrt(W))^2<=w-W. Thus the squared
centroid distance from the regular orbit is at most K_m(w-W), for finite
K_m. W=0 is immediate. This is precisely the type of hypothesis needed
