GAUSSIAN FITTING: COVARIANCE CONVEXITY AND EXACT SCALE REDUCTION
31 October 2026. strong_signal_prior_art. Separate corollary for review.

Dependency: covariance concavity for every nu in P2(R^n), proved in

Assume n>=1. Let mu=E Y, M=E|Y-mu|^2, and
  F(C)=sup E[X.Y], X~N(0,C), Y~nu, C>=0.
Centering nu does not change F because E X=0. Independent coupling gives
F>=0; Cauchy--Schwarz gives F(C)<=sqrt(M tr C). Also
F(tC)=sqrt(t)F(C) for t>=0, and F is continuous on the closed PSD cone.

1. The square is concave, including its zeros.
For C,D>=0 and any 0<alpha<1, concavity and homogeneity give
  F(C+D)>=sqrt(alpha)F(C)+sqrt(1-alpha)F(D).
Taking the supremum over alpha gives
  F(C+D)^2>=F(C)^2+F(D)^2.
If just one value is zero, use alpha tending to0 or1; if both vanish,
the conclusion follows from F>=0. Thus no division by a zero value or
positive-rank assumption is needed. Degree-one homogeneity of F^2 now
turns this superadditivity into concavity on the entire PSD cone.

2. Joint convexity and existence of a nearest Gaussian.
The exact centered second-moment identity is
  W2^2(N(a,C),nu)=|a-mu|^2+tr C+M-2F(C).                 (1)
Consequently the objective is jointly convex in the ordinary coordinates
(a,C), with C>=0, and every minimizer has a=mu. This is not a claim about
square-root coordinates or Bures interpolation.
Continuity and the bound
  W2^2(N(a,C),nu)>=|a-mu|^2+(sqrt(tr C)-sqrt(M))^2
make every nonempty sublevel bounded in a and C. It is also closed, hence
compact in this finite-dimensional parameter space. A minimum therefore
exists when singular Gaussians are allowed. No uniqueness of the optimal
covariance is asserted for arbitrary nu.

3. Optimize scale exactly, then optimize shape.
Let Q>=0 have tr Q=1. For t>=0, equation(1) at the optimal mean gives
  W2^2(N(mu,tQ),nu)
    =M+t-2sqrt(t)F(Q)
    =M-F(Q)^2+(sqrt(t)-F(Q))^2.
The minimizing scale is t=F(Q)^2, including t=0. Since the trace-one PSD
set is compact and F is continuous, its shape maximum is attained. Hence
  min_(a in R^n,C>=0) W2^2(N(a,C),nu)
    =min_(C>=0) W2^2(N(mu,C),nu)
    =M-max_(Q>=0,tr Q=1) F(Q)^2,                         (2)
and every maximizing Q supplies the optimal covariance
  C=F(Q)^2 Q.                                           (3)
The shape problem maximizes a concave function over a compact convex set.
This is an exact convex optimization formulation, not an evaluation oracle
or a claim that evaluating F is computationally inexpensive.

If M=0, nu is the point mass at mu, F is identically0, all shapes maximize
with value0, and every formula(3) yields the same covariance C=0. The
nearest law in this special case is the point mass itself. Directions Q
with F(Q)=0 always have best scale0, even when other shapes do better.
If M>0, some one-dimensional projection S=u.(Y-mu) is nonconstant. Its
increasing quantile coupling with a standard normal Z has E[ZS]>0:
half the expectation of (Z-Z')(S-S') is positive. Lifting that scalar
coupling to Y shows F(uu^T)>0. Thus the optimal covariance then has positive
trace, though it need not have full rank. Every minimizer is obtained from
(3) by normalizing its positive trace. Moreover tr C<=M at any optimum.

Important mean convention: with a fixed mean a instead of optimizing it,
the minimum in(2) has the additional term |a-mu|^2. In particular a fixed
zero-mean Gaussian against an uncentered target does not have value(2).

Historical scope
