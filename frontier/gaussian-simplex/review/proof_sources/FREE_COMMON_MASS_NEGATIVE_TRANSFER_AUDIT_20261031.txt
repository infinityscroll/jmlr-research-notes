  dist_F(b(k),O)^2 <= K(w-||b(k)||_F^2), b_i(k)=E[Xk_i(X)],
where E k_i=1/m and K>=1 is finite. In particular W(k)<=w. If W(k)=w,
then b(k)=c' in O, and the identity

forces k to be the regular winning partition with centroids c'. This
derives endpoint equality directly from H_m, rather than adding it as a

1. Uniform qualitative localization, including the nearest frame
Call a pair competitive if B_{-r}(f,h)<=B_{-r}(g,g). Parseval and
simplex-valuedness give, in every finite dimension,
  ||b(f)||_F^2 <= sum_i Var(f_i)<=1-||p||_2^2<=1.
The degree>=2 bilinear Hermite remainder has absolute value at most r^2:
Cauchy--Schwarz bounds it by r^2 times the product of the two centered
L2 norms. The analogous reference remainder is also at most r^2. Thus,
with U=(1/m,...,1/m),
  ||p-U||_2^2 <= r(<b(f),b(h)>-w)+2r^2 <= r+2r^2,       (1)
  <b(f),b(h)> >= w-2r.                                   (2)
Consequently all masses are positive if, for example, r<=1/(4m^2).

Claim: for every eta>0 there is delta_eta>0 such that every competitive
pair in R^d, at 0<r<delta_eta, has
  d(F,g_c):=(1/2)sum_i||F_i-(g_c)_i||_1 < eta,
where F=(f+h)/2 and c is ANY nearest member of O to b(F).

Proof by contradiction. Choose r_n->0 and violating pairs. The feasible
simplex assignments form a bounded weakly closed subset of L2(gamma_d)^m.
Pass to a weakly convergent subsequence for both f_n,h_n. Equations(1)
and(2), and weak continuity of each degree-one moment, imply that the
limits f_*,h_* are balanced and
  <b(f_*),b(h_*)> >= w.
H_m and Cauchy--Schwarz give the reverse chain
  <b(f_*),b(h_*)> <= ||b(f_*)|| ||b(h_*)|| <= w.
Equality yields b(f_*)=b(h_*) and both squared norms equal w. Endpoint
equality from section0 therefore gives f_*=h_*=g_* for the SAME ordered
regular partition. Weak convergence to this indicator implies strong L1:
  sum_i||f_{n,i}-g_{*,i}||_1
    =2-2sum_i<f_{n,i},g_{*,i}> ->0,
and the same holds for h_n and F_n. In particular b(F_n)->c_*.
If c_n is any nearest centroid matrix, then
  ||c_n-b(F_n)||<=||c_*-b(F_n)|| ->0,
so c_n->c_*. Away from the finitely many score-tie hyperplanes the
winning labels for c_n eventually equal those for c_*. Dominated
convergence gives g_{c_n}->g_* in L1, contradicting the violation.
This proves uniform localization. It supplies all smallness needed for
the anchor slabs below, with no unproved quantitative endpoint extension
to unbalanced assignments.

2. Exact mean ordering and a global damped potential gap
Work in any regular reference frame and write G_i=T_{-r}g_i. On reference
cell i, G_i is the unique smallest potential, off the tie hyperplanes.
Here is a quantitative proof for r<=1/16. Set s=sqrt(1-r^2), t=r/s,
z=(v_i-v_j).x/sqrt(2)>=0, and delta=sqrt(2)t z. In the independent-normal
winner representation, subtract label j's mean. Label j has mean0,
label i has mean -delta, and every other mean is at most
  U_x=sqrt(2)t|x|.
Conditional on the maximum M of the other m-2 utilities, the difference
G_j-G_i equals integral_M^infinity H_delta(u)du, where
  H_delta(u)=phi(u)Phi(u+delta)-phi(u+delta)Phi(u).
This is positive for delta>0: Phi(u)/phi(u) is strictly increasing,
as follows from its representation integral_0^infinity exp(uy-y^2/2)dy.
Raising all the other means to U_x can only decrease the difference.
For these raised means Pr(M<=U_x)=2^{-(m-2)}. For u>=0 and 0<=delta<=1,
  partial_delta H_delta(u)
    =phi(u)phi(u+delta)+(u+delta)phi(u+delta)Phi(u)
    >=phi(u)phi(u+delta).
Integrating u over [U_x,U_x+1] therefore gives
  G_j-G_i >= c_m exp(-U_x^2-3U_x) min(delta,1),
  c_m=exp(-5/2)/(2^{m-1}pi).
For delta>=1 use monotonicity when label i's mean is lowered. Since
  3U_x<=U_x^2+9/4,
  2U_x^2=4r^2|x|^2/(1-r^2)<=|x|^2/32  (r<=1/16),
we obtain
  G_j-G_i >= c'_m min(r z,1) exp(-|x|^2/32),              (3)
  c'_m=exp(-19/4)/(2^{m-1}pi)>0.
Thus for ANY relaxed k, with no balance condition,
  D(k):=sum_i<k_i-g_i,G_i> >=0,
and D(k)=0 iff k=g almost everywhere. D is affine on assignments and
D(F)=(D(f)+D(h))/2.

9. Existence and indicator reduction without a false bilinear-extreme claim
For any fixed finite N the set of relaxed pairs with common free masses
is weakly compact in L2(gamma_N)^m times itself. The OU operator T_{-r}
is compact: Hermite eigenspaces have finite multiplicities and |r|^k->0.
Thus the bilinear objective is jointly weakly continuous on this bounded
set, and a minimizer exists.

Fix a minimizing pair and its attained mass vector p. Holding h fixed,
minimize the continuous linear functional in f over the weakly compact
fixed-p simplex-assignment set. Its minimum face has an extreme point,
and an extreme point of a face is extreme in the full fixed-p set.
Every such extreme point is an indicator: otherwise some fixed pair of
labels is uniformly fractional on a positive-measure set; opposite label
perturbations on two disjoint equal-measure subsets preserve all integrals
and split the assignment nontrivially. Choose an indicator minimizer f1.
Then fix f1 and repeat for h, obtaining an indicator h1 at the same p and
same global minimum. This sequential argument, not a general assertion
about extrema of bilinear functions on convex sets, supplies integrality.

10. Equality of original fractional pairs
After section11 identifies every indicator minimizing pair as (g,g),
the sequential construction has f1=h1=g and p=U. The earlier pair (g,h)
has the same minimum, but its objective excess is exactly D(h). By
strict reference ordering, h=g. The original pair then has h=g, and
the identical argument gives f=g. Thus the equality classification is
valid for all original fractional minimizers, not merely for an indicator
representative. This also avoids using positive definiteness of T_{-r},
which would be false.
