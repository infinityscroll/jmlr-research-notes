1. DUAL EXISTENCE, MASS CALIBRATION, AND DIFFERENTIABILITY

For fixed positive masses p_i, the dual objective is at least
max_i h_i-sum_i p_i h_i >= (min_i p_i)(max_i h_i-min_i h_i).
On a normalized offset hyperplane this is coercive. Continuity and finite
growth give a minimizer. At positive definite source covariance every tie
between distinct sites has Gaussian measure zero. Dominated differentiation
in h gives the winning masses; the stationary conditions make them exactly
p_i. The winning assignment thus attains the coupling bound. This proves
the displayed dual, including primal attainment, without an unmentioned
semi-discrete transport regularity theorem.

Affine independence identifies every offset class with a unique translation a.
Every source stratum is the translated normal cone of the corresponding target
simplex face; each has the stated relative dimension and positive intrinsic
Gaussian mass. Its facets correspond to adjoining one target vertex. There is
no combinatorial change as a varies through finite values.

For fixed target sites, the dual as a function of a and positive definite C is
a Gaussian convolution of a linearly growing support function plus a linear
term. Differentiating the Gaussian density gives locally dominated derivatives
of every required order. The offset Hessian has quadratic form
sum_edges (mu_e/ell_e)(h_i-h_j)^2. All edge weights are positive, so its only
kernel is the common offset. The implicit function theorem supplies smooth
calibrated offsets and permits the Schur complement used in the Hessian.
This addresses multiple junctions without assuming differentiability of a
moving diagram merely from a drawing of its cells.

2. INTRINSIC GAUSSIAN BOUNDARY IDENTITIES

Let L_S be the tangent space of Gamma_S. Its Gaussian density in orthonormal
intrinsic coordinates is a constant times a standard Gaussian, restricted to
a translated convex polyhedral cone. Thus its intrinsic density derivative
in direction v in L_S is -(x dot v) gamma_d(x). The outward normal on the
boundary S+k is minus the stated n_(S,k). Integrating that derivative gives
mu_S P_S m_S=sum_k mu_(S+k)n_(S,k), with the positive sign in source(2).

For the facet covariance formula, integrate the directional derivative of
((x-m_e) dot u)gamma_d in tangent direction v. The interior integral is
mu_e[u dot v-Cov_e(u dot X,v dot X)]. The boundary integral is
sum_k mu_(e+k)[(m_(e+k)-m_e) dot u](-n_(e,k) dot v).
This is exactly source(3), including its transpose and sign. Both conditional
means lie in the same affine facet, so their difference is tangent. The
apparently ambient identity has zero normal components on both sides.

Truncation by intrinsic balls is legitimate: the additional spherical boundary
integrals are bounded by a polynomial in the radius times a Gaussian tail,
with any fixed translation contributing only a linear term in the exponent.
They tend to zero. Higher intersections have lower intrinsic boundary measure
and do not create extra terms in this divergence theorem. For a one-dimensional
stratum, endpoints use counting measure and density gamma_d(a), exactly as
specified. Zero-dimensional strata require no integration by parts.

The covariance bound Cov_e<=P_e is valid in these intrinsic coordinates. One
may apply Brascamp--Lieb to the quadratic Gaussian potential plus nonnegative
smooth convex approximations to the cone indicator potential. The Hessian is
at least I. Normalizations converge to a positive Gaussian cone mass, and
Gaussian domination passes first and second moments to the restricted law.
Only the non-strict covariance inequality is needed.

3. FACET BASES AND THE FACTORIZATION, RECONSTRUCTED

Use the source convention D_g=2Area(g)/mu_g and c_h=mu_h/(6Vol3(h)).
Let v_k=-grad l_k for global affine barycentric coordinates. Then
sum_k v_k=0 and v_k dot(y_i-y_j)=delta_(k,j)-delta_(k,i).
For an edge e={i,j} and k outside it, put u_(e,k)=P_e(y_i-y_k).
The vectors u_(e,k) form a basis of its tangent space, and v_k are their
dual vectors there. In particular v_k is tangent to the facet.

The target triangle height is 2Area(g)/ell_e, so the boundary column is
N_e[:,g]=u_(e,k)/D_g and the inverse row is D_g v_k^T.
The Gaussian mean identity yields N_e^(-1)P_e m_e=(1/w_e)1. Therefore
B_e[g,g'] = D_g v_k dot(m_e-m_g')
           = 1/w_e-D_g v_k dot m_g'.
This agrees with source(9); the affine normal component of m_e makes no
contribution because v_k is tangent.

For distinct triangles g,g' sharing edge e, k=g\e is outside g'. Hence
v_k lies in the tangent space of Gamma_g'. In its mean identity it annihilates
all projected target differences except that from adding k. Its pairing with
the surviving normalized normal is the reciprocal distance from y_k to the
affine plane of target triangle g'. That distance is 3Vol3(h)/Area(g'), for
h=g union g'. This formula uses intrinsic three-volume even when d>3.
Consequently
  v_k dot m_g' = mu_h Area(g')/(3Vol3(h)mu_g').
No unaccounted projection or ambient dimension factor occurs here.

If s_(e,g) is the oriented triangle boundary incidence, the off-diagonal
entry of A-B is s_(e,g)s_(e,g')D_gD_g' c_h. The two triangular faces of h
meeting at e have opposite induced edge orientations after multiplication
by their tetrahedral incidence signs. Thus
  s_(e,g)s_(e,g') = -s_(g,h)s_(g',h).
This gives the off-diagonal entry of -D partial_3 diag(c_h) partial_3^T D.
Two distinct triangles sharing no edge cannot lie together in a tetrahedron,
so both matrices have zero in that position.

For the g diagonal, summing the three edge contributions yields
  (A-B)[g,g] = D_g(sum_(k in g) v_k) dot m_g
             = -D_g(sum_(l outside g) v_l) dot m_g.
Each outside l sees only boundary h=g+l in the same mean identity. Its
contribution is -D_g^2 c_h. This proves the entire diagonal. In dimension2
there is no outside l and the difference vanishes, including at the
zero-dimensional triple stratum. Thus in every dimension d>=2,
  B-A = D partial_3 diag(c_h) partial_3^T D.

An independent geometric interpretation of the diagonal cancellation is that
the three barycentric coordinates of the projection of a fourth target vertex
onto a triangle sum to1. This was how I first reconstructed it. The global
barycentric-vector proof in the source is shorter and avoids choosing such
projections explicitly.

4. CHAIN EXACTNESS AND GAUGE ADMISSIBILITY

The complete simplex contains every triangle and tetrahedron in question.
Coning chains to a fixed vertex proves that graph cycles are im(partial_2)
and ker(partial_2)=im(partial_3). This is the full simplex chain complex;
there is no assumption that a selected family of tetrahedra is independent.

For any fixed edge cycle j, choose a representative lambda. Minimizing the
strictly positive quadratic norm lambda^T D lambda over the affine subspace
lambda+im(partial_3) gives the unique closest representative. Its condition is
partial_3^T D lambda=0. Existence holds on this finite-dimensional closed
affine subspace even when the tetrahedron coefficient map has a kernel.
Every tetrahedral boundary equation is satisfied, not merely equations for
an arbitrarily selected independent subfamily.

This gauge leaves partial_2 lambda=j and lambda^T A lambda=j^T W^(-1)j
unchanged. It annihilates the exact discrepancy above, hence lambda^T B lambda
equals the same value. It does not modify masses, source covariance, target
sites, or primal/dual transport offsets. In dimension2 the gauge space is
zero and B=A directly. This step has no hidden invertibility requirement.

The covariance identity says E_e=N_e T_e^T=T_e N_e^T. Thus
T_e=E_e N_e^(-T) and B_e=N_e^(-1)E_e N_e^(-T)>=0.
The square root of the PSD E_e proves
|z_e dot T_e eta_e|^2 <= (z_e^T E_e z_e)(eta_e^T B_e eta_e)
without inverting E_e. In each oriented triangle the differences z_i-z_j
telescope, allowing its m_g to be subtracted from the edge means. Summing and

For completeness, let Lb have components b_i-b_j. At the weighted least

6. THE COVARIANCE HESSIAN AND STRICTNESS

Along C(t)=I+tH, the Gaussian driver can be kept standard and the sites
multiplied by sqrt(I+tH). The first and second square-root derivatives at0
are H/2 and -H^2/4. For the optimized dual, the second derivative is the
minimum over h' of the interface quadratic plus the interior acceleration.
The interface quadratic is sum_e w_e E[(X dot H d_e/2+h_i'-h_j')^2|e].
Putting b=2h' supplies the factor4 in source(17).

Gaussian integration by parts for the piecewise constant winning vector
gives E[X Y(X)^T]=sum_e w_e d_e d_e^T. Each unoriented facet contributes once:
its jump is d_e and the normal pointing toward that winner is d_e/ell_e.
The acceleration is therefore -(1/4)sum_e w_e|H d_e|^2. Together these facts
give exactly source(17), including the sign and factor. The offset Hessian
established above makes this minimum the genuine envelope Schur complement.

The moving-interface quadratic can also be derived by differentiating the
first derivative over each moving cell: the relative interface speed is the
score derivative difference divided by ell_e. Gaussian domination justifies
truncation, and multiple ties have zero facet measure. No higher-stratum
term belongs to this second derivative. The prior Gaussian-convolution
regularity ensures that this calculation represents the actual Hessian.

Applying the cycle estimate with z_i=H y_i cancels the within-facet variances
and leaves -(1/4)sum_e w_e(d_e^T H d_e)^2/ell_e^2. Every weight is positive.
The d edges from one vertex give a basis b_i, and all other edges are their
differences. Vanishing of all their quadratic forms forces first the diagonal
and then all off-diagonal entries of H in that basis to vanish. This proves
negative definiteness for every nonzero H, in every d. There are exactly
d(d+1)/2 edge quadratic forms, with no dimension deficit.

At C0>0, the change of source variable by C0^(-1/2) and the fixed target change
by C0^(1/2) reduce any direction K to H=C0^(-1/2)K C0^(-1/2). This is an
identity of covariance laws and costs; it does not assume commuting matrices
or differentiate a noncommuting product as though it were a scalar. Integrating
the Hessian on positive definite line segments gives concavity there.

7. PSD BOUNDARY, FINITE LIFTING, AND P2 APPROXIMATION

Any coupling of X=C^(1/2)G and Y can be lifted to a coupling with a standard G,
even for singular C, by the Gaussian conditional law of G given X. Coupling
the same G,Y to another square root and applying Cauchy--Schwarz proves(19).
Adding epsilon I to both covariance arguments and passing to the limit then
extends simplex concavity to PSD matrices. Boundary strictness is unnecessary.

The lifted sites (y_i,epsilon e_i) are affinely independent. A source of law
(X,0) has exactly the old cost, so the equality in(20) holds for all C>=0.
After subtracting one lifted site, all target differences lie in an
(m-1)-dimensional linear space S. The subtracted constant changes expected
cost by zero because the source mean is zero. Projection onto S preserves
the remaining cost and gives covariance P_S diag(C,0) P_S, linear in C.
Conversely any projected-source coupling lifts by conditional Gaussian
randomization to the original source. Therefore projection preserves the
optimal value as well as the pointwise cost. Applying the simplex PSD theorem
on S proves finite-target concavity, with no limit in the lift parameter.

Finally glue any source-target coupling with an optimal target-target coupling.
The cost difference is bounded by sqrt(tr C) W2(nu,nu'). Reversing the targets
proves(21). Finite quantization of a truncated P2 target converges in W2.
Passing to the limit at the three covariance matrices in a concavity inequality
proves the assertion for every P2 target. The elementary relation to squared
Wasserstein distance has the indicated affine terms and negative factor2.
One-point target laws have F=0 and are the trivial dimension0 case; spelling
this out would be an optional clarity improvement, not a missing argument.

8. BALANCED ENDPOINT AND CENTROID COERCIVITY

For regular uniform target sites, permutations preserve the support functional.
Averaging a zero-sum score covariance of trace m(m-1) over all permutations
gives K_*=mI-11^T. Concavity therefore makes K_* a global maximizer. Its value
is sqrt(m) E max_i Z_i because the regular winner partition has equal masses.

The strict Hessian just proved supplies local quadratic coercivity directly;
the older two-group argument is not needed as an additional premise. The
gradient at the regular point is scalar on the zero-sum space by permutation
symmetry, so its trace-zero directional derivative vanishes. Continuous
negative definite Hessian then gives a local quadratic gap.

I checked the compact-segment argument in ALL_CELL_REGULAR_LOCAL_COERCIVITY.
Choose a single theta>0 taking the entire compact trace domain into that
local neighborhood along segments from K_*. Concavity gives
theta[J(K_*)-J(K)] >= J(K_*)-J((1-theta)K_*+theta K),
and the local bound gives a global constant times ||K-K_*||_F^2. This proves
global uniqueness as well as a quadratic gap, including singular K.

For balanced relaxed assignments with centroids b_i and W=sum|b_i|^2>0,
normalizing B_i=sqrt(m(m-1)/W)b_i gives score covariance K. The assignment
itself is feasible in the balanced score dual, whence
sqrt(m(m-1)W)<=J(K)<=sqrt(m)mu_m. Therefore W<=mu_m^2/(m-1)=w.
If equality holds, K=K_* and the score-assignment inequality is equality.
The regular pointwise winner is balanced and unique almost everywhere, so
the relaxed assignment is exactly the regular simplex winner partition,
up to orthogonal transformations, labels, and null sets.

The difference-of-square-roots identity bounds the covariance support gap by
a finite constant times w-W. Procrustes alignment with the regular row Gram
has squared distance sum_j(sqrt(lambda_j(K))-sqrt(m))^2, at most
||K-K_*||_F^2/m. Rescaling centroids adds at most a finite multiple of w-W.
This yields finite centroid-orbit coercivity in ambient dimension at least
m-1; W=0 is immediate. It is sufficient in dimension m-1 for the separately
audited transfer hypotheses. No explicit uniform-in-m numerical constant is
obtained here, and no all-correlation noise theorem follows from this audit.
