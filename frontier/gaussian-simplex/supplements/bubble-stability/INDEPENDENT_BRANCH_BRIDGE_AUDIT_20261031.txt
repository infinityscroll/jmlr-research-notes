4. Rotation-group BV quotient and heat-content constant

On the Gaussian product space, V=(y,-x) has zero Gaussian divergence,
and its flow is an orthogonal rotation. It therefore induces a strongly
continuous L1-isometry group. For F(x,y)=1_E(x), Gaussian integration by
parts gives a signed directional measure supported on partial*E times
R^N, with variation sqrt(2/pi)P_gamma(E): conditional on the boundary
normal, Y.normal is standard normal. Finite Gaussian perimeter is enough;
compact truncations are justified by this integrable variation formula.

The stated group-convolution proof works. F_epsilon=integral k_epsilon(t)
U_t F dt is in the L1 generator domain (differentiate the smooth scalar
kernel), and the same distributional derivative is the convolution of
the finite derivative measure. The latter has variation at most the
original variation because the flow preserves the Gaussian reference.
This gives the L1 norm bound on its generator. Integrating the generator
and passing F_epsilon to F proves the quotient upper bound. Testing the
unsmoothed quotient against compact smooth functions gives weak derivative
convergence; total-variation lower semicontinuity supplies the lower bound.
No smoothness of the cell boundary or uniform-in-cell remainder is assumed.

The identity ||U_theta F-F||_1=2[gamma(E)-<1_E,T_cos(theta)1_E>], together
with sin(theta)=sqrt(1-rho^2), gives one-cell coefficient
P_gamma(E)/sqrt(2pi). Summing and subtracting yields exactly
  lim [S_rho(g)-S_rho(A)]/sqrt(1-rho^2)
