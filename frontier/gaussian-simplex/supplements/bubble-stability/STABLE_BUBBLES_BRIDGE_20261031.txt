STATUS AND SCOPE
The primary conditional theorem's equal-mass hypothesis is exactly
settled by our internally audited all-m balanced first-moment theorem.
Together with our centroid coercivity, it yields a dimension-independent
QUADRATIC Gaussian perimeter-stability inequality for every fixed number
of equal-volume cells. It also yields a quantitative rho -> 1 asymptotic
for each fixed finite-perimeter competitor. It does NOT establish one
uniform interval near rho=1 on which every competitor loses. This note
uses Heilman's published geometric theorem; it is not an independent
reproof or full audit of that external paper.

Problem1.15 (pp8-9) fixes m>3, N=n+1>=m-1, positive a_i summing to1,
and the apex w of the translated regular partition with these masses.
It writes w^(i)=w/a_i and asks for the maximum of
  sqrt(pi/2) sum_i | integral_(A_i) (x-w/a_i) d gamma_N |^2
among measurable partitions having gamma_N(A_i)=a_i. Conjecture1.16
(p9) says the maximizers are translated regular-simplex cones.

Let b_i=integral_(A_i)x d gamma_N. Then sum b_i=0 and
  integral_(A_i)(x-w/a_i)d gamma_N=b_i-w,

endpoint W=sum|b_i|^2, multiplied by sqrt(pi/2). Our equality classification
also supplies its requested optimizer classification, in every N>=m-1.

The variational problem and ensuing theorem keep the mass vector fixed;
the equal-mass application uses no conjecture at other mass vectors.

2. PERIMETER AND DISTANCE CONVENTIONS
Let phi_N(x)=(2pi)^(-N/2) exp(-|x|^2/2). A partition A=(A_1,...,A_m)
is understood modulo Gaussian-null sets and has gamma_N(A_i)=1/m.
For cells of finite Gaussian perimeter write
  P_gamma(A)=sum_i integral_(partial* A_i) phi_N dH^(N-1),
  P_H(A)=sqrt(2pi) P_gamma(A).
Here P_gamma is TOTAL CELL perimeter, counting each interface twice.
The usual interface-only perimeter is P_gamma(A)/2. P_H is exactly the
paper's sum_i gamma_(N-1)(partial A_i), with reduced boundaries in the
finite-perimeter interpretation. Infinite perimeter makes our lower
bound immediate.

Let g be any regular-simplex partition in an (m-1)-dimensional subspace,
extended cylindrically to R^N. Let
  mu_m=E max_(1<=i<=m) Z_i, Z_i iid N(0,1),
  w_m=mu_m^2/(m-1),
  delta(A)=inf_(O in O(N), labels) sum_i gamma_N(A_i triangle O g_i).
Labels are redundant because simplex symmetries already realize them.
Allowing O(N) avoids an unnecessary distinction between rotations and
reflections. All comparisons below require N>=m-1.

One may explicitly write the reference perimeter as
  P_gamma(g)=sqrt(2) mu_m,   P_H(g)=2 sqrt(pi) mu_m.       (2)
Indeed take v_i=e_i-1/m in the usual sum-zero subspace. The winning
partition has centroids c_i=mu_m v_i/(m-1), so sum_i c_i.v_i=mu_m.
Gaussian integration by parts gives c_i=-integral_(partial g_i) n_i phi_N.
Each interface contributes |v_i-v_j|=sqrt(2) to the sum of these dot
products, so its interface-only Gaussian perimeter is mu_m/sqrt(2).
The integration by parts is legitimate by radial cutoff and Gaussian
decay. Cylindrical extra coordinates integrate out.

3. THE QUADRATIC MULTI-BUBBLE COROLLARY
For every integer m>=3 there is kappa_m>0, independent of N>=m-1, such
that every measurable equal-volume m-partition satisfies
  P_H(A)-2 sqrt(pi) mu_m >= kappa_m delta(A)^2.           (3a)
More specifically, one valid choice is
  kappa_m=epsilon_m/(4 L_m),
where epsilon_m is as above and L_m is defined explicitly below.
Equivalently,
  P_gamma(A)-sqrt(2)mu_m
       >= epsilon_m delta(A)^2/[4 sqrt(2pi) L_m].        (3b)
Divide both sides by2 for the interface-only convention.
In particular equality in the Gaussian multi-bubble inequality forces
A to be a regular partition, up to its orthogonal/label orbit and null sets.

Proof, including the quantitative step. Our balanced endpoint gives
Delta=w_m-W(A)>=0. Applying the fixed equal-mass instance of the primary
perimeter theorem yields
  P_H(A)-P_H(g)>=epsilon_m Delta.                       (3)
Our global centroid coercivity gives a nearest regular centroid frame c
with q=sum_i|b_i-c_i|^2<=K_m Delta. Let g_c be its winning partition and
eta=half sum_i gamma_N(A_i triangle g_(c,i)). Set
  J=w_m-sum_i c_i.b_i,   2J=Delta+q.
If a=|c_i-c_j|=sqrt(2)mu_m/(m-1), the gap Gamma between the highest two
scores c_i.X obeys
  Pr(Gamma<=t)<=beta_m t,
  beta_m=[m(m-1)/2] sqrt(2/pi)/a.
This is a union bound over pair differences, each a centered normal of
standard deviation a. At each wrong label the score loss is at least
Gamma. If p(X) is the wrong-label indicator, then E p=eta and
  J>=E[p Gamma]>=integral_0^infinity(eta-beta_m t)_+dt
    =eta^2/(2beta_m).
Consequently
  eta^2<=beta_m(1+K_m)Delta=:L_m Delta,
  delta(A)^2<=4 eta^2<=4 L_m Delta.                    (4)
Combine (2)--(4). This proof is dimension-independent, since pair-score
variances and the centroid-orbit bound are. It also proves the same
assignment-distance estimate for relaxed balanced assignments; perimeter
here is asserted for indicator partitions only.

For completeness, the following entirely explicit K_m is available from
our separately audited covariance gap. Put
  r_m=1/(2sqrt(3m)),
  R_m=sqrt(2m)(m-1)+2sqrt(m-2)/m+r_m,
  b_m=exp(-R_m^2/2)/[sqrt(2pi) Gamma(m/2)(2sqrt(6m))^(m-2)],
  g_m=b_m/[8(3m)^(3/2)sqrt((m-1)(m-2))],
  K_m=2+2/[m(m-1)g_m],
  L_m=beta_m(1+K_m).                                   (5)
All constants in (5) are positive and finite for each fixed m>=3.
The geometric primary theorem still leaves epsilon_m existential for
m>=5, so (3) is quantitative with a dimension-free positive coefficient,
not an explicit numerical all-m coefficient.

Optional four-cell numerical specialization. The separately frozen and
independently audited four-cell covariance improvement gives the stronger
same-frame bound eta^2<=250000 Delta. Combining it with epsilon_4=1/400
permits
  P_H(A)-P_H(g)>=delta(A)^2/400000000,
  P_gamma(A)-P_gamma(g)>=delta(A)^2/[400000000sqrt(2pi)]. (6)
This uses that separate numerical dependency; the general analytic
corollary (3)--(5) does not rely on an interval-computation certificate.

4. A PRECISE RHO -> 1 COROLLARY, AND ITS QUANTIFIER LIMIT
For every FIXED equal-volume finite-Gaussian-perimeter partition A,
  lim_(rho -> 1-) [S_rho(g)-S_rho(A)]/sqrt(1-rho^2)
      =[P_H(A)-P_H(g)]/(2pi)
      >=epsilon_m delta(A)^2/(8pi L_m).                (7)
The limit exists. Thus every fixed nonregular such competitor loses to
g for all rho sufficiently close to1, but the threshold here can depend
on that competitor and on N. There is no common rho_m<1 supplied by(7)
for all competitors, and no uniform positive lower bound when a sequence
of competitors approaches the regular orbit. An optimizing partition
can itself depend on rho. A uniform remainder or further local rigidity
would be necessary to deduce exact Standard Simplex optimality on an
entire interval near1. We claim neither here.

Here is a normalization-checked finite-perimeter proof of the limit,
avoiding the inconsistent intermediate coefficients in published Section12.
For one cell E put F(x,y)=1_E(x) on R^(2N), with product standard Gaussian
measure. Let U_theta be pullback by the rotation
  (x,y) -> (x cos theta+y sin theta,-x sin theta+y cos theta).
This is a strongly continuous group of L1 isometries, preserving Gaussian
measure, with generator V=(y,-x). The distributional directional derivative
D_V F is a finite signed measure; its variation is
  |D_V F|(R^(2N))
    =integral_(partial*E) E_Y|Y.nu_E(x)| phi_N(x)dH^(N-1)
    =sqrt(2/pi) P_gamma(E).                            (8)
Here P_gamma(E) denotes ONE cell perimeter. The unbounded y factor is
integrable uniformly in the unit normal, and compactly supported tests
plus Gaussian truncation justify the product integration by parts.

The general directional BV quotient identity for this group is
  lim_(theta -> 0) ||U_theta F-F||_1/|theta|=|D_V F|.
For clarity, its proof needs no smooth boundary: convolve F with a smooth
approximate identity in the group parameter. The resulting F_epsilon
belongs to the generator domain and its generator has L1 norm at most
|D_V F|, because the translated derivative measures have unchanged total
variation. The fundamental theorem of calculus in L1 and then
F_epsilon -> F give the upper bound ||U_theta F-F||_1<=|theta||D_V F|.
For the lower bound, the difference-quotient measures converge against
compact smooth tests to D_V F; lower semicontinuity of total variation
gives the reverse liminf. This proves the quotient identity.

Since both rotated coordinates have the same marginal law,
  ||U_theta F-F||_1
     =2[gamma_N(E)-<1_E,T_(cos theta)1_E>].
Equations(8) and theta/sin(theta)->1 prove
  lim_(rho -> 1-) [gamma_N(E)-<1_E,T_rho1_E>]
                      /sqrt(1-rho^2)
     =P_gamma(E)/sqrt(2pi).
Sum over cells and subtract the corresponding regular partition identity
to obtain (7). The halfspace partition checks the coefficient exactly:
P_H=2 and 1-S_rho=arccos(rho)/pi.

The paper's Corollary1.11 and Remark1.12(pp6-7) express a weak small-noise
bound with a competitor-dependent little-o. Our (7) states its safe
quantitative consequence with independently verified constants. In
particular, we do not copy its printed sqrt(pi/2) penalty coefficient:
the final perimeter normalization and intermediate factors in Section12
are not all consistent with one another. This is a coefficient bookkeeping
issue for that displayed corollary; it does not obstruct (3) or the
independent limit proof just given.

5. EARLIEST VERIFIED QUESTION AND DEMONSTRATED CONSEQUENCES
The bounded primary-history check is separately pinned below. Its earliest
verified explicit balanced endpoint formulation is Heilman1211.7138v1,
submitted30November2012, Section6 Conjecture3(p32), with equation(11) and

The explicit all-m>3 prescribed-mass endpoint appears in the2019
Problem1.15/Conjecture1.16 examined above. The published2026 paper makes
its consequence for Gaussian multi-bubble stability explicit. This note
establishes that bridge for equal masses, with a quadratic assignment
asymmetry bound, rather than the quartic illustrative bound shown for
three cells in its Example1.7. No claim is made that this is the earliest
possible stability result in every formulation or that external priority
review is complete.

Khot--Naor's2008/2009 propeller/kernel-clustering antecedents optimize

imply the endpoint by differentiation, but that is different from a
separately stated endpoint question. Full-range MAX-m-CUT consequences
