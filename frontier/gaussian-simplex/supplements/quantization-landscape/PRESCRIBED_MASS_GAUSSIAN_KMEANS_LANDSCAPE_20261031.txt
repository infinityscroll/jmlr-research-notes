SUMMARY
Conditional only on the already internally audited covariance-concavity
theorem, prescribed-positive-mass Gaussian quantization in source dimension
N>=m-1 has no nonglobal local minima. This includes coincident centers and
arbitrary fixed positive masses. At every pairwise-distinct nonglobal
stationary configuration the objective is C2 and has a strictly negative
Hessian direction. Thus this stronger strict-saddle statement also holds
for arbitrary prescribed masses; it does not assume their global optimum
has full affine rank or is unique.

For equal masses, the global centers are exactly a centered regular
(m-1)-simplex of the explicit radius below. No claim is made for ordinary
unconstrained k-means (whose masses vary with the centers), empirical data,
mixtures, N<m-1, or convergence of Lloyd iterations.

1. PRECISE OBJECTIVE AND INPUT
Fix m>=2, p_i>0, sum p_i=1, N>=m-1. Put D_p=diag(p_1,...,p_m).
For A in R^(m x N), whose rows a_i are the centers, set
  J_p(A)=sup_f sum_i integral f_i(x) a_i.x dgamma_N(x),
where f_i>=0, sum_i f_i=1, integral f_i=p_i. The supremum is attained
by an indicator partition; relaxed and indicator values agree by the
atomless linear assignment extreme-point argument. Define
  H_p(A)=J_p(A)-(1/2)sum_i p_i|a_i|^2,
  D_p^quant(A)=N-2H_p(A).                              (1)
The superscript distinguishes distortion from the diagonal matrix D_p.
Equivalently this distortion is W2^2(gamma_N,sum_i p_i delta_(a_i)).
It uses the optimal CAPACITY-CONSTRAINED Laguerre assignment, not ordinary
nearest-center Voronoi assignment with free cluster masses.

Covariance-concavity input:
  C -> MCov(N(0,C),nu) is concave on the closed PSD cone
for every fixed finite target nu (indeed every nu in P2 in our source).

2. CENTERING, COVARIANCE AND EXISTENCE
Let mu=sum_i p_i a_i and A0=A-1 mu^T. Then p^T A0=0 and
  J_p(A)=J_p(A0),
  H_p(A)=H_p(A0)-|mu|^2/2.                            (2)
The first identity follows from E X=0; the second is weighted variance
decomposition. Thus every local maximum of H_p and every stationary
point is centered. In the rest of the proof K=A A^T has Kp=0.
Write V=p-perp in R^m, dimension m-1, and let P_V be its orthogonal
projection. The score vector Z=A X lies in V and has covariance K.
The discrete target takes value P_V e_i with probability p_i. It is
centered because sum_i p_i P_V e_i=0. Its pairing with Z is Z_i.
Consequently
  J_p(A)=F_p(K),
  h_p(K)=F_p(K)-(1/2)tr(D_p K)                        (3)
is concave on {K>=0:Kp=0}. This covariance description is valid at rank
deficiency and at K=0: relaxed couplings give the same value as the
original atomless assignment problem. Conversely every such K is
realizable by a centered A in R^(m x N), since N>=m-1.

The function F_p is continuous on the closed cone, by the main theorem
or W2 continuity, and 0<=F_p(K)<=sqrt(tr K). Indeed independent labels
give zero and every selected score is bounded by |Z|. Therefore
  h_p(K)<=sqrt(tr K)-(p_min/2)tr K ->-infinity
as tr K->infinity. A global maximum exists on a compact subset of the
closed cone. Distortion minimizers exist in every permitted dimension.
No uniqueness or rank assertion is needed for general p.

3. NO SPURIOUS LOCAL MINIMA: CONTINUOUS POLAR LIFT
Let A be centered, K=A A^T. Extend its polar partial isometry to a map
O:R^N->V such that
  O O^T=P_V,   A=K^(1/2) O.
Such an extension exists because N>=dim V; choose orthonormal images for
the missing row directions in the orthogonal complement of the original
source row span.

If K is not globally maximizing h_p, choose K* with h_p(K*)>h_p(K), and
for 0<t<=1 define
  K_t=(1-t)K+t K*,  A_t=K_t^(1/2) O.
Then A_t A_t^T=K_t, A_t->A by continuity of the PSD square root, and
  H_p(A_t)=h_p(K_t)
     >=h_p(K)+t[h_p(K*)-h_p(K)]>H_p(A).               (4)
This gives arbitrarily nearby strict improvement, even at singular K or
coincident centers. A noncentered configuration is improved arbitrarily
nearby by reducing mu in(2). Hence every local minimum of distortion is
global, for every prescribed positive mass vector p.

The path need not be differentiable in t at0: this is why(4) alone does
not prove a Hessian strict-saddle theorem. Sections5--7 supply the missing
boundary differentiability at every pairwise-distinct site configuration.

4. EXACT BALANCED GLOBAL MINIMIZERS
Now p_i=1/m. Put d=m-1, P=I-(1/m)11^T, and
  mu_m=E max_(1<=i<=m) Z_i,  Z_i independent N(0,1).
The audited balanced trace optimum gives, for K>=0,K1=0 and T=tr K,
  F_(1/m)(K)<=mu_m sqrt(T/d),                         (5)
with equality for T>0 only when K=(T/d)P. Optimizing
mu_m sqrt(T/d)-T/(2m) yields
  T*=m^2 mu_m^2/d,
  K*=alpha* P,  alpha*=m^2 mu_m^2/d^2,
  min_A D_(1/m)^quant(A)=N-m mu_m^2/d.                (6)
Thus the centers have weighted mean0, Gram K*, and form a regular
(m-1)-simplex up to orthogonal embeddings and label permutations. Their
individual norms and mutual distances are, respectively,
  |a_i|=mu_m sqrt(m/d),
  |a_i-a_j|=sqrt(2)m mu_m/d.
There are no other local minima by Section3. The equality and positive
scale exclude coincident centers at a balanced optimum.

5. C2 GAUSSIAN AFFINE-MAX LEMMA, INCLUDING DEFICIENT SITE RANK
Let A have pairwise-distinct rows. For offsets h in R^m set
  G(A,h)=E max_i(a_i.X+h_i)-sum_i p_i h_i.
The offset minimum is normalized by sum_i h_i=0. It exists: Jensen gives
  G(A,h)>=max_i h_i-p.h>=p_min(max_i h_i-min_i h_i),
which is coercive on that hyperplane. Pairwise score ties have Gaussian
measure zero. Hence G_h equals the cell-mass vector minus p, and a
minimizing h gives every cell its strictly positive prescribed mass.
Cells are the polyhedra
  E_i={x:a_i.x+h_i>=a_j.x+h_j for every j}.

For any configuration (A,h) with distinct rows and every cell of positive
mass, no three affine scores tie on an entire codimension-one hyperplane.
If this happened, their slopes would be collinear, one of the three
slopes would lie strictly between the other two, and the corresponding
offset would be the same convex combination. That middle affine score
could never be a strict winner, so its cell would have Gaussian mass0.
This contradiction also handles arbitrary affine rank deficiency.
Therefore triple-winning strata have codimension at least2.

For i<j let Sigma_ij be the common winning facet (possibly empty),
  w_ij= integral_(Sigma_ij) phi_N(x)dH^(N-1)(x)/|a_i-a_j|,
and let L be the weighted Laplacian with L_ij=-w_ij and
L_ii=sum_(j!=i)w_ij. Empty facets have w_ij=0. All weights are finite,
since Gaussian hyperplane mass is at most1/sqrt(2pi) and every
|a_i-a_j| is positive.

The following exact second derivative holds for arbitrary row variation
V=(v_i) and offset variation beta:
  D2 G(A,h)[(V,beta),(V,beta)]
    =sum_(i<j) 1/|a_i-a_j|
       integral_(Sigma_ij)
          [(v_i-v_j).x+beta_i-beta_j]^2 phi_N(x)dH^(N-1)(x).
                                                               (7)
In particular G_hh=L. This formula holds as a C2 derivative, not merely
as a formal boundary variation, at all configurations just specified.

Justification of(7) and its global tail control. On a fixed bounded ball,
exclude O(|t|)-neighborhoods of triple-winning strata. The region in which
three scores are within O(|t|) of the winner has volume O(t^2): the two
independent affine difference equations give codimension2, while the
finite collinear triples cannot be winning and have a positive gap on
compact sets. The integrand's Taylor remainder there is O(|t|), so this
part contributes O(|t|^3). Elsewhere only two affine functions exchange
the maximum. Integrating their elementary two-line crossing in the normal
direction gives half t^2 times the corresponding integrand in(7), plus
o(t^2). This is uniform for variations in a bounded parameter set.

For completeness, the tail truncation is uniform after division by t^2.
Subtract the first-order winning affine branch. The convex-max remainder
is zero unless some pair i,j has
  |(a_i-a_j).x+h_i-h_j|<=C|t|(1+|x|),
and on that event it is at most C|t|(1+|x|). Write x=y+u n along the
fixed pair's unit normal. For small |t|, the displayed strip confines u
to an interval centered at u0=-(h_i-h_j)/|a_i-a_j|, of length at most
C'|t|(1+|y|), and |u|<=C0+C'|t|(1+|y|). Integration in u bounds the
remainder divided by t^2 by a constant times
  (1+|y|)^2 phi_(N-1)(y).
The additional condition |x|>R forces |y|>=cR-C0 (uniformly for small t).
Its Gaussian integral tends to zero as R->infinity. This proves the
required global o(t^2), with no unproved regularity at infinity.

The coefficients in(7) vary continuously near (A,h). Parameterize each
pair hyperplane by continuously chosen orthonormal tangent coordinates.
Every third-score equality is either a genuine tangent hyperplane or
a nonzero constant: an identically zero third score was excluded above.
Facet indicators therefore converge for almost every tangent point.
Gaussian polynomial domination, with uniformly bounded offsets and
positive pair distances, proves continuity of their integrals. Positivity
of every cell persists in a neighborhood by ordinary dominated
convergence of its indicator. These observations justify the asserted
C2 regularity, including topology changes with vanishing facets.

The positive-weight adjacency graph is connected: full-dimensional
positive-mass cells partition R^N, and a generic polygonal path between
two cell interiors crosses finitely many facets while avoiding all
codimension2 strata. Hence L is strictly positive on1-perp. The offset
minimum is unique modulo constants; strict positivity gives local strict
convexity, and global convexity rules out a second minimum. The implicit
function theorem applied to G_h=0 shows normalized h=h(A) is C1 locally.
Consequently J_p(A)=G(A,h(A)) is C2 on the open set of distinct centers.
Its gradient has rows
  b_i=integral_(E_i) x dgamma_N(x).                   (8)
The envelope Hessian is equivalently the minimum of(7) over beta with
sum beta_i=0. No assumption of full affine rank was used.

6. GAUSSIAN IBP AND A FINITE BOUNDARY COVARIANCE SUPERGRADIENT
Gaussian integration by parts on each polyhedral cell gives
  b_i=sum_(j!=i)w_ij(a_i-a_j),  hence b=L A.            (9)
Indeed the outward normal of E_i on Sigma_ij is
(a_j-a_i)/|a_i-a_j| and integral_E x phi=-integral_boundary n phi.
Truncation by balls and Gaussian decay justify the formula on unbounded
cells. Euler homogeneity, or the winning-score identity, also gives
  J_p(A)=sum_i a_i.b_i=tr(LK).                         (10)

Append q independent standard Gaussian source coordinates Z. The initial
site matrix is [A,0]. For a row matrix B in R^(m x q), perturb to[A,eps B].
On each original facet the added-coordinate law remains an independent
standard Gaussian. Thus the mixed offset derivative in direction[0,B]
is zero, since E Z=0. The minimizing offset derivative is zero modulo
constants. Formula(7) and E ZZ^T=I give
  J_p([A,eps B])=J_p(A)+(eps^2/2)tr(LBB^T)+o(eps^2).  (11)
This is an exact boundary derivative calculation, including rank-deficient
A. The Laplacian weights are unchanged by the cylindrical extension.

Let K' be any PSD covariance on V=p-perp and choose B B^T=K'. Use
  A_t=[sqrt(1-t)A,sqrt(t)B],
so A_t A_t^T=(1-t)K+tK'. The C2 Taylor expansion at[A,0], (10)--(11), gives
  F_p((1-t)K+tK')
    =F_p(K)+(t/2)tr[L(K'-K)]+o(t).                   (12)
The linear term from scaling is -(t/2)J_p(A); the added-coordinate
first derivative is zero. Cross and scaling-quadratic terms are o(t).
Concavity along this segment now proves the GLOBAL supporting inequality
  F_p(K')<=F_p(K)+(1/2)tr[L(K'-K)].                   (13)
Thus L/2 is a finite covariance supergradient at every Gram represented
by distinct centers, even when K lies on the PSD boundary. No formal
inverse of K and no full-rank regularization argument is needed.

7. STRICT SADDLES FOR ALL PRESCRIBED POSITIVE MASSES
Every ordinary stationary point of distortion with distinct centers is
centered by(2). Equations(8)--(9) give
  (L-D_p)A=0.                                        (14)
Define M=P_V(L-D_p)P_V, symmetric on V. In particular tr(MK)=0.
Suppose A is nonglobal. Some K' on the centered PSD cone has h_p(K')>h_p(K).
Subtracting the linear trace penalty in(13) yields
  0<h_p(K')-h_p(K)<= (1/2)tr[M(K'-K)]
                      =(1/2)tr(MK').                (15)
Therefore M has a strictly positive eigenvalue on V. If rank(A)=m-1,
its range is V and(14) forces M=0, a contradiction. Hence
  rank(A)<m-1<=N.
Choose a unit unused source direction z with A z=0, and a unit v in V
with v^T M v>0. Perturb the centers by
  A_eps=A+eps v z^T.
This preserves p-centering and has exact Gram K+eps^2 vv^T. Applying(11),
now within the available source coordinates, gives
  d^2/deps^2 D_p^quant(A_eps)|_(eps=0)
     =2v^T(D_p-L)v=-2v^T M v<0.                      (16)
Thus every pairwise-distinct nonglobal stationary point is a strict
saddle of the C2 distortion objective. This holds also for unequal p,
even if a global optimum has deficient rank. The proof uses only the
existence of a better covariance, not a full-rank or unique optimizer.

The phrase 'strict saddle' here means a strictly negative Hessian
eigenvalue at a stationary point; it does not mean all other Hessian
eigenvalues are nonzero. Rotation symmetries naturally produce flat
directions. Collision points are excluded from this C2 statement but
are already covered by the no-spurious theorem and the next direct test.

8. COLLISIONS HAVE AN EXPLICIT LINEAR IMPROVEMENT
Suppose a_i=a_j=a. Take an optimal indicator assignment and let E be the
union of these two cells, with Gaussian mass p_i+p_j. Pick any unit source
direction z. The distribution of z.X restricted to E has no atoms and
is not concentrated at one point, because E has positive Gaussian measure.
Split E at its conditional quantile into E_+ of mass p_i (the larger
z-projections) and E_- of mass p_j. Keep the other cells unchanged.
Perturb
  a_i(eps)=a+eps z,
  a_j(eps)=a-(p_i/p_j)eps z,  eps>0.
The weighted center remains fixed. Evaluating the new assignment yields
  J_p(A_eps)>=J_p(A)+eps c,
  c=integral_(E_+) z.x dgamma
        -(p_i/p_j)integral_(E_-) z.x dgamma >0.
The strict inequality is p_i times the difference of the two conditional
means. The weighted squared-norm change is exactly
  eps^2[p_i+p_i^2/p_j].
Consequently
  H_p(A_eps)-H_p(A)>=eps c-(eps^2/2)[p_i+p_i^2/p_j]>0
for all sufficiently small positive eps. Thus collisions admit an
explicit linear ascent for H_p, independent of the polar-lift argument.
No collision can be a global optimum or a local minimum of distortion.

9. EXACT THREE-CELL FACTOR CHECK
For m=3,N>=2,p_i=1/3, let q=Phi^(-1)(2/3) and a=3phi(q). The centered
collinear configuration (-a e_1,0,a e_1) has balanced quantile cells
with boundaries at +/-q. Their cell first-moment vectors are (-phi(q)e_1,0,phi(q)e_1),
so it is stationary. Its facet Laplacian is exactly
  L=(1/3)[[1,-1,0],[-1,2,-1],[0,-1,1]].
The nonzero eigenvalues are1/3 on (-1,0,1) and1 on (1,-2,1).
With v=(1,-2,1)/sqrt(6) and unused z=e_2, (16) gives distortion second
derivative exactly -4/3. This checks the factor2, the independent-noise
Hessian, and the direction of the strict-saddle conclusion without a
numerical approximation or a generic-rank assumption.

10. SCOPE, PRIORITY AND ALGORITHMS
The mathematical input is the internal covariance-concavity proof pinned
in Section1. The present note is a new conditional corollary proof and
is being separately audited. Root is performing a bounded current-primary
literature comparison; this source does not infer novelty from absent
search hits. It is a theorem about the population Gaussian objective
with prescribed masses and enough source dimensions. It is not a proof
of empirical k-means behavior, unconstrained Lloyd convergence, global
convergence from every initialization, or an avoidance theorem for saddle
manifolds. Any such dynamical statement requires additional arguments.
