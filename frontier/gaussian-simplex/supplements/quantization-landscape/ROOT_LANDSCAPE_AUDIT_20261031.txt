The checked objective is W2^2(gamma_N,sum_i p_i delta_(a_i)), with p fixed

1. Gaussian score reduction and continuous lifting
Weighted centering separates the squared common mean exactly. In V=p-perp,
the projected coordinate target P_V e_i has weighted mean zero and pairs
with a score Z in V as Z_i. Both directions of the coupling reduction are
valid, including singular score covariance: conditional lifting gives a
relaxed assignment, and atomlessness gives the same indicator optimum.
The centered objective is the concave function
  h_p(K)=F_p(K)-tr(D_p K)/2.
Its coercive trace bound proves existence.

For any centered A, N>=dim V allows completion of the polar partial
isometry to a coisometry O with OO^T=P_V and A=K^(1/2)O. Therefore every
linear covariance segment has a continuous factor path starting at A.
Concavity gives a strict nearby improvement whenever K is nonglobal.
This proof needs neither differentiability nor an invertible covariance;
it covers collisions directly. The square-root path may have an infinite
derivative at zero, which is why it is not itself a strict-saddle proof.

2. Balanced minimizer and constants
The inherited trace optimum is F(K)<=mu_m sqrt(tr K/(m-1)), with strict
equality classification. Optimizing the scalar trace gives
  K*=m^2 mu_m^2/(m-1)^2 (I-11^T/m),
  distortion*=N-m mu_m^2/(m-1).
The row norms and pair distances in the source agree with this Gram.
Translation and rotation conventions are correct. No unequal-mass
regularity, rank or uniqueness conclusion is inferred from symmetry.

3. Distinct-site C2 lemma
An affine-score triple tying on a codimension-one hyperplane would make
one affine function the convex combination of the other two everywhere.
That label could never win strictly, contradicting its positive mass.
Thus the relevant triple strata have codimension at least two, including
at deficient site rank. The two-line crossing calculation gives the
facet Hessian with denominator |a_i-a_j| and no missing factor of two.

On a bounded ball, the triple-strip volume is O(t^2), and the integrand
remainder is O(t), so this part is o(t^2). For the tails, the pair strip
is confined along its normal to O(t)(1+|y|), where y is tangential. Its
remainder is bounded by O(t)(1+|y|). After dividing by t^2 the remaining
bound is a Gaussian-integrable quadratic polynomial in y, uniformly in
small t. The condition |x|>R forces a tangential tail uniformly, proving
the required vanishing tail bound. This is the necessary justification
for using the local crossing formula on unbounded Gaussian polyhedra.

The continuous facet integral argument is valid: third-score restrictions
on a pair hyperplane are nonzero constants or nontrivial affine functions,
so their equality sets are negligible there. Gaussian domination and
positive pair distances give continuity even when a facet disappears.
The adjacency graph of positive-volume cells is connected, hence the
offset Laplacian is positive on 1-perp. Implicit differentiation of the
normalized minimizing offsets consequently gives the claimed C2 envelope.

4. Boundary covariance supergradient
Gaussian integration by parts gives b=L A with the stated signs. Euler
homogeneity yields J=tr(LK). In unused independent Gaussian coordinates,
the perturbation's mixed derivative with offsets vanishes because those
coordinates have mean zero. The envelope Hessian is therefore exactly
tr(LBB^T), with Taylor coefficient one half. Appending sufficiently many
independent coordinates is legitimate for calculating a covariance value;
it is not an unauthorized change to the source dimension in the final
strict-saddle conclusion.

Combining this perturbation with sqrt(1-t) scaling gives the right
covariance directional derivative tr[L(K'-K)]/2 at a singular K.
Concavity then gives the global supergradient inequality. No inverse
of a singular matrix has been used.

5. Strict saddle and collisions
Stationarity is (L-D_p)A=0, with A centered. If K is nonglobal, the
supergradient paired with a better centered PSD covariance is positive.
Thus M=P_V(L-D_p)P_V has a positive eigenvalue. Full row rank on V would
force M=0, so an unused source direction exists in the original R^N.
The perturbation A+epsilon v z^T has Gram K+epsilon^2 vv^T and distortion
curvature -2v^T Mv<0. This also works when the unequal-mass global optimum
has deficient rank. The conclusion is a negative Hessian direction,
allowing other zero directions due to symmetries.

At coincident sites, re-splitting their union by an atomless projection
preserves all prescribed masses and their original optimal value. The
weighted split perturbation has a strictly positive linear score gain
and only a quadratic norm cost. This independently excludes collision
local minima without claiming differentiability there.

The three-cell collinear example has facet weights exactly 1/3, with
the transverse label eigenvalue 1 and stationary value 1/3. The source
curvature -2(1-1/3)=-4/3 is correct.
