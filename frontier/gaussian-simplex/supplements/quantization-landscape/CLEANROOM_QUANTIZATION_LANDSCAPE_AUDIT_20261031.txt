1. Objective, centering, and the Gram representation
The distortion identity is exact:
inf sum_i integral f_i|X-a_i|^2
 =N+sum_i p_i|a_i|^2-2J_p(A).
The relaxed atomless assignment problem has an indicator optimizer;
its weakly compact maximizing face has an extreme point, and any
genuinely fractional assignment admits a zero-integral transfer
between two labels on a positive-measure region.

Subtracting the weighted center mu does not change J_p, because the
assignments sum to1 and E X=0. The weighted quadratic term splits
into its centered part plus |mu|^2. This gives nearby improvement
at every noncentered point, without a differentiability assumption.

For centered A, Kp=0 and AX lies in V=p-perp. Pairing it with
P_V e_i gives exactly its ith score. The target law with probabilities
p_i is centered, since P_V p=0. Any coupling of scores and labels
lifts to a relaxed assignment on X by conditional expectation;
indicator purification preserves the optimal value. Thus J_p(A)=F_p(K)
also holds at deficient rank, at coincident rows, and at K=0.
Conversely every centered PSD Gram matrix is realizable when N>=m-1.
The upper bound F_p(K)<=E|Z|<=sqrt(tr K) and p_min>0 prove coercivity
of h_p and existence of a global optimizer.

4. Affine-max C2 lemma: the necessary distinct-site argument
The offset coercivity inequality follows directly from Jensen:
G>=max h-p.h>=p_min(max h-min h). Distinct slopes make pair ties
Gaussian-null, so the first offset derivative is the mass vector
minus p. At its normalized minimum every cell has positive mass.

Three affine functions tying on a full hyperplane would have
collinear slopes and offsets with the middle function an exact
convex combination of the two extremes. It could never win
strictly anywhere, contradicting its positive mass. This exclusion
does not require full affine rank and is strong enough to ensure
that triple-winning strata have codimension at least2.

On a compact ball, independent affine differences make the near-triple
region O(t^2); the max remainder there is O(t), hence O(t^3).
Dependent collinear triples have a fixed nonwinning gap on that
compact ball. Away from the triple region, two-line crossing gives
one-half t^2 times the displayed facet integral. The denominator
is |a_i-a_j|, and summing i<j counts each crossing exactly once.

The source also supplies the essential global remainder estimate.
In normal/tangent coordinates for a fixed pair, the switching strip
has normal width O(|t|(1+|y|)) after moving the O(|t||u|) term to
the left. Its max remainder is O(|t|(1+|y|)). Integration in u,
after division by t^2, is bounded by a Gaussian-integrable constant
times (1+|y|)^2. The tail condition forces |y|>=cR-C0 uniformly
for small t. Thus the normalized tail remainder vanishes with R.
This justifies a global second derivative, not a merely formal
boundary calculation.

For continuity, pair hyperplanes admit continuous orthonormal
parameterizations locally. Their third-score tests are nontrivial
tangent affine functions, or nonzero constants. Their zero sets
are negligible unless absent, so facet indicators converge almost
everywhere. Bounded offsets, positive pair distances, and Gaussian
polynomial domination give continuity of the Hessian coefficients.
This also covers disappearing facets. Positive cell masses persist
locally. The C2 assertion is therefore valid at every specified
distinct-site point, including rank deficiency.

5. Envelope and offset uniqueness
The positive-facet adjacency graph is connected: a generic path
between interiors crosses facets and avoids all codimension2
strata. Hence its Laplacian L is positive definite on1-perp.
This gives local strict convexity of the offset objective at its
minimum; global convexity excludes a second normalized minimum.
The implicit-function theorem gives C1 offsets h(A), and the
envelope gradient then gives a C2 J_p(A).
The envelope Hessian is the minimum of the joint second derivative
over zero-sum offset variations. This is precisely the Schur
complement with the positive offset block, with no rank assumption
on the site matrix.

7. Arbitrary-mass strict saddle
Stationarity gives (L-D_p)A=0. On V, let
M=P_V(L-D_p)P_V. Then tr(MK)=0. A better covariance and the
support inequality force tr(MK')>0, so M has a positive direction.
Full rank would imply M=0 because the range of A is all of V;
therefore rank A<m-1<=N and an unused source coordinate exists.

For Az=0 and v in V, the perturbation A+eps v z^T has exact Gram
K+eps^2 vv^T and keeps the weighted center fixed. The independent
Gaussian coordinate calculation applies after an orthogonal
change of source coordinates, within the original dimension.
Its distortion curvature is
2v^T(D_p-L)v=-2v^T Mv<0.
The argument does not assume a unique or full-rank global optimum
at unequal masses. Rotational zero directions do not contradict
the stated meaning of strict saddle.

8. Collision descent and the exact collinear check
For coincident sites, any optimal split of their union gives the
same original linear value. The conditional scalar Gaussian
projection on a positive-measure union is atomless and nonconstant.
The upper/lower quantile split has strictly ordered conditional
means. The prescribed opposite site displacements cancel the
weighted linear norm term and increase J_p linearly, whereas the
quadratic penalty is only O(eps^2). This verifies explicit descent
of distortion at every collision independently of the polar lift.

For the equal-mass three-cell collinear example, each adjacent
facet weight is phi(q)/(3phi(q))=1/3 and the nonadjacent facet
