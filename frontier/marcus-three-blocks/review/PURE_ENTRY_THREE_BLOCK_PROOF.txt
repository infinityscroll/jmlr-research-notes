An analytic pure-entry proof for three blocks of arbitrary size
30 October 2026. Internal proof record; no priority claim.

THEOREM.
Let A be a complex Hermitian positive semidefinite matrix of order 3k,
partitioned into three-by-three blocks A_ij of size k. Put G_ij=per(A_ij).
Then
  per(A)>=per(G)
for every k>=1 and at every Gram rank.

For k>=3 the proof gives the stronger polynomial inequality
  ||PQR||_Fock^2 >= per Gram_Fock(P,Q,R)
for arbitrary homogeneous degree-k polynomials in d complex variables
whenever d<=8k^2/9. In particular it holds for d<=2k. The Fock convention
is ||z^alpha||^2=alpha!. No equality characterization is asserted.

The arbitrary number-of-blocks Marcus conjecture is not proved here.
The k=2 trace argument and the rank-reduction argument are included below,
so this proof requires no earlier finite certificate or local theorem.

1. THE SEED AND ITS EXACT GROUP AVERAGE.
Let V=C^d, H=S_k wreath S_3, and W=sum_{pi outside H} P_pi on V^{tensor 3k}.
For a transport matrix M with row and column sums k, let z(M) count its
entries equal to k, and define
  r_i=2-sum_j(1_{M_ij=k}+1_{M_ji=k}),
  b_i=3r_i/[6-2z(M)].
When M is k times a permutation matrix, define every b_i to be zero.
For other M, the denominator is positive and sum_i b_i=3.

Set Q=sum_pi b_0(M_pi)(P_pi)^{T_block0}, restricted to
  E=bar(Sym^k V) tensor Sym^k V tensor Sym^k V.
Internal block permutations do not change the coefficients. Independent
permutations of the two ordinary rows and columns do not change b_0.
The restriction to E therefore loses no relevant symmetric-block term.
Conjugation averaging over H gives the exact formal group-algebra identity
  W=average_{h in H} P_h [Q^{T_block0}] P_h^*.
Indeed that average is (b_0+b_1+b_2)/3=1 outside H and zero on H.

Thus Q>=0 suffices for nonnegative W expectation on every product of
three symmetric block tensors. Whole-block partial transpose is
nonnegative on such products, and H preserves block-product tensors.

2. EXACT CONTRACTION FACTORIZATION.
Let Q_l be the part with M_00=k-l. For p=0,...,l, C_(l,p) is the literal
fixed-slot trace contracting p first-block slots with the first ordinary
block and l-p with the second. Put
  E_(l,p)=binom(k,p)binom(k,l-p) C_(l,p).
The output degrees are (k-l,k-p,k-l+p). Direct permutation counting gives
the common coefficient
of its remaining-ordinary-permutation sum is
  A_l=(k!)^2/(k-l)!.
All squared norms below are quadratic forms evaluated on the same
arbitrary input tensor F in E; for example ||B_l||^2 means ||B_l F||^2.
This factor can also be checked directly: choose the l crossing slots
at each end of the first block, choose the ordinary crossing slots,
multiply by (l!)^2 for the cross bijections and by (k-l)! for the
uncrossed first-block permutations. The ordinary binomial factors are
exactly those absorbed into E_(l,p).

3. EVERY NONFINAL LAYER IS POSITIVE.
For 1<=l<k, M_00=k-l is positive and less than k. Hence neither the first
row nor the first column can contain an entry k, so r_0=2. At most one
ordinary entry can equal k. Consequently the coefficient is exactly
  b_0=1+(1/2)1_{an ordinary entry equals k}.                       (1)

Write n=2k-l and r=k-l. The constant part of (1) is
  Q_l^constant=A_l n! ||Sym_n sum_p E_(l,p)||^2                   (2)
in quadratic-form notation, with the remaining dual identity understood.
It is positive.

The exceptional entry k is possible only when both input and output
contraction types are boundary types p=0 or p=l. Reorder those outputs
so that the shorter ordinary group of size r is first. The ordinary
permutations producing the entry k are exactly the r! k! permutations
preserving the two remaining groups. They act identically on the
symmetric group tensors. The four boundary input/output choices therefore
give the positive delta term
  Q_l^delta=(k!)^3/2 * binom(k,l)^2 ||B_l||^2,                    (3)
where B_l is the sum of the two pure l-fold contraction maps, with the
shorter ordinary group first. Thus Q_l>=0 for every l<k. Also Q_0=0.

4. THE LAST LAYER HAS ONLY ONE SIMPLE NEGATIVE TERM.
At l=k the remaining ordinary tensor has degree k and there is no dual
block left. Call a type boundary when p=0 or p=k; otherwise call it
interior. The transport coefficient is independent of the remaining
ordinary permutation:
  interior/interior: 1;
  boundary/interior or interior/boundary: 3/4;
  boundary/boundary: 0.
The last case is a permutation transport, where the coefficient was
defined to be zero. The middle case has exactly one pure entry k and
r_0=1, giving 3/4.

Let B=B_k=C_(k,0)+C_(k,k), and let
  I=Sym_k sum_{p=1}^{k-1} E_(k,p).
The boundary output is already symmetric. The ordinary permutation sum
is k! Sym_k and A_k=(k!)^2, giving exactly
  Q_k=(k!)^3 [||I||^2+(3/2)Re<B,I>]
      =(k!)^3 [||I+(3/4)B||^2-(9/16)||B||^2].                  (4)

5. AN ORDINARY TRACE BOUND COMPENSATES (4).
For l=k-1, B_(k-1) takes values in
  bar(V) tensor V tensor Sym^k V.
Trace the first two factors by tau. Completing the remaining pure trace
gives the exact identity
  tau B_(k-1)=B_k=B.
The literal trace has tau tau^*=d I, hence
  ||B_(k-1)||^2 >= ||B||^2/d.
Equation (3) and binom(k,k-1)=k yield
  Q_(k-1)^delta >= (k!)^3 k^2/(2d) ||B||^2.                      (5)

Keep (5), discard the other positive terms, and add (4):
  Q >= (k!)^3 {||I+(3/4)B||^2
                    +[k^2/(2d)-9/16]||B||^2}.                  (6)
Therefore Q>=0 whenever d<=8k^2/9. In particular, for k>=3 and d<=2k,
the last coefficient is at least (4k-9)/16>0.
All factorials in (3)-(6) use literal tensor traces; no Fock annihilation
normalization is implicit. This completes the uniform positivity proof.

6. THE QUADRATIC ENDPOINT, INCLUDED EXPLICITLY.
Let k=2 and d=4. Put C=B_1, C_s=Sym_3 C and C_h=(I-Sym_3)C.
The l=1 identity above gives
  Q_1=112||C_s||^2+16||C_h||^2.
In the notation of the last-layer formula, B=B_2 and I=4 Sym_2 C_(2,1),
so Q_2=8[||I||^2+(3/2)Re<B,I>]. Let tau contract the dual slot with
the first ordinary slot, and put A=tau C_s, D=tau C_h. Symmetrizing
the three ordinary slots gives the exact trace identities
  A=(B+I)/3, D=(2B-I)/3,
and therefore B=A+D, I=2A-D. Substitution yields
  Q=112||C_s||^2+16||C_h||^2
       +56||A||^2-20 Re<A,D>-4||D||^2.                          (7)

The two trace constants can be computed directly. On Sym^2 V, tau^*T
has entries delta_ij T_kl. Symmetrizing its ordinary indices j,k,l and
then tracing i=j gives (d+2)T/3. Thus
  tau Sym_3 tau^*=(d+2)I/3,
  tau(I-Sym_3)tau^*=2(d-1)I/3,
where the second identity uses tau tau^*=dI. At d=4 both constants
are 2, so ||C_s||^2>=||A||^2/2 and ||C_h||^2>=||D||^2/2.
Equation (7) is consequently at least
  112||A||^2-20 Re<A,D>+4||D||^2
    =87||A||^2+4||D-(5/2)A||^2>=0.
Smaller dimensions embed isometrically in dimension four. At k=1 the
matrix G is A itself, so the desired inequality is equality.

7. POLYNOMIAL TRANSFER AND ALL GRAM RANKS.
Associate a symmetric tensor a with P(z)=a(z,...,z)/sqrt(k!), and similarly
for the other two blocks. A direct permutation expansion gives
  <a tensor b tensor c, W(a tensor b tensor c)>
    =(k!)^3 [||PQR||_Fock^2-per Gram_Fock(P,Q,R)].
The preceding seed proof therefore gives the polynomial inequality in
the stated dimension range.

For completeness, the needed Fock product inequalities follow directly
from normal ordering. Multiplication by z_i has adjoint partial_i.
Repeated use of partial_i z_i=z_i partial_i+1 gives, for polynomials F,T,
  ||FT||^2=sum_alpha 1/alpha! ||(partial^alpha F)^*(D)T||^2,       (8)
where the star conjugates coefficients and replaces variables by partial
derivatives. If F is homogeneous of degree N, the |alpha|=N terms sum
to ||F||^2||T||^2. Hence ||FT||^2>=||F||^2||T||^2. If P,Q have the
same positive degree, the alpha=0 term for F=P,T=Q is |<P,Q>|^2 and
is distinct from the top-degree terms. Thus
  ||PQ||^2>=||P||^2||Q||^2+|<P,Q>|^2.                            (9)

For a Gram matrix A of 3k vectors, each block polynomial is a product of
k linear forms, and its Fock Gram entries are per(A_ij). Let U span the
vectors in the first two blocks, so dim U is at most 2k. Expand the third
polynomial in orthogonal variables as
  R(x,y)=sum_beta y^beta R_beta(x),  rho=sum_{beta!=0} beta!||R_beta||^2.
Equation (8), with F=PQ, gives
  ||PQ R_beta||^2>=||PQ||^2||R_beta||^2.
Let C=||P||^2||Q||^2+|<P,Q>|^2. Orthogonality of the y monomials gives
  ||PQR||^2=sum_beta beta!||PQ R_beta||^2.
The Gram permanent changes by exactly rho*C upon replacing R_0 by R:
only the squared norm of R changes, while its inner products with P and
Q are unchanged. Thus the original defect equals the projected defect
plus
  sum_{beta!=0} beta![||PQ R_beta||^2-C||R_beta||^2],
which is at least rho[||PQ||^2-C]>=0 by (8)-(9). R_0 remains a product
of the projected linear factors. The projected problem has at most 2k
variables and has just been proved, including k=2 via Section6.
Zero polynomials and singular Gram matrices require no inversion or
limiting argument in this reasoning. This proves the theorem for every k.
