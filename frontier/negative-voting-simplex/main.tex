\begin{abstract}
We present a proof candidate for the single-assignment negative Gaussian
simplex inequality when the number of parts is sufficiently large and the
correlation belongs to the full negative voting interval. The argument
reduces the remaining exterior regime to a summed radial covariance
estimate. We prove that estimate using a calibrated diagonal supporting
covariance, with a coordinatewise second-derivative bound that remains
uniform when the total Frobenius perturbation is large. An exactly centered
equal-row proxy is then used as a quadratic test, without replacing the
original assignment. A Gaussian maximum comparison pays its second-chaos
loss, while a dimension-free hypercontractive estimate retains the entire
signed higher tail. The conclusion holds for all measurable simplex-valued
assignments, with no prescribed masses, in every finite ambient dimension
at least one less than the number of parts. The proof is relative to the
explicitly listed previously internally audited endpoint, localization,
and local comparison propositions. The comparison is direct for arbitrary
competitive assignments. We also state the negative discrete plurality
consequence and the exact UGC-conditional MAX-$m$-CUT threshold.
\end{abstract}
