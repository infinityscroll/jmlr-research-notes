31 October 2026. strong_signal_branch_count. Independent audit requested.

PURPOSE AND SCOPE
This is the structured coefficient lemma for the diagonal-covariance
support argument. The perturbation is small in coordinate maximum norm;
its Frobenius norm may grow with m. No Frobenius Taylor remainder is used.
This note does not itself assert a full-noise transfer or global rigidity.

THEOREM
Fix A>0. Put L=1+log m, mu=E max_(1<=i<=m)Z_i and
alpha=mu/(m-1). Let sum_i xi_i=0 and max_i|xi_i|<=A. Set
v_i(t)=1+t xi_i, sigma_i=sqrt(v_i), and consider independent scores
sigma_i Z_i+h_i(t), with the unique mean-zero offsets giving every
label mass1/m. For all sufficiently large m, on

                         0<=t<=c_A/L^2,                    (1)

define, as functions of the common score threshold x,
F_i=Phi((x-h_i)/sigma_i), f_i=partial_x F_i, a_i=f_i/F_i,
W=prod_i F_i, A_h=sum_i a_i,
rho_ij=W a_i a_j, omega_ij=int_R rho_ij dx,
ell_i(t)=int_R W A_h a_i dx=sum_j omega_ij.
Here omega_ii is a useful analytic quantity; it is not a physical facet.
For i!=j, omega_ij is exactly the physical offset weight.

Write M1=m^-1sum|xi_i| and M2=m^-1sum xi_i^2. There are constants
depending only on A such that

 c alpha/m<=omega_ij<=C alpha/m, including i=j,
 |h_i'|<=C sqrt(L)(|xi_i|+M1),
 |h_i''|<=C L^(3/2)(xi_i^2+M2),                            (2)
 |ell_i''(t)|<=C alpha L^2(xi_i^2+M2).                     (3)

Let T have density proportional to phi(x)^2 Phi(x)^(m-2), and write
M=E T, V=Var T. Then EXACTLY

 ell_i(0)=alpha,
 ell_i'(0)=-alpha eta_m xi_i,
 eta_m=[m-1-mV]/[2(m-2)] in[1/4,1/2].                      (4)

Consequently
|ell_i(t)-alpha+alpha eta_m t xi_i|
                  <=C alpha L^2 t^2(xi_i^2+M2).             (5)
The constants and m threshold are independent of ||xi||_2. The diagonal
support assembly is a separate argument; (5) is its explicit input.

1. A whole-threshold moment lemma

We first work with arbitrary parameters satisfying

 max_i|v_i-1|=epsilon<=delta0/L,
 max_i|h_i|=H<=delta0/sqrt(L),                              (6)

where delta0>0 is a fixed small numerical constant. No balance is needed
for this lemma. For every fixed pair of nonnegative integers p,q,

 c alpha/m<=omega_ij<=C alpha/m,
 E_(ij)[(m max_k a_k(x))^p(1+|x|)^q]
                                  <=C_(p,q)L^((p+q)/2),    (7)

uniformly over all i,j, including i=j. E_(ij) denotes normalization of
rho_ij dx by omega_ij. Only finitely many fixed p,q are needed below;
their constants and eventual m thresholds do not depend on the direction
xi or its Frobenius norm.

Here are details controlling both tails. For x>=2H set
z=(x+H)/sigma_min>=0. Every standardized argument y_k=(x-h_k)/sigma_k
lies between z-Cepsilon z-CH and z and is nonnegative. Gaussian density
ratios and Phi(y_k)>=1/2 give

 rho_ij(x)dx
 <=C phi(z)^2 Phi(z)^(m-2)
          exp[Cepsilon z^2+CHz+CH^2]dz,
 max_k a_k(x)<=C [phi(z)/Phi(z)]
          exp[Cepsilon z^2+CHz+CH^2].                      (8)

For i=j, the extra factor1/F_i is at most2 in this region, so the same
bound holds. For a lower weight bound use y=(x-H)/sigma_max and restrict
to x>=2H. The corresponding lower estimate is a constant times
phi(y)^2 Phi(y)^(m-2)exp[-Cepsilon y^2-CHy-CH^2]. The omitted interval
near0 carries exponentially small baseline mass. Since the baseline T
has E T^2<=CL, Jensen or a fixed central quantile interval proves the
lower bound in(7).

For completeness, the upper moment bound in(8) has no hidden exponential
factor in m. Write u=Phi(z), s=1-u. On z>=0, Mills bounds give
phi(z)<=C s sqrt(log(e/s)), and z^2<=2log(1/s). The factor exp(CHz)
is bounded by exp(CHsqrt(L)/2) exp[CH z^2/(2sqrt(L))]. Thus the integrals
in(8), after multiplying by (m max a)^p(1+|x|)^q, are bounded by a constant
times

 m^p int_0^(1/2) (1-s)^(m-p-2)
     s^(p+1-2theta)[log(e/s)]^((p+q+1)/2)ds,
 0<=theta<=C_(p,q)(epsilon+H/sqrt(L))<=C_(p,q)delta0/L.

Substituting y=ms and splitting y<=1 and y>1 bounds this by
C_(p,q) m^(-2+2theta)L^((p+q+1)/2). Indeed the first interval has an
integrable fixed power times log(1/y), and the second has an exponential
factor e^(-y/2). Since m^(2theta) is bounded and

 Z0:=int phi(x)^2 Phi(x)^(m-2)dx=alpha/m
                                      comparable to sqrt(L)/m^2, (9)

division by Z0 gives(7). Identity(9) follows by integration by parts in
mu=m int x phi(x)Phi(x)^(m-1)dx. The Gaussian maximum estimates also
give the displayed order and the baseline fixed moments used above.

On x<=-2H, all F_i are bounded above by a Gaussian left-tail envelope;
their product is at most 2^-m exp(-c m x^2), while each hazard is at most
C(1+|x|). On [-2H,2H], all F_i are bounded above by a fixed number less
than1. These regions therefore contribute at most C_(p,q)m^p c0^m to
the unnormalized moments, with c0<1. They are negligible compared with
Z0. This completes(7), including negative thresholds and the analytic
diagonal omega_ii.

2. Offset differentiation and a componentwise inverse

For positive v, the Gaussian scores have full support modulo their
common additive coordinate. The balanced offsets exist, are unique
modulo constants, and vary smoothly. Equivalently the strictly convex
offset dual has mass Hessian the complete Laplacian with weights
omega_ij>0. These facts also follow directly from the one-dimensional
positive integral formulas and the usual mass-offset dual. We fix sum h=0.

When the weights satisfy(7), the Laplacian mathcal L has
c alpha P<=mathcal L<=C alpha P on H=1-perp. More precisely, for any
mean-zero b and y=mathcal L^-1 b,

 |y_i|<=C/alpha (|b_i|+m^-1sum_j|b_j|).                     (10)

To see the component estimate, subtract c alpha/m from every edge weight.
The remainder is a graph Laplacian L_extra. On H,
exp(-s mathcal L)=exp(-c alpha s)exp(-s L_extra).
The latter semigroup is doubly stochastic and is L1-contracting, so
||y||1<=||b||1/(c alpha). The row equation
deg_i y_i=b_i+sum_(j!=i)omega_ij y_j, with deg_i>=c alpha and
omega_ij<=C alpha/m, now gives(10). L1 contraction alone is not being
mistaken for a componentwise estimate.

Define the threshold velocity
 b_i(x)=h_i'+u_i(x-h_i), u_i=xi_i/(2v_i).
The density transport identity partial_t f_i=-partial_x(f_i b_i)
and integration by parts in the winning-probability integral give

 0=p_i'=sum_(j!=i)int rho_ij(b_i-b_j)dx.                    (11)

Solving for h' with(10) and E_(ij)|x|<=C sqrt(L) gives the first
derivative bound in(2), as long as(6) holds.

This closes the required uniform box by continuation. At t=0, h=0.
The derivative bound implies ||h(t)||infty<=C_A t sqrt(L). Choose a
bootstrap bound twice this size, then choose c_A sufficiently small.
For t<=c_A/L^2 it lies strictly inside H<=delta0/sqrt(L), while
max|v_i-1|<=At<=delta0/L. The weight constants in(7) are fixed in that
region, independently of the bootstrap constant. The resulting strict
improvement proves the bound over the whole interval(1). No norm
||h||2 or ||xi||2 is required to stay small.

3. Exact differentiated flux and the second offset derivative

Put
 L_i(x)=partial_t log f_i
       =-xi_i/(2v_i)+xi_i(x-h_i)^2/(2v_i^2)
                                      +h_i'(x-h_i)/v_i,
 S(x)=sum_k a_k b_k,
 P_ij=L_i+L_j+a_i b_i+a_j b_j-S.                            (12)
Then partial_t log rho_ij=P_ij, including i=j. In particular all
other-label contributions are retained in S; replacing it by only
the pair terms would be incorrect.

For each fixed finite k, the moment lemma and the first derivative
bound imply
 ||b_i||_(L^k(ij))<=C_k sqrt(L)(|xi_i|+M1),
 ||P_ij||_(L^k(ij))<=C_k L(|xi_i|+|xi_j|+M1).               (13)
For the summed term, use
sum a_k(|xi_k|+M1)<=2m max_k a_k M1,
then(7). This is why no extra m appears in(13).

Write
 c_i(x)=-xi_i^2(x-h_i)/(2v_i^2)-xi_i h_i'/(2v_i).
Then b_i'=h_i''+c_i. Differentiating(11) gives EXACTLY

 mathcal L h''=-b^(2),
 b_i^(2)=sum_(j!=i)int rho_ij
                    [P_ij(b_i-b_j)+c_i-c_j]dx.             (14)

Hölder, (13), M1^2<=M2 and(7) yield
 |b_i^(2)|<=C alpha L^(3/2)(xi_i^2+M2),
 m^-1sum|b_i^(2)|<=C alpha L^(3/2)M2.
The right side is mean zero by its pairwise antisymmetry. Applying(10)
proves the second derivative bound in(2). This is a bound on the actual
balanced offset acceleration, not a claim that it can be discarded.

4. The structured second derivative of ell_i

Differentiate rho_ij twice:
 partial_t^2 rho_ij=rho_ij(P_ij^2+P_ij').
All expressions are integrable by(7), so differentiation under the
threshold integral is justified. The explicit derivative of L_i in(12)
contains terms bounded by
C[xi_i^2(1+x^2)+|xi_i h_i'||x|+(h_i')^2+|h_i''||x|]
with harmless bounded-v and small-h factors. Its conditional L^k norm
is therefore at most C_k L^2(xi_i^2+M2).

Also a_i'=a_i(L_i+a_i b_i), so
(a_i b_i)'=a_i[(L_i+a_i b_i)b_i+b_i'].
Using(7), (2), and(13), its conditional L^k norm is bounded by the same
C_k L^2(xi_i^2+M2). For the full summed derivative S', apply the pointwise
max-hazard bound before summing:
sum a_k|xi_k|^2<=m max a_k M2,
sum a_k(|xi_k|+M1)^2<=C m max a_k M2.
Terms with a_k^2 are bounded by a further max a_k. Equation(7) then
gives ||S'||_(L^k(ij))<=C_k L^2 M2. In particular,

 E_(ij)(P_ij^2+|P_ij'|)
                     <=C L^2(xi_i^2+xi_j^2+M2).            (15)

The same estimates hold when j=i; no physical interpretation of that
diagonal is needed. Summing(15) with omega_ij<=C alpha/m gives

 |ell_i''|<=sum_j omega_ij E_(ij)(P_ij^2+|P_ij'|)
            <=C alpha L^2(xi_i^2+M2),

which is(3). This is the required coordinatewise remainder; replacing
it by a bound involving only ||xi||2 would lose the intended application.

5. Exact coefficient at the regular point

At t=0 all sigma_i=1 and h_i=0. The balanced mass equation(11), with
sum xi=0, gives h_i'(0)=-M xi_i/2, where M=E T. Put
a(x)=phi(x)/Phi(x). Direct differentiation gives

 partial_t log a_i|0=k(x)xi_i,
 k(x)=[(x+a(x))(x-M)-1]/2.

Both partial_t W|0 and partial_t A_h|0 vanish because sum xi=0.
Therefore ell_i'(0)=alpha E_T k(T) xi_i, while ell_i(0)=mZ0=alpha.

The log derivative of the tie density is -2x+(m-2)a(x).
Integration by parts against x-M gives
 -2 Var T+(m-2)Cov(T,a(T))=-1.
Consequently
 E_T k(T)=[m Var T-m+1]/[2(m-2)]=-eta_m,
proving(4). The density has log second derivative at most-2, so the
one-dimensional Brascamp--Lieb variance bound gives Var T<=1/2.
For the reverse bound Var T>=1/m, decompose independent regular scores
into their common mean Zbar and the centered vector. Conditioning on
Z_i=Z_j and on their being maximal depends only on the centered vector.
Thus T=Zbar+T_H with independent Zbar of variance1/m. This yields the
claimed eta_m interval exactly.

As a normalization check, the physical degree is ell_i-omega_ii.
At the regular point omega_ii'=-2alpha eta_m xi_i/m, so Price's formula
gives the variance-coordinate Hessian

 F_v''[xi,xi]=-alpha/4[(m-1)/m-Var T]||xi||2^2.

For standard-deviation coordinates the corresponding balanced Hessian
is alpha Var T||xi||2^2. These local identities are checks on the
coefficient, not substitutes for the uniform remainder proved above.
Integrating(3) twice proves(5).

6. Exact support identity and its limited role here

For D=P diag(v_i)P, the physical offset weights satisfy
omega_ij=int W a_i a_j for i!=j. Hence, for every centered PSD matrix C,

 tr(L_D C)=int W[A_h sum_i a_i C_ii-(Pa)^T C(Pa)]dx.         (16)

This holds at arbitrary C; only the supporting covariance D is diagonal.
The non-additive term is an exact PSD square with a minus sign. Euler
homogeneity gives F_U(D)=tr(L_D D), and covariance concavity therefore
implies F_U(C)<=tr[L_D(C+D)]/2. In that upper bound the square term is
nonpositive because C+D>=0, so dropping it is permitted when forming a
lower certificate on the tangent remainder. The coefficient bound(5)
is designed to control the remaining diagonal part. The global
coercivity assembly and any full-noise consequence are not asserted by
this derivative note.
