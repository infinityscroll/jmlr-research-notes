2. Representation, attainment, and a uniform value error
The square-completion identity gives
 V_m(p)=max_{C>=0}g(C,p).
The accepted covariance theorem makes F_p concave in C, strictly concave
on the positive definite cone for the simplex target. For positive masses
F_p is jointly smooth in (C,p) on C>0, with the offset gauge fixed by
sum_i h_i=0. Its mass derivatives are

 grad_p F_p=-h,   D_pp F_p=-L^{-1} on H,                 (3)

where L is the positive complete-graph offset Hessian. Thus D_pp F_p is
negative semidefinite, and D_Cp F_p[T,u]=-u dot D_C h[T].

For every relaxed assignment the squared first-moment sum is at most1:
its first Hermite coefficients obey
 sum_i |integral x f_i dgamma|^2
 <=sum_i (integral f_i^2-(integral f_i)^2)
 <=sum_i p_i(1-p_i)<=1.
Hence F_p(C)<=sqrt(tr C) and g(C,p)<=2sqrt(tr C)-tr C.
This proves coercivity uniformly in p. The unconstrained maximum is
attained and its optimizing trace is at most4, since C=0 has value0.
For eps(m-1)<=1, the regularized maximum is at least -1 (test eps I_H),
so every regularized optimizer has trace less than9. Strict concavity
makes that optimizer unique.

Adding independent centered Gaussian noise to any coupling proves
 F_p(C+eps I_H)>=F_p(C).
Testing C*+eps I_H at an unconstrained optimizer C* therefore gives the
particularly useful uniform bound

 0<=V_m(p)-Veps(p)<=eps(m-1),                            (4)
 Veps(p)=max_(C>=eps I_H) g(C,p).

This needs no positive rank for C*. It also shows that every convergent
sequence of regularized optimizers with eps->0 has a true optimizing limit
at the limiting mass vector. Compactness and joint continuity consequently
make their distance to the true optimal set tend to0 uniformly over P_m.

3. KKT and local Lipschitz regularity for each fixed eps
All covariance coordinates in this section are in H. The score Gaussian
integration-by-parts identity gives 2D_C F_p=L, so the covariance gradient
of g is L-I_H. The first-order optimality inequality is

 <D_C g(Ceps(p),p), D-Ceps(p)> <=0
                     for every D>=eps I_H.             (5)

In particular L<=I_H and
 (I_H-L)(Ceps-eps I_H)=0.
If B B^T=Ceps and M=L B is its matrix of cell first moments, then

 ||B-M||_F^2=eps tr[(I_H-L)^2]<=eps(m-1).                (6)

These KKT statements are also part of the graph proof's input; no invertible
true optimizer is required.

For fixed eps and m, on the compact covariance set C>=eps I_H,tr C<=9 and
mass window P_m, strict negative covariance Hessians and continuity give
 D_CC g[T,T]<=-kappa_eps |T|_F^2 with kappa_eps>0.
The mixed derivatives are bounded there. Applying (5) to two optimizers
at p and q, and adding the two inequalities, gives

 0<=<D_C g(Ceps(q),q)-D_C g(Ceps(p),p), Ceps(q)-Ceps(p)>.

Splitting the difference first in C then in p yields
 kappa_eps ||Ceps(q)-Ceps(p)||_F
       <=M_eps |q-p|.
Thus the optimizer is Lipschitz on P_m for each fixed eps. The constants
may diverge as eps->0; they will not enter the final semiconcavity estimate.
All derivatives are taken in the open positive-mass simplex, so endpoints
of the compact mass window cause no differentiability ambiguity.

4. The constrained envelope bound without active-set smoothness
Restrict to any mass segment p(t)=p0+t u in P_m. The optimizer C(t)=Ceps(p(t))
is Lipschitz, hence differentiable for almost every t. Danskin's theorem
here follows directly from uniqueness, continuity, and the two optimality
comparisons at p and p+delta: Veps is differentiable in mass with

 (Veps)'(t)=D_p g(C(t),p(t))[u].                         (7)

The right side is Lipschitz along the segment for fixed eps. Therefore its
second derivative exists almost everywhere, and the chain rule gives

 (Veps)''(t)=D_pp g[u,u]+D_Cp g[C'(t),u].                (8)

At a differentiability point, apply the preceding monotonicity inequality
to t and t+s, divide by s^2, and let s->0. This yields

 E := -D_CC g[C',C'] <= B := D_Cp g[C',u].               (9)

This inequality is the entire contribution of the changing active set.
It is not replaced by an unjustified interior Euler equality. In particular
B>=E>=0.

By (2), (3), and g=2F-tr,
 B^2=4|u dot D_C h[C']|^2
     <=4A_m |u|^2(-D_CC F[C',C'])
     =2A_m |u|^2 E.
Combining with E<=B gives B<=2A_m |u|^2 (including B=0). Since D_pp g<=0,
(8) proves

 (Veps)''(t)<=2A_m |u|^2 for almost every t.              (10)

The second derivative is an actual derivative of a Lipschitz first derivative,
so integration loses no singular positive measure. This proves uniform
semiconcavity on every mass segment, with coefficient2A_m independent of eps.

5. Passage to the singular limit and anchoring at uniform masses
Equation(4) gives uniform convergence Veps->V. The integrated semiconcavity
inequality therefore passes directly to V, including mass vectors whose
optimizing Gram is singular. This conclusion did not require a uniform
Lipschitz constant for Ceps or any differentiability of the limiting optimizer.

At U, uniqueness of Ceps and permutation invariance force Ceps(U) to be a
scalar multiple of I_H. The centered optimal offsets are then all0, so
D_p Veps(U)[u]=0 for sum_i u_i=0. Integrating (10) from U to p yields
 Veps(p)<=Veps(U)+A_m |p-U|^2.
Pass eps->0 and use the accepted balanced endpoint V(U)=w_m to obtain(1).

6. Exact scope
The statement is global over the displayed fixed relative mass box, not
over the full probability simplex. Constants may depend on its fixed lower
and upper relative-mass bounds. The sufficiently-large-m threshold and the
auxiliary eps_0 are not computed. Singular optimizer Grams are allowed in
