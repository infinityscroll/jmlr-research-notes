THEOREM
Fix A>0. For sufficiently large m depending on A, let N>=m-1 and let
f be any measurable simplex-valued assignment with masses p_i in
[1/(2m),3/(2m)]. Let B_i=int x f_i dgamma be its ACTUAL centroid rows.
Assume their Procrustes distance from the regular centroid matrix is
d(B)<=A/m. With the usual regular reference g, U_i=1/m, L=1+log m,
alpha=mu_m/(m-1), and R_U(B)=(w+||B||^2)/2-F_U(B), one has uniformly
for0<r<=1/(m-1)

 S_-r(f)-S_-r(g)>=||p-U||^2/2+r R_U(B).                       (1)

Equality, or competitiveness S_-r(f)<=S_-r(g), forces a rotated regular
simplex partition. This is a centroid-SCORE-BALL theorem: it does not
prove that all competitive minimizers enter that ball. No conicality,
interface regularity, or L1-neighborhood assumption on f is used.

The new ingredient is a structured radially weighted, ALL-AMPLITUDE cap.
Its moderate-amplitude Holder exponent is fixed at C log m. Arbitrarily
tiny amplitudes use a fixed L32 normal-sup argument, not a Holder exponent
depending on log(1/amplitude). Every source below remains unchanged.

1. Exact energy and the reference cancellation
Align B with a regular centroid matrix B0. Write f0=g_(B,p) for its
affine Gaussian allocation with masses p and mean-zero offsets h.
Let M be the centroid matrix of f0, v=f-f0, z=p-U, d=||B-B0||,
 D=d+||z||,
 L_route=F_p(B)-||B||^2=<B,M-B>>=0.
The last equality uses the hypothesis B=M_f, not stationarity.
Let q0=T_-r Pi_(>=2)g and qB=T_-r Pi_(>=2)f0. Put
 u=T_sqrt(r)Pi_(odd>=3)v.
Since ||v||_2<=sqrt2, one has ||u||<=sqrt2 r^(3/2).
Hermite orthogonality gives EXACTLY

 S_-r(f)-S_-r(f0)
 =r[2L_route+||M-B||^2]+2<v,qB>
                    +||T_sqrt(r)Pi_(even>=2)v||^2-||u||^2.

The last negative square equals
||u||^2-2<v,T_sqrt(r)u>. Dropping only the nonnegative even square,

 S_-r(f)-S_-r(f0)
 >=r||M-B||^2+||u||^2
                  +2[rL_route+<v,q0+a>],
 a=qB-q0-T_sqrt(r)u.                                       (2)

Here u already belongs to odd degrees>=3. No signed tail was replaced
by an absolute additive error at this stage.

At a point in old cell i of f0, write
 c_ij=(B_i-B_j).x+h_i-h_j>=0,
 delta l_i=(B_i-B0_i).x+h_i,
 c_ij=(B0_i-B0_j).x+delta l_i-delta l_j.
The exact regular negative-noise gap[R] supplies a positive coefficient
k_ij(x) such that
 (T_-r g)_j-(T_-r g)_i
           =r k_ij(x)(B0_i-B0_j).x.
At a reference tie use its continuous divided-difference value. Thus

 r c_ij+q0_j-q0_i
        =r k_ij c_ij+r(1-k_ij)(delta l_i-delta l_j).          (3)

This identity is valid whether the old affine winner i is a reference
winner or not. The ratio in[R] is positive on both sides of a reference
face, by swapping i,j; its quantitative bounds depend only on the full
coordinate range, not on the winning label.

2. A common damping and a smooth range majorant
Let P0 be the projection on the regular score span. Put
 M0=[E(max_i Z_i)^2-1]/mu_m=O(sqrt L),
 eta=4(M0^2+1)r^2/(1-r^2),
 kappa(x)=exp(-1-eta|P0 x|^2)<=1.
The exact range bounds[R] imply k_ij(x)>=kappa(x) for every pair.

For a smooth upper bound on their deviations use X_i=(B0_i/alpha).x and
 W(x)=log(sum_i exp X_i)+log(sum_i exp(-X_i)).
Then W>=max X_i-min X_i, W>=0, |grad W|<=2, and the Hessian has
operator and Hilbert--Schmidt norms bounded by2. For an absolute C,

 e_r(x)=C[r^2+r sqrt(L)W+r^2 W^2]
                    exp(C[r sqrt(L)W+r^2W^2])
                         >=max_(i,j)|k_ij(x)-1|.             (4)

This follows directly from[R]'s exp(plus/minus[4UM0+4U^2])/sigma
bounds, U=r*range/(2sigma), sigma=sqrt(1-r^2). The possible common
mean of X is zero and the span has dimension m-1.

Define nonnegative label forcings

 psi_i=kappa^(-1)[e_r |delta l_i|+|a_i|/r].                  (5)

For every old winner i and j!=i, (3)-(5) give
 r c_ij+(q0+a)_j-(q0+a)_i
                         >=r kappa(c_ij-psi_i-psi_j).
The diagonal j=i is left equal to0; it is NOT included as a spurious
zero-cost reward. Summing against the new probabilities f_j and using
kappa<=1 yields

 rL_route+<v,q0+a> >=-r Bcap(psi),                           (6)
 Bcap(psi)=int max(0,max_(j!=i(x))[psi_i+psi_j-c_ij])dgamma.

3. Uniform moment and normal-derivative estimates for the damping
All affine diagrams here are conditioned at scale alpha and in the
positive mass box. We use the facet bounds, Laplacian bounds, and
normal-coordinate geometry proved in[U, Section1]. In particular every
facet has intrinsic probability >=m^(-C), normal offset O(sqrt L), and
its conditional Gaussian mean has norm O(sqrt L).

The following bounds hold with a FIXED q=C1 L, and also at every fixed
multiple of q used in Holder:
 ||kappa^(-1)||_q<=C,
 ||e_r/r||_q<=C L^(3/2).                                    (7)
They hold under gamma_N and under every normalized facet law. The same
assertion with a fixed polynomial bound m^C holds on each entire pair
hyperplane shifted in its normal coordinate by |s|<=1, including its
first two normal derivatives after dividing e_r by r.

Details. Under any of these Gaussian planes, W has L^q norm at most
C(L+sqrt q), because its coordinate means are O(sqrt L), its coordinate
variances are bounded, and a Gaussian maximum satisfies the elementary
union tail bound. The same is true under a facet law by conditioning
at an exponent C2 L and its polynomial probability bound. Alternatively
the convex Gaussian restriction is subgaussian about its mean, by the
linear-tilt Brascamp--Lieb estimate. The exponential in(4) then has
bounded moments: r L^(3/2) tends0 and q r^2 tends0 uniformly on voting r.
This proves the second estimate in(7), with its derivatives by direct
differentiation of the explicit smooth W.

For the radial factor, a convex Gaussian restriction satisfies
E exp(t.Y)<=exp(t.mean(Y)+|t|^2/2). A Gaussian auxiliary integral therefore
gives for any orthogonal projection P of rank<=m and small b>0,
 E exp(b|P Y|^2)
 <=(1-2b)^(-m/2)exp[b|P mean(Y)|^2/(1-2b)].
The same bound is immediate for an entire Gaussian plane. Apply it with
b=q eta. Since q eta*m=O(L^2/m) and |mean(Y)|^2=O(L), the result is
bounded. Normal shifts by at most1 do not change this conclusion.
The derivatives of kappa^(-1) involve only its product with polynomials
in P0 x of degree at most2, with coefficients eta and eta^2. Fixed
moments of these derivatives are at most a fixed power of m. These
facts prove every assertion above with no ambient-N determinant.

4. Smoothed forcing amplitudes
Fix a small t=c/sqrt L as in[H]'s OU restriction lemma. The affine
boundary trace[T], integrated first along the balanced score path and
then along the mass path, gives

 qB-q0=T_t v_D,       ||v_D||<=C r^2 L^(5/2)D.               (8)

This is exactly[H, Section4] with lower Hermite cutoff2 instead of3:
the boundary factor is ((r/t)^2 Lambda)^(2/2)=O(r^2L^2), and the
velocity norm contributes sqrt L*D. Also

 T_sqrt(r)u=T_t v_u,  ||v_u||<=C r^(3/2)L^(3/2)||u||,         (9)

because u starts in degree3. All factorization times have absolute
value at most1 for large m. Hypercontractivity at the fixed exponent
q=C1 L is allowed by making c smaller once.

The offset and score bounds in[U] give
 ||h||<=C L D,
 sum_i||delta l_i||_q^2<=C L^2D^2.
Combine these with(7)-(9), using fixed multiples of q in Holder, to get

 (sum_i||psi_i||_q^2)^(1/2)
                  <=C[r L^(5/2)D+sqrt(r)L^(3/2)||u||].       (10)

On the facets, the OU restriction lemma[H, Section2] and the bounded
weighted graph degrees additionally give

 sum_(e=ij)w_e E_nu(psi_i^2+psi_j^2)
                   <=C[r^2 L^5D^2+r L^3||u||^2].             (11)

For the first term use ||e_r/kappa||_(L^8(nu))<=Cr L^(3/2),
conditional linear moments, and sum_e w_e(|delta B_i|^2+|delta B_j|^2)
<=C d^2. The offset part is bounded by C||h||^2. For the two smoothed
terms, conditional L^8 OU restriction bounds each label by C times its
input L2 norm; sum with the graph degrees. The radial factor is paid
by its fixed conditional moments in(7). This proves(11) without a sum
of m unweighted label probabilities.

5. The structured cap is quadratic at EVERY amplitude
Set
 theta=C2 L^4[r D+sqrt(r)||u||],                              (12)
with a fixed sufficiently large C2. If theta=0, the forcing vanishes
and Bcap=0. Otherwise put v_i=psi_i/theta. By(10), its vector L^q norm
is bounded by1 after choosing C2; by(11),

 sum_e w_e E(v_i^2+v_j^2)<=C.                                (13)

Since D<=A/m+C/sqrt m, ||u||<=sqrt2 r^(3/2), and r<=1/(m-1),
theta<=C_A L^4/m^(3/2)<=m^(-5/4) for large m. We prove

                         Bcap(psi)<=C theta^2.               (14)

For moderate amplitudes theta>=m^(-B), use the exact source/target
margin decomposition from[V]. Explicitly, for c>=0,
(a+b-c)_+<=[(2a-c)_++(2b-c)_+]/2. Thus Bcap is bounded by one-half
the sum of target-entry margins with reward2psi_j and source-exit
margins with reward2psi_i. Each margin has sublevel measure <=C s
for0<s<=c0/m, by[V, Sections1-2]. Its L^q bathtub bound therefore gives
 C[m^(1/(q+1))theta^(2q/(q+1))
                                +(c0/m)(Ctheta*m/c0)^q].
Here q=C1 L is FIXED independently of theta. For the stated polynomial
amplitude interval the first term is <=C_B theta^2 and the second is
smaller, exactly as in[V]. Factors2 are absorbed by choosing the fixed
small-margin radius as in[V], or by a fixed change in its constants.

For all smaller amplitudes we spell out why the normal-sup argument
of[S] applies to this new structured forcing. On every pair plane,
and |s|<=1 in its normal coordinate, the first two normal derivatives
of each analytic factor in v_i have L32 norms <=m^C. For the polynomial
part, divide e_r by r and use rD/theta<=C L^(-4), (7), and the linear
delta l_i coefficients. For a smoothed part use
 ||v_D||/(r theta)<=C rL^(5/2)D/theta<=C,
 ||v_u||/(r theta)<=C sqrt(r)L^(3/2)||u||/theta<=C.
The exact one-dimensional OU row, its first two normal derivatives,
and tangential L32 hypercontractivity give the required bound for
T_t times each normalized input; normal offsets are O(sqrt L).
Products with kappa^(-1) have the same polynomial bounds by Holder
and(7). Absolute values in(5) are locally Lipschitz; their a.e. first
normal derivative is bounded by the absolute derivative of the
underlying analytic function. Thus their normal Lipschitz supremum is
controlled by the same analytic first- and second-derivative bounds.
No division by a vanishing label amplitude or by r remains in these
normalized estimates. Full-space L32 bounds are controlled as well.

Let M=theta^(-1/8). On good projected points the normal sup of
|v_i|+|partial_n v_i| is <=M. Positive pair rewards then have normal
width at most C theta M/alpha. The fiber Taylor and Gaussian-density
error is O(m^C theta^3 M^3). A projected point outside the actual
facet but contributing from an old cell lies in a third-label strip
of width C theta M/alpha; full-rank conditioning bounds the relevant
tangential altitudes below by c alpha. Summing the at most m such
strips and all ordered pairs retains the same polynomial error.
Bad projected points contribute at most m^C theta M^(-31), by L32;
normal distances>1 contribute at most m^C theta^32. These are precisely
the estimates proved in[S, Section2], applied separately to the entry
and exit rewards above. The main term is bounded by
C theta^2 sum_e w_e E(v_i^2+v_j^2), rather than by squared differences.
Equation(13) pays it. Consequently, for fixed B0,K,

 Bcap(psi)<=C theta^2+C m^K theta^(21/8), theta<=m^(-B0).

Choose the fixed B larger than B0 and8K/5. For every theta<=m^(-B)
the remainder is absorbed by Ctheta^2. Together with the moderate
regime this proves(14) for ALL amplitudes in(12), including arbitrarily
tiny ones. Neither q nor any damping integrability exponent grows as
theta tends to0. The cutoff B only joins two proved regimes; it is
not a restriction on the theorem's amplitude.

6. Relative replacement of the actual assignment
Equations(6),(12),(14) give
 rL_route+<v,q0+a>
                  >=-C[r^3 L^8D^2+r^2 L^8||u||^2].
Insert this into(2). For large m uniformly throughout voting r,
C r^2L^8<=1/2, so the remaining odd square is nonnegative. Hence

 S_-r(f)-S_-r(f0)
          >=r||M-B||^2-C r^3 L^8[d^2+||p-U||^2].             (15)

This retains the crucial positive centroid-residual term. It is a
relative estimate because d^2 is paid by the finite score remainder,
not an absolute O(r^3 polylog/m) error. No Euler equation for f was
used; its actual-centroid identity and its mass constraints suffice.

7. Exact combination with the affine comparison
The full signed affine comparison[H, Equation4] states
 S_-r(f0)-S_-r(g)
 >=[1-C_A(r^2mL^7+r^3L^10)]||z||^2
       +r[w-W(f0)]-C_A[r^2sqrt(m)L^13+r^3mL^11]R_U(B).
Use the finite gap[F], d^2<=C_A m sqrt(L)R_U(B), to pay(15).
Since W(f0)=||B||^2+2L_route+||M-B||^2, the first-chaos terms combine
EXACTLY to

 r[w-W(f0)]+r||M-B||^2
       =r[w-||B||^2-2L_route]=2r R_p(B),
 R_p(B)=(w+||B||^2)/2-F_p(B).                                (16)

Thus, after harmless enlargement of constants,
 S_-r(f)-S_-r(g)
 >=[1-C_A(r^2mL^7+r^3L^10)]||z||^2
       +2rR_p(B)-C_A[r^2sqrt(m)L^13+r^3mL^11]R_U(B).         (17)

The balanced optimal offsets h_U(B) are an admissible dual choice for
every p, so F_p(B)<=F_U(B)-h_U.z and R_p>=R_U+h_U.z.
The audited deficit-relative offset certificate[O] gives
||h_U||^2<=C L^2 R_U. Young's inequality therefore yields
2h_U.z>=-R_U/2-C L^2||z||^2. Substitution into(17) proves

 S_-r(f)-S_-r(g)
 >=[1-C_A(rL^2+r^2mL^7+r^3L^10)]||z||^2
    +[3/2-C_A(r sqrt(m)L^13+r^2mL^11)]r R_U(B).

All error coefficients tend0 uniformly for0<r<=1/(m-1).
For sufficiently large m this is(1).

8. Equality and the exact remaining global gate
If f is competitive, (1) forces p=U and R_U(B)=0. For its balanced
affine allocation with centroid M, the exact identity
2R_U(B)=w-||M||^2+||M-B||^2 and the accepted balanced endpoint theorem
force M=B to be a regular centroid matrix. Since B is also the centroid
matrix of f and f is balanced, W(f)=w. Endpoint equality then forces f
to be the same regular simplex allocation, up to rotation and labels.
This also covers fractional assignments, because the regular linear
argmax has almost surely a unique winner.

The theorem is unrestricted in the measurable geometry of f, but its
CENTROID score-ball premise is explicit. Existing mass and additive
moment localization do not automatically give d(B)<=A/m. There is no
claim here that all competitive minimizers satisfy that premise. The
result is a genuine full-voting finite comparison within that premise,
not a completed global Plurality/Standard Simplex proof.
