THEOREM
Fix c_sep>0. For sufficiently large m depending only on c_sep, let
N>=m-1 and let f be ANY measurable simplex-valued balanced assignment
in Gaussian R^N. Write B_i=E[X f_i] for its ACTUAL centroid rows.
Assume
 |B_i| is the same for every i,
 |B_i-B_j|>=c_sep/m for every i!=j.                           (1)
No affine, conical, rank, interface, or score-neighborhood hypothesis
on f is made. With alpha=mu_m/(m-1), w=alpha^2(m-1), L=1+log m,
and R(B)=(w+||B||_F^2)/2-F_U(B), uniformly for0<r<=1/(m-1),

                    S_-r(f)-S_-r(g)>=r R(B).                (2)

In particular competitiveness forces a regular-simplex assignment.
The two centroid hypotheses in(1) remain EXPLICIT. Existing actual
pair-separation theorems do not supply equality of row norms or exact
balance. This is not the unrestricted named conjecture.

The proof extends the structured weighted cap of[L] to weak radial
exposure of order m^-2, paying only logarithmic losses. It retains the
positive centroid-residual term, so it does not assume a generic global
conversion of an affine score deficit into a partition moment deficit.
The new rank-free offset input[O] is used at its exact stated scope.

1. Reductions and exact deficit conventions
Put a=||B||_F, and align a regular centroid matrix B0 so that
d=||B-B0||_F is the Procrustes distance. Balance implies sum B_i=0.
The balanced first-moment theorem[C] gives a^2=W(f)<=w.
Let f0 be the balanced affine optimizer of B, with mean-zero offsets h,
and let M be its centroid matrix. Sites are distinct by(1), so the
assignment is pure except on Gaussian-null hyperplanes, even if its
affine span has deficient dimension. Let
 L_route=F_U(B)-a^2=<B,M-B> >=0.
Then

 R=(w-a^2)/2-L_route>=0,
 2R=w-||M||_F^2+||M-B||_F^2.                                (3)

Nonnegativity is the accepted balanced endpoint dual, not an Euler
equation for f. Comparing F_U(B) with the regular assignment also gives
 R<=d^2/2. The usual centroid entropy bound yields |B_i|<=C alpha.

If d<=1/m, theorem[L] with its fixed A=1 already gives(2). Its stronger
large-m coefficients may be used for the equality discussion. Hence
the new proof concerns d>1/m.

If a^2<w/4, a direct parity comparison suffices. Rare-cell
hypercontractivity gives an adverse odd tail at most C r^3 L^3/m for
f, while A2(g)<=C L^2/m and its even tail>=4 is at most C r^4 L^4/m.
Consequently
 S(f)-S(g)>=r(w-a^2)-C[r^2L^2+r^3L^3+r^4L^4]/m
           >=(3/4-o(1))r w.
But R<= (w-a^2)/2<=w/2. Thus(2) holds, strictly beyond its coefficient
for large m. We may henceforth assume

                 sqrt(w)/2<=a<=sqrt(w), d>1/m.               (4)

Normalize only when applying a theorem that requires it:
Bhat=(sqrt(w)/a)B and hhat=(sqrt(w)/a)h. The assignment f0 is unchanged.
The exact relation is
 R(B)=(a-sqrt(w))^2/2+(a/sqrt(w))R(Bhat).
Thus R(Bhat)<=2R(B). The row cap for Bhat is still C alpha.
The global offset certificate[O] gives

             ||h||^2<=C L^2 R(B), hence ||h||<=C Ld.          (5)

The first inequality follows by scaling its normalized conclusion:
R(Bhat)/(sqrt(w)/a)^2<=R(B)/(sqrt(w)/a)<=R(B).

2. Weak exposure supplies rank-free polynomial geometry
Equal row norms imply the exact weak-exposure identity

 B_i.(B_i-B_j)=|B_i-B_j|^2/2>=c_sep^2/(2m^2).               (6)

This is weaker than the fixed-alpha^2 exposure hypothesis in[E]. We
therefore derive every changed bound, rather than importing that theorem
with a parameter that secretly depends on m.

For any affine diagram of these sites, Gaussian integration by parts
gives M'_i=sum_j w_ij(B_i-B_j), w_ij=mu_ij/ell_ij. If its i-th cell has
mass at most C'/m, its centroid norm is at most C sqrt(L)/m. Dot with
B_i and use(6). This gives

 deg_i=sum_j w_ij<=C L,  sum_j mu_ij<=C alpha L,
 c_sep/m<=ell_ij<=C alpha.                                  (7)

For a one-label offset INCREASE STARTING FROM THE BALANCED f0,
consider the path only until p_i reaches2/m. Along this path
1/m<=p_i<=2/m, so the centroid entropy bound is
|M'_i|<=p_i sqrt(2log(1/p_i))<=C sqrt(L)p_i. Equation(6) therefore
gives the relative bound p_i'<=C m L p_i. Gronwall on an interval
of length c/(mL), with c sufficiently small, prevents reaching2/m.
An offset DECREASE FROM THE SAME BALANCED f0 only reduces its mass;
the absolute degree bound(7) remains valid. Thus the degree bound
holds on both short one-label offset intervals based at f0.
No relative ODE bound is asserted for an arbitrary diagram with
arbitrarily tiny initial p_i, where sqrt(log(e/p_i)) must be retained.
Exact entry/exit set identities at the balanced f0 then give

 gamma(c_i^-<=s, old cell i),
 gamma(c_j^+<=s, outside old cell j) <=C Ls,
                                      0<s<=c/(mL).          (8)

Every triple has a polynomial lower altitude. Choose its longest side
and the opposite vertex B_i; its perpendicular projection Q lies on
the segment joining the other two vertices. Equation(6) gives
B_i.(B_i-Q)>=c_sep^2/(2m^2). Since |B_i|<=C alpha, the smallest altitude
is at least

                             c/(m sqrt L).                  (9)

In particular no triple is collinear. This argument is the one in[E],
with the weak exposure tracked explicitly. Full label rank is not used.
The positive balanced endpoint masses also give, for every pair plane,

                |h_i-h_j|/ell_ij<=sqrt(2log m).              (10)

All pair distances, triple altitudes, and offsets entering the finite
normal-fiber estimates are therefore bounded by fixed powers of m.

3. Weighted plane summation with no minimum facet probability
For q0=C0 L, the incident effective weights satisfy
 sum_j mu_ij^(1-1/q0)/ell_ij
 <=(m/c_sep)m^(1/q0)(C alpha L)^(1-1/q0)
 <=C L^(3/2).                                               (11)
This is just Holder over at most m-1 incident labels and(7).

On a pair hyperplane at normal coordinate b, the standard Gaussian
surface law is phi(b) times its tangent Gaussian law. Holder therefore
gives, for any label field P_i,
 int_(facet ij) |P_i|^2 dgamma_surface
 <=mu_ij^(1-1/q0)phi(b)^(1/q0)
                     ||P_i||_(L^(2q0)(whole pair plane))^2. (12)
The factor phi(b)^(1/q0) is bounded above. Equations(11)-(12) sum
whole-plane estimates with a loss of at most C L^(3/2).

For smoothed L2 fields, the exact one-normal-coordinate OU row and
tangent hypercontractivity bound their whole-plane L^(2q0) norms by
C times the input L2 norm at time t=c0/sqrt L, with c0 sufficiently
small. Here(10) bounds b^2 by C L. Equivalently one may use the exact
offset cancellation in[T]. For products below, choose c0 smaller once
to accommodate the finitely many fixed multiples of q0 in Holder.
No intrinsic facet probability, inverse Gram matrix, or ambient
Gaussian determinant enters this estimate.

4. Exact signed energy and the reference gap
Set v=f-f0, q0=T_-r Pi_(>=2)g, qB=T_-r Pi_(>=2)f0, and
u=T_sqrt(r)Pi_(odd>=3)v. Then ||u||<=sqrt2 r^(3/2). Precisely as in[L],
Hermite orthogonality and retaining the odd square give

 S(f)-S(f0)>=r||M-B||^2+||u||^2
                         +2[r L_route+<v,q0+A>],
 A=qB-q0-T_sqrt(r)u.                                        (13)

Only the nonnegative even square has been dropped. The positive
r||M-B||^2 term will be used, not absorbed into an error.

Let i be the old affine winner. Write
c_ij=(B_i-B_j).X+h_i-h_j>=0 and delta l_i=(B_i-B0_i).X+h_i.
The exact regular Mehler gap[R] provides positive k_ij with
(T_-r g)_j-(T_-r g)_i=r k_ij(B0_i-B0_j).X. Thus

 r c_ij+q0_j-q0_i
           =r k_ij c_ij+r(1-k_ij)(delta l_i-delta l_j).       (14)

This identity applies even when i is not the regular winner. Swapping
the two labels gives the same positive ratio on the other side of a
regular face, and its divided-difference extension handles that face.

Use exactly the common damping and smooth range majorant of[L]:
 M0=[E(max Z_i)^2-1]/mu_m=O(sqrt L),
 eta=4(M0^2+1)r^2/(1-r^2),
 kappa=exp(-1-eta|P0 X|^2)<=1,
 Wrange=log(sum exp X_i)+log(sum exp(-X_i)), X_i=B0_i.X/alpha,
 e_r=C[r^2+r sqrt(L)Wrange+r^2 Wrange^2]
                     exp(C[r sqrt(L)Wrange+r^2 Wrange^2]).
Then k_ij>=kappa and |k_ij-1|<=e_r. Define

                psi_i=kappa^(-1)[e_r|delta l_i|+|A_i|/r].    (15)

For j!=i, the cost in(14) including A is bounded below by
r kappa(c_ij-psi_i-psi_j). The same-label j=i term stays exactly0;
it is excluded from the rewards. Since kappa<=1, summing against f
gives

 r L_route+<v,q0+A> >=-r Bcap(psi),
 Bcap(psi)=int max(0,max_(j!=i(X))[psi_i+psi_j-c_ij]).         (16)

5. Whole-plane forcing bounds, including all radial moments
All moment exponents in this section are fixed multiples of q0=C0 L.
They do not depend on the amplitude of f-f0 or on r. On the full
Gaussian space and on every pair plane, with normal shifts |s|<=1,

          ||kappa^(-1)||_q<=C, ||e_r/r||_q<=C L^(3/2).        (17)

The same quantities and their first two normal derivatives have fixed
polynomial m bounds when only fixed moments such as L32 are required.
To verify these statements, Wrange has L^q norm at most C(L+sqrt q):
its coordinate means on a plane are O(sqrt L), variances at most1,
and the Gaussian maximum union bound applies. Its gradient norm is
at most2 and its Hessian operator and Frobenius norms at most2.
The explicit exponent above is integrable at the chosen q since
r L^(3/2)->0 and q r^2->0 uniformly on voting r.

For the radial factor, a Gaussian on a plane has mean of norm O(sqrt L)
and covariance at most I. The auxiliary Gaussian integral gives
 E exp(b|P0Y|^2)<=(1-2b)^(-m/2)
                  exp[b|P0 E Y|^2/(1-2b)].
Apply this at b=q eta. The bound q eta m=O(L^2/m) makes it uniform.
Normal derivatives add only fixed-degree polynomials with eta or eta^2
coefficients. This proves(17) and the needed normal bounds without
conditioning on a possibly tiny facet. Restricting to actual facets
will instead use(11)-(12).

Let epsilon0=(1/2)||f0-g||_1. The global routing argument[G, Sections2-3]
uses only optimality of f0 at B and W(f0)<=w, not ||B||^2=w. Thus

                     epsilon0<=min(1,C sqrt(m)d).            (18)

Indeed its regular routing loss is at most2d^2 by the same Cauchy
argument. At t=c0/sqrt L, rare-cell hypercontractivity also gives
||T_t(f0-g)||^2<=C epsilon0/m, as in[G, Section4]. Exact factorization
therefore yields

 qB-q0=T_t v_D, ||v_D||<=C r^2 L^2 sqrt(epsilon0/m),
 T_sqrt(r)u=T_t v_u, ||v_u||<=C r^(3/2)L^(3/2)||u||.          (19)

The first identity uses v_D=T_(-r/t^2)Pi_(>=2)T_t(f0-g).
All times have absolute value at most1 for large m. This replaces the
conditioned score-path estimate in[L]; no score-rank assumption is used.

From(5), whole-plane linear norms give
 (sum_i||delta l_i||_q^2)^(1/2)<=C Ld.
Combining with(17)-(19),
 (sum_i||psi_i||_q^2)^(1/2)
 <=C[r L^(5/2)d+r L^2 sqrt(epsilon0/m)
                                     +sqrt(r)L^(3/2)||u||]. (20)
The same labelwise estimates hold on every whole pair plane, with
the right label's coefficient/input norm. Equations(11)-(12), using
fixed extra Holder factors for kappa and e_r, then give

 sum_(e=ij)ell_e^-1 int_(facet e)(psi_i^2+psi_j^2)
 <=C[r^2L^7 d^2+r^2L^6 epsilon0/m+rL^5||u||^2].              (21)

For example the reference part costs L^(3/2) from(11) times
r^2L^3 sum_i[L|delta B_i|^2+h_i^2], at most Cr^2L^(13/2)d^2.
The other terms cost L^(3/2) times ||v_D||^2/r^2 or ||v_u||^2/r^2.
The displayed integer powers safely bound these quantities.

6. A structured cap at every amplitude under weak exposure
Set
 theta=C1 L^6[r(d+sqrt(epsilon0/m))+sqrt(r)||u||].             (22)
If theta=0 the forcing is zero. Otherwise v_i^*=psi_i/theta has
bounded vector L^q norm by(20), and its summed facet squares are
bounded by(21). Since d<=2sqrt w, epsilon0<=1 and
||u||<=sqrt2r^(3/2),
 theta<=C L^(13/2)/m^(3/2)<=m^(-5/4)
for large m. We prove

                         Bcap(psi)<=C L theta^2.             (23)

For theta>=m^(-B_*) use the source/target margin split
(a+b-c)_+<=[(2a-c)_++(2b-c)_+]/2. Equation(8) and the elementary
L^q bathtub proof in[V] give
 C[(CL)^((q-1)/(q+1))m^(1/(q+1))
       theta^(2q/(q+1))+a_* (Ctheta/a_*)^q],
 a_*=c/(mL).
At the FIXED exponent q=C0 L and polynomial amplitude interval
m^(-B_*)<=theta<=m^(-5/4), this is at most C_(B_*) L theta^2.
The second term is smaller because theta/a_*<=C L m^(-1/4).

For arbitrarily smaller theta, the complete fiber proof of[S] applies
with the following explicit substitutions. Pair lengths are at least
c/m, triple altitudes at least c/(m sqrt L), and normal offsets at most
C sqrt L by(7),(9),(10). These are polynomial geometry bounds, so all
slab widths and third-label strip estimates in that proof cost only a
fixed power of m. In particular a positive reward on a good normal
fiber has width at most C m theta H, where H is its normal-sup cutoff;
off-facet projections lie in third-label strips of polynomially bounded
width. No inverse full Gram matrix or label rank is involved.

On each entire pair plane and normal shifts |s|<=1, the normalized
fields v_i^* have L32 normal-sup and normal-Lipschitz bounds at most
m^K, with fixed K. Here are the amplitude checks. For the reference
part use e_r/r, (17), and r d/theta<=C L^-6. For smoothed inputs use
 ||v_D||/(r theta)<=C r L^2 sqrt(epsilon0/m)/theta<=C,
 ||v_u||/(r theta)<=C sqrt(r)L^(3/2)||u||/theta<=C.
The one-dimensional OU row, its first two normal derivatives, and
tangent hypercontractivity control these inputs on every whole plane.
Products with kappa^(-1) are paid by(17). The absolute values in(15)
are locally Lipschitz; their first derivative is bounded by that of
the analytic underlying factor. Analytic second derivatives control
the first-derivative normal supremum. Thus no reciprocal r or vanishing
label amplitude remains. Full-space L32 bounds hold as well.

Choose H=theta^(-1/8). The good-fiber Taylor and off-facet strip errors
are at most m^K theta^3 H^3; bad projected points cost at most
m^K theta H^-31; normal distances beyond1 cost at most m^K theta^32.
These are the exact types of errors in[S] and[L], now using the above
polynomial pair and triple bounds in place of conditioning. The main
term is at most C theta^2 times the summed INDIVIDUAL facet squares
of v_i^*, as in[L]'s positive-forcing proof. Equation(21) pays it.
Hence, below a fixed polynomial threshold,
 Bcap(psi)<=C theta^2+C m^K theta^(21/8).
Choose B_* larger than that threshold exponent and8K/5. The remainder
is quadratic for every theta<=m^(-B_*). This meets the preceding
moderate regime and proves(23) for all amplitudes.

The Holder exponents and radial-moment exponents remain fixed functions
of m throughout. There is no log(1/theta) exponent and no lower
amplitude floor. Equations(22)-(23), with harmless larger integer
powers, imply

 Bcap(psi)<=C[r^2L^16(d^2+epsilon0/m)+rL^16||u||^2].         (24)

7. The global relative actual-to-affine transfer
Insert(24) into(13),(16). Since Cr^2L^16 tends to zero uniformly on
voting r, the positive ||u||^2 absorbs its error, leaving

 S(f)-S(f0)>=r||M-B||^2-Cr^3L^16(d^2+epsilon0/m).            (25)

The global covariance bootstrap[G, Section1] also applies to the

R(B) is exactly that tangent remainder. The same initial curvature
integration therefore gives
 R>=c alpha^2 e^2/m min(1,c/[L e]),
 e=||K-P||_F>=q:=||K^(1/2)-P||_F=d/alpha.
Since d>1/m, it again yields R>=c q/m^3. Also a<=sqrt w gives
q<=2sqrt(m-1). Consequently

 d^2<=C m^(3/2)L R,
 epsilon0/m<=C m^(3/2)sqrt(L)R,                              (26)

the second using(18). Thus(25) becomes

 S(f)-S(f0)>=r||M-B||^2-Cr^3m^(3/2)L^17R.                   (27)

This is relative to the exact own-score remainder. An additive
rare-cell tail was not substituted for it.
