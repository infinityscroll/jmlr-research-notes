2. RECIPROCAL TRANSFORMATION: NO ANISOTROPIC-COST SUBSTITUTION

Let A=Sigma^(1/4), symmetric positive definite. The two transformed
reference covariances are

  A I A=A^2,
  A^(-1) Sigma A^(-1)=A^2.

Thus x'=Ax and y'=A^(-1)y have the same Gaussian reference eta. Their
cross term is unchanged:

  (Ax) dot (A^(-1)y)=x dot y.

The transformed squared cost differs from the original squared cost only
by marginal integrals. These integrals are finite because exp(-q)/Z is
bounded above and below and both reference Gaussians have finite moments.
The bijection of coupling sets therefore preserves exactly the minimizers.

There is also a direct gradient verification. If U=grad psi sends eta to
nu'=A^(-1)#nu, then

  T(x)=A U(Ax)=grad[psi(Ax)],
  DT(x)=A DU(Ax) A.

This sends standard gamma to nu and is a gradient of a convex potential.
Brenier uniqueness identifies it as the intended optimizer. The two A
factors form a congruence, not an unsupported chain of independently
optimal maps. An arbitrary linear postcomposition BT is not asserted to
be a Brenier map anywhere in the argument.

3. UPPER AND LOWER HESSIAN CONSTANTS

The transformed reference potential has Hessian A^(-2)=Sigma^(-1/2).
With K_q=sup ||D^2q||op, the transformed perturbation q'(z)=q(Az) obeys

  D^2q'(z)=A D^2q(Az) A >= -||A||op^2 K_q I.

If supp q is compactly contained in B_R, then supp q' is compactly
contained in B_(||A^(-1)||op R+1). The extra 1 is harmless. The reference
normalization is correct, and c_(q')=-log Z has the required sign.
CFJ1.1 therefore yields Lip(U)<=C_0 and

  Lip(T)<=||A||op^2 C_0=:L.

For the reverse transport nu->gamma, additive normalization constants
do not affect Hessians. Its source Hessian is

  Sigma^(-1)+D^2q <= [lambda_max(Sigma^(-1))+K_q] I,

and its target Hessian is I. CFJ3.2 consequently yields

  Lip(S)<=K=[lambda_max(Sigma^(-1))+K_q]^(1/2).

No lower bound or convexity of the reverse source potential is required.
This is the decisive reason the reverse application survives a possibly
nonconvex compact perturbation q.

Reversal of the optimal coupling makes S and T inverses almost everywhere.
They have continuous global Lipschitz representatives, and their measures
have full support. The inverse identities extend from dense full-measure
sets to all points. Thus

  |T(x)-T(z)|>=K^(-1)|x-z|.

At differentiability points, DT is symmetric PSD and all singular values
are >=K^(-1); hence all eigenvalues have that lower bound. The claimed
constant

  m=[lambda_max(Sigma^(-1))+K_q]^(-1/2)

is correct. Forward Lipschitz regularity excludes a singular Hessian part,
so the two Hessian inequalities also hold distributionally.

As an analytic normalization check, when q=0 one has U=Id and
T(x)=Sigma^(1/2)x. The proposed reverse bound becomes exactly
m=sqrt(lambda_min(Sigma)); the forward congruence gives the corresponding
sqrt(lambda_max(Sigma)) bound when C_0=1. No exponent or inverse is lost.

4. EVERYWHERE SMOOTHNESS AND ALL-BASE CLOSURE

Both original densities are smooth and strictly positive everywhere.
Each is bounded above and away from zero on every compact set; no global
positive lower density bound is needed. The full-space extension following
CFJ2.2 therefore gives phi in C^infinity_loc(R^d). This means smooth at
every finite point, not bounded higher derivatives on all of R^d.

Continuity of D^2phi upgrades its almost-everywhere bounds to every point.
The already established global inverse and the inverse function theorem
then give a smooth global diffeomorphism. There is no circular use of
diffeomorphism regularity to prove the inverse identities.

For an invertible B, direct density transformation gives

  B#nu=Z^(-1) exp[-q(B^(-1)z)] g_(B Sigma B^T)(z) dz.

The covariance remains positive definite and the perturbation remains
smooth and compactly supported. Applying the proposition anew at each
target proves all-base closure. The constants may deteriorate with B,
dimension, or the approximation index. No part of the pointwise Peano
or subsequent touching argument demands uniformity of those constants.
The note correctly makes no assertion of quadratic transport tails.

Accordingly this replacement removes the specified 2026 regularity
dependency for this density class and its invertible linear images.
It does not extend the classical theorem to every globally Lipschitz
perturbation, which is unnecessary here.

5. RANK-ONE HESSIAN-MEASURE NOTE: THE MAIN ATTACK

The dangerous potential error would be treating j_M(H), concave in M,
as a generally lower-semicontinuous integrand under weak matrix-measure
convergence. The submitted argument explicitly avoids that assertion.

The skew minimization formula is correct: in an eigenbasis of M, minimizing
lambda_i(H_ij+K_ij)^2+lambda_j(H_ij-K_ij)^2 gives coefficient
4lambda_i lambda_j/(lambda_i+lambda_j), with K skew. The displayed
minimizer satisfies ||K||F<=||H||F, including zero eigenvalues. Substitution
into the two minimizing problems gives the stated nuclear-norm continuity
bound with constant 4.

For P=nn^T, tau=tr M, e=tr[(I-P)M], the block decomposition yields

  ||M-tau P||_* <= 2e+2sqrt[(tau-e)e] <=4sqrt(tau e).

Since j_(tau P)(H)=tau(n^THn)^2, the constant 16 in the resulting
rank-one comparison follows. Integrating and using Cauchy--Schwarz gives
the stated weighted estimate. All quantities discarded in the global
liminf proof are nonnegative.

On an interior facet patch, local weak convergence of PSD Hessian measures
forces the tangential trace mass to zero, while total trace mass converges.
The comparison estimate therefore gives actual local convergence of the
j-integral, including a varying constant matrix H_k->H. This is stronger
than the needed local lower limit and requires no uniform ellipticity.

Strong L2(gamma) convergence of transport maps follows from W2 target
convergence, uniqueness of the Gaussian-source optimizer, convergence in
probability, and uniform integrability. Local distributional derivative
convergence upgrades to vague Hessian-measure convergence because PSD
trace mass controls every matrix component. Compact facet patches can be
chosen with disjoint neighborhoods; finite Gaussian surface measure and
nonnegativity justify exhaustion without a global-tail limit.

The facet jump coefficient is |a'|nn^T, not |a'|^2nn^T. With
a'=C_0^(1/2)a and H_0=C_0^(-1/2)Delta C_0^(-1/2),

  n^T H_0 n=(a^TDelta a)/(a^TC_0a).

Writing w=area_gamma/|a'| then gives exactly
w(a^TDelta a)^2/(a^TC_0a). There is no missing factor of two because each
unoriented facet is listed once. The intrinsic e_i sites should be read
as their projections onto 1-perp, or equivalently as coordinate functionals
restricted to that space; their common component does not affect any edge.

6. LOWER-TOUCHING NOTE: SIGNS, SMOOTHNESS, AND MOVING BASES

The claimed C^2 regularity is restricted to a full affinely independent
simplex with positive masses. This restriction matters. Every offset
vector modulo constants corresponds to a unique translation of that fan.
The dual objective is the Gaussian convolution of its support function,
plus the barycenter linear term, and is smooth jointly in translation
and positive definite covariance. Positive masses put the barycenter in
the simplex interior, making the objective coercive. All pair facets have
positive Gaussian area; the offset Laplacian is positive on constants-
orthogonal vectors. The implicit function theorem therefore does give
smooth local minimizing offsets and a C^2 value. No assertion about
arbitrary affinely dependent site families is needed.

For f_n->f uniformly and q=f-eta t^4, interior minimizers t_n of f_n-q
converge to zero. Differentiability gives equal first derivatives there.
The minimum inequality gives the LOWER bound q''(t_n)/2 on the second
quotient of f_n. Combining it with the smooth UPPER bound -J_n/8 yields

  J_n<=-4q''(t_n).

Thus the measure liminf has the needed sign and exact factor 4. The
limits are first in the increment for each n, then in n; a uniform Peano
remainder or convergence of second derivatives is not used.

The moving-base identity follows from reciprocal linear transformations
of the two marginals, preserving their dot product. It changes the
target law; it does not compose an old Brenier map with a covariance
square root. W2 stability and continuity of the positive square root
give the required new target convergence.

The auxiliary global trace estimate

  integral tr(D^2phi_n) dgamma=E[X dot T_n(X)]
     <=sqrt(d)||T_n||L2

is valid by Gaussian integration by parts and radial cutoffs. Positivity
of the trace and L2 moment control justify the limit even without a
uniform Hessian bound. It controls the varying direction matrix error.
The rank-one lemma already handles varying matrices locally, so this
global estimate is a valid additional route rather than a hidden need.

Finally, projection onto the affine span is exact: one marginal Gaussian
can be lifted conditionally from its projection while preserving any
coupling with the finite target. Only the intrinsic direction Q^THQ can
have strict curvature. The audit correctly excludes ambient-null directions.

These checks support the assembled finite-simplex normal-strain transfer.
They leave the separate smooth Hodge/Peano hypothesis explicit.
