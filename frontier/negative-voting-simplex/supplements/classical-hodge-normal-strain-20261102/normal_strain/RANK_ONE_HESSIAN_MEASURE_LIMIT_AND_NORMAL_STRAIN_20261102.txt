RANK-ONE HESSIAN-MEASURE LIMIT AND THE NORMAL-STRAIN COEFFICIENT
Date: 2026-11-02
Status: PROVED local-to-global liminf and coefficient identification.
Scope: the measure-theoretic passage from smooth Brenier Hessians to the
normal strain of a finite polyhedral transport. Transfer of the resulting
curvature bound through viscosity/barrier arguments is handled separately.

TARGET

The smooth Hodge route supplies the curvature quantity

  j_M(H) = sum_i lambda_i H_ii^2
           + sum_{i<j} [4 lambda_i lambda_j/(lambda_i+lambda_j)] H_ij^2,

in an orthonormal eigenbasis of the positive matrix M. A zero-over-zero
coefficient is defined as zero. H is symmetric.

We prove that when smooth convex Brenier Hessians converge as measures
to the Hessian of a finite polyhedral potential, the Gaussian integral
of j has the required lower limit. The proof exploits rank-one facet
measures. It does NOT claim weak lower semicontinuity of j for arbitrary
positive matrix-valued measures; indeed j is concave in M.

1. A MATRIX FORMULA AND TWO EXPLICIT ESTIMATES

For every symmetric positive semidefinite M,

  j_M(H) = min_{K^T=-K} tr[(H+K)^T M(H+K)].                 (1.1)

To verify this, diagonalize M. The diagonal summands are lambda_i H_ii^2.
For each i<j, minimize

  lambda_i(H_ij+K_ij)^2 + lambda_j(H_ij-K_ij)^2.

If lambda_i+lambda_j>0, a minimizer is

  K_ij=(lambda_j-lambda_i)/(lambda_i+lambda_j) H_ij.

If both eigenvalues vanish, choose K_ij=0. The minimum is the displayed
harmonic coefficient in j, proving (1.1). This choice obeys

  ||K||_F <= ||H||_F.                                      (1.2)

It follows that for two PSD matrices M,N,

  |j_M(H)-j_N(H)|
    <= 4 ||H||_F^2 ||M-N||_*,                              (1.3)

where ||.||_* is the trace/nuclear norm. For example, substitute an
N-minimizer into the M-functional in (1.1), and bound its linear difference
by ||M-N||_* ||(H+K)(H+K)^T||_op; (1.2) bounds the second factor by
4||H||_F^2. Reverse M and N for the other inequality. This also proves
continuity at singular PSD matrices without making eigenbasis choices.

For fixed M, j_M is a nonnegative quadratic form in H, and

  0 <= j_M(H) <= tr(M H^2) <= (tr M)||H||_op^2.             (1.4)

The middle inequality follows either by choosing K=0 in (1.1), or by
4ab/(a+b)<=a+b in the eigenbasis. Quadratic-form Cauchy--Schwarz yields

  |j_M(H)-j_M(G)|
    <= (tr M)(||H||_op+||G||_op)||H-G||_op.                (1.5)

Fix a unit vector n, put P=nn^T, Q=I-P, tau=tr M, and e=tr(QM).
In the splitting span(n) plus n-perp, write

  M = [a  b^T; b  B],       a>=0, B>=0, tr B=e, a+e=tau.

Positivity gives |b|^2<=a e: each squared component b_i^2 is at most
a B_ii, and summation proves the claim. The block decomposition of
M-tau P therefore gives

  ||M-tau P||_* <= 2e+2|b| <= 4 sqrt(tau e).               (1.6)

This includes a=0 or tau=0. Since j_{tau P}(H)=tau(n^T Hn)^2, (1.3)
and (1.6) imply the pointwise estimate

  |j_M(H)-tau(n^T Hn)^2|
    <= 16 ||H||_F^2 sqrt(tau e).                           (1.7)

Consequently, for any nonnegative weight f,

  |integral f j_M(H) - (n^T Hn)^2 integral f tau|
    <= 16 ||H||_F^2
       sqrt[(integral f tau)(integral f e)].              (1.8)

Only Cauchy--Schwarz is used to pass from (1.7) to (1.8).

2. HESSIAN-MEASURE CONVERGENCE FROM TRANSPORT CONVERGENCE

Let T_k=grad phi_k be smooth gradients of convex functions on R^d, and
suppose T_k -> T=grad phi in L^2(gamma). Let M_k=D^2 phi_k and
mu=D^2 phi, with the last Hessian interpreted as a PSD matrix-valued
Radon measure. Then

  M_k dx -> mu vaguely on compact sets.                    (2.1)

Here is a direct justification. On every compact set the Gaussian density
has a positive lower bound, so L^2(gamma) convergence implies local L^1
convergence of T_k. Thus its distributional derivatives converge:

  integral zeta (M_k)_ij dx
    = -integral (T_k)_i partial_j zeta dx
    -> -integral T_i partial_j zeta dx
    = integral zeta d mu_ij

for smooth compactly supported zeta. For a compact K choose a smooth
nonnegative cutoff zeta equal to one on K. Positivity and the trace identity

  integral zeta tr M_k dx = -integral grad zeta dot T_k dx

give a uniform bound on trace mass over K. Every matrix component has
total variation controlled locally by trace mass (by positivity). Smooth
approximation of continuous test functions therefore upgrades distributional
convergence to (2.1). No additional normalization or convergence theorem
for the potentials is required.

For completeness, the L^2 premise holds in the intended approximation.
If eta_k -> eta in W2 and T_k,T are their Brenier maps from gamma, optimal
couplings (Id,T_k)#gamma have only the unique coupling (Id,T)#gamma as a
weak subsequential limit: tightness, continuity of the W2 optimal value,
and lower semicontinuity of cost show that every limit is optimal.
Concentration on this unique graph gives T_k -> T in gamma-probability.
For a polyhedral T this last step also follows immediately from the
continuous mapping theorem away from its finite union of facet hyperplanes,
a gamma-null set. W2 convergence makes |T_k|^2 uniformly integrable, so
Vitali's argument upgrades convergence in probability to L^2(gamma).

If eta_k=C_k^{1/2}#nu_k, C_k -> C_0>0, and nu_k -> nu in W2, then
eta_k -> C_0^{1/2}#nu in W2. Indeed, use any couplings with
E|Y_k-Y|^2 -> 0 and estimate

  ||C_k^{1/2}Y_k-C_0^{1/2}Y||_2
    <= ||C_k^{1/2}||_op ||Y_k-Y||_2
       + ||C_k^{1/2}-C_0^{1/2}||_op ||Y||_2.

Thus (2.1) applies at varying positive definite covariance base points.

3. LOCAL CONVERGENCE ON AN INTERIOR FACET PATCH

Suppose that on an open set U the limiting Hessian measure is

  mu|U = a nn^T H^{d-1}|(F intersect U),

where a>0 and n is a fixed unit normal. There are no other facets in U.
Let f>=0 be continuous with compact support in U, and H_k -> H in the
space of symmetric matrices. Define

  A_k=integral f tr M_k dx,
  E_k=integral f tr[(I-nn^T)M_k] dx.

By (2.1),

  A_k -> a integral_F f dH^{d-1} =: A,
  E_k -> 0.                                               (3.1)

Applying (1.8), with ||H_k||_F bounded, shows

  integral f j_{M_k}(H_k) dx
    - (n^T H_k n)^2 A_k -> 0.

Hence the following is an actual local limit, not just a liminf:

  integral f j_{M_k}(H_k) dx
    -> a(n^T Hn)^2 integral_F f dH^{d-1}.                 (3.2)

This is the precise use of extremality of a rank-one PSD matrix. The
tangential mass tends to zero, forcing the normalized approximating
matrices into that rank-one direction in the quantitative sense (1.8).

4. GLOBAL GAUSSIAN LIMINF FOR A POLYHEDRAL POTENTIAL

For the application, let nu=sum_i p_i delta_{y_i} have affinely independent
sites y_1,...,y_{d+1} and strictly positive masses p_i. The standard-Gaussian
Brenier potential for any invertible linear image of these sites has the
form

  phi(x)=max_i [y_i' dot x+b_i].

Let F_e denote each codimension-one interface between adjacent cells,
listed once. Its jump vector is a_e'=y_i'-y_j', and choose

  n_e=a_e'/|a_e'|.

The distributional derivative of the piecewise constant gradient is

  D^2 phi = sum_e |a_e'| n_e n_e^T H^{d-1}|F_e.             (4.1)

To see the coefficient, the gradient jumps by a_e' in its normal direction;
integration by parts in the two polyhedral cells gives a_e' tensor n_e.
This equals |a_e'|n_e n_e^T. Cell interiors contribute zero. The lower
dimensional intersection skeleton has H^{d-1} measure zero and carries
no separate distributional Hessian mass. In dimension one (4.1) means
the usual atomic derivative at jump points.

Let g be the standard Gaussian density. Under (2.1) and H_k -> H,

  liminf_k integral g(x) j_{M_k(x)}(H_k) dx
    >= sum_e |a_e'|(n_e^T H n_e)^2
           integral_{F_e} g dH^{d-1}.                    (4.2)

Proof. For each facet choose a compact subset K_e of its relative interior,
away from the intersection skeleton. There are finitely many facets, so
the K_e have pairwise disjoint open neighborhoods U_e, each meeting only
its own facet. Choose smooth cutoffs 0<=chi_e<=1, supported in U_e and
equal to one on K_e. Then sum_e chi_e<=1. Positivity (1.4) gives

  integral g j_{M_k}(H_k) >= sum_e integral chi_e g j_{M_k}(H_k).

Each term converges by (3.2), with f=chi_e g. The resulting lower bound
is at least the right side of (4.2) with F_e replaced by K_e. Exhaust
each facet's relative interior by such compact subsets. Gaussian surface
measure is finite on each facet and gives zero mass to its boundary, so
inner regularity/monotone exhaustion proves (4.2).

The argument neither assumes nor needs a uniform global Hessian bound
in k, tightness of the full unweighted Hessian mass, or convergence of
the Gaussian integral outside the compact patches. Its discarded terms
are nonnegative. It is therefore applicable when the approximation's
ellipticity constants deteriorate.

5. IDENTIFICATION WITH THE NORMAL-STRAIN EXPRESSION AT C_0

Take original sites y_i, a covariance C_0>0, and a covariance direction
Delta=Delta^T. The transformed target sites and normalized direction are

  y_i'=C_0^{1/2}y_i,
  a_e=y_i-y_j,
  a_e'=C_0^{1/2}a_e,
  H_0=C_0^{-1/2}Delta C_0^{-1/2}.

Writing d_e=a_e^T C_0 a_e=|a_e'|^2, one has

  n_e^T H_0 n_e = (a_e^T Delta a_e)/d_e.

Let s_e=integral_{F_e} g dH^{d-1} be the physical Gaussian facet area,
and define the usual coarea weight

  w_e=s_e/|a_e'|.

Then each summand in (4.2) is exactly

  s_e |a_e'|(n_e^T H_0 n_e)^2
    = w_e (a_e^T Delta a_e)^2/(a_e^T C_0 a_e).              (5.1)

More generally, if C_k -> C_0>0 and Delta_k -> Delta, set
H_k=C_k^{-1/2}Delta_k C_k^{-1/2}; then H_k -> H_0 and the varying-base
argument in Section 2 proves

  liminf_k E_gamma j_{D^2 phi_k}(H_k)
    >= sum_e w_e (a_e^T Delta a_e)^2/(a_e^T C_0 a_e).      (5.2)

Here phi_k transports gamma to C_k^{1/2}#nu_k for any smooth approximants
nu_k -> nu in W2 for which the smooth Hodge curvature estimate is valid.
Such approximants can be taken from the compact log-Gaussian density class
already treated by the classical regularity replacement note.

For y_i=P e_i on the intrinsic space 1-perp, where P=I-11^T/m, and
p_i=1/m, put C_0=K and Delta=H. These sites are the score labels e_i
modulo their common constant direction, and their differences remain
a_e=e_i-e_j. Formula (5.2) supplies exactly the coefficient on the right of
equation (11) in

  work/field_breakthrough_targets_20261030/stature_reset/parallel_attacks/
  stochastic/full_correlation_bridge_20261031/
  BALANCED_FACET_DENSITY_BOOTSTRAP_AND_FINITE_COVARIANCE_GAP_20261031.txt:

  sum_e w_e(K,h_K) (a_e^T H a_e)^2/(a_e^T K a_e).

The equality of the physical coarea weights with the fixed-score-coordinate
weights is the ordinary Gaussian linear-change/coarea identity used there.
All spaces and matrices in this identification are restricted to 1-perp;
K is positive definite on that intrinsic space.

DEPENDENCY BOUNDARY

Combining (5.2) with a separately justified transfer of the smooth curvature
inequality -4F_k'' >= E j to the finite-target limit recovers the desired
normal-strain curvature inequality. This note supplies the lower-limit
step and its exact coefficients. It does not infer convergence of second
derivatives, and it makes no unproved C^2 assertion for a finite-target
value function. Those points belong to the separate viscosity/barrier
transfer. No old finite-target normal-strain theorem is used in the proof
of (1.1)--(5.2).
