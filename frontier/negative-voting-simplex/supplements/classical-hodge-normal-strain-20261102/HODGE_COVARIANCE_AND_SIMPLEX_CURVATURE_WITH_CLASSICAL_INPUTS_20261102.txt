GAUSSIAN COVARIANCE CONCAVITY AND SIMPLEX CURVATURE VIA HODGE THEORY
2 November 2026

This successor removes the recent-preprint regularity dependency from the
independent Hodge proof route. It also recovers the finite-simplex
normal-strain inequality required by the quantitative negative-voting
argument. The frozen original chain-complex proof is not an input here.
This is an internally audited analytic proof package, not external review
or a claim that the full named conjecture has been resolved.

THEOREM 1: EVERY TARGET WITH FINITE SECOND MOMENT

Let nu be a probability law on R^d with finite second moment, and set
  F_nu(C)=sup E[X.Y],  X~N(0,C), Y~nu.
Then F_nu is concave on the ordinary positive-semidefinite covariance cone.
Consequently W2(N(0,C),nu)^2 is convex in C, since it equals
  tr C + integral |y|^2 dnu(y) - 2F_nu(C).
The covariance path here is an affine path in matrices, not a Bures
geodesic. No log-concavity, finite support, or balance is assumed on nu.

THEOREM 2: THE QUANTITATIVE FINITE-SIMPLEX INPUT

Let nu=sum_(i=1)^(d+1) p_i delta_(y_i), with affinely independent sites
y_i and positive masses p_i. At C>0 the Brenier map from standard gamma_d
to C^(1/2)#nu is the gradient of
  phi_C(x)=max_i[(C^(1/2)y_i).x+h_i].
Let F_ij(C) be its physical i,j interface, s_ij its Gaussian surface area,
a_ij=y_i-y_j, and
  w_ij(C)=s_ij/|C^(1/2)a_ij|.
The optimized finite-target value F_nu is smooth at C>0, with derivative
  D F_nu(C)[H]=(1/2)sum_(i<j) w_ij(C) a_ij^T H a_ij,
and satisfies

  -4 D^2 F_nu(C)[H,H]
    >= sum_(i<j) w_ij(C) (a_ij^T H a_ij)^2/(a_ij^T C a_ij)       (1)

for every symmetric H. Every pair has positive facet weight. Simplex
edge dyads span the symmetric matrices, so the Hessian is strictly
negative in every nonzero symmetric direction. An affinely independent
simplex of r+1 sites embedded in a larger ambient space is handled on its
r-dimensional affine span; strictness is asserted only for nonzero
compressed directions. No extension to an arbitrary affinely dependent
family of sites is asserted here.

For y_i=P e_i on 1-perp, P=I-11^T/m, and p_i=1/m, equation (1) is exactly
the normal-strain input in equation (11) of the earlier finite covariance
gap proof. Thus the new route supplies BOTH its concavity input and its
quantitative curvature input. Claiming only concavity would not have
replaced the latter dependency.

PROOF MODULE A: THE WEAK GAUSSIAN HODGE ESTIMATE

Let M=D^2 phi be smooth and mI<=M<=LI globally. For every weak L2
Gaussian-solenoidal v there is a skew A with delta_gamma A=v and
  J(A)=E tr(A^T M A)<=E v^T Mv.                              (2)
The complete proof is in
  weighted_partition_attack_20261102/primary/higher_dimension/
  HESSIAN_HODGE_LOCAL_STABILITY_20261102.txt, Sections 2--4.

Briefly, the exact integration-by-parts remainder separates into
nonnegative repeated-index squares and three-index forms
  q=-2(beta ab+gamma bc+alpha ca).
The corresponding weighted exterior derivative is
  k=(alpha+beta)a+(beta+gamma)b+(gamma+alpha)c.
The matrix identity q+k^2/[2(alpha+beta+gamma)]>=0 gives an augmented
Hodge estimate. Minimize J over delta_gamma A=v in its equivalent L2
Hilbert norm. Feasibility follows from the Gaussian OU resolvent;
variations by codifferentials of 3-forms give d(MA+AM)=0. Compact cutoffs
and Friedrichs graph approximation pass the augmented estimate to this
weak minimizer, proving (2). Only Hessian symmetry, not an unrelated
matrix-weight inequality, cancels the derivative terms.

PROOF MODULE B: LOCAL COVARIANCE CURVATURE WITHOUT MAP DIFFERENTIATION

For symmetric H, (2) implies
  |E(H grad phi).v|^2 <= c_* E v^T Mv,
  c_*=E sum_(i<j) [(lambda_i-lambda_j)^2/(lambda_i+lambda_j)] H_ij^2.
At each point the displayed matrix entries are taken in an eigenbasis
of M only for algebra; no eigenvector field is differentiated.
Put
  j_M(H)=sum_i lambda_i H_ii^2
          +sum_(i<j) [4lambda_i lambda_j/(lambda_i+lambda_j)] H_ij^2.
Then E j_M(H)=E tr(H^2M)-c_*.

For nu=(grad phi)#gamma and Q_t=sqrt(I+tH), the exact coupling identity is
  F_nu(I+tH)=E grad phi(G).Q_t G
     +sup_(G,S both gamma){E[(Q_t-I)grad phi(G)].(S-G)-E D_phi(S,G)}.
Strong convexity forces optimizing displacements O(t) in L2. Their
conditional velocities have weak Gaussian-solenoidal limits. Fenchel
duality, with the uniform inverse-Hessian bound, gives the energy liminf
E v^T Mv/2. This avoids any unjustified uniform Taylor remainder in random
displacements. The resulting local upper Peano bound is
  limsup_(t->0) [F_nu(I+tH)-F_nu(I)-(t/2)tr(H E M)]/t^2
     <= -(1/8)E j_M(H).                                    (3)
See the complete derivation in
  weighted_partition_attack_20261102/proof/
  HODGE_TO_COVARIANCE_LOCAL_TRANSFER_20261102.txt, Sections 1--5.

PROOF MODULE C: ESTABLISHED REGULARITY, NOT THE 2026 PREPRINT

For nu=Z^-1 exp(-q)g_Sigma, Sigma>0 and q smooth compactly supported,
set A=Sigma^(1/4) and change coordinates reciprocally:
  x'=Ax, y'=A^-1y.
Both reference marginals become N(0,Sigma^(1/2)); x'.y'=x.y, so
quadratic transport costs differ only by fixed marginal terms. The
transformed optimizer therefore falls literally under CFJ2017 Theorem
1.1, with one identical Gaussian reference. Returning to original
coordinates preserves the convex-gradient property by congruence.
The reverse classical Caffarelli estimate gives a positive lower
Hessian bound; local smoothness follows from the cited full-space
regularity statement. The complete reduction is in
  regularity/CLASSICAL_CFJ_BRENIER_REGULARITY_REPLACEMENT_20261102.txt.
The established source is Colombo--Figalli--Jhaveri (2017):
  https://doi.org/10.2422/2036-2145.201510_009
  https://people.math.ethz.ch/~afigalli/papers-pdf/Lipschitz-changes-of-variables-between-perturbations-of-log-concave-measures.pdf
No theorem from arXiv:2609.04052 is used in this successor.

PROOF MODULE D: THEOREM 1 BY DENSITY AND AN ELEMENTARY BARRIER

The density class in Module C is closed under every invertible linear
target transformation. At each C0>0 use
  F_nu(C0^(1/2) D C0^(1/2))=F_((C0^(1/2))#nu)(D)
and apply (3). On every covariance segment, the pointwise upper second
quotient is nonpositive. If concavity failed, subtract a violated chord
and a small positive quadratic. An interior minimum would give a
positive lower second quotient, contradicting (3). No uniformity of
ellipticity constants across base points is needed.

Every P2 law is the W2 limit of densities
  rho_n=(1-epsilon_n)f_n+epsilon_n gamma_density,
where f_n are smooth compact probability densities and epsilon_n->0.
Indeed rho_n=Z_n^-1 exp(-q_n)gamma_density with
  q_n=-log[1+((1-epsilon_n)/epsilon_n) f_n/gamma_density]
smooth and compactly supported. The inequality
  |F_nu(C)-F_eta(C)|<=sqrt(tr C)W2(nu,eta)
passes concavity to arbitrary targets. Source-Gaussian W2 continuity
then includes singular covariance matrices. This proves Theorem 1.
The full approximation and scalar argument are in Sections 8--9 of
the transfer note; its former Section 7 is replaced by Module C here.

PROOF MODULE E: RANK-ONE FACET MEASURES RETAIN NORMAL STRAIN

The matrix quantity has the useful representation
  j_M(H)=min_(K skew) tr[(H+K)^T M(H+K)]
for every PSD M, with zero-over-zero terms defined as zero. A minimizer
has ||K||_F<=||H||_F. Consequently j is continuous even at singular M.
For a fixed unit normal n, let tau=tr M and e=tr[(I-nn^T)M]. Then
  |j_M(H)-tau(n^T Hn)^2|<=16||H||_F^2 sqrt(tau e).          (4)

Smooth Brenier maps to W2-approximating targets converge in L2(gamma).
Their Hessians converge as local PSD matrix measures. On the interior
of a polyhedral interface, the limit measure is
  |C0^(1/2)a_ij| n_ij n_ij^T H^(d-1)|F_ij.
Tangential trace mass on a compact facet patch tends to zero. Applying
(4) and Cauchy--Schwarz gives the actual local limit of j; disjoint
patch exhaustion and positivity give the global Gaussian liminf.
This is a rank-one argument, not a false general weak-lower-semicontinuity
claim for the concave function M -> j_M(H).

The proof, including varying covariance bases, is in
  normal_strain/RANK_ONE_HESSIAN_MEASURE_LIMIT_AND_NORMAL_STRAIN_20261102.txt.
The resulting lower bound is exactly the right side of (1), because
  |C0^(1/2)a| [n^T C0^-1/2 H C0^-1/2 n]^2
    = (1/|C0^(1/2)a|) (a^T H a)^2/(a^T C0 a).

PROOF MODULE F: PASSING CURVATURE TO THE SIMPLEX VALUE

For affinely independent sites, varying all height offsets merely
translates a fixed collection of simplex normal cones. Integrating
their Gaussian densities gives smooth masses and values. The offset
Laplacian is positive on the height gauge, so the implicit-function
theorem makes the optimized finite-target value smooth at C>0.
This smoothness assertion is made at simplex scope; no assertion about
combinatorially degenerate arbitrary sites is required.

The stated first derivative is the Gaussian heat identity followed by
the envelope theorem. For fixed heights, differentiating the Gaussian
covariance pairs H/2 with the distributional Hessian of the max-affine
potential. On an i,j facet this Hessian is a_ij a_ij^T/|a_ij| times
surface measure. Whitening converts its coefficient to
s_ij/|C^(1/2)a_ij|=w_ij. The height derivative drops out at the balancing
optimum. In score coordinates this is exactly the offset Laplacian/2.

Write f(t)=F_nu(C0+tH), and approximate nu by the densities in Module D.
Uniform W2 continuity gives f_n->f locally uniformly. Fix eta>0 and let
  q(t)=f(t)-eta t^4.
Minima t_n of f_n-q on a small closed interval are interior and tend to
zero. At each minimum, first derivatives agree and
  q''(t_n)/2 <= limsup_(h->0)
     [f_n(t_n+h)-f_n(t_n)-h f_n'(t_n)]/h^2
     <= -J_n/8,
where J_n is the smooth Hodge curvature integral at the ACTUAL base
C_n=C0+t_nH. Module E supplies its liminf. Passing to the limit gives
  -4f''(0)>=liminf J_n>=the right side of (1).
This proves Theorem 2, without convergence of second derivatives or
differentiation of Brenier maps. The separate audit is
  normal_strain_audit/LOWER_TOUCHING_AND_MOVING_BASE_NORMAL_STRAIN_AUDIT_20261102.txt.
