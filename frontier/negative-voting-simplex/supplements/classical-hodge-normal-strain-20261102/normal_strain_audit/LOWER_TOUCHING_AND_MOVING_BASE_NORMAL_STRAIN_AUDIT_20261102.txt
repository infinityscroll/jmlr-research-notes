LOWER-TOUCHING TRANSFER OF HODGE CURVATURE TO A FINITE SIMPLEX
Date: 2 November 2026
Scope: analytic audit of the proposed transfer, conditional on the smooth
Hodge/Peano estimate and the separately supplied rank-one measure lemma.
No global weighted-perimeter inequality (N), chain factorization,
or differentiation of Brenier maps is used.

VERDICT

PASS at the required finite-simplex scope. The lower-touching sign is
correct, no uniform-in-approximation Peano remainder is needed, moving
covariance bases are harmless after an exact congruence transformation,
and a fixed-direction rank-one measure lemma suffices. The constants give
exactly the previous normal-strain bound, including its factor 4.

This audit does not prove the separate rank-one measure lemma or the
all-base smooth-target regularity input. With those inputs established,
it removes the chain-complex proof from this particular curvature step.
It does not by itself certify the whole negative-voting theorem.

1. PRECISE HYPOTHESES

Let nu=sum_(i=1)^m p_i delta_(y_i), all p_i>0, sum p_i=1.
Initially take m=d+1 affinely independent sites in R^d.
Put
  F_nu(C)=sup E[X dot Y], X~N(0,C), Y~nu.
Fix C0>0 and a symmetric H. Choose b>0 so C(t)=C0+tH>0
for |t|<=b.

Let nu_n be smooth positive target laws with nu_n->nu in W2 and
finite second moments. Require, for EVERY base C on this segment,
the applicability of the already proved smooth-target estimate to the
actual Brenier map from standard gamma_d to (C^(1/2))#nu_n.
It is enough that each such map has smooth positive Hessian with
individual finite upper/lower bounds. Those bounds need NOT be uniform
in n or C for the touching argument.

Write
  K_C=C^(-1/2) H C^(-1/2),
  M_n,C=D^2 phi_n,C,
  J_n(C,H)=integral j_(M_n,C)(K_C) dgamma_d,
where, in an eigenbasis of a PSD matrix M with eigenvalues lambda_i,
  j_M(K)=sum_i lambda_i K_ii^2
       +sum_(i<j) 4 lambda_i lambda_j/(lambda_i+lambda_j) K_ij^2.
The 0/0 coefficient is zero.

The smooth input is differentiability and, at every such C,
  limsup_(s->0) [F_nu_n(C+sH)-F_nu_n(C)
                 -s DF_nu_n(C)[H]]/s^2 <= -J_n(C,H)/8.       (P)

This is the exact hypothesis, not a smoothness assertion about a family
of Brenier maps as C varies. The all-base regularity dependency in the
existing Hodge note remains explicit.

2. UNIFORM CONVERGENCE OF THE VALUES

Gluing a target W2 coupling onto a covariance-maximizing coupling and
applying Cauchy--Schwarz gives, in both directions,
  |F_nu_n(C)-F_nu(C)| <= sqrt(tr C) W2(nu_n,nu).             (2.1)
Therefore f_n(t)=F_nu_n(C(t)) converges uniformly to
f(t)=F_nu(C(t)) on [-b,b]. Positive definiteness is not needed for (2.1);
compactness of the covariance set supplies the uniform trace bound.

No rate of W2 convergence and no second-derivative convergence is assumed.

3. WHY f IS C^2 AT THE REQUIRED SIMPLEX TARGET

For a full affinely independent simplex, offsets h modulo constants
correspond bijectively to translations a through h_i=c-y_i dot a.
The dual objective becomes
  Phi(C,a)=E sigma_Y(X-a)+(sum_i p_i y_i) dot a,
  sigma_Y(x)=max_i y_i dot x, X~N(0,C).
It is smooth jointly for C>0 and a: move the translation to the Gaussian
density and differentiate that density, integrating against the fixed
support function sigma_Y, which has linear growth. All derivatives
needed locally are dominated by a Gaussian times a polynomial.

Positive masses make the offset minimizer finite. Its offset Hessian
is the weighted adjacency Laplacian with weights
  integral_(facet ij) gamma_C dH^(d-1) / |y_i-y_j|.
For a simplex every pair facet is nonempty of dimension d-1; its Gaussian
area is positive. The Laplacian is positive on the constant-orthogonal
offset space. The implicit function theorem therefore supplies smooth
optimal offsets and smooth F(C) locally. In particular f is C^2.

This argument uses the translated fixed simplex fan. It does not silently
assert C^2 for every affinely dependent finite target through a combinatorial
transition. Such an extension is unnecessary for the dependency under audit.

4. TOUCH FROM BELOW: SIGNS AND CONSTANTS

Fix any eta>0 and set
  q(t)=f(t)-eta t^4.
This is a valid C^2 test function on a sufficiently small interval, and
f-q has its unique minimum at 0. Also q''(0)=f''(0).
Let t_n minimize f_n-q on that closed interval. Uniform convergence implies
t_n->0. Thus for all sufficiently large n, t_n is an interior minimizer.

At that minimum, f_n'(t_n)=q'(t_n), and for all small h,
  f_n(t_n+h)-f_n(t_n)-h f_n'(t_n)
    >= q(t_n+h)-q(t_n)-h q'(t_n).
Consequently
  q''(t_n)/2
    <= liminf_(h->0) [f_n(t_n+h)-f_n(t_n)-h f_n'(t_n)]/h^2
    <= limsup_(h->0) [same quotient]
    <= -J_n(C(t_n),H)/8.                                  (4.1)

Hence
  J_n(C(t_n),H) <= -4 q''(t_n).                            (4.2)
Since q''(t_n)->f''(0), any lower bound L<=liminf_n J_n gives
  L <= liminf J_n <= limsup J_n <= -4 f''(0).               (4.3)

Lower touching is essential. An upper touching test would reverse the
minimum inequality and would NOT yield (4.1). The factor 1/2 in the
test-function Taylor expansion converts -1/8 into -1/4 for F''.
The limits are taken first in h for each fixed n, then in n; uniform
Peano errors are unnecessary.

An alternative quadratic test with curvature f''(0)-epsilon works with
an epsilon limit. The quartic choice above is stronger and simpler because
f is already C^2 on a neighborhood. It is legitimate for an existence
argument even though q is defined using the unknown limiting function.

5. MOVING BASES: EXACT TARGET TRANSFORMATION

Put C_n=C(t_n), K_n=C_n^(-1/2) H C_n^(-1/2), and
eta_n=(C_n^(1/2))#nu_n. Let eta_0=(C0^(1/2))#nu.
The exact congruence identity is
  F_nu_n(C_n+sH)=F_eta_n(I+sK_n).                          (5.1)
It is not an assertion that the old Brenier map can be composed with
C_n^(1/2); generally that composition need not be a gradient map.

Since C_n->C0,
  W2(eta_n,eta_0)
   <= ||C_n^(1/2)||op W2(nu_n,nu)
      +||C_n^(1/2)-C0^(1/2)||op (integral |y|^2 dnu)^(1/2)
   ->0.                                                   (5.2)
Also K_n->K0 in operator norm. The source in every problem remains the
same standard Gaussian. The covariance segment stays in the open PD cone.

6. TRANSPORT AND HESSIAN-MEASURE CONVERGENCE

Let T_n=grad phi_n be the actual Brenier maps gamma_d->eta_n, and
T the map gamma_d->eta_0. W2 convergence gives convergence of the
optimal transport costs. Tightness of the couplings, lower semicontinuity
of squared distance, and uniqueness of the optimal Gaussian-source
coupling identify every subsequential limit as (Id,T)#gamma_d.
This implies T_n->T in probability; Lusin approximation handles a
measurable T, and in the finite-simplex case its discontinuities are
only Gaussian-null facets. W2 convergence of eta_n also gives uniform
integrability of |T_n|^2. Hence
  T_n -> T strongly in L^2(gamma_d).                       (6.1)

For each compact source set, the Gaussian density is bounded below.
Thus (6.1) implies L^2_loc convergence of the gradients. Distributionally
  D T_n = D^2 phi_n -> D T = D^2 phi.
These are PSD matrix measures. If chi is a smooth compactly supported
nonnegative cutoff equal to one on a compact K, then
  integral chi tr(D^2 phi_n) = -integral grad chi dot T_n
is bounded uniformly. Therefore distributional convergence upgrades to
local weak-* convergence of the matrix measures. Gaussian-weighted
versions follow by multiplying compact test functions by the continuous
positive Gaussian density.

Equivalently one may normalize potentials by their mean on a fixed ball,
use anchored Poincare and convexity to get local uniform convergence,
and then take Hessian measures. Neither route differentiates T_n with
respect to C. No common compact support of the target approximants is
required: W2 convergence is the needed moment/tightness condition.

The finite-target potential has the form
  phi(x)=max_i {z_i dot x+h_i}, z_i=C0^(1/2)y_i.
Its Hessian measure is
  D^2 phi = sum_(i<j) ell_ij n_ij n_ij^T
             H^(d-1)|_(facet ij),
  ell_ij=|z_i-z_j|, n_ij=(z_i-z_j)/ell_ij.                 (6.2)
Each unoriented facet occurs once. Multiple junctions have codimension
at least two and contribute no Hessian mass.

7. REDUCING THE MOVING MATRIX K_n TO ONE FIXED MATRIX

For PSD M, j_M is a nonnegative quadratic form. Its formula gives
  j_M(K)<=tr(M K^2)<=tr(M)||K||op^2.
Quadratic Cauchy--Schwarz then yields
  |j_M(K_n)-j_M(K0)|
    <=tr(M)(||K_n||op+||K0||op)||K_n-K0||op.               (7.1)

There is a uniform GLOBAL Gaussian trace bound:
  integral tr(M_n) dgamma_d
     = E[X dot T_n(X)]
     <= sqrt(d) ||T_n||_(L^2(gamma_d)).                   (7.2)
To justify the identity without a uniform Hessian bound, integrate by
parts with monotone radial cutoffs. The cutoff-gradient term vanishes
by the L^2 bound; positivity of tr(M_n) permits the limiting passage.
The RHS is uniformly bounded by (5.2).

Integrating (7.1) and using (7.2) shows
  integral j_(M_n)(K_n) dgamma_d
   -integral j_(M_n)(K0) dgamma_d ->0.                    (7.3)
Thus the separate measure lemma only needs the fixed K0. No hidden
uniform ellipticity of M_n survives the atomic-target limit.

8. THE EXACT REMAINING MEASURE INPUT AND THE RESULT

The required independent lemma, in the notation above, is
  liminf_n integral j_(M_n)(K0) dgamma_d
    >= sum_(i<j) area_gamma(facet ij) ell_ij
                    (n_ij^T K0 n_ij)^2.                 (8.1)

It should be proved locally near compact portions of the regular facets,
then extended by nonnegativity and exhaustion. This audit does NOT replace
it by generic lower semicontinuity: j_M(K) is concave and homogeneous in M
(as an infimum of linear functions), so ordinary convex-integrand measure
lower-semicontinuity is not available. Rank-one structure of (6.2) is the
substantive feature of the proposed lemma.

Combining (8.1), (7.3), and (4.3), and writing a_ij=y_i-y_j, gives
  -4 D^2 F_nu(C0)[H,H]
    >= sum_(i<j) area_gamma(facet ij) |C0^(1/2)a_ij|
        [n_ij^T C0^(-1/2)H C0^(-1/2)n_ij]^2
     = sum_(i<j) [area_gamma(facet ij)/|C0^(1/2)a_ij|]
        [(a_ij^T H a_ij)^2/(a_ij^T C0 a_ij)].             (8.2)

The equality follows from
  n_ij^T K0 n_ij=(a_ij^T H a_ij)/(a_ij^T C0 a_ij).
This is exactly the old normal-strain expression, with no extra
power of C0 or factor of two. At C0=I it becomes
  -4 F'' >= sum_(i<j) (area_gamma/|a_ij|)
                         (a_ij^T H a_ij)^2/|a_ij|^2.

For a full simplex, all facet areas are positive. If every edge quadratic
vanishes, take the basis y_i-y_0; the diagonal quadratics vanish, and the
other edges force every cross term to vanish. Therefore H=0. Strict
negative curvature in every nonzero symmetric direction follows.

9. LOWER AFFINE RANK AND CENTERED SCORE COVARIANCES

Suppose the same affinely independent sites are embedded in R^D with
affine span dimension r=m-1<D. Let Q have orthonormal columns spanning
their differences, y_i=y_0+Q b_i. The common y_0 contributes zero because
the Gaussian source is centered. Conditional lifting of Gaussian couplings
gives the exact identity
  F_nu(C)=F_(sum p_i delta_bi)(Q^T C Q).                  (9.1)
For C>0, Q^T C Q>0. Consequently the classical smoothness and curvature
argument applies intrinsically in dimension r, with direction Q^T H Q.
Full ambient strictness is not claimed: directions with Q^T H Q=0 leave
the value unchanged and make every edge quadratic in (8.2) vanish.
A one-point target has F=0 and an empty facet sum.

For the manuscript's centered m-label covariance, work on 1^perp from
the outset. A covariance positive definite on 1^perp is an ordinary
PD matrix in r=m-1 orthonormal coordinates. The centered sites form
a full simplex there. There is no need to invert the singular full
m-by-m covariance, or to assert ambient strictness across its fixed null
direction.

This covers the actual lower-rank issue. Arbitrary affinely dependent
site families with more than r+1 positive-mass sites are outside this
classical C^2 assertion. They are not needed for the original strict
normal-strain dependency.

10. DEPENDENCY VERDICT

Validated here:
  - lower-touching direction, interior minimizing sequence, and factor 4;
  - uniform target-value convergence on covariance compact sets;
  - exact congruence at moving bases;
  - W2-to-local-Hessian-measure convergence;
  - the uniform Gaussian trace bound and K_n-to-K0 reduction;
  - simplex C^2 regularity and intrinsic affine-span reduction;
  - exact conversion of the facet expression to normal strain.

Not supplied here:
  - the separately requested rank-one measure lemma (8.1);
  - proof of the smooth all-base regularity theorem used to obtain (P);
  - the full Hodge proof, taken from the already audited source;
  - the rest of the negative-voting theorem's dependency graph.

No global N, cycle-gauge identity, or chain-complex factorization enters
any validated step above. Subject to the explicitly isolated inputs,
this is an independent route to the finite-simplex normal-strain bound.
