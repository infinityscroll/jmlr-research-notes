Proposition. Fix d >= 1, Sigma > 0, and q in C_c^infinity(R^d). Put

  d nu(y) = Z^{-1} exp(-q(y)) g_Sigma(y) dy,
  Z = integral exp(-q(y)) g_Sigma(y) dy,

where g_Sigma is the centered Gaussian density with covariance Sigma.
The ordinary Euclidean quadratic-cost Brenier map T=grad phi from the
standard Gaussian gamma to nu is a smooth global diffeomorphism. There
are constants 0 < m <= L < infinity such that

  m I <= D^2 phi(x) <= L I                         for every x in R^d. (P)

The constants may depend on Sigma and q. No uniformity over an approximating
sequence, over covariance base points, or over dimensions is required.

1. THE COORDINATE REDUCTION PRESERVES EUCLIDEAN QUADRATIC OPTIMALITY

Let A=Sigma^{1/4}, the symmetric positive definite fourth root. Introduce
different, reciprocal transformations on source and target:

  x'=A x,                  y'=A^{-1} y.

The transformed reference Gaussian measures coincide:

  A#N(0,I)=N(0,A^2),
  A^{-1}#N(0,Sigma)=N(0,A^{-1}Sigma A^{-1})=N(0,A^2).

Write eta=N(0,A^2). The actual transformed target is

  nu'=A^{-1}#nu=Z^{-1} exp(-q'(y')) eta,
  q'(y')=q(A y').

Thus q' is again smooth and compactly supported. The normalizing constant
is the same Z by change of variables.

The map pi -> (A,A^{-1})#pi is a bijection between the two coupling sets.
Because A is symmetric,

  (A x) dot (A^{-1} y)=x dot y.

For any original coupling pi,

  integral |x'-y'|^2 d pi'
    = integral |A x|^2 d gamma + integral |A^{-1}y|^2 d nu
      - 2 integral x dot y d pi,

whereas its original quadratic cost is

  integral |x-y|^2 d pi
    = integral |x|^2 d gamma + integral |y|^2 d nu
      - 2 integral x dot y d pi.

Their difference depends only on the prescribed marginals. All moments
are finite because exp(-q)/Z is bounded above and below. Therefore the
same coupling minimizes the ordinary Euclidean squared distance in each
coordinate problem. There is no substitution of an anisotropic cost.

Equivalently, if U=grad psi is the Brenier map eta -> nu', then

  T(x)=A U(A x)=grad[psi(A x)]

pushes gamma to nu and is a gradient of a convex function. Brenier uniqueness
identifies it with the original quadratic optimizer. This is a congruence
of one potential; it is not a composition of unrelated optimal transports.

2. THE FORWARD HESSIAN UPPER BOUND

The common Gaussian reference has normalized potential

  V(z)=(1/2) z^T A^{-2} z +(d/2)log(2 pi)+(1/2)log det(A^2).

It is minimized at zero, and

  lambda I <= D^2 V=A^{-2} <= Lambda I,
  lambda=lambda_min(Sigma^{-1/2})>0,
  Lambda=lambda_max(Sigma^{-1/2})<infinity.

Choose R with supp q compactly contained in B_R, and set

  K_q = sup_y ||D^2 q(y)||_op,
  R' = ||A^{-1}||_op R + 1,
  lambda_q' = ||A||_op^2 K_q.

Then supp q' is compactly contained in B_R', and

  D^2 q'(z)=A D^2 q(Az) A >= -lambda_q' I.

Every hypothesis of CFJ Theorem 1.1 is now satisfied with the SAME reference
potential V on both sides; its normalization parameter is c_{q'}=-log Z.
It supplies a finite global Lipschitz constant C_0 for U. Hence

  Lip(T) <= ||A||_op^2 C_0 =: L < infinity.                 (1)

The convex Brenier potential consequently has a C^{1,1} representative,
and 0 <= D^2 phi <= L I almost everywhere (also distributionally).

3. THE REVERSE MAP SUPPLIES A POSITIVE LOWER BOUND

Apply input (B) directly in the original coordinates to the Brenier map
S from nu back to gamma. Up to additive normalization constants, the source
and target potentials for this reverse problem are

  V_rev(y)=(1/2)y^T Sigma^{-1}y+q(y),
  W_rev(x)=|x|^2/2.

Set Lambda_rev=lambda_max(Sigma^{-1})+K_q > 0. Then

  D^2 V_rev <= Lambda_rev I,       D^2 W_rev=I.

The first inequality is all that is required on the reverse source; q may
make V_rev nonconvex. Thus

  Lip(S) <= sqrt(Lambda_rev)=: K < infinity.                (2)

Both T and S have everywhere-defined continuous Lipschitz representatives.
Reversing the unique optimal coupling shows S(T(x))=x gamma-almost
everywhere and T(S(y))=y nu-almost everywhere. Each measure has full support,
so each of these sets of full measure is dense. Continuity extends both
identities to every point. Thus S=T^{-1} globally, and

  K^{-1}|x-z| <= |T(x)-T(z)| <= L|x-z|.                    (3)

At every differentiability point of T, the lower metric inequality gives
|DT(x)v| >= K^{-1}|v| for all v. Since DT=D^2 phi is symmetric positive
semidefinite, all its eigenvalues are >= K^{-1}. Therefore

  m I <= D^2 phi <= L I almost everywhere,
  m = [lambda_max(Sigma^{-1})+K_q]^{-1/2} > 0.              (4)

The C^{1,1} property means there is no singular Hessian part, so these bounds
also hold distributionally. This argument obtains the lower bound without
applying CFJ Theorem 1.1 to a nonconvex reference potential.

4. SMOOTHNESS AND EVERYWHERE HESSIAN BOUNDS

The original densities g_I and Z^{-1}exp(-q)g_Sigma are C^infinity and
strictly positive on R^d. On each compact set they are bounded above and
away from zero. Their supports are all of R^d, a convex set. Input (C)
therefore gives phi in C^infinity_loc(R^d), hence C^infinity on R^d.
The same applies to the reverse potential.

By continuity of D^2 phi, the almost-everywhere bounds (4) hold at every
point. In particular det D^2 phi>0 everywhere. The global inverse already
constructed is smooth, either by inverse function theory and the global
identities or by regularity of the reverse potential. This proves (P).

The proof only needs ordinary local smoothness. No global bounds on third
or higher derivatives and no assertion of exactly quadratic tails are made.

5. LINEAR CLOSURE AND THE ALL-BASE COVARIANCE APPLICATION

For every invertible linear map B,

  B#nu = Z^{-1} exp(-q_B(z)) g_{B Sigma B^T}(z) dz,
  q_B(z)=q(B^{-1}z) in C_c^infinity(R^d).

Consequently the proposition applies independently to the standard-Gaussian
Brenier map for each transformed target. In particular B=C_0^{1/2} is
admissible at every positive definite base covariance C_0.

This proves exactly the regularity closure needed in Sections 6--8 of the
transfer note. It does not assert that B T is a Brenier map, or that the
transport potential is quadratic outside a compact set. The density class,
not a condition on Brenier-potential tails, is the closed object.

The constants for different C_0 may differ. The transfer note's pointwise
upper second quotient and one-dimensional concavity argument do not require
a uniform bound over all C_0. Likewise its W2 approximation uses no uniform
regularity constants over the approximating density sequence.

DEPENDENCY CONSEQUENCE

For this dense target class and all its invertible linear images, CFJ2017
Theorem 1.1 plus classical Caffarelli reverse-map and local-smoothness results
replace Gwozdz2026 completely. The 2026 theorem need not be accepted or
audited for this application. This is a reduction to established results,
not a new proof of those established regularity theorems.

The separate remaining mathematical obligation in the Hodge route is the
all-dimensional weighted Hodge estimate in the weak-L2 smooth-uniform-Hessian
class specified in the transfer note. This regularity note does not establish
that estimate, and does not turn an optional independent-proof route into a
new completion requirement.
