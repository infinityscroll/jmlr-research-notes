1. EXACT STATEMENT AND REQUIRED BRANCH INTERFACES

There must be ONE absolute integer m0 such that, for every m>=m0, every
finite N>=m-1, every 0<r<=1/(m-1), and every measurable
f:R^N -> Delta_m,
  S_{-r}(f)>=S_{-r}(g),
with equality exactly regular indicator assignments up to orthogonal maps,
label permutations and null sets. g is the regular Gaussian m-simplex
assignment in the same ambient space. No masses are prescribed for f.

Put L=1+log m, mu=E max_i Z_i, alpha=mu/(m-1),
w=alpha^2(m-1), beta^2=w/m. For actual assignment centroids
B_i=E[X f_i], let p_i=E f_i, U=1/m,
  delta=|p-U|^2, W=||B||_F^2, Delta=w-W,
  D=delta+rDelta, R=(w+W)/2-F_U(B), J=D/r.
Let d be the Frobenius distance of B from the regular centroid orbit.

Required local interface, with A=1 fixed:
  d<=1/m, p_i in [1/(2m),3/(2m)]
  ==> S_{-r}(f)-S_{-r}(g)>=delta/2+rR.                 (L)
It concerns actual centroids of arbitrary fractional f.

Required exterior interfaces after competitive localization and d>1/m:
  R<=J, J>=c/(m^3 sqrt L),
  A2(f)-A2(g)>=-C L^3 J,                             (E2)
  |sum_(k>=3)(-r)^k(Ak(f)-Ak(g))|
                 <=C r^3 m^(7/4)L^(7/2) J.          (ET)
There is no assumption that f is an affine maximizer, stationary, pure,
or an attained full-noise minimizer in these interfaces.

2. THE MASS-WINDOW ESTIMATE: TRANSITIVE PROOF RECONSTRUCTED

The required bound is
  V(p):=sup_(E f=p)||M_f||^2 <= w+C L^2 |p-U|^2
on a fixed box c/m<=p_i<=Cbar/m, with c,Cbar fixed absolutely.
The source represents V(p)=max_(C>=0) g(C,p),
  g(C,p)=2 F_p(C)-tr C,
where C is a covariance on H=1-perp. This representation also covers
fractional assignments: square completion converts the centroid norm to
sup_B(2< B,M_f >-||B||^2), and the finite prescribed-mass linear assignment
support is the same for pure and fractional assignments. A pure maximizing
linear assignment exists by the finite Gaussian dual, splitting duplicate-
score ties measurably if needed. Hence every fractional actual W<=V(p).
No full-noise purity assertion follows or is used.

For C>=epsilon I_H, strict concavity gives a unique maximizer Ceps(p).
The elementary bound F_p(C)<=sqrt(tr C) gives a common trace bound.
Adding an independent Gaussian to a coupling gives F_p(C+epsilon I)>=F_p(C),
so uniformly in p,
  0<=V(p)-Veps(p)<=epsilon(m-1).                       (2.1)
The mixed estimate at these optimizers is
  |D_C h[A]|^2<=K(c,Cbar) L^2[-F_CC[A,A]].            (2.2)
Here h is the mean-zero mass offset. I checked its source rather than
assuming the source's historical audit status.

2a. Why the regularized graph supplies (2.2)

At every true covariance optimum, square completion gives B=M. Re-split
any two cells, keeping their union fixed, along a direction perpendicular
to B_i+B_j. The unnormalized projected union has density at most phi(0).
Its upper/lower masses p_i,p_j then have projected centroid difference at
least p_i p_j/phi(0). Since the old split maximizes the squared norm,
  |B_i-B_j|>=sqrt(2pi) p_i p_j.
The entropy centroid bound supplies the corresponding O(sqrt L/m) upper
bound. Compactness of the CLOSED OPTIMIZER GRAPH transfers both estimates
with slack to Ceps(p), uniformly in the fixed mass box for each fixed m.
No continuity of individual optimizer fibers or positive rank of a limiting
optimum is required.

At a regularized optimizer, Price/envelope differentiation gives
2 D_C F_p=mathcal L, and KKT gives
  0<=mathcal L<=I_H,
  (I_H-mathcal L)(Ceps-epsilon I_H)=0.
Thus physical graph degrees are at most one. Gaussian isoperimetry for
unions of cells and the upper edge-length bound give cut conductance
h0=c/[4Cbar sqrt(pi L)]. Delete edges of weight less than m^-4. A cut
loses at most |S|m^-3, so the retained graph still has conductance h0/2
and counting-measure spectral gap
  lambda_*>=c^2/[128 Cbar^2 pi L].
All constants are dimension-free.

For retained edges, pair separation implies the intrinsic facet probability
pi_e>=pi c^2/m^6; positive cell masses imply normalized offset
q_e^2<=2L. Intrinsic Ehrhard gives
  a(pi_e)^-1<=[56 exp(3/2)]L,
where a(p)=1+Phi^-1(p)p/phi(Phi^-1(p)). These are statements about actual
facet probabilities, not cell masses substituted for facet probabilities.

2b. The retained cycle-refined inequality

The needed estimate is
  -4F_CC[A,A]>=sum_e w_e {x_e^2
                   +a(pi_e)[2h'_e-q_e x_e]^2},         (2.3)
where x_e=d_e^T H d_e/|d_e| at a whitened covariance base and
q_e=h_e/|d_e|. This is stronger than normal strain alone.

In the original finite-fan proof, let E_e=w_e(P_e-Cov_e X),
N_e T_e^T=E_e, B_e=N_e^-1 E_e N_e^-T, and triangle-weight matrix Dg.
The boundary integration identities and global barycentric vectors give
  A-B=-Dg partial_3 diag(mu_h/(6Vol_h)) partial_3^T Dg.
I checked the off-diagonal shared-edge terms and diagonal outside-vertex
sums in Sections 3--5 of that proof. All target simplex faces exist and
have positive intrinsic Gaussian mass; every N_e is a square invertible
tangent-space matrix. The identity uses no inverse covariance deficit.
Choose the weighted gauge partial_3^T Dg lambda=0 while retaining the
fixed cycle j=partial_2 lambda. This gives the exact cycle norm.

For the actual least-squares minimizing offset b, put
j_e=w_e(z_e.m_e+db_e), eta_(e,g)=(partial_2)_(e,g)lambda_g,
and r_e=z_e-N_e^-T eta_e. The two exact identities are
  sum_e r_e^T E_e r_e=sum_e z_e^T E_e z_e-CYC,
  r_e.P_e m_e=-db_e-z_e.(I-P_e)m_e.
The first follows by expansion using both cross and quadratic cycle terms;
the second uses N_e^-1 P_e m_e=1/w_e and sum_g eta_(e,g)=j_e.
Intrinsic Ehrhard bounds E_e below by
w_e a(pi_e)(P_e m_e)(P_e m_e)^T. Applying it to r_e, not z_e, and taking
z_i=H y_i, b_i=2h_i', gives (2.3) with its exact factor four.
Thus the entire cycle energy has been retained and subtracted before the
positive offset term is used. The source does not make the invalid move of
replacing the corrected deficit by the uncorrected facet deficit.

Now 4(h'_e)^2<=(a(pi_e)^-1+q_e^2)
[x_e^2+a(pi_e)(2h'_e-q_e x_e)^2]. Sum over retained edges and use their
gap lambda_* to obtain (2.2). This closes the mixed-estimate obligation.

2c. Constrained envelope and singular limit

For fixed epsilon, strict covariance curvature on the compact feasible
region and smooth mixed derivatives make p->Ceps(p) Lipschitz. On a mass
segment p(t)=p0+tu, differentiate almost everywhere. Optimality inequalities
at two times give
  E=-g_CC[C',C'] <= B=g_Cp[C',u],
while (2.2) gives B^2<=2K L^2 |u|^2 E. Therefore B<=2K L^2|u|^2.
The envelope first derivative is g_p, so
  Veps''=g_pp[u,u]+g_Cp[C',u]<=2K L^2|u|^2,
because F_pp=-mathcal L^-1 is negative. Its first derivative is Lipschitz
for fixed epsilon, so integrating loses no singular positive measure.
At U, permutation symmetry forces zero centered offsets and Veps'(U)=0.
Pass epsilon down to zero using (2.1), then use V(U)=w to get the desired
mass-window bound. Auxiliary epsilon0 may depend on m; this does not alter
the uniform coefficient or the eventual m threshold of the noise theorem.

3. UNIVERSAL COMPETITIVE LOCALIZATION, INCLUDING FRACTIONAL f

Assume only S_{-r}(f)<=S_{-r}(g). First apply the scalar global tangent
bound for H(p)=phi(Phi^-1(p))^2:
  sum_i H(p_i)<=mH(1/m)+B_m delta, B_m=O(log m).
The individual centroid rearrangement |B_i|<=sqrt(H(p_i)) is valid for
0<=f_i<=1. Hermite parity gives
  S_{-r}(f)>=sum_i p_i^2-rW-r^3,
  S_{-r}(g)<=1/m-rw+r^2 A2(g)+r^4.
The source's finite coefficient bound implies m^2 delta<=L_m, where
L_m tends to 2-2 EulerGamma<1. The Mills/Gumbel argument includes both
tail directions and uniform integrability of the centered maximum's
first two moments. In particular one may fix c=.01, Cbar=2 absolutely,
and all sufficiently large m give c/m<=p_i<=Cbar/m, uniformly in r.

The assignment-independent inequality
  A2(f)>=(W-P)_+^2/(2P), P=sum_i p_i^2,
follows from conditional Jensen in each centroid direction and weighted
Cauchy. The positive part makes this a globally convex perspective in
(W,P); hence its tangent at (w,1/m) applies even when W<P.
Write ell=mw-1. Its tangent yields
  A2(f)>=F0-(ell/r)D+c_(m,r)delta,
  F0=ell^2/(2m), c_(m,r)=ell[1/r-(ell+2)/2]>=0.
The exact regular second-chaos formula and Var(max_i Z_i)=O(1/log m)
give 0<=A2(g)-F0<=C/m. Keeping degree two in the parity bounds now gives
  (1-r ell)D+r^2 c_(m,r)delta<=C r^2/m+r^3+r^4.
The mass-window result proves
  D>=delta/2+r(V(p)-W)>=0
once r K L^2<=1/2. Therefore
  delta<=2D, 0<=D<=C r^2/m, |W-w|<=D/r<=C r/m.        (3.1)
Consequently max_i|mp_i-1|<=C sqrt(m)r<=C/sqrt(m), and the improved
box [1/(2m),3/(2m)] follows. All steps concern arbitrary fractional f.
No choice of a full-noise optimizer or optimizer sequence occurs.

5. EXHAUSTION OF THE TWO BRANCHES AND STRICTNESS

Every f is either noncompetitive, already strictly worse than g, or is
competitive and satisfies all preceding inputs. Every competitive f has
either d<=1/m or d>1/m. There is no annular region left between branches.

In the exterior, the full Hermite expansion is absolutely summable since
sum_k Ak(f)<=1 and |r|<1. Exactly,
  S_{-r}(f)-S_{-r}(g)=D+r^2[A2(f)-A2(g)]+Htail(f)-Htail(g).
Applying (E2),(ET) and J=D/r gives
  S_{-r}(f)-S_{-r}(g)
  >=D[1-CrL^3-Cr^2 m^(7/4)L^(7/2)]>=D/2>0.           (5.1)
Strict positivity uses (4.3) and r>0, rather than assuming D>0 globally.
This contradicts competitiveness. The endpoint losses are bounded by
C L^3/(m-1) and C m^(7/4)L^(7/2)/(m-1)^2, both tending to zero.

In the local branch, (L) and R>=0 imply delta=R=0 under competitiveness.
Formula (4.2) then gives BB^T=alpha^2P. Its rows are an actual regular
centroid matrix. The nonnegative routing loss
  max_i B_i.X-sum_i f_i B_i.X
has expectation w-W=0. Pairwise ties are Gaussian-null since every row
difference has nonzero length. Thus f is the indicator of that regular
winner almost surely. This argument includes fractional assignments and
needs no regularity of their boundaries. Conversely every rotated or
relabelled regular assignment has the same noise value. This proves the
claimed equality classification once the two branch interfaces are proved.

6. WHY ONE m0 SUFFICES

Fix c=.01, Cbar=2, the consequent entropy row cap A, and local radius
constant A_local=1 before choosing m. All source constants in Sections
2--4 then depend on these fixed absolute values only. Set m0 to the maximum
of their finitely many eventual thresholds, the local/exterior thresholds,
and thresholds ensuring the two errors in (5.1) sum to at most one half.
The only limiting statements used are powers of log m divided by positive
powers of m, and the explicit Gaussian-max asymptotics in Section 3.
None depends on f, N, or a positive lower bound for r.

The finite-graph compactness parameter epsilon0 can depend on m, but is
removed by a uniform-in-p value limit at that fixed m. It introduces no
m-dependent loss into K L^2 and hence no extra quantifier into m0.
All actual centroids have rank at most m-1; covariance calculations take
place on the label space H. Gaussian isoperimetry, Poincare, and
hypercontractivity are dimension-free. The residual assignment can depend
on every ambient coordinate; no reduction pretending f depends only on
its centroid span is used. N>=m-1 supplies the regular reference and the
same-dimensional score approximations. Cylindrical embedding would give
the inequality comparison below that dimension as well, but is not needed
for the stated theorem.

At r=1/(m-1), all bounds remain valid and Gaussian correlation has absolute
value less than one for m>=3. At r down to zero, all divisions are by the
fixed positive r and are paid by D=O(r^2/m); no absolute truncation error
is left unscaled in (5.1). r=0 is intentionally excluded from rigidity.
The function is a single-assignment stability throughout; no two-rule
comparison or common-mass condition is silently introduced.
