1. FACET TILT AND BALANCING, WITH DIMENSION-FREE CONSTANTS

Work on H=1-perp. Put P=I-11^T/m, mu=E max_i G_i,
alpha=mu/(m-1), w=alpha^2(m-1), L=1+log m, and b=Phi^-1(1-1/m).
Throughout, m is larger than an absolute threshold, so b^2 is comparable
with L. The regular score-facet weight is w0=alpha/m.

The normalized regular facet Gaussian has mean mu_e with |mu_e|<=Cb
and covariance S_e satisfying ||S_e-P||_F<=C. To check the latter
without a dimension factor: the normal eigenvalue is zero; the one other
distinguished eigenvalue lies in [0,1]; the m-3 remaining eigenvalues are
V=1-O(b^2/m). Squaring and summing their deviations gives O(1+b^4/m).
The formulas follow from the tied-max density proportional to
phi(t)^2 Phi(t)^(m-2). The conditional remaining coordinates are iid
Gaussians truncated at t before removal of the common coordinate.
The covariance upper bound also follows directly from convex Gaussian
conditioning on the facet cone. Its Poincare constant is at most one.

For K=P+E, ||E||_F=epsilon<=1/8, and shifted score mean h in H, let
D=P-K^-1 and ell=K^-1 h. On a fixed facet the density ratio is exp(c+q),
q(y)=y^T D y/2+ell.y, c=-log det_H(K)/2-h^T K^-1 h/2.
The deterministic trace terms cancel:
 |tr_H(D)-log det_H(K)|<=C epsilon^2.
Together with the facet mean/covariance bounds this yields
 |c+E q|<=C(epsilon b^2+b|h|+|h|^2+epsilon^2).
There is no uncancelled trace of size sqrt(m)*epsilon.

For the exponential tilt exp(tq), 0<=t<=1, its tangent potential has
Hessian at least 3I/4. Thus its covariance is at most 2I and
 Var_t(q)<=C(epsilon^2+|D mu_t+ell|^2),
 |mu'_t|<=sqrt(2 Var_t(q)).
Writing v_t=D mu_t+ell gives
 |v_t|'<=C epsilon(epsilon+|v_t|).
Gronwall yields |v_t|<=C(epsilon b+|h|), and hence
 Var_t(q)<=C(epsilon^2 b^2+|h|^2),
 |mu_1-mu_0|<=C(epsilon b+|h|).
These estimates justify both density-ratio bounds and the perturbed facet
mean bound, with no unbounded-coordinate Taylor assumption. The quadratic
potential remains uniformly confining, so Gaussian domination justifies
the differentiations. Convex cone approximation gives the same inequalities
for the nonsmooth boundary.

Consequently, for every facet e,
 exp(-A)<=w_e(K,h)/w0<=exp(A),
 A=C(epsilon b^2+b|h|+|h|^2+epsilon^2 b^2).

Along K_t=P+tE, balancing offsets h_t satisfy

 c_(t,e)=a_e^T E K_t^-1(mu_(t,e)-h_t)/2.
The sign and factor come from the covariance heat identity and the outward
normal -a_e/sqrt(2). When w_e is between w0/2 and 2w0, the offset Laplacian
on H has eigenvalues between m w0/2 and 2m w0. Also
 sum_e w_e c_e^2<=C m w0 b^2 epsilon^2,
by sum_e a_e a_e^T=mP. Therefore |h'_t|<=C b epsilon.
This estimate does not multiply the individual facet bounds by m^2.

Choose a large fixed bootstrap coefficient K0 after the last C, then an
absolute small c1 so epsilon<=c1/b^2 and |h|<=K0 b epsilon force A<log 2.
Integration strictly improves the assumed offset bound and continues to
t=1. Thus all weights are between w0/2 and 2w0 on the covariance ball
||K-P||_F<=c1/b^2. Every constant is independent of ambient dimension.

2. LOCAL CURVATURE INPUT AND NORMALIZATION

Let F(K) be the balanced score value for Gaussian covariance K on H.
The new Hodge/normal-strain theorem gives
 -4 D2F(K)[E,E]>=sum_e w_e(K,h_K)(a_e^T E a_e)^2/(a_e^T K a_e).
The score and physical facet weights agree by coarea. On the above ball,
denominators are at most 3. The exact centered-matrix identity is
 sum_e(a_e^T E a_e)^2=m sum_i E_ii^2+(tr E)^2+2||E||_F^2.
It follows that
 -D2F(K)[E,E]>=w0||E||_F^2/12.
The derivative at P is DF(P)[E]=(alpha/2)tr_H E. In particular
alpha F(P)=w and alpha DF(P)[E]=alpha^2 tr E/2. The 1/2 factor matters.

3. ALL PSD COVARIANCES, NOT JUST A SMALL SCORE BALL

For ANY centered score matrix B, in any finite N>=m-1, set
 K=BB^T/alpha^2, E=K-P, e=||E||_F.
K may be singular and e may be large. Invariance under isometries gives
 F_U(B)=alpha F(K),
 R_U(B)=(w+||B||_F^2)/2-F_U(B)
       =alpha[F(P)+DF(P)[E]-F(P+E)].
For 0<=t<1, P+tE is positive definite on H. Set
 s=min(1/2,c1/(b^2 e)) if e>0.
On [0,s], the preceding curvature estimate yields
 -d2/dt2 [alpha F(P+tE)]>=alpha^2 e^2/(12m).
On the rest of the ray, the concavity theorem ensures a nonnegative
curvature measure. Integration of that measure with weight 1-t therefore
gives, including singular K by continuity,
 R_U(B)>=c alpha^2 e^2/m * min(1,c/(L e)).                 (A)
For a fully elementary endpoint argument, first integrate up to any
T<1 and use the tangent deficit at T; as T increases to one, the initial
interval's weight T-t tends 1-t and the remainder stays nonnegative.
No global positive lower eigenvalue of K is assumed.

4. DISTANCE AND THE EXACT EXTERIOR SCALE

Let d=inf_R||B-alpha Q R||_F, QQ^T=P, with R an orthonormal embedding.
Procrustes gives q^2:=d^2/alpha^2=sum_i(sqrt(lambda_i(K))-1)^2.
Since |lambda-1|=|sqrt(lambda)-1|(sqrt(lambda)+1), e>=q even if K is
singular. The function x^2 min(1,c/(Lx)) is increasing for x>=0, so (A)
implies the same lower bound with e replaced by q.

Competitive norm localization gives ||B||_F^2=O(w), hence q<=C sqrt(m).
