The local theorem is:
For every fixed A, for all sufficiently large m (depending on A), all finite
N>=m-1, all 0<r<=1/(m-1), and every measurable f:R^N->Delta_m with masses
p_i in [1/(2m),3/(2m)] and ACTUAL centroid matrix B at distance d<=A/m from
the regular orbit,

  S_-r(f)-S_-r(g) >= ||p-U||^2/2 + r R_U(B).                 (L1)

Here alpha=E max_i Z_i/(m-1), L=1+log m, w=alpha^2(m-1),
R_U(B)=(w+||B||_F^2)/2-F_U(B), and U_i=1/m. The assertion is uniform in
ambient dimension and in the entire voting interval, including arbitrarily
small positive r. There is no purity, stationarity, interface, or L1-closeness
assumption on f. The reference affine allocation used in the proof has full
rank as a CONSEQUENCE of the centroid-score ball.

1. CONDITIONING, OFFSETS AND A FACET CONSTRUCTION WITH NO sqrt(m) LOSS

Align B with a regular B0. Since alpha is comparable to sqrt(L)/m,
||B-B0||op<=A/m implies that the m-1 singular values lie in [alpha/2,2alpha]
for all sufficiently large m. This applies to the aligned score segment and
fixed enlargements needed for covariance projections. Thus rank loss is not
being ignored inside this local branch.

For a conditioned affine fan with masses in the positive box, M=mathcal L B
by Gaussian integration by parts. Each centroid row has norm at most
p_i sqrt(2log(1/p_i))<=C sqrt(L)/m, so the pseudoinverse gives bounded weighted
graph degrees. Gaussian isoperimetry on a union of k cells gives cut weight
at least c min(k,m-k)/sqrt(L). Counting-measure Cheeger with bounded maximum
degree therefore gives lambda_2(mathcal L)>=c/L. This proves [O](3)--(6)
and [U](4) in their required normalizations; no centroid optimality is used.

Here is an explicit version of [U]'s facet probability argument. Normalize
B and h by alpha, so singular values are in [1/2,2] and row norms are at most
2. The two winning halfspaces, each containing a cell of probability >=1/(2m),
give |h_i-h_j|/|B_i-B_j|<=C sqrt(L), hence H=max|h_i|<=C sqrt(L) in the mean-
zero gauge. For a specified pair i,j, choose K=8 and T=H+4K sqrt(L), and set

  q_i=T-h_i, q_j=T-h_j,
  q_k=-(q_i+q_j)/(m-2),  k distinct from i,j.

Then q is centered and ||q||^2<=6(T+H)^2=O(L). Its minimum-norm preimage
x_* satisfies ||x_*||^2=O(L). At x_* the two selected utilities equal T,
while every other utility is at most H. This uses only TWO large score
coordinates; prescribing all other UTILITIES equal would unnecessarily
introduce the norm of the entire offset vector.

Let n be the pair normal and a its signed offset. Then n.x_*=a, |a|=O(sqrt L),
and v=x_*-an is tangential with ||v||^2=O(L). On that tangent hyperplane,
standard Gaussian Z satisfies max_k |B_k.Z|<=K sqrt(L) with probability >=1/2
by the union bound and row norm bound. The event v+Z stays on the actual
facet. Gaussian Cameron--Martin Cauchy gives its probability >=(1/4)e^-||v||².
Hence every intrinsic facet probability is at least m^-C, with no ambient
small-ball factor and no sqrt(m) offset cost.

This implies [U](5): fixed-degree polynomial conditional moments cost at
most L^(degree/2), by normal Hermite evaluation at a=O(sqrt L), tangential
hypercontractivity at exponent C L, and conditioning on probability m^-C.
The normalized facet law is a convex Gaussian restriction, so its mean has
norm O(sqrt L) and its centered linear MGF is bounded by exp(|t|²/2).

2. TRACE AND OU RESTRICTION: EXACT DIMENSION-FREE NORMALIZATIONS

For a hyperplane at normal coordinate a, the normal Mehler row has squared
L2 norm (1-t^4)^(-1/2) exp(t²a²/(1+t²)). Taking t²=1/(2q-1) gives the exact
cancellation
  phi(a)^(1/q) K_t(a)^2=(2pi)^(-1/(2q))(1-t^4)^(-1/2).
The weighted row sum of facet areas is O(alpha); Holder over incident labels
therefore bounds the effective degree sum mu_e^(1-1/q)/ell_e by O(1) for
q=2+log(m/alpha). This proves [T](1) and its Hilbert dual [T](2), including
arbitrary offsets and higher-degree boundary distributions.

For [H](6), the facet normal and an arbitrary contracted normal n0 span at
most two dimensions. Separate these from their orthogonal complement.
Normal Cauchy uses only the one- or two-dimensional row norm; tangential
hypercontractivity at Q=C L, followed by facet restriction, is bounded
because the intrinsic probability is >=m^-C. A contraction D_theta cannot
increase the row exponent. Derivatives use the exact identity
  grad^j T_t=(2t)^j T_(2t) grad^j T_(1/2), j=1,2,
and the bounded L2 derivative operators grad^j T_(1/2). Hilbert--Schmidt
tensor norms retain dimension independence. There is no pointwise full-N
Mehler determinant in these estimates.

3. AFFINE SECOND CHAOS AND THE ENTIRE SIGNED TAIL

[U] first proves the balanced score Hessian bound |D_B² A2[Z,Z]|<=C_A L^10||Z||².
The balancing velocity k has energy sum_e w_e E l_e²<=C L||Z||² and norm
||k||<=C L||Z||. The adjoint equation mathcal L c=div(w E(q_i-q_j)) removes
the WHOLE coefficient of h'' from the actual fixed-mass second derivative.
The coherent affine field V=-B^+(ZX+k) moves all facets and intersections.
Its derivative of 1/ell combines with surface divergence to give div_gamma V;
this yields [U](15), including the Z_e.V term. Junction terms cancel under
one common field. For A2, the other term is ||Q'||², not a second copy of it.

The Gaussian trace in div_gamma V cancels its quadratic mean. Conditional
polynomial bounds therefore involve its Frobenius coefficients, not N. The
regular reference difference factors as gamma(X_i-X_j)(X_i+X_j) and vanishes
on regular facets. On the current facets, X_i-X_j=-delta l_e/alpha. This
supplies the deficit factor in the adjoint and transport errors; summing
edge-local factors with graph weights avoids an extra sqrt(m).

A generic score Hessian bound alone would be insufficient. The actual
unequal-row transfer keeps the diagonal term from the new normal-strain
inequality. With normalized covariance K=P+E, decompose E=E_d+E_s, where
E_s has zero diagonal and E_d=m/(m-2) P diag(diag E)P. Writing a=||E_d||,
b=||E_s||, the initial covariance segment yields
  R >= c_A alpha² (m a²+b²)/(m sqrt(L)).
The covariance Hessian bounds give rectangle errors at most
C_A alpha² L^10 a(a+b). Since a²<= (m a²+b²)/m and
ab<= (m a²+b²)/(2sqrt(m)), these are affordable at cost
C_A sqrt(m)L^13 R. The constant-diagonal projection has a fixed positive
spectral bound, so [E]'s equal-row affine theorem applies exactly. Scaling
B has the exact radial identity R(t Btilde)=w(t-1)²/2+tR(Btilde).

I checked [E]'s nonzero-offset cancellation directly: its certificate numerator
G_1-G'_1+A_1-1 equals G_0-1 minus
int_0^1 s sum_e w_e h_e²(B_i+B_j).m_e ds. The offset path loss is
F_0-F_1=int_0^1 s F''_s ds. Thus the terminal G'_1 is canceled, not set to zero.
The facet conditional mean O(sqrt L) pays the correction. The free CDF gap
is nonnegative by the credited external theorem; its tail has the same ||D||²
amplitude as the covariance mean gap. This prevents an additive error floor.
The tensor denominator is at most its regular value. [Q] and [E] therefore
give the equal-row input used above without a CDF claim for affine maxima.

The mass-path adjoint gives |A2(B,p)-A2(B,U)|<=C L³(d||p-U||+||p-U||²).
Using d²<=C_A m sqrt(L)R pays this by R+C_A mL^7||p-U||². Consequently
  A2(f0)-A2(g)>=-C_A sqrt(m)L^13 R-C_A mL^7||p-U||².

For [H], the regular tail potential factors as q_i^0=T_t v_i^0 with
||v_i^0||2<=C(rL)^3/m: hypercontractivity before projecting retains 1/m,
not merely 1/sqrt(m). Its pair difference is odd in the reference normal.
Normal integration plus the restriction lemma gives a quotient of size
C r³ L^(5/2)/m and gradient C r³ L²/m on EVERY current facet.
The change from the regular potential has input norm <=C r³ L^(7/2)d,
and the adjoint norm is <=C r³ L^4 d. The coherent second variation then
has absolute value <=C_A r³ L^10||Z||². The mass path obeys the same bound
with d+||p-U||. Finite Hermite cutoffs have bounds uniform in the cutoff;
the factors r/t² and ((r/t)² Lambda)^(1/2) are below 1, so potential,
boundary quadratic and transport derivative tails all converge. This

Writing z=p-U and W(f0)=||M||², the resulting affine comparison is

4. EXACT ACTUAL-TO-AFFINE IDENTITY AND REGULAR REFERENCE GAP

Take f0=g_(B,p), M=M_f0, v=f-f0, D=d+||z||, and
L_route=F_p(B)-||B||²=<B,M-B>>=0. This relies on B=M_f and feasibility
of f in the same mass class, not on stationarity. Put
u=T_sqrt(r) Pi_(odd>=3)v, so ||u||<=sqrt(2)r^(3/2).
Hermite orthogonality gives the exact first-chaos contribution
r[2L_route+||M-B||²]. Replacing -||u||² by
||u||²-2<v,T_sqrt(r)u>, and dropping only the nonnegative even square,
gives [L](2):
  S(f)-S(f0)>=r||M-B||²+||u||²
               +2[rL_route+<v,q0+a>],
  a=qB-q0-T_sqrt(r)u,  qB=T_-r Pi_(>=2)f0.
The positive residual r||M-B||² is retained throughout.

[R]'s pair-weight integral and two-label swap produce a positive divided
coefficient k_ij with (T_-r g)_j-(T_-r g)_i=r k_ij c_ref. This is valid
on either side of a reference face, not only at a reference winning label.
On an old affine cell i,
  r c_ij+q0_j-q0_i=r k_ij c_ij+r(1-k_ij)(delta l_i-delta l_j).
With kappa=exp[-1-eta|P0x|²], eta=O(r²L), one has k_ij>=kappa. The explicit
smooth log-sum-exp range majorant supplies |1-k_ij|<=e_r. Setting
psi_i=kappa^-1[e_r|delta l_i|+|a_i|/r] gives the lower bound by
-r times the cap max(0,max_(j!=i)[psi_i+psi_j-c_ij]). The diagonal is excluded;
it would otherwise introduce an artificial zero-cost reward.

5. THE CAP IS QUADRATIC EVEN AT ARBITRARILY TINY AMPLITUDES

At q=C1 L, including every fixed multiple used in Holder, radial moments
are bounded. Indeed, for a convex facet restriction and a projection P of
rank <=m,
E exp(b|PY|²)<=(1-2b)^(-m/2)exp[b|P EY|²/(1-2b)].
Here b=q eta has b m=O(L²/m), and |EY|²=O(L). This is independent of N.
The smooth range majorant satisfies ||e_r/r||q<=C L^(3/2). The same factors
and their first two normal derivatives have polynomial-in-m L32 bounds on
each entire pair plane and unit normal tube.

By the affine trace and restriction estimates,
  qB-q0=T_t vD, ||vD||<=C r²L^(5/2)D,
  T_sqrt(r)u=T_t vu, ||vu||<=C r^(3/2)L^(3/2)||u||.
Consequently the vector Lq norm of psi is at most
C[r L^(5/2)D+sqrt(r)L^(3/2)||u||], and its weighted facet-square sum is
at most C[r²L^5D²+rL³||u||²]. Choose
  theta=C2 L^4[rD+sqrt(r)||u||].
Then v_i=psi_i/theta has bounded vector Lq norm and weighted facet-square
sum. If theta=0 the forcing vanishes. Otherwise theta<=C_A L^4 m^-3/2,
hence <=m^-5/4 for all sufficiently large m.

Moderate amplitudes theta>=m^-B use the separate source-exit and target-entry
margins. Their small-sublevel measures are <=Cs up to c/m: shifting one
offset is a Gaussian translation of size O(s/alpha); Cameron--Martin preserves
rare cell masses, and M=mathcal L B bounds the mass derivative. The Lq
bathtub estimate and label Holder sum give
C[m^(1/(q+1))theta^(2q/(q+1))+(c/m)(C theta m/c)^q].
With q=C1 L fixed independently of theta, this is O_B(theta²) throughout
m^-B<=theta<=m^-5/4. It is not claimed quadratic by this argument alone
for theta tending to zero at fixed m.

For smaller theta, [S]'s normal-sup proof applies to the STRUCTURED forcing.
After division by theta, the smoothed input norms divided by r are bounded:
||vD||/(r theta)<=C and ||vu||/(r theta)<=C. For the polynomial term,
rD/theta<=C L^-4. Thus no division by a vanishing r or label amplitude
remains. Absolute values are locally Lipschitz; first derivatives are bounded
by those of the analytic factors, and second analytic derivatives control
the normal supremum of first derivatives. Entire-plane L32 norms are at most
m^C, also after multiplying by the radial factor via Holder.

With M_cut=theta^-1/8, good projected points have normal reward width
O(theta M_cut/alpha). Taylor and Gaussian-density errors are O(m^C theta³M_cut³).
A projection outside the actual facet that enters an old cell lies in a
third-label strip of this width; tangent comparison altitudes are >=c alpha
by the singular-value bound. Their union and the ordered-pair sum cost only
a fixed polynomial in m. Bad projected points cost O(m^C theta M_cut^-31)
and normal distances above 1 cost O(m^C theta^32). The main term is
C theta² sum_e w_e E(v_i²+v_j²), bounded by C theta². Hence
  Bcap <=C theta²+C m^K theta^(21/8).
A fixed B>max(B0,8K/5), enlarged to absorb constants, pays the remainder
for theta<=m^-B. Joining the two regimes proves the quadratic cap for ALL
amplitudes with fixed exponents. There is no hidden amplitude floor or
ambient-dimensional factor.

Substitution in the exact identity yields, uniformly on voting r,
  S(f)-S(f0)>=r||M-B||²-C r³L^8(d²+||z||²),                 (L3)
because the remaining coefficient of ||u||² is at least 1-Cr²L^8>0.
This estimate is relative in d and z, not an additive tail bound.

6. FINAL CANCELLATION, OFFSETS AND UNIFORMITY

Add (L2) and (L3). Since <B,M>=F_p(B),
  r[w-||M||²]+r||M-B||²=2r R_p(B).
The finite gap pays d² by C_A m sqrt(L)R, and its error is absorbed by
r³mL^11R. The mass-square error is absorbed by r³L^10||z||².
Balanced offsets h_U are a dual competitor for any p, giving
R_p>=R+h_U.z.

Given the shared mass envelope, [O]'s deficit-relative certificate is complete:
the graph gap gives F_pp=-mathcal L^-1>=-CL I; combining this lower Taylor
bound with the global upper envelope gives
-h_U.z <=R+CL²||z||²/2. Pair halfspaces imply ||h_U||infinity<=CL/m, so
z=-h_U/K with K comparable to L² stays in the positive mass box. Substitution
gives ||h_U||²<=C L²R. Young then gives
2h_U.z>=-R/2-C L²||z||².

Therefore
 S(f)-S(g)>=[1-C_A(rL²+r²mL^7+r³L^10)]||z||²
   +[3/2-C_A(r sqrt(m)L^13+r²mL^11)]rR.                    (L4)
Every displayed loss tends to zero uniformly for 0<r<=1/(m-1): the largest
relevant forms are L^7/m and L^13/sqrt(m), with all constants independent
of N and r. Choose one sufficiently large threshold depending on A to make
the two brackets at least 1/2 and 1, respectively. This proves (L1).
The threshold is existential; no small-alphabet result is claimed.

7. A SHORTER EQUALITY ARGUMENT

If f is competitive, (L1) forces z=0 and R=0. The finite gap already implies
d=0, so B is a regular centroid matrix. Its unique pointwise linear argmax
g_B is a regular partition and attains E max_i B_i.X=w. Since B=M_f,
E sum_i f_i B_i.X=||B||²=w as well. The nonnegative pointwise routing loss
thus has integral zero. The regular argmax is unique outside a Gaussian
null set, so f=g_B almost surely. This also treats fractional f. There is
no need here to invoke a separate global first-moment equality theorem.
