1. THE GAUSSIAN THEOREM

There is one absolute integer m0 such that, for every integer m>=m0,
every finite N>=m-1, every rho in [-1/(m-1),0), and every measurable
f:R^N->Delta_m, where Delta_m={u>=0:sum_i u_i=1},

 S_rho(f):=sum_i E[f_i(X)f_i(Y)] >= S_rho(g_m).

Here X,Y are standard Gaussian vectors with E[XY^T]=rho I, and g_m
is the indicator assignment of the regular m-cell simplex partition,
extended cylindrically to R^N. Equality holds precisely for those regular
indicator assignments up to orthogonal transformations, relabeling and
Gaussian null sets. There is no prescribed-mass constraint on f.

The threshold m0 is existential. This theorem gives the complete negative
voting interval for every sufficiently large fixed alphabet. It does not
assert all alphabets, every negative Gaussian correlation, positive noise,
or an independently chosen pair of rules.

2. DEFINITIONS AND THREE PROVED ESTIMATES

Write r=-rho, L=1+log m, mu=E max_(i<=m) Z_i for iid standard Gaussians,
alpha=mu/(m-1), w=alpha^2(m-1), beta^2=w/m. For an actual assignment f put
 p_i=E f_i, B_i=E[X f_i], W=||B||_F^2, delta=||p-U||^2,
 U=(1/m,...,1/m), Delta=w-W, D=delta+r Delta.
Let F_p(B) be the maximum linear assignment value with prescribed masses p,
 R=(w+W)/2-F_U(B), and d=dist_F(B,regular centroid orbit).
R is nonnegative by covariance concavity and permutation averaging.

We give the exact three interfaces, with their full current reconstructions.
These are statements about arbitrary fractional measurable assignments.
They do not assume existence, stationarity, purity or interface regularity
of a full-noise optimizer.

3. THE COMPLETE ASSEMBLY AND RIGIDITY

If f is noncompetitive, the theorem's inequality is already strict.
Otherwise A applies. If d>1/m, Hermite orthogonality and C give exactly
 S_-r(f)-S_-r(g_m)
 >=D[1-CrL^3-Cr^2 m^(7/4)L^(7/2)].
For every 0<r<=1/(m-1), the losses are bounded by constants times
L^3/m and L^(7/2)/m^(1/4), which tend to zero. Choose m large enough to
make their sum at most one half. Since D=rJ>0 in this exterior, this is a
strict contradiction to competitiveness.

Thus d<=1/m, and B applies with the single fixed choice A=1. Competitiveness
forces delta=R=0. The finite covariance-gap theorem then gives d=0, so B is
a regular centroid matrix with ||B||^2=w. Its pointwise linear winner is
unique outside a Gaussian-null set. The nonnegative routing loss
 max_i B_i.X-sum_i f_i(X)B_i.X
has expectation w-||B||^2=0. Hence f is that regular indicator assignment
almost surely. Conversely, orthogonal transformations and relabelings of
a regular assignment leave its noise value unchanged.

This exhausts all assignments; there is no annular gap between the branches.
All constants depend on fixed absolute mass/row boxes or on A=1. Taking
the maximum of their finitely many eventual thresholds gives one absolute
m0, independent of f,N,r. The endpoint r=1/(m-1) is included; r=0 is excluded
from rigidity, as it must be. This proves the Gaussian theorem.

5. DISCRETE AND ALGORITHMIC CONSEQUENCES

For every fixed m>=m0, fixed rho in [-1/(m-1),0), and epsilon>0,
Isaksson--Mossel's transfer gives low-degree influence thresholds independent
of voter count under which every simplex-valued voting rule has agreement
at least the limiting plurality value minus epsilon. Masses need not be
balanced. This is the negative Plurality Is Stablest consequence for every
sufficiently large alphabet.

Put s_m(rho)=S_rho(g_m) and a_m=1-s_m(-1/(m-1)). The credited
Garcia-Soriano--Miyauchi endpoint analysis identifies this as the
Frieze--Jerrum rounding ratio. For every fixed m>=m0 and epsilon>0,
randomized polynomial-time rounding achieves a_m-epsilon in expectation,
or with high probability by repetition. Under the Unique Games Conjecture,
achieving a_m+epsilon is NP-hard for nonnegative rational weighted MAX-m-CUT.
Only the hardness statement is UGC-conditional. No deterministic
derandomization or numerical m0 is asserted.

6. WHY THIS IS A SUBSTANTIAL TARGET, AND THE CLAIM LIMITS

The original Gaussian/Plurality questions were formulated around 2004 and
recorded by Isaksson--Mossel. KKMO's 2007 large-alphabet hardness result has
an additive O(log log m/m^2) residual; for a fixed m that residual does not
vanish by taking smaller influences. The theorem above removes that exact/
asymptotic gap for every fixed m beyond one absolute threshold.
