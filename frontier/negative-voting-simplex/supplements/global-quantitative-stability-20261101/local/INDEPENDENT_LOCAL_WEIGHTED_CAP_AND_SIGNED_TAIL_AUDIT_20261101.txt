1. Exact cancellation, independently derived
--------------------------------------------
Write p=E f, B_i=E[X f_i], let f0 be the affine maximizer for score B
with prescribed masses p, and write M_i=E[X f0_i], v=f-f0. Then

 E v=0,
 E[X v]=B-M,
 Lroute=F_p(B)-||B||^2=<B,M-B> >= 0.

The last inequality follows because f is a feasible competitor in F_p(B).
It uses no stationarity and no purity of f. The source's string
"=<B,M-B>>=0" denotes the equality followed by >=0, not Lroute=0.

For rho=-r, qB=T_{-r} Pi_{>=2} f0, and
u=T_{sqrt(r)} Pi_{odd>=3} v, Hermite orthogonality gives

 S(f)-S(f0)
 =r[2Lroute+||M-B||^2]+2<v,qB>
   +||T_{sqrt(r)} Pi_{even>=2}v||^2-||u||^2.

Because <v,T_{sqrt(r)}u>=||u||^2, dropping only the displayed
nonnegative even square gives

 S(f)-S(f0)
 >=r||M-B||^2+||u||^2
   +2[r Lroute+<v,qB-T_{sqrt(r)}u>].                 (A1)

The masses really must match here; they do by definition of f0. The
bounded-simplex assumption gives sum_i v_i^2<=2 pointwise, hence
||u||<=sqrt(2) r^(3/2), independently of dimension or label masses.

Let B0 be the aligned regular centroid matrix, q0=T_{-r}Pi_{>=2}g,
and a=qB-q0-T_{sqrt(r)}u. At a point with old affine winner i, set
c_ij=(B_i-B_j).x+h_i-h_j, and delta_l_i=(B_i-B0_i).x+h_i.
The exact regular potential difference is

 (T_{-r}g)_j-(T_{-r}g)_i
 =r k_ij (B0_i-B0_j).x.

Its first chaos contributes r(B0_i-B0_j).x, so

 r c_ij+q0_j-q0_i
 =r k_ij c_ij+r(1-k_ij)(delta_l_i-delta_l_j).         (A2)

This identity uses the difference of any two utilities, and does not
require i to win the regular diagram. Swapping the pair in the exact
utility integral proves the same positive divided-difference bounds on
either side of the tie. Thus using it on the current affine cells is valid.

For k_ij>=kappa, |k_ij-1|<=e, and
psi_i=kappa^{-1}[e |delta_l_i|+|a_i|/r], (A2) gives

 r c_ij+(q0+a)_j-(q0+a)_i
 >=r kappa(c_ij-psi_i-psi_j).

Keep j=i exactly zero. Sum against arbitrary probabilities f_j(x), then
bound negative contributions using kappa<=1. Since
E sum_j f_j c_ij=Lroute, this proves

 r Lroute+<v,q0+a> >=-r E max(0,max_{j!=i}(psi_i+psi_j-c_ij)).

No indicator-valued f, interface, differentiability, or local topology
of f occurs in this argument. This checks the most important scope claim.

2. The damping moments do not hide an ambient determinant
--------------------------------------------------------
The relevant projection P0 has rank m-1 even when N is arbitrarily large.
For eta=O(r^2 L), kappa^{-1}=exp(1+eta|P0 X|^2). Under any convex
Gaussian facet restriction, the linear-tilt variance bound implies

 E exp(t.Y)<=exp(t.EY+|t|^2/2).

Integrating an independent standard Gaussian auxiliary vector in the
range of P0 yields, for 0<b<1/2,

 E exp(b|P0Y|^2)
 <=(1-2b)^(-(m-1)/2)
   exp(b|P0 EY|^2/(1-2b)).                         (A3)

With q=C1 L and b=q eta, b*m=O(L^2/m) on r<=1/(m-1). Thus all fixed
multiples of q needed in Holder have uniformly bounded damping moments,
once m is sufficiently large. No factor involving N appears.

For W=log sum exp X_i+log sum exp(-X_i), each X_i has bounded variance.
On a containing hyperplane at normal offset O(sqrt L), its mean is
O(sqrt L). Union Gaussian tails give ||W||_q<=C(L+sqrt q). Restricting
to a facet of probability at least m^{-C} costs a constant at exponent
C' L. Therefore e/r, with the explicit e of source 1a489344, has q-norm
O(L^(3/2)); its exponential has bounded moments because r L^(3/2)->0.
The normal derivatives introduce only fixed-degree polynomials and the

3. Explicit repair of the facet-probability construction
-------------------------------------------------------
Normalize C=B/alpha and a=h/alpha. Conditioned singular values and the
positive mass box give |a_i-a_j|<=C sqrt L, hence |a_i|<=C sqrt L in
mean-zero gauge. Fix ij and its containing hyperplane. Let x*=d n be
its closest point to zero, with |d|<=C sqrt L, and Y a standard Gaussian
on n-perp. The normalized scores C_k Y all have bounded variances.

Set z=T P(e_i+e_j), v=C^+ z, where T=C_* sqrt L. Then

 C v=z,    (C_i-C_j).v=0,    |v|<=C T.

Thus v is tangent to the pair hyperplane. It raises the i and j utilities
equally, by T relative to every other label, regardless of all the other
offsets. The event E={max_k |C_k Y|<=C_3 sqrt L} has probability >=1/2
by a union bound. For C_* large, x*+v+E lies in the actual ij facet,
because every pre-shift utility difference at x* is O(sqrt L).

Gaussian Cauchy--Schwarz/Cameron--Martin gives

 gamma_{n-perp}(E+v)>=gamma_{n-perp}(E)^2 exp(-|v|^2)>=m^{-C}.

This proves the needed intrinsic probability, including for every pair
and every extra ambient dimension. It avoids any claim that ||a|| is
O(sqrt L), and supplies the explicit missing construction behind the
terse paragraph in 5f2ef775, Section 1.

 qB-q0=T_t vD,       ||vD||<=C r^2 L^(5/2)D.          (A4)

 ||vu||<=(sqrt(r)/t)^3||u||<=C r^(3/2)L^(3/2)||u||.   (A5)

Combining (A3)--(A5), Holder at fixed multiples of q=C1L, and the offset
bound ||h||<=CLD gives

 (sum_i ||psi_i||_q^2)^(1/2)
 <=C[r L^(5/2)D+sqrt(r)L^(3/2)||u||].

The facet estimate sums label norms with the graph degrees, not with m
unweighted facet probabilities. Conditional OU restriction gives

 sum_{e=ij} w_e E_e(psi_i^2+psi_j^2)
 <=C[r^2L^5D^2+rL^3||u||^2].                        (A6)

For theta=C2 L^4(rD+sqrt(r)||u||), the normalized forcing v_i=psi_i/theta
therefore has vector Lq norm <=1 and bounded weighted facet square sum.

These bounds follow separately from the two nonnegative summands in
theta. There is no unbalanced inverse r left when r is arbitrarily

5. Independent all-amplitude cap lemma
-------------------------------------
For a conditioned affine fan, suppose a nonnegative forcing theta*v has:
(i) sum_i||v_i||_q^2<=1 for q=C1L;
(ii) sum_e w_e E_e(v_i^2+v_j^2)<=C;
(iii) on every pair plane and normal shifts |s|<=1, the normal supremum
of |v_i|+|partial_n v_i| has L32 norm <=m^K; and full-space L32 norms
are polynomial in m.
The structured forcing above satisfies these conditions. The absolute
values in it are harmless: a.e. derivatives are bounded by derivatives
of the underlying analytic factors, and their normal suprema follow
from the first two derivatives of those factors.

For moderate theta in [m^{-B},m^{-5/4}], separate source and target
rewards using (a+b-c)_+<=[(2a-c)_++(2b-c)_+]/2. A one-label offset
shift has exact entry or exit measure <=Cs for s<=c/m. This follows
from the bounded weighted degree along that offset interval; the
Gaussian translation changes the rare mass by a bounded factor.

For a nonnegative cost with measure{c<=s}<=Cs, a reward of Lq norm V
has capped integral <=C V^(2q/(q+1))+V^q/(c/m)^(q-1). Summing labels
with the vector Lq norm gives

 C m^(1/(q+1)) theta^(2q/(q+1))
   +(c/m)(C theta m/c)^q.                            (A7)

The first term divided by theta^2 is <=exp((1+2B)/C1) up to an absolute
constant; the second is negligible uniformly on theta<=m^{-5/4}.
The constant can depend on the fixed B. It need not tend to one.

For theta<=m^{-B}, put M=theta^{-1/8}. On good projected points, a
positive pair reward has width at most C theta M/alpha. Taylor expansion
and the one-dimensional Gaussian density ratio give an error at most
m^K theta^3 M^3. A projection outside the actual facet but inside a
source cell after displacement lies in one of at most m third-label
strips. Conditioned score rank gives the tangential altitudes >=c alpha,
so those strips have measure at most m^K theta M. Their contribution
has the same m^K theta^3 M^3 bound. This accounts for junctions.

Bad projected points contribute at most
m^K theta E[W 1_{W>M}]<=m^K theta M^{-31}; remote normal distances >1
contribute at most m^K theta^32. The good main term is bounded by
C theta^2 sum_e w_e E_e(v_i^2+v_j^2). The use of sums, rather than
differences, is correct for the two nonnegative source/target rewards.

The exponents are

 theta^3 M^3=theta^(21/8),
 theta M^(-31)=theta^(39/8),
 theta^32=theta^32.

Therefore the complete cap is <=C theta^2+C m^K theta^(21/8).
Choosing fixed B>8K/5 absorbs the remainder below m^{-B}; (A7) covers
the remainder of the allowed amplitude interval. The choice of B is
not a lower amplitude restriction. In particular, theta=e^{-m^100}
is easier in the small-amplitude estimate than theta=m^{-B}.

This identifies why the usual rare/tall measurable forcing objection
does not falsify this cap: it is not an L2-only cap. The normal smoothing,
fixed L32 bounds, and the facet quadratic norm are all being used.

6. Full signed affine tail: independent checks of the delicate factors
--------------------------------------------------------------------
The normal Mehler row satisfies exactly

 ||M_t(a,.)||_2^2=(1-t^4)^(-1/2) exp(t^2 a^2/(1+t^2)).

Tangential L2-to-LQ hypercontractivity with Q=C1L and t=c/sqrt L,
followed by polynomial-probability restriction, gives a dimension-free
facet norm. Composing with a contraction in any one fixed normal
direction changes only a two-dimensional normal calculation. For
derivatives use the exact commutation identity

 nabla^j T_t v=(2t)^j T_{2t}(nabla^j T_{1/2}v), j=1,2.

Hermite orthogonality bounds the L2 norm on the right before T_{2t},
because sup_k (k)_j 4^{-k}<infinity. Thus the derivative gain is t^j,
with Hilbert--Schmidt tensor norms and no ambient-dimension loss.

For q_i^0=T_{-r}Pi_{>=3}g_i, the exact three-factor representation is

 q_i^0=T_t v_i^0,
 v_i^0=T_{-r/t^2}Pi_{>=3}T_tg_i.

Since ||T_tg_i||_2<=m^{-1/(1+t^2)}<=C/m,
||v_i^0||_2<=C(rL)^3/m. This preserves the rare-label factor 1/m.
Permutation oddness in the ij normal coordinate gives

 q_i^0-q_j^0=(X_i-X_j)P_e,
 ||P_e||_{Lq(facet)}<=C r^3 L^(5/2)/m,
 ||nabla P_e||_{Lq(facet)}<=C r^3 L^2/m.             (A8)

On the current facet, alpha(X_i-X_j)=-delta_l_e. Differentiating the
original quotient, rather than a facet-only identity, gives exactly

 grad(q_i^0-q_j^0).V
 =-[P_e l_e+P_e delta_B_e.V+delta_l_e grad(P_e).V]/alpha.

This is the cancellation that saves an otherwise non-small reference
term. The relative current-potential change is
q-q0=T_t v, ||v||<=C r^3 L^(7/2)d, by the same trace calculation as
(A4) with cutoff 3. On the mass path replace d by d+||p-U||.

Choose c by L_graph c=div(w E(q_i-q_j)) and set u=q-c. Then
div(w E u_e)=0, cancelling the entire coefficient of the balancing
offset acceleration. Energy Cauchy plus the gap c/L gives
||c||<=C r^3L^4d on the balanced path.

For the coherent field V=-B^+(ZX+k), the second derivative is

 2<f',T_{-r}Pi_{>=3}f'>
 +2 sum_e w_e E[l_e grad u_e.V+u_e Z_e.V
                         +u_e l_e div_gamma V].

The material derivative of 1/ell_e cancels the normal component of
surface divergence, since n_e.DV.n_e=-ell_e'/ell_e. The single global
affine field transports every junction coherently; cyclic label test
differences cancel the junction terms. Extra coordinates in Z are allowed
in this field and are integrated, not fixed at a point.

The boundary quadratic term has absolute value <=C(rL)^3 N_l
<=C r^3L^4||Z||^2. Conditional fixed-degree moments give
||V||_q<=C sqrt L K, ||div_gamma V||_q<=CL K,
K<=CL||Z||/alpha. Together with (A8), the weighted Cauchy sums charge
the remaining terms by C r^3 L^8(1+d/alpha)||Z||^2; the printed L^10
is safe. No sqrt(sum_e w_e)=sqrt(m) factor is required.

Finite Hermite cutoffs preserve all symmetries and the quotient. Replacing
cutoff 3 by cutoff k gives geometric factors (r/t^2)^k and (rL)^k.
They are <1 for sufficiently large m. This justifies uniform convergence
of the potential, its first two parameter derivatives, and the adjoint,

7. Final local combination and its dependencies
------------------------------------------------
The cap inserted in (A1) gives

 S(f)-S(f0)>=r||M-B||^2-C r^3L^8(d^2+||p-U||^2),

because Cr^2L^8<1/2 absorbs the odd square uniformly on the voting
interval. The affine comparison supplies the term r[w-||M||^2]. Their
sum is exactly

 r[w-||M||^2]+r||M-B||^2
 =r[w-||B||^2-2Lroute]=2r R_p(B).

Using R_p>=R_U+h_U.(p-U), ||h_U||^2<=CL^2R_U, and the separately
supplied finite gap d^2<=C_A m sqrt L R_U leaves

 [1-C_A(rL^2+r^2mL^7+r^3L^10)]||p-U||^2
 +[3/2-C_A(r sqrt(m)L^13+r^2mL^11)]r R_U.

Each error decreases with r and vanishes at its largest allowed value
as m tends to infinity. This yields delta/2+rR_U for sufficiently large
m. No positive lower bound on r is needed.

Equality uses the balanced endpoint equality case, not the cap alone.
Once p=U and R_U=0, the endpoint and
2R_U=w-||M||^2+||M-B||^2 force regular M=B. The regular linear argmax
is unique almost surely; a fractional f attaining its first-moment
value must put full probability on that winner. This is the required
argument extending equality to arbitrary measurable fractional f.

8. Explicit admissible stress families
--------------------------------------
(a) Unequal-mass fractional contamination. Let f=(1-epsilon)g+epsilon e_1,
0<epsilon<=1/[2(m-1)]. Then p is in the mass box, B=(1-epsilon)B0,
and d=epsilon sqrt(w), which is inside A/m for large m. Write
Q=1/m-S_{-r}(g)>0. Direct bilinear expansion gives

 S(f)-S(g)=(2epsilon-epsilon^2)Q+epsilon^2(1-1/m).

The mass term is exactly delta=epsilon^2(1-1/m). This family produces
no small-r or unequal-mass failure. Its exact R_U is w epsilon^2/2.

(b) Arbitrarily rare and remote pure/fractional routing. Add an independent
Gaussian coordinate Y. Let A be any measurable event in Y, e.g. Y>T,
and let Pi transpose two labels. Put h=Pi g-g and
f=g+epsilon 1_A(Y)h, 0<=epsilon<=1. Every f is simplex-valued and balanced.
Its extra-coordinate centroids vanish, because E_X h=0. In the regular
span its centroid matrix is B=B0+epsilon a(Pi B0-B0), where a=P(A).
Thus d<=2epsilon a sqrt(w), arbitrarily small as T increases.

Let b=P(A(Y) and A(Y')) for the rho-correlated extra coordinates, and
Dpi=<Pi g,T_{-r}g>-S_{-r}(g)>0. Exact tensor-product expansion gives

 S(f)-S(g)=2Dpi epsilon(a-epsilon b)>=0,             (A9)

since b<=a. For epsilon=1 this is a pure partition with an arbitrarily
remote exceptional set; 0<epsilon<1 gives fractional routing. The event
can have arbitrary measurable geometry. The positivity of Dpi follows
pointwise from the exact reversed ordering of the regular negative-noise
potentials on the two swapped cells. For a nontrivial event and |rho|<1,
b<a, so the gap is strict. This directly defeats a natural rare/tall
counterexample attempt without invoking the theorem being audited.

(c) Dimension-opening score paths. For N>m-1, B(s)=[B0,s a] with a in
1-perp has full row rank m-1 for small s; its Gram matrix is
alpha^2 P+s^2 a a^T. Such a path rotates the row span at first order and
changes covariance at second order. The local arguments use B^+ and
Frobenius/HS norms and cover it. Since a centered m-row matrix has rank
at most m-1, there is no additional rank-opening singularity inside this
regular score ball. Rank-boundary objections belong to the exterior
arguments, not this local calculation.

9. Regular top-two margin, requested stability cross-check
--------------------------------------------------------
Let G be the gap between the largest and second largest of m independent
standard normals, mu=E max_i Z_i, alpha=mu/(m-1). Its exact density is

 f_G(t)=m(m-1) int phi(x+t)phi(x)Phi(x)^(m-2) dx
       =mu E exp(-t T-t^2/2), t>=0,

where T has density phi(x)^2Phi(x)^(m-2)/w0, w0=mu/[m(m-1)].
The tilted variance is <=1/2, and E T>=0 by reflection. Hence
E exp(-tT)<=exp(-t E T+t^2/4), proving f_G(t)<=mu for all t>=0.
The regular SCORE gap alpha G consequently has density <=m-1.

For any measurable simplex-valued f in any finite ambient dimension,
let i(X) be the regular winner, a(X)=1-f_{i(X)}(X), and epsilon=E a,
which is half the L1 distance to that regular assignment. The routing
loss obeys

 R0=E sum_j f_j(B0_i-B0_j).X >= E[a alpha G]
    >=epsilon^2/[2(m-1)].                         (A10)

The last step is the bathtub/layer-cake bound using 0<=a<=1 and the
score-gap sublevel estimate. It allows a to depend on every additional
coordinate and to be fractional. Equation (A10) is exact with the stated
constant and supplies the regular routing estimate needed by a possible
global quantitative stability successor. If that successor instead uses
the full L1 distance, replace epsilon by one-half that distance.
