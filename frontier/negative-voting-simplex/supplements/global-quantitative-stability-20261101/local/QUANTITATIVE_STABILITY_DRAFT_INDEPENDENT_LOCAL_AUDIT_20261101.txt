1. Regular top-two constant
--------------------------
For G=max Z_i-secondmax Z_i, the density is

 h_m(t)=m(m-1) int phi(x+t)phi(x)Phi(x)^(m-2) dx
       =mu E exp(-tT-t^2/2), t>=0,

where T has density proportional to phi^2 Phi^(m-2). Integration by
parts gives h_m(0)=mu. Its tilted potential has curvature >=2. Hence
K(t)=log E exp(-tT) has K''<=1/2; reflection gives ET>=0 and therefore
K'(0)<=0. It follows that

 h_m(t)<=mu exp(-t^2/4)<=mu.

Thus the score gap alpha G has density <=mu/alpha=m-1, uniformly over
its entire range, not just near zero. This calculation matches the draft
with no missing factor of m, two, or alpha.

2. Fractional and extra-coordinate routing
-----------------------------------------
For a regular winner i(X), put a(X)=1-f_i(X), epsilon=E a. Then
0<=a<=1 and epsilon=||f-g||_1/2 exactly, even for fractional f. The
inequality a<=1 is the only admissibility restriction used in the
layer-cake step. For every t>=0,

 E[a 1_{alpha G>t}]>=epsilon-P(alpha G<=t)
                         >=epsilon-(m-1)t.

Integrating the positive part over t proves

 E[a alpha G]>=epsilon^2/[2(m-1)].

No independence of a and G is asserted. The weight can depend arbitrarily
on all coordinates outside the regular span. Thus the finite-N and
measurable/fractional scope is correct.

3. Fourth-power conversion, constants included
-----------------------------------------------
Let B be the actual centroid matrix, B0 a closest regular centroid, and
d=||B-B0||. Its routing cost is exactly

 R0=E[max_i B0_i.X-sum_i f_i B0_i.X]=w-<B0,B>.

Combining the gap estimate with Cauchy gives

 epsilon^2<=2(m-1)R0<=2(m-1)sqrt(w)d.

Consequently

 ||f-g0||_1^4=16epsilon^4<=64(m-1)^2 w d^2.

Since w=mu^2/(m-1)=O(L/m), this is <=C mL d^2. Taking the orbit infimum
can only reduce the left side. The draft's numerical coefficient 64 is
correct. A coefficient 4 would instead correspond to half the L1 norm.

4. Covariance-to-score conversion
---------------------------------
For C=BB^T/alpha^2 on 1-perp and q=d/alpha, orthogonal Procrustes gives
q^2=sum_{j=1}^{m-1}(sqrt(lambda_j(C))-1)^2, including zero eigenvalues.
Meanwhile e^2=||C-P||_F^2=sum_j(lambda_j(C)-1)^2, so e>=q because
sqrt(lambda)+1>=1. This includes singular endpoints.

The function F(x)=x^2 min(1,c/(Lx)) is increasing for x>=0. Conditional
on the displayed initial-curvature estimate (8), replacing e by q is
therefore legitimate. The localized bound W/w=1+O(r/L) gives q<=C sqrt m.
It follows that

 R>=c alpha^2 q^2/(m^(3/2)L),
 d^2<=C m^(3/2)L R.

The radial check R=d^2/2 is exact. Extra-column score perturbations are
covered by Procrustes and do not add ambient-dimension factors.

5. Energy assembly and small r
-------------------------------
On the small-excess class E<=r^2/m, the local branch gives
E>=delta/2+rR. The exterior branch gives E>=D/2 and delta+rR<=3D.
Thus both imply E>=(delta+rR)/6. This also proves E>=0 on that class,
without assuming it at the start. If E>r^2/m, the saturated lower bound
in (T) follows by taking its absolute constant at most one.

Combining the preceding score/L1 conversion yields the proposed

 E>=c min{r^2/m, delta+r d1^4/(m^(5/2)L^2)}.

The three claimed small-excess consequences (S1), (S2), and (S3) follow
with absolute constants. Dividing by r in (S3) is allowed because the
theorem excludes r=0; every antecedent estimate is uniform for r>0.

The equality argument uses compactness only of the regular orbit in each
fixed finite dimension. It does not invoke existence of a full-noise
optimizer. Pointwise convergence of regular partitions away from tie
hyperplanes and dominated convergence prove Gaussian L1 compactness.
