PROPOSAL AND VERDICT

The proposed change is to replace the small-excess assumption
  E(f)<=r^2/m
by
  E(f)<=r/m^2,
where 0<r<=1/(m-1), without changing the preserved quantitative proof
with SHA d02043adb87c785f8d03af93a612c536e9713df06cef323d1232ca19bc919508.

The new covariance/proxy/exterior route is valid provided the separately
audited slack localization gives

  p_i in[1/(2m),3/(2m)], 0<=D<=C r/m^2,
  delta<=2D, |W-w|<=D/r.                                  (L)

The change requires one explicit hypothesis repair in the proxy citation:
the frozen manuscript's geometric lemma assumes E_rad<=C r L^2/m.
The new E_rad<=C L^2/m^2 does not imply that inequality at the actual
noise parameter r when r is much smaller than 1/m. Either re-run the
geometric-median proof below, or invoke that purely geometric lemma with
the separate auxiliary parameter r_geom=1/(m-1). Its conclusion does
not contain the actual noise parameter. This repair is sufficient.

After it, the exterior second-chaos and signed-tail estimates are valid
with the same constants and the same final error coefficients. They do
not use the old J=O(r/m) bound. The needed upper bound is J=O(m^-2),
used to obtain the uniform row lower bound and a small median gradient.

This is a proof review of the changed route, not independent validation
of the foundational mass-semiconcavity, local full-noise, hypercontractive,

1. NOTATION AND LOCALIZATION CONSEQUENCES

Let B_i=E[X f_i], W=||B||_F^2, U=(1/m,...,1/m),
delta=||p-U||^2, Delta=w-W, D=delta+r Delta, J=D/r, and R=R_U(B).
Put L=1+log m, alpha=mu/(m-1), w=alpha^2(m-1), beta^2=w/m.
All constants below are absolute and independent of r and finite N.

From (L), J<=C/m^2 and

  |W/w-1| <= (C/m^2)/(c L/m) <= C/(mL).                 (1)

Thus s=sqrt(W/w) is bounded above and below, and s^2=1+O(1/(mL)).
The actual fractional assignment satisfies the entropy row bound and
the exact operator inequality

  |B_i|<=p_i sqrt(2log(1/p_i))<=C alpha,
  B^T B<=p_max I<=C I/m.                                 (2)

The first estimate and (1) provide a fixed absolute normalized row cap
for Bhat=B/s. This is essential: the cap cannot be allowed to grow with
m, as shown by the rank-one counterexample in the covariance audit.

The offset certificate uses only this cap and the fixed mass window.
Feasibility of the actual assignment and the balanced dual offsets give

  R<=Delta/2+CL sqrt(R delta)
   <=Delta/2+R/2+C L^2 delta,
  0<=R<=Delta+C L^2 delta<=Delta+delta/r=J.                (3)

For the last inequality choose sufficiently large m so CrL^2<=1.
The argument remains valid when Delta<0. It uses no sign assumption on
the total excess E(f), no stationarity, and no optimizer attainment.

2. THE UNIFORM ROW LOWER BOUND AT THE NEW SCALE

The exact scaling identity gives

  R=w(s-1)^2/2+s R_U(Bhat),
  R_U(Bhat)<=C R<=C/m^2.                                 (4)

The normalized global one-row theorem, with the fixed cap from (2),
therefore yields

  max_i ||Bhat_i|^2/beta^2-1|^2
      <=C (m^2/L)R_U(Bhat)<=C/L,
  max_i ||Bhat_i|^2/beta^2-1|<=C/sqrt(L)=o(1).            (5)

Multiplying by s^2 only adds O(1/(mL)), so the actual rows have the
same uniform estimate. This proves the needed lower bound directly;
one must not import the old actual-minimizer estimate with its smaller
right side C sqrt(mr/L) under the new excess threshold.

3. SUMMED RADIAL COST AND THE MEDIAN CONSTRUCTION

The normalized summed-row theorem and (4) give

  E_rad=sum_i(|Bhat_i|-beta)^2
       <=C L^2 R_U(Bhat)<=C L^2 R<=C L^2/m^2.             (6)

Let u_i=Bhat_i/|Bhat_i|. Equations (2) and (5) imply

  ||m^-1 sum_i u_i u_i^T||op
     <=C ||Bhat^T Bhat||op/(m beta^2)<=C/L.              (7)

On the row span, define Phi(c)=m^-1 sum_i |Bhat_i-c|. For a fixed
sufficiently small c0>0 and |c|<=c0 beta, all distances are comparable
to beta, and the normalized direction frame is bounded by
C/L+C|c|^2/beta^2. Thus

  Hess Phi(c)>=c1 beta^-1 I                              (8)

for sufficiently large m. Centering of Bhat gives the independent
gradient estimate

  |grad Phi(0)|
    <=C ||Bhat||op sqrt(E_rad)/(m beta^2)
    <=C sqrt(m) sqrt(E_rad)/L
    <=C/sqrt(m)=o(1).                                    (9)

Consequently the radial derivative is positive on the boundary of the
small beta-ball. Phi has an interior median c there, with

  |c|<=C beta |grad Phi(0)|,
  sum_i(Bhat_i-c)/|Bhat_i-c|=0,
  m|c|^2<=C E_rad/L.                                     (10)

Define Z_i=beta(Bhat_i-c)/|Bhat_i-c|. Then, in the same row span,

  sum_i Z_i=0, |Z_i|=beta,
  ||Z-Bhat||_F^2<=C E_rad<=C L^2 R.                       (11)

The radial identity in (4) also gives ||B-Bhat||^2<=2R, so

  E_Z=||Z-B||_F^2<=C L^2 R<=C L^2 J.                     (12)

This is the entire geometric requirement in the exterior proof. It
does not need Z to be actual centroids, a routing optimizer, full rank,
or pair separated. Duplicate test rows are permitted.

If d=dist(B,regular centroid orbit)>1/m, set q=d/alpha and

  c/sqrt(L)<=q<=C sqrt(m).

In particular the exterior floor is independent of the old r-dependent

5. SECOND-CHAOS CERTIFICATE: NO OLD UPPER BOUND FOR J IS USED

Use the exact equal-row matrix Z from (11), V=max_i Z_i.X, and its
regular counterpart V_*. The existing equal-threshold Gaussian maximum
comparison applies at this score scale without a rank hypothesis. It
gives EV<=w and, for T>=1,

  (EV_*^2-EV^2)_+
     <=C beta T(w-EV)+C beta^2 m exp(-T^2/2).             (14)

For the actual assignment's nonnegative routing loss
c_f=V-sum_i f_i Z_i.X, the two nonnegative quantities Ec_f and w-EV
have sum

  w-<Z,B>=(Delta+E_Z)/2<=C L^2 J.                         (15)

There is no need to assume Delta>=0 or exact balance. The pointwise
quadratic inequality V^2-sum_i f_i(Z_i.X)^2<=2V c_f and a unit-marginal
Gaussian union bound give

  EV^2-E sum_i f_i(Z_i.X)^2
   <=2beta T Ec_f+C beta^2 m(T^2+1)exp(-T^2/2).            (16)

At T=sqrt(32L), beta T<=C L/m. The numerator of the quadratic tensor
test is exactly E sum_i f_i(Z_i.X)^2-beta^2; the equality uses only
sum_i p_i=1. Its denominator is at most
D_*=alpha^4(m-1)(m-2)/m, since tr(ZZ^T)=w and rank(ZZ^T)<=m-1.
The regular coefficient T_*/D_*<=Cm then gives

  A2(f)-A2(g)>=-C L^3 J-C L^2 exp(-16L).                 (17)

Equation (13) absorbs the absolute tail into C J, proving

  A2(f)-A2(g)>=-C L^3 J.                                 (18)

Every use of localization in this calculation is displayed in (12),
(13), and (15). In particular no factor R<=C r/m, J<=C r/m, or
E_rad<=C rL^2/m is used after the proxy is constructed.

6. ENTIRE SIGNED TAIL: THE TWO CASES STILL CLOSE

For the closest regular rule g0, let epsilon=||f-g0||_1/2. The exact
regular routing identity is R0=(Delta+d^2)/2>=0. The scalar top-two
margin bound gives epsilon<=min(1,C sqrt(m R0)). Rare-label
hypercontractivity uses only p_i<=3/(2m), and yields the entire-tail
estimate

  |H_r(f)-H_r(g0)|<=C r^3 L^3 sqrt(epsilon)/m.             (19)

If J>=d^2, then R0<=J and

  |Hdiff|/J <= C r^3 L^3 m^(-3/4) J^(-3/4)
             <=C r^3 m^(3/2)L^(27/8),                    (20)

where the last step uses only (13). If J<d^2, then R0<=d^2, and with
d=alpha q and J>=c q/m^3,

  |Hdiff|/J <=C r^3 m^(7/4)L^(13/4)q^(-1/2)
             <=C r^3 m^(7/4)L^(7/2).                     (21)

No old upper bound on J occurs in either calculation. The second bound
dominates the first, and rotation preserves the regular Hermite weights.

7. THE FINAL SIGNED EXTERIOR COMPARISON AND NEW MODULUS

The exact Hermite decomposition, (18), and (20)-(21) imply

  E(f)>=D[1-C rL^3-C r^2 m^(7/4)L^(7/2)]>=D/2             (22)

for sufficiently large m, uniformly in 0<r<=1/(m-1). The largest error
coefficients remain O(L^3/m) and O(L^(7/2)/m^(1/4)). They are unchanged
by the new small-excess threshold.

The local theorem for d<=1/m has the same hypotheses as before because
the actual centroids and fixed mass window are unchanged. It gives
E>=delta/2+rR there. Thus, assuming (L) from the separate slack audit,
the full small-excess argument still proves

  E>=(delta+rR)/6  whenever E<=r/m^2.

Together with the already reviewed covariance-to-rule estimate
d1(f)^4<=C m^(5/2)L^2 R, the proposed sharpened conclusion is

  E(f)>=c min{r/m^2,
               delta+r d1(f)^4/[m^(5/2)L^2]}.             (23)

This is a genuine improvement for small r. After division by r, its
threshold term is 1/m^2 rather than r/m. For fixed balanced f, taking
r down to zero therefore leaves a nonzero first-moment stability
modulus. This limit observation is conditional on the same proof inputs
and uses no additional uniformity in N or m beyond the displayed one.
