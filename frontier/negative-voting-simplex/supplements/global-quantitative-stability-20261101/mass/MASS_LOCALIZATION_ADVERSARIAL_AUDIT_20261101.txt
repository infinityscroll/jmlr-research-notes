1. THE FRACTIONAL VALUE AND DIMENSION REDUCTION, EXPLICITLY
Fix m>=3 and d=m-1. For a prescribed positive p, define V_rel,N(p) as
supremum of sum_i |E[X f_i]|^2 over simplex-valued f with masses p.
The feasible set in (L-infinity(gamma_d))^m is weak-star compact: it is a
bounded weak-star closed set, with the mass constraints and pointwise
simplex constraints weak-star closed. Each first-moment coordinate is a
weak-star continuous functional because x_j is integrable. Consequently
V_rel,d is attained. This is finite first-moment attainment only.

For ANY f in R^N its rows b_i sum to zero, hence span a subspace S of
dimension k<=m-1. Condition f on the projection of X onto S. The masses
are unchanged and its first-moment rows are precisely the original b_i.
The resulting k-dimensional fractional assignment can be embedded in
R^d, and any d-dimensional assignment embeds in R^N when N>=d. Therefore
V_rel,N(p)=V_rel,d(p), uniformly in finite N>=d. No norm bound involving N
or infinite-dimensional compactness enters this reduction.

Here is a direct proof that V_rel,d is also attained by a pure assignment.
It simultaneously validates the pair-separation step in12dc for fractional
maximizers. Let f be a relaxed maximizer, b_i its rows, fix i!=j, and put
q=f_i+f_j, a=p_i, b=p_j. Choose a unit e perpendicular to b_i+b_j;
d>=2 makes this possible. Under the weighted measure q(x)dgamma_d(x),
the one-dimensional variable t=e.x has total mass a+b, zero unnormalized
first moment, and density bounded by M=phi(0), since 0<=q<=1.
Reassign all q to label i on its upper a-tail and to j on its lower
b-tail. The threshold has no atom. If it is s, the density bound gives

  int_upper(t-s)q dgamma >= a^2/(2M),
  int_lower(s-t)q dgamma >= b^2/(2M).

The two conditional means thus differ by at least(a+b)/(2M). Because
their combined moment is zero, the difference of their unnormalized
moments is at least ab/M. Other labels and the vector sum b_i+b_j are
unchanged. Since |b_i|^2+|b_j|^2=(|b_i+b_j|^2+|b_i-b_j|^2)/2,
maximality forces

  |b_i-b_j| >= ab/M = sqrt(2pi) p_i p_j.

This comparison is feasible even when q is fractional; no purity premise
was used. In particular every pair of maximizing rows is distinct.

A relaxed first-moment maximizer also maximizes its own linear score:
if a mass-p assignment h had sum_i b_i.E[X h_i]>sum_i|b_i|^2, then
sum_i|E[X h_i]|^2 >= 2sum_i b_i.E[X h_i]-sum_i|b_i|^2 would exceed V_rel.
The finite offset dual for the linear score has a minimizer: its objective
is at least max_i h_i-sum_i p_i h_i, which controls the offset range by
p_min. Distinct rows imply that every pairwise affine tie has Gaussian
measure zero. The dual minimizer therefore has a unique winning label
a.e., and differentiation of offsets yields exactly the masses p.
Every maximizing coupling must route entirely to that unique winner.
Thus the relaxed maximizer is pure. In particular

  V_pure,N(p)=V_rel,N(p)=V_rel,d(p)  (N>=m-1).

This supplies the omitted elementary bridge in11a Section2/12dc Section2.
It is not necessary to assume stationarity or purity of the full-noise f.

2. COVARIANCE REPRESENTATION AND WHAT COMPACTNESS REALLY DOES
Square completion with centered score rows B gives

  sup_f ||M_f||^2 = sup_B [2 F_p(B)-||B||^2]
                 = max_(C>=0 on 1-perp) [2F_p(C)-tr C].

The factor dimension is d; F_p(C) depends only on the centered Gaussian
score covariance. Coercivity F_p(C)<=sqrt(tr C) follows from ||M_f||<=1.
At a true optimizing C=BB^T, any maximizing coupling satisfies B=M_f by
square completion. The previous pair-replacement bound therefore applies
to every true optimizer, including rank-deficient ones.

For regularized C>=eps I on H, testing C*+eps I gives
0<=V-V_eps<=eps(m-1). The test is valid because an optimal coupling can be
extended by independent mean-zero Gaussian noise, leaving its score
expectation unchanged; the new supremum cannot decrease. Together with
coercivity and joint continuity this proves CLOSED-GRAPH convergence:
if eps_n->0 and(p_n,C_n)->(p,C), then C optimizes at p.

This does NOT assert convergence to the optimizer fiber at the varying
p_n. The clarification6d19 states the correct weaker assertion. It is
sufficient: if a regularized edge bound with fixed slack failed for
arbitrarily small eps at some p in the compact mass box, a subsequence
would converge to a true optimizer violating the continuous edge bound.

The quantifiers are
  for every sufficiently large m, there exists eps0(m,c,C0)>0,
  for all p in the m-dimensional mass box and 0<eps<=eps0, ... .
All subsequent estimates have coefficient K(c,C0)L_m^2 independent of
eps and m. Taking eps->0 separately for EACH m preserves that coefficient.
There is no assertion of a compact parameter space across all m and no
need for an m-independent eps0. This is why graph compactness does not
secretly destroy the sufficiently-large-m conclusion.

3. QUANTITATIVE GRAPH INPUT, CHECKED WITHOUT ASSUMING CONNECTEDNESS
On c/m<=p_i<=C0/m, the preceding pair bound and entropy cap transfer to
regularized optimizers with

  sqrt(2pi)c^2/(2m^2) <= ell_ij <=4C0 sqrt(2L_m)/m,
  L_m=1+log(m/c).

At positive regularized covariance the sites are affinely independent,
so the translated simplex fan has all pair facets. Their Gaussian masses
are positive. Price differentiation yields F_C=L/2, and the KKT sign is
L<=I_H, not the reverse. Hence weighted degrees are <=1. No stationarity
of the arbitrary competitor f is used; these are optimized V_eps scores.

For a label subset S with k=min(|S|,m-|S|), the Gaussian perimeter of its
finite polyhedral union is sum_cut mu_ij. Gaussian isoperimetry and the
mass lower bound give sum_cut mu_ij>=2phi(0)ck/m. Dividing by ell_max
produces sum_cut w_ij>=h0 k with h0=c/[4C0 sqrt(pi L_m)]. Deleting edges
of weight below m^-4 loses at most k m^-3 across any cut, hence for large m
the retained graph has conductance at least h0/2. Its connectedness is a
CONCLUSION of this inequality. Counting-measure Cheeger, with dmax<=1,
gives exactly

  lambda_2(L_str)>=c^2/[128 C0^2 pi L_m].

Both cells lie in opposite winning halfspaces, so |h_ij/ell_ij|^2<=2L_m.
On a retained edge its intrinsic tangential probability is
pi_ij=mu_ij/phi(h_ij/ell_ij)>=pi c^2/m^6. This is not confused with a
cell mass. The Ehrhard coefficient a(p)=1+Phi^-1(p)p/phi(Phi^-1(p)) obeys
1/a(p)<=8exp(3/2)[1+log(1/p)]. Thus on retained edges
1/a(pi_ij)<=56exp(3/2)L_m. All m-dependence is explicit here.

The exact cycle-corrected Hessian inequality ofd261 is

 -4F_CC[A,A] >= sum_e w_e{x_e^2+a(pi_e)(2h'_e-q_e x_e)^2}.

It cannot be replaced by the raw covariance deficit before cycle
subtraction. I checked the stated residual algebra: choose the weighted
cycle representative with partial_3^T D lambda=0, put
r_e=z_e-N_e^(-T)eta_e, and expand. The two cross terms give -2 CYC,
the final quadratic term gives+CYC, leaving exactly K=sum r_e^T E_e r_e.
The facet identity N_e 1=w_e P_e m_e then gives
r_e.P_e m_e=-(b_i-b_j)-z_e.(I-P_e)m_e. Applying Ehrhard to these corrected
vectors produces the offset term with the displayed factor2. This
retains one global offset derivative and introduces no edgewise freedom.

On each retained edge, elementary weighted Cauchy yields
4(h'_e)^2 <=(a(pi_e)^-1+q_e^2)[x_e^2+a(pi_e)(2h'_e-q_e x_e)^2].
After summing and applying the retained spectral gap this gives

 ||D_C h[A]||^2 <= K(c,C0)L_m^2[-F_CC[A,A]],
 K=128 C0^2 pi[56exp(3/2)+2]/c^2.

No unused ambient coordinate, full-rank true optimizer, or unproved
uniform graph weight lower bound is present.

4. CONSTRAINED ENVELOPE AND THE ACTIVE-SET ISSUE
For fixed m,eps>0, strict covariance Hessian plus compactness gives a
strong-concavity constant kappa_(m,eps)>0 on the bounded optimizer set.
It may tend to zero with m or eps. It is used ONLY to obtain Lipschitz
regularity of C_eps(p), not in the final semiconcavity coefficient.

On a mass segment p(t)=p0+tu, the unique optimizer is Lipschitz. The
mass derivative of V_eps is g_p, and is itself Lipschitz for fixed eps.
At an a.e. differentiability point the two constrained variational
inequalities yield
  E0=-g_CC[C',C'] <= B0=g_Cp[C',u].
No interior Euler equation is substituted. In particular B0>=E0>=0.
Since g=2F-tr, the quantitative graph bound gives
  B0^2<=2K L_m^2 |u|^2 E0.
Hence B0<=2K L_m^2|u|^2, while g_pp=-2L^-1 is negative semidefinite.
Therefore V_eps''<=2K L_m^2|u|^2 a.e. The first derivative is Lipschitz,
so integration cannot hide a positive singular second-derivative measure.

Uniform convergence0<=V-V_eps<=eps(m-1) passes the integrated inequality
to V without differentiability of a singular optimizer. At U, permutation
symmetry and uniqueness of the regularized optimizer give centered offsets
zero. Integrating from U, then taking eps->0 and using balanced V(U)=w,
gives V(p)<=w+A_m|p-U|^2 with A_m=K L_m^2. This is precisely the bound
needed. No fiber continuity, rank persistence, or uniform optimizer
Lipschitz constant has been smuggled into the limiting step.

5. EVERY COMPETITIVE FRACTIONAL ASSIGNMENT, INCLUDING W>w
The initial45e proof uses only scalar Gaussian rearrangement and Hermite
parity. Its scalar Bregman bound includes p_i=0 or1. Its limit
m^2 delta<=2-2gamma+o(1)<1 therefore places EVERY competitive assignment
inside the fixed window c=.01,C0=2, for all sufficiently large m.
The second-chaos inequality251 is also universal: conditional Jensen
and weighted Cauchy give A2(f)>=(W-P)_+^2/(2P). The positive part is
essential, and its convex perspective supplies the tangent even for W<P.
The algebraic formula for Delta2=A2(g)-(mw-1)^2/(2m) was checked, including
its two negative terms. The centered maximum variance O(1/log m) makes
Delta2=O(1/m), rather than O(log^2 m/m).

For delta=|p-U|^2 and D=delta+r(w-W), the value bound gives

  D=delta+r(w-V)+r(V-W)
   >=(1-rA_m)delta+r(V-W)
   >=delta/2+r(V-W)>=0

once rA_m<=1/2. This requires only r<=1/(m-1) and a sufficiently large
m, since A_m=O(log^2 m). It is valid regardless of the sign of w-W.
Retaining degree two then gives D<=C r^2/m and delta<=2D. If W>w,
then delta>D and W-w=(delta-D)/r lies between0 and D/r. If W<=w,
then0<=w-W=(D-delta)/r<=D/r. Thus |W-w|<=D/r in BOTH cases.
The fractional value bridge in Section1 proves V-W>=0 for the actual f.
There is no additional minimizing, stationarity, or interface premise.
