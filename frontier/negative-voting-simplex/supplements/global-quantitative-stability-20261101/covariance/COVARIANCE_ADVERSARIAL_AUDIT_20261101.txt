Independent adversarial audit, 1 November 2026

VERDICT AND SCOPE

I found no falsification of the normalized summed-row theorem under its
stated fixed-row-cap hypothesis. I independently reconstructed the
covariance Hessian/cycle cancellation, the coordinatewise hazard
derivatives, the support argument, and the singular-endpoint passage.
The conclusion here is deliberately restricted to that chain. It is not
an audit certificate for the negative-noise theorem, its mass localization,
its full-noise comparison, or its discrete applications.

There is an explicit falsification of the cap-free/A-uniform extension:
rank-one matrices supported on two labels violate it by an unbounded
factor. See EXACT_COMPUTATIONS_20261101.txt, calculation 1. Thus the
quantifier is essential: for each fixed A there are c_A>0 and m_A,
uniform in m>=m_A and ambient dimension N. It cannot be replaced by
one universal positive c that works for row caps increasing with m.
The manuscript keeps the correct quantifier. A downstream use must
establish an absolute normalized row cap, not merely a cap for each m.

I also checked the parent's requested unnormalized global covariance
remainder. It does give

  d(B)^2 <= C m^(3/2) L R_U(B)

whenever d(B)/alpha <= C0 sqrt(m), including arbitrarily small d(B),
radial changes of scale, and singular target covariance. The initial
curvature argument must retain the trace term; the written sources do.
Its derivation appears in Section 7 below.

No pre-existing source, manuscript, numerical file, or master state was
edited. Only this new covariance audit directory was created/written.

2. INDEPENDENT COVARIANCE HESSIAN RECONSTRUCTION

Let y_0,...,y_d be an affinely independent target in R^d with positive
masses. At a positive source covariance the optimized offsets are smooth:
their Hessian is the complete facet Laplacian, whose edge weights are
strictly positive. Every offset vector is a translated simplex fan,
because h_i=c-y_i.a. This geometry is special and essential to this proof.

At covariance I, for a facet e={i,j}, put d_e=y_i-y_j,
l_e=|d_e|, w_e=mu_e/l_e and let P_e project onto its tangent space.
Let m_e and Sigma_e denote its conditional mean and covariance. Intrinsic
Gaussian integration by parts, with the OUTWARD normal equal to minus
the inward projected score difference, gives

  w_e(P_e-Sigma_e) = N_e T_e^T = T_e N_e^T,

where N_e has columns (mu_(e+k)/l_e)n_(e,k), and T_e has columns
m_e-m_(e+k). This transpose equality uses symmetry of the covariance
deficit. The convex Gaussian variance inequality gives the PSD sign.

For global barycentric vectors v_k=-grad lambda_k,

  v_k.(y_i-y_j)=delta_kj-delta_ki,  sum_k v_k=0.

If g=e+k is a triangle, D_g=2 area(g)/mu_g. Its altitude identity gives
row_g(N_e^-1)=D_g v_k^T. Consequently

  B_e=N_e^-1 [w_e(P_e-Sigma_e)] N_e^-T >= 0,
  (B_e)_(g,g')=1/w_e-D_g v_k.m_(g').

This inversion is on the (d-1)-dimensional tangent space. No covariance
deficit is inverted. In particular a singular deficit creates no gap.

Let partial_2 and partial_3 be the oriented complete-simplex boundary
matrices. Set A=partial_2^T W^-1 partial_2 and assemble B from the B_e.
For distinct triangles sharing e, g=e+k and g'=e+l, put h=e+k+l.
The stratum IBP for g' gives

  v_k.m_(g') = mu_h area(g')/(3 vol_3(h) mu_(g')).

Only the boundary adding k survives: v_k annihilates the other projected
gap vectors. The same formula summed over k outside g gives the diagonal
entries. Keeping the orientation signs yields exactly

  A-B = -D partial_3 diag(mu_h/(6 vol_3(h))) partial_3^T D.

The minus sign is important. This does not say B<=A on all triangle
coordinates. Instead choose, for a fixed edge cycle j, the representative
lambda with partial_2 lambda=j minimizing lambda^T D lambda. Its Euler
equation is partial_3^T D lambda=0, so lambda^T B lambda=j^T W^-1 j.
The representative exists without injectivity of partial_3. Changing it
changes only cycle coordinates; it does not change any optimizing offset.

For arbitrary cell vectors z_i, telescope z_i-z_j around each triangle.
PSD Cauchy-Schwarz then proves

  min_b sum_e w_e[(z_i-z_j).m_e+b_i-b_j]^2
    <= sum_e w_e[|P_e(z_i-z_j)|^2
                          -Var_e((z_i-z_j).X)].             (C1)

The minimum on the left uses one global vector b. This is exactly the
weighted edge-cycle duality, not independently chosen facet offsets.

For C(t)=I+tH, the score square root has derivatives H/2 and -H^2/4.
The interface term is the optimized square, and the interior derivative
is obtained from Gaussian IBP. Therefore

  4 F''(I)[H,H]
   = min_b sum_e w_e E_e[(d_e.HX+b_i-b_j)^2]
                                 -sum_e w_e |H d_e|^2.

Here E_e denotes conditional expectation only in this display. Apply
(C1) with z_i=H y_i. The remaining normal component gives

  4 F''(I)[H,H] <= -sum_e w_e (d_e^T H d_e)^2/|d_e|^2.      (C2)

All edge quadratic forms vanishing forces H=0: use the basis y_i-y_0
and polarize its pair differences. Whitening proves the same conclusion
at every positive covariance. This establishes the advertised interior
concavity without a dimension-dependent inequality constant.

PSD continuity follows from coupling both Gaussian sources through a
common standard G, using conditional randomization in any null directions:

  |F_nu(C)-F_nu(C')|
   <= (E|Y|^2)^(1/2) ||C^(1/2)-C'^(1/2)||_F.

This is sufficient for all singular targets used in the summed proof.
The arbitrary-finite-target extension in 225 also checks out: lift sites
to (y_i,epsilon e_i), restrict diag(C,0) to their difference span, and
apply the simplex theorem on that span. Translation of target sites
does not change the value because the Gaussian has zero mean. General
P2 targets follow from the stated W2-continuity estimate. No quantitative
strictness for such arbitrary limits is needed or inferred here.

3. HAZARD DERIVATIVES: WHERE M-UNIFORMITY IS ACTUALLY PROVED

Let v_i=1+t xi_i, sum xi_i=0, ||xi||_infinity<=A1. The offsets h(t)
are chosen to give every label mass 1/m. Define F_i, f_i, a_i=f_i/F_i,
W=product F_i and rho_ij=W a_i a_j, including the analytic diagonal.

The whole-threshold moment bound is the crucial analytic estimate. In
the box epsilon=max|v_i-1|<=delta/L, H=max|h_i|<=delta/sqrt(L), change
variables on x>=2H to z=(x+H)/sigma_min, then s=1-Phi(z). For any of
the finitely many fixed exponents needed below the resulting integral is
bounded by a constant times

  m^p integral_0^(1/2) (1-s)^(m-p-2) s^(p+1-2theta)
                        log(e/s)^((p+q+1)/2) ds,
  theta <= C_(p,q)(epsilon+H/sqrt(L)) <= C_(p,q)delta/L.

Substitute u=ms. Its size is
O(m^(-2+2theta) L^((p+q+1)/2)); m^(2theta) is uniformly bounded.
Divide by Z0=integral phi^2 Phi^(m-2)=alpha/m asymptotic to sqrt(L)/m^2.
This proves the needed conditional moments of m max a_k and |x|.
The left and middle threshold regions are exponentially small in m;
their hazards grow only linearly in |x|. For i=j, the reciprocal F_i
is bounded on the right region and the same direct hazard bound handles
the left region. The analytic diagonal has not been silently omitted.

The balancing Laplacian has c alpha/m<=omega_ij<=C alpha/m. Its
componentwise inverse is proved by subtracting a complete-graph
Laplacian, using the remaining doubly stochastic semigroup to bound the
l1 norm, and then using one row equation. Explicitly

  |(L^-1 b)_i| <= C/alpha (|b_i|+mean_j |b_j|).

Neither its spectral bound alone nor an l1 bound alone would prove this
componentwise conclusion. The source uses both required steps.

With b_i(x)=h_i'+xi_i(x-h_i)/(2v_i), density transport yields

  0=p_i'=sum_(j!=i) integral rho_ij(b_i-b_j).

It follows that |h_i'|<=C sqrt(L)(|xi_i|+M1). Integrating closes the
coordinate box for 0<=t<=c_A1/L^2. The constants are fixed on a larger
box before choosing the short time, so this is a legitimate continuation
argument and does not assume small ||xi||_2.

Writing L_i=partial_t log f_i and S=sum a_k b_k gives exactly

  partial_t log rho_ij=L_i+L_j+a_i b_i+a_j b_j-S=:P_ij.

The summed term S is indispensable. Its estimate uses
sum a_k(|xi_k|+M1)<=2m max a_k M1, not a separate uniform bound for each
of m terms. The second mass derivative then gives

  |h_i''|<=C L^(3/2)(xi_i^2+M2).

The explicit identity

  L_i'=xi_i^2/(2v_i^2)-xi_i^2(x-h_i)^2/v_i^3
       -2xi_i h_i'(x-h_i)/v_i^2+h_i''(x-h_i)/v_i-(h_i')^2/v_i

has conditional norm O(L^2(xi_i^2+M2)). Apply the same moment estimates
to (a_i b_i)' and sum S' using mean xi_i^2=M2. This proves

  E_ij(P_ij^2+|P_ij'|)<=C L^2(xi_i^2+xi_j^2+M2),
  |ell_i''|<=C alpha L^2(xi_i^2+M2).                      (H1)

All moment exponents here are fixed; none grows with m, the perturbation
amplitude, or ambient dimension. Summing omega_ij=O(alpha/m) is what
preserves M2 instead of ||xi||_2^2 in each coordinate estimate.

At t=0 the mass equation gives h_i'=-M xi_i/2, where T has density
proportional to phi^2 Phi^(m-2) and M=ET. Direct logarithmic differentiation
gives

  ell_i'(0)=-alpha eta_m xi_i,
  eta_m=(m-1-m Var(T))/(2(m-2)).

The 1D log-density has curvature at most -2, hence Var(T)<=1/2.
For the reverse inequality, condition regular independent scores on the
pair tie and on that pair being maximal. The common Gaussian mean is
independent of all these centered conditions. It contributes variance
1/m to the raw tie threshold, so Var(T)>=1/m. Therefore exactly
1/4<=eta_m<=1/2. This raw-threshold/common-mean normalization is correct.

4. SUPPORT CERTIFICATE AND SINGULAR TARGETS

At a positive independent-score covariance D=P diag(v)P, put

  H_D=integral W(Pa)(Pa)^T dx >= 0.

The ordinary offset Laplacian is diag(ell)-integral W aa^T and kills 1.
Projecting gives exactly

  L_D=P diag(ell)P-H_D.

The covariance gradient is L_D/2; degree-1/2 homogeneity gives
Gamma(D)=tr(L_D D). Interior concavity therefore yields

  Gamma(C)<=tr[L_D(C+D)]/2
           <=sum_i ell_i(C_ii+D_ii)/2                    (S1)

for EVERY centered PSD C. The discarded quantity is
-tr[H_D(C+D)]/2<=0, because the trace of a product of PSD matrices is
nonnegative. An entrywise sign assertion is neither true nor needed.

For a singular C, apply the first inequality to C+epsilon P and pass
to the limit. Its supporting point D stays positive and fixed. No
derivative, facet convergence, offset convergence, or rank persistence
is required at C. This point removes the main potential endpoint trap.

5. THE SUM, THE ROW CAP, AND THE CONSTANT

Normalize tr C=n=m-1. Set z_i=C_ii-n/m, kappa=1-2/m,
xi_i=z_i/kappa. If C_ii<=A with fixed A, ||xi||_infinity<=3(A+1).
The supporting path satisfies exactly

  D_t=P+tP diag(xi)P, tr D_t=n,
  (D_t)_ii=n/m+t z_i.

For t=b_A/L^2 inside (H1)'s interval,

  ell_i(t)=alpha-alpha eta_m t xi_i+e_i,
  |e_i|<=C_H alpha L^2 t^2(xi_i^2+mean xi^2)/2.

Insert this into (S1). The constant term is alpha n=mu. The first
derivative term is not estimated in absolute value:

  sum_i xi_i[C_ii+(D_t)_ii]=kappa(1+t)sum_i xi_i^2.

The error is at most C_H(A+2)alpha L^2 t^2 sum xi_i^2/2. Choose

  b_A<=min(c_H,1,1/(2A1),1/(24 C_H(A+2))).

Then eta_m>=1/4 and kappa>=1/3 give

  mu-Gamma(C)>=alpha kappa t sum xi_i^2/16
             >=alpha b_A sum z_i^2/(16 L^2).

Since R_U(B)=alpha[mu-Gamma(C)] for C=BB^T/alpha^2 with ||B||^2=w,
the stated normalized summed theorem follows. All constants can depend
on A, but not on m, N, target rank, off-diagonal operator norm, or
||C-P||_F. In particular ||C||op may grow with m under a fixed row cap;
it is never estimated in this proof.

6. UNNORMALIZED CONVERSION AND THE GEOMETRIC PROXY

For B=s Bhat with ||Bhat||^2=w, positive homogeneity gives exactly

  R_U(B)=w(s-1)^2/2+s R_U(Bhat).

The row-variance vector scales by s^2. Its squared norm is therefore
bounded by C s^3 alpha^2 L^2 R_U(B). The reference-radius correction is

  (||B||^2-w)^2/m
    =w^2(s+1)^2(s-1)^2/m
    <=2w(s+1)^2 R_U(B)/m.

Dividing the squared row-length errors by beta^2=w/m is valid even
when a row vanishes: (r-beta)^2<=(r^2-beta^2)^2/beta^2. This proves
the two displayed unnormalized conclusions at their stated scales.
With an explicit normalized cap the estimates only need an upper
bound for s>0; the stated lower bound is useful when deriving that
normalized cap from an absolute unnormalized one. No objection arises
from retaining this stronger hypothesis in the source.

The geometric-median construction likewise has a direct dimension-free
proof once the stated actual-centroid operator bound and uniform row
lower bound are available. The operator bound gives
||m^-1 sum u_i u_i^T||op=O(1/L); the Hessian of the average distance
function is then uniformly positive on a sufficiently small beta-ball.
Its gradient at zero satisfies

  |g0|<=C ||B||op sqrt(E_rad)/(m beta^2).

The new radial budget makes g0=o(1), locates an interior median c, and
gives m|c|^2<=C E_rad/L. Radial projection about c produces exactly
centered equal-length rows at cost O(E_rad), without increasing the
row span. These are geometric statements only. They do not identify
the proxy as actual centroids and do not compare noise energies.

7. UNNORMALIZED GLOBAL COVARIANCE FLOOR, INCLUDING TINY/RADIAL ERRORS

The local facet bootstrap 47 has the following relevant output:
for ||K-P||_F<=c/L,

  -Gamma''(K)[E,E]>=c (alpha/m)||E||_F^2.                 (G1)

Its dimension-free ingredient is the cancellation of the Gaussian
determinant trace against the facet quadratic mean. At a regular facet
the mean is O(sqrt L), while ||Sigma_e-P||_F=O(1). The latter follows
from one missing normal eigenvalue, one distinguished eigenvalue in
[0,1], and m-3 eigenvalues differing from 1 by O(L/m). The quadratic
tilt has Hessian uniformly bounded below. Gaussian Poincare plus a
Gronwall estimate controls its mean drift. The balancing ODE then
keeps all physical facet weights comparable to alpha/m on this ball.
Finally the exact identity

  sum_(i<j)((e_i-e_j)^T E(e_i-e_j))^2
       =m sum_i E_ii^2+(tr E)^2+2||E||_F^2

and (C2) prove (G1). No trace-free assumption is used in that identity.

For arbitrary centered PSD C, let E=C-P and e=||E||_F, and put
phi(t)=alpha Gamma(P+tE). The exact initial data are

  phi(0)=w,  phi'(0)=alpha^2 tr E/2,
  R_U(alpha C^(1/2))=phi(0)+phi'(0)-phi(1).

Thus the remainder already includes radial/trace errors. On
0<=t<=s=min(1/2,c/(Le)), (G1) gives
-phi''(t)>=c alpha^2 e^2/m. Concavity supplies the correct sign after
that interval. Integrating with weight 1-t, then passing by continuity
if C is singular, proves

  R_U(B)>=c alpha^2 e^2/m min(1,c/(Le)).                 (G2)

There is no lower cutoff for e. If q=||C^(1/2)-P||_F=d(B)/alpha,
then q<=e because |sqrt(lambda)-1|<=|lambda-1| for lambda>=0.
The function x^2 min(1,c/(Lx)) is increasing. If q<=C0 sqrt(m),
(G2) therefore yields

  R_U(B)>=c alpha^2 q^2/(m^(3/2)L)
         =c d(B)^2/(m^(3/2)L).

For q<=c/L the actual lower estimate is stronger, of order d(B)^2/m.
As a separate exact check, B=s B0 gives R_U(B)=d(B)^2/2. Therefore the
small-q and radial cases are fully included, not exceptional regimes.
The assumption q<=C0 sqrt(m) follows, for example, from ||B||<=C sqrt(w)
by Procrustes and triangle inequality. No rank assumption is required.
