2. IDENTIFICATION OF THE COLLISION PROBABILITY
Let (X_i,Y_i), i=1,...,m, be independent standard bivariate normal pairs
with correlation rho. Write
  P_m(rho)=P(argmax_i X_i = argmax_i Y_i).
Subtracting each vector's common coordinate mean leaves its argmax
unchanged. On the subspace perpendicular to the all-ones vector, the
centered score vectors are standard jointly rho-correlated Gaussians.
The regions argmax_i x_i are precisely the regular-simplex cones.
Consequently P_m(rho)=s_m(rho) in the manuscript's notation; no rescaling
of rho or dimension-dependent normalization is involved.

3. THE SHARP CONSTANT IN ENDPOINT FORM
The manuscript's Corollary 7.2 and frozen transfer source9482f085 state,
conditional on the internally audited Gaussian theorem, that for every
sufficiently large fixed m the exact UGC approximation threshold is
  a_m = min_{-1/(m-1)<=rho<=0}
        [m/(m-1)] [1-s_m(rho)]/(1-rho).
The credited endpoint theorem applies on the larger interval ending
at 1, and hence on this interval. Since 1-a=m/(m-1), it gives
  a_m = 1-s_m(-1/(m-1)) = 1-P_m(-1/(m-1)).               (1)
Thus, for each such fixed m and every epsilon>0, the candidate yields
a polynomial-time (a_m-epsilon)-approximation for nonnegative rational
edge-weighted MAX-m-CUT, and, assuming UGC, NP-hardness of an
(a_m+epsilon)-approximation. The optional simple-unweighted transfer
retains exactly the separate hypotheses and proof in9482f085.

No endpoint-minimizer discovery is claimed here. The potential new
content is the unrestricted Gaussian inequality and its matching
all-algorithm hardness consequence in the sufficiently-large-m regime.
The manuscript's previous sentence declining to assert endpoint
minimization was conservative; equation (1) now cites the published
source rather than assigning that result to this project.

4. A DIRECT INTEGRAL AND AN EXPLICIT TAIL ERROR
For -1<rho<1 let phi_rho and Phi_rho denote the bivariate standard
normal density and joint distribution function. Conditioning on
(X_1,Y_1)=(x,y), the other m-1 independent pairs must each lie below
(x,y) for label1 to win both coordinates. Summing the m disjoint
winning-label events gives the exact representation
  P_m(rho)=m integral_R^2 phi_rho(x,y)Phi_rho(x,y)^(m-1) dx dy. (2)
Ties have probability zero. This applies at the voting endpoint for
all m>=3. It is an integral representation, not an elementary closed
form or a numerical evaluation.

Let I_T denote the same integral over [-T,T]^2, including the factor m.
The integrand is nonnegative and Phi_rho^(m-1)<=1. The union bound on
the two standard-normal marginals therefore proves, for T>0,
  0 <= P_m(rho)-I_T <= 4m barPhi(T)
     <= [4m/(T sqrt(2*pi))] exp(-T^2/2).                  (3)
If a rigorous finite-domain integration encloses I_T in [l,u], then
(1)--(3) give the certified constant interval
  a_m in [max(0,1-u-4m barPhi(T)), min(1,1-l)].           (4)
This statement specifies how numerical bounds would be certified;
no numerical integration or advertised numerical alphabet is asserted.

5. LIMITS OF THE CONSEQUENCE
All threshold/hardness conclusions retain the original existential m0.
Computing (1) for m=4, or any other chosen number, does not prove that
m>=m0. The Gaussian and discrete conclusions retain their original
single-rule scope. Positive correlations and all smaller alphabets are
not supplied by this note. The candidate still awaits external scrutiny
and a stronger assessment of competing work. No new algorithmic ratio,
full named-conjecture resolution, or goal completion is inferred merely
from rewriting the same constant in endpoint form.
