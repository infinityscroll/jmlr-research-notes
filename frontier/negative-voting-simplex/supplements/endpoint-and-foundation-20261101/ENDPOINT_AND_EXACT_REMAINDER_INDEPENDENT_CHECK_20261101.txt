1. P_m(rho)=s_m(rho) HAS THE CORRECT NORMALIZATION

Let U be an m by (m-1) matrix with orthonormal columns spanning 1-perp,
so UU^T=P. For the m independent correlated Gaussian pairs, the vectors
U^T X and U^T Y have covariance I and cross-covariance rho I. Subtracting
the coordinate mean is exactly X -> PX=UU^T X and similarly for Y.
Every coordinate difference is unchanged. Thus the winning labels are
the regular-simplex partition labels in (m-1)-dimensional standard
Gaussian space, with the same rho.

The regular sites U^T e_i have Gram matrix P. Multiplying all of them
by the manuscript's positive common scale alpha changes no winner.
The manuscript defines S_rho(g) as the SUM over labels of equal-label
joint probabilities, not the stability of one individual cell. Hence

  s_m(rho)=sum_i P(g(X)=i,g(Y)=i)=P_m(rho).

There is no missing m, no extra 1/m, and no correlation rescaling.

2. ENDPOINT CANCELLATION AND SOURCE SCOPE

The external Theorem 4.8 uses

  A_m(rho)=m(1-P_m(rho))/[(m-1)(1-rho)],
  rho in [-1/(m-1),1), m>=3,

and places its minimum at a=-1/(m-1). In particular that same endpoint
minimizes the restriction to [a,0]. At a,

  1-a=m/(m-1),
  A_m(a)=1-P_m(a)=1-s_m(a).

The external source's upper endpoint is half-open at 1; this has no

the hardness conclusion on the manuscript's Gaussian/transfer premises.

4. BIVARIATE REPRESENTATION AND CERTIFIED INTERVAL

Given (X_1,Y_1)=(x,y), each other independent pair lies coordinatewise
below it with probability Phi_rho(x,y). Thus label 1 wins both scores
with conditional probability Phi_rho(x,y)^(m-1). Summing the m disjoint
winning-label events gives

  P_m(rho)=m integral phi_rho(x,y)Phi_rho(x,y)^(m-1) dx dy.

Ties have probability zero. At rho=-1/(m-1), m>=3, one has |rho|<1,
so the bivariate density is nonsingular. The finite-window integral I_T
includes the factor m. Since 0<=Phi_rho^(m-1)<=1,

  0<=P_m-I_T
    <=m P(|X_1|>T or |Y_1|>T)
    <=4m barPhi(T)
    <=4m exp(-T^2/2)/(T sqrt(2 pi)).

This bound uses only the standard-normal marginals and is valid at
negative correlation. It is deliberately coarse but rigorous. If
l<=I_T<=u is a rigorous enclosure, then

  l<=P_m<=u+4m barPhi(T),

and taking 1-P_m and intersecting with [0,1] gives exactly the interval
in [E]. Any numerical implementation must use a certified upper bound
for the tail; the displayed Mills bound already provides one. The note
claims an enclosure procedure, not an executed numerical certificate.

5. RESIDUAL AND WEIGHTED CYCLE GAUGE

Use the source notation A=Q^T W^(-1)Q and
lambda^T B lambda=sum_e eta_e^T B_e eta_e. Let b_* minimize
(c+Gb)^T W(c+Gb), put r=c+Gb_*, j=Wr, and J=r^TWr.
The residual r is unique even though b_* is defined modulo constants.
Its normal equation gives G^T j=0. Hence j belongs to im(Q).

The representing set {lambda:Q lambda=j} is an affine translate of
ker(Q)=im(R). The positive D inner product has a unique minimum-norm
representative in that affine set. Its first-order condition is
R^T D lambda=0. Redundant columns of R do not affect existence or this
condition. The cited factorization therefore gives

  lambda^T B lambda=lambda^T A lambda=j^T W^(-1)j=J.

For s_e=N_e^(-T)eta_e this is exactly

  sum_e s_e^T E_e s_e=J.

No inverse of E_e appears; its possible singularity is harmless.

6. THE CROSS TERM IS J, NOT MERELY BOUNDED BY J

The source definition has T_e columns m_e-m_g, and T_e=E_e N_e^(-T).
Consequently

  sum_e z_e^T E_e s_e
   =sum_{e,g} Q_(e,g)lambda_g z_e dot(m_e-m_g)
   =j dot c-sum_g lambda_g m_g dot(sum_e Q_(e,g)z_e)
   =j dot c.

The last sum vanishes because z_e=z_i-z_j telescopes around each
oriented triangle. Finally,

  j dot c=j dot(r-Gb_*)=r^TWr-(G^Tj) dot b_*=J.

Expanding the square now proves exactly

  sum_e (z_e-s_e)^T E_e(z_e-s_e)
   =sum_e z_e^T E_e z_e-2J+J
   =sum_e z_e^T E_e z_e-min_b(c+Gb)^T W(c+Gb).

This verifies identity (A), including its sign and its normalization.
For j=0 the choice lambda=0 gives s_e=0 and the same identity directly.
The construction uses one global residual and one weighted gauge, not
independent facet offsets or an additional optimization over s_e.

7. EXACT HESSIAN IDENTITY AND STRICTNESS

For symmetric H, set z_i=Hy_i, so z_e=Hd_e and c_e=z_e dot m_e.
The cited optimized Hessian formula splits into

  4F''(I)[H,H]
   =J+sum_e w_e z_e^T Cov_e(X)z_e-sum_e w_e|z_e|^2
   =J-sum_e z_e^T E_e z_e
      -sum_e w_e|(I-P_e)z_e|^2.

Insert (A). As the facet normal is d_e/ell_e,

  |(I-P_e)Hd_e|^2=(d_e^T H d_e)^2/ell_e^2.

This gives (B) with the exact factor 4, exact normal-square coefficient,
and the full nonnegative second square subtracted. There is no omitted
mixed term.

For a full simplex in its ambient span, choose one vertex as origin and
write its edge basis b_i. If every edge quadratic form vanishes, then
b_i^T H b_i=0 and (b_i-b_j)^T H(b_i-b_j)=0, forcing all b_i^T H b_j=0.
Therefore H=0. Since every w_e>0, any nonzero symmetric H makes the
first sum positive. Strictness requires no positive definiteness of
the facet deficits E_e. It is not a strictness claim for arbitrary
target laws after lifting or limits.

At general positive C0, the cited congruence transforms both the sites
and the direction: y_i becomes C0^(1/2)y_i and H becomes
C0^(-1/2)K C0^(-1/2). The identity is applied with the corresponding
transformed facet data, not the old weights reused without change.
