FROM A WEIGHTED HODGE ESTIMATE TO LOCAL COVARIANCE CONCAVITY
Date: 2026-11-02
Scope: complete transfer conditional only on the specified all-dimensional
weighted Hodge estimate. The planar estimate is proved separately. This note
does not certify the all-dimensional Hodge lemma or its analytic realization.
No differentiation of Brenier maps, global (N), or covariance concavity is
assumed in this transfer.

1. HYPOTHESES AND THE EXACT HODGE INPUT

Let gamma be standard Gaussian measure on R^d. Let phi be smooth with

    m I <= M(x)=D^2 phi(x) <= L I,  0<m<=L<infinity,
    T=grad phi,  nu=T#gamma,  B=E M.

Quadratic-outside-compact phi is permitted, but not necessary for this
transfer. Let v in L^2(gamma;R^d) be Gaussian-solenoidal, meaning
E[v dot grad psi]=0 for all smooth compactly supported psi. Put I_v=E v^T Mv.

The required weighted Hodge input is:

  For every such v there is a skew matrix field A with delta_gamma A=v,
  in the weak sense, such that

    J_A := E tr(A^T M A) <= I_v.                             (H)

Here (delta_gamma A)_i=sum_j(x_j A_ij-partial_j A_ij), and finite J_A suffices
for the integrations against smooth, square-integrable gradients below.
Under uniform Hessian bounds, J_A is equivalent to the L^2 matrix norm.
This is the exact analytic input being audited separately.

2. THE DISCRIMINANT FOLLOWS DIRECTLY, WITH A SHARPER CONSTANT

Let H be a fixed symmetric matrix. Define

    b_v=E[(HT) dot v],   a=E tr(H^2 M).

Weak Gaussian integration by parts, using delta_gamma A=v, yields

    b_v=E sum_{i<j} A_ij [(HM)_ij-(HM)_ji].                  (2.1)

At each point choose an orthonormal eigenbasis of M, with eigenvalues lambda_i,
and write H-tilde for H in that basis. All following expressions are basis
invariant; repeated eigenvalues cause no ambiguity. Define

    c_* = E sum_{i<j} ((lambda_i-lambda_j)^2/(lambda_i+lambda_j))
                       H-tilde_ij^2.

Weighted Cauchy-Schwarz in (2.1), followed by (H), gives

    |b_v|^2 <= J_A c_* <= I_v c_* <= I_v a.                 (2.2)

The final comparison is pointwise: the off-diagonal coefficient in a is
lambda_i+lambda_j, whereas (lambda_i-lambda_j)^2/(lambda_i+lambda_j) is at
most that value. The diagonal contributions to a are nonnegative.

The integration in (2.1) is legitimate by L^2 approximation/cutoffs: HT has
at most linear growth, its derivative HM is bounded, and A,v are L^2.

This proves the desired weighted-solenoidal discriminant directly. A local
nuclear-variation theorem by itself is not silently substituted for (H).

3. AN EXACT VARIATIONAL FORMULA REPLACES BRENIER-MAP DIFFERENTIATION

Write F_nu(C)=MCov(N(0,C),nu). For C=I+tH>0 let

    Q_t=(I+tH)^{1/2},   R_t=Q_t-I.

Lift couplings through T as usual, and let (G,S) range over all couplings of
two standard Gaussians. Since phi has at most quadratic growth,
E phi(S)=E phi(G). Consequently, with the usual Bregman divergence

    D_phi(S,G)=phi(S)-phi(G)-T(G) dot (S-G),

one has the exact identity

    F_nu(I+tH)
      = E[T(G) dot Q_t G]
        + sup_{G,S ~ gamma} {
            E[(R_t T(G)) dot (S-G)] - E D_phi(S,G)
          }.                                               (3.1)

The baseline admits the expansion

    E[T(G) dot Q_t G]
      = tr B + (t/2)tr(BH) - (t^2/8)a + o(t^2).             (3.2)

Optimal couplings exist. Their excess objective over the identity coupling
is nonnegative. Since D_phi(S,G)>=(m/2)|S-G|^2, Cauchy-Schwarz in (3.1) gives

    ||S-G||_2 <= (2/m)||R_t T||_2 = O(|t|).                  (3.3)

For a sequence t->0, set u_t(G)=E[(S-G)/t | G]. A subsequence converges
weakly in L^2 to v. Taylor expansion of each compactly supported smooth psi,
using the equal Gaussian marginals and (3.3), shows

    E[grad psi(G) dot v(G)]=0.

Thus every such limit v is Gaussian-solenoidal.

4. THE BREGMAN LOWER LIMIT IS RIGOROUS WITHOUT TAIL RESTRICTIONS ON u_t

For any fixed q in L^2(gamma;R^d), Fenchel duality gives

    D_phi(S,G) >= t q(G) dot (S-G)
      - [phi^*(T(G)+t q(G))-phi^*(T(G))-t q(G) dot G].

The bracket divided by t^2 converges pointwise to
(1/2) q^T M^{-1}q. It is bounded by |q|^2/(2m), since phi is m-strongly
convex. Dominated convergence and weak convergence of u_t yield

    liminf E D_phi(S,G)/t^2
      >= E q dot v - (1/2)E q^T M^{-1}q.

Taking the supremum over q (or choosing q=Mv) gives

    liminf E D_phi(S,G)/t^2 >= (1/2)E v^T Mv.                (4.1)

This avoids an invalid uniform Taylor expansion of the random Bregman
increment; rare large increments are fully covered by the dual argument.

5. LOCAL COVARIANCE CURVATURE

Equations (3.1)--(4.1), and R_t/t -> H/2, imply

 limsup_{t->0}
 [F_nu(I+tH)-tr B-(t/2)tr(BH)]/t^2
   <= -a/8 + sup_{div_gamma v=0} { b_v/2-I_v/2 }.

Using (2.2), the supremum is at most c_*/8. Therefore

 limsup_{t->0}
 [F_nu(I+tH)-F_nu(I)-(t/2)tr(BH)]/t^2
   <= -(a-c_*)/8.                                         (5.1)

Moreover

    a-c_*
      = E [sum_i lambda_i H-tilde_ii^2
           +sum_{i<j} (4lambda_i lambda_j/(lambda_i+lambda_j))
                         H-tilde_ij^2]
      >= m ||H||_F^2.                                     (5.2)

Thus the local upper covariance curvature is strictly negative in every
nonzero symmetric direction. If a classical second derivative exists,

    D^2 F_nu(I)[H,H] <= -(a-c_*)/4 <= -(m/4)||H||_F^2.

For a positive definite quadratic phi, this is precisely the familiar
Gaussian-to-Gaussian Hessian; the commutator expression reproduces the
correct coefficients.

6. WHAT IS STILL NEEDED FOR A FULL ALL-TARGET CONCAVITY THEOREM

The transfer above is complete once (H) holds for the weak L^2 solenoidal
limit fields. A proof only for smooth A or v must include its density/closure
argument before it supplies (H) here.

At a general covariance C0>0, the congruence identity transforms the target
to (C0^{1/2})#nu. Applying (5.1) there requires the same regularity and Hodge
hypotheses for that target's Brenier map, or a justified approximation theorem.
The property that the initial phi is quadratic outside a compact set is NOT
obviously preserved by this transformation of the target. This closure step
must not be assumed. Likewise, (5.1) alone for one target at one covariance
is not yet a proof of global concavity for all targets.

7. ALL-BASE REGULARITY: AN EXPLICIT RECENT EXTERNAL DEPENDENCY

The regularity boundary in Section 6 can be addressed by the following
specific external statement, conditional on its correctness:

  Maja Gwozdz, Caffarelli Estimates under Lipschitz Perturbations,
  arXiv:2609.04052v1, 3 September 2026,
  https://arxiv.org/html/2609.04052v1
  Theorem 3.2 / Corollary 3.6, and Proposition 3.8.

The stated theorem bounds the Brenier Hessian above when source/target
reference potentials have the appropriate one-sided positive quadratic
curvature bounds and both log-density perturbations are globally Lipschitz.
Proposition 3.8 supplies smooth full-space diffeomorphism regularity for
smooth positive densities. Applying the Hessian estimate forward and backward
supplies positive lower and finite upper Hessian bounds. The actual theorem
statements were read. This is a recent preprint, and its proof has not been
independently audited here.

Apply this to the class of targets with densities

    rho(y)=Z^{-1} exp(-q(y)) g_Sigma(y),
    Sigma>0,  q in C_c^infinity(R^d),

where g_Sigma is a nondegenerate Gaussian density. Forward and reverse
reference Hessians are positive definite constant matrices; q is globally
Lipschitz. At every base covariance, the congruence-transformed target remains
in this same class, since an invertible linear change of variables transforms
g_Sigma into another elliptical Gaussian and q into another smooth compactly
supported function. Thus the stated external result gives the uniform
Hessian bounds required in Sections 1--5 at every positive definite base.
It is the target-density class that is closed; no false closure assertion
about quadratic-outside-compact Brenier potentials is needed.

This step still requires the separately audited Hodge lemma to hold for
smooth uniformly bounded positive Hessians, not merely Hessians that are
constant outside a compact set. The external regularity theorem does not
imply that a transport Hessian is constant in the tails.

8. POINTWISE UPPER SECOND QUOTIENTS IMPLY GLOBAL CONCAVITY

For targets in the class of Section 7, the usual uniqueness of the Brenier
coupling makes F differentiable at every positive definite covariance. This
also follows from the support-function argument and fixed-marginal uniform
integrability; it does not require differentiating transport maps.

Along any compact covariance segment in the positive definite cone, put
f(s)=F(C_0+sH). Sections 5 and 7 give, at every interior s,

    limsup_{h->0} [f(s+h)-f(s)-h f'(s)]/h^2 <= 0.             (8.1)

An elementary one-dimensional argument now proves concavity. If concavity
fails, subtract a chord on a subinterval [alpha,beta], obtaining g with zero
endpoint values and a strictly negative interior value. For sufficiently
small epsilon>0, g(s)-epsilon s^2 has its minimum at an interior point s_*.
Differentiability gives g'(s_*)=2epsilon s_*. The minimum inequality then
implies, for every sufficiently small h,

    g(s_*+h)-g(s_*)-h g'(s_*) >= epsilon h^2,

contradicting (8.1). No C^2 dependence of F and no uniform control of its
pointwise little-oh remainders is required.

9. AN EXPLICIT DENSE TARGET CLASS AND PASSAGE TO ALL P2 TARGETS

Let nu be any probability measure with finite second moment. Choose smooth
compactly supported probability densities f_n with f_n dx -> nu in W2,
for instance by truncating and then mollifying. Let epsilon_n decrease to
zero and let g be the standard Gaussian density. Define

    rho_n=(1-epsilon_n)f_n+epsilon_n g.

This is smooth and strictly positive, and it has the exact representation

    rho_n=Z_n^{-1} exp(-q_n) g,
    q_n=-log[1+((1-epsilon_n)/epsilon_n) f_n/g] in C_c^infinity,
    Z_n=1/epsilon_n.

Thus it belongs to the target class in Section 7. An independent mixture
coupling gives

    W2(rho_n dx,f_n dx)^2
      <= epsilon_n E|X_n-G|^2 -> 0,

because the second moments of f_n are bounded. Hence rho_n dx -> nu in W2.
For every covariance C>=0, gluing a W2 target coupling with a maximal-
covariance coupling and using Cauchy-Schwarz gives

    |F_nu(C)-F_eta(C)| <= sqrt(tr C) W2(nu,eta).              (9.1)

Concavity for rho_n therefore passes to nu at positive definite covariances.
The Gaussian-source W2 continuity as C approaches a semidefinite covariance
extends the inequality to the closed positive-semidefinite cone.
