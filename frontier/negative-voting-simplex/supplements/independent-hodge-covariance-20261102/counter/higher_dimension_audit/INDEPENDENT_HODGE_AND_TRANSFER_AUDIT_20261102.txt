INDEPENDENT AUDIT: HIGHER-DIMENSIONAL HODGE ARGUMENT AND LOCAL COVARIANCE TRANSFER
Date: 2026-11-02
Verdict: the algebra is correct. The existence/regularity step can be closed
under uniform Hessian bounds. The Bregman transfer supplied subsequently by
the parent is valid under those same bounds and bypasses differentiable polar
factorization. A global extension to arbitrary target laws still requires its
own approximation and all-covariance-base regularity theorem.

1. CONVENTIONS AND SAFE DOMAIN

Use gamma=N(0,I_n), D_j^*=x_j-partial_j, and for a skew matrix field A set
  (delta A)_i=sum_j D_j^* A_ij.
For vectors delta v=sum_i D_i^* v_i=-div_gamma(v). Thus delta(delta A)=0.
Let phi be smooth with Hessian M satisfying mI<=M(x)<=LI globally, 0<m<=L.
Being quadratic outside a compact set is a sufficient stronger condition but
is not needed in the algebra or cutoff proof below. Set T=grad(phi).

The Hodge minimization below works for every v in L^2(gamma;R^n) satisfying
delta v=0 weakly. For the literal local-flow theorem one may impose v smooth
and compactly supported; its flow is then complete. For the quadratic-form
inequality it suffices additionally that the antisymmetric derivative of Mv
has finite displayed weighted energy.

2. THE MINIMIZER EXISTS: NO HIDDEN FEASIBILITY ASSUMPTION

On the Hilbert space of skew L^2(gamma) matrix fields, minimize
  J(A)=E tr(A^T M A)
subject to delta A=v in distributions. Uniform bounds on M make J a strictly
positive quadratic Hilbert norm equivalent to the ordinary L^2 norm.
The affine constraint is closed: each distributional test against a smooth
compact vector field is a continuous linear functional of A in L^2(gamma).

It is nonempty. Write L0=Delta-x dot grad for the componentwise OU operator,
take u=(1-L0)^(-1)v, and set
  A_ij=partial_j u_i-partial_i u_j.
Gaussian Hermite expansion justifies this construction for L^2 data. On
homogeneous chaos degree k, the resolvent multiplies by 1/(k+1). The operator
delta raises degree by one, so delta v=0 implies delta u=0. Also Du is L^2.
Directly, with these signs,
  delta A=(1-L0)u-grad(delta u)=v.
The formula can first be checked on polynomials and passed to distributions.
Consequently a unique minimizer A exists.

3. EULER EQUATION AND LOCAL REGULARITY

For skew variations B with delta B=0, stationarity gives
  E <MA+AM,B>_F=0.
In particular use B=delta K for every smooth compactly supported alternating
three-form K. Since delta^2=0 and the Gaussian adjoint of delta is ordinary
exterior differentiation, the minimizer satisfies
  d(MA+AM)=0                                                (3.1)
weakly. In dimension two this closure equation is automatic, as expected.

The coupled first-order system
  delta A=v,       d(MA+AM)=0
is elliptic in the injective-symbol sense. At a nonzero covector xi its
homogeneous symbol is
  i_xi A=0,       xi wedge (MA+AM)=0.
The latter says MA+AM=xi wedge alpha for some one-form alpha. Pairing with A
and using the former yields <MA+AM,A>=0. Uniform positivity gives A=0.
Thus standard interior regularity for an overdetermined elliptic first-order
system gives A in H^1_loc for L^2 v, and smooth A for smooth v. One can invoke
the usual interior elliptic estimate, or apply the second-order operator
P^*P, whose principal symbol is positive definite by this calculation.
This is the precise regularity theorem being used; global weighted regularity
is proved separately below and is not being assumed.

4. HESSIAN SYMMETRY REMOVES ALL DERIVATIVES OF M

Let F=MA+AM. In the cyclic sum
  partial_k F_ij+partial_i F_jk+partial_j F_ki,
every term containing a derivative of M cancels with its partner because
partial_k M_ij is symmetric in all three indices and A is skew.
At any fixed point choose a constant orthogonal coordinate system diagonalizing
M there, with eigenvalues lambda_i. Do not differentiate a moving eigenframe.
For each distinct triple i,j,k, define
  a=partial_k A_ij, b=partial_i A_jk, c=partial_j A_ki.
Equation (3.1) is precisely
  (lambda_i+lambda_j)a+(lambda_j+lambda_k)b
    +(lambda_k+lambda_i)c=0.                                (4.1)

5. THE REMAINDER IS POSITIVE, WITH THE STATED INDEXING

Define
  R(x)=sum_{i,k,j,l} M_ik partial_l A_ij partial_j A_kl.
In the diagonal coordinates above, this is
  sum_{i,j,l} lambda_i partial_l A_ij partial_j A_il.
Terms with j=l are positive squares
  sum_{i!=j} lambda_i (partial_j A_ij)^2.
Terms with i=j or i=l vanish. Every distinct unordered triple contributes
  q=-2(lambda_j a b+lambda_k b c+lambda_i c a).

Eliminate c using (4.1). The exact result is

  q=2/(lambda_i+lambda_k) [
       (lambda_i a+lambda_k b)^2
       +lambda_j(lambda_i a^2+lambda_k b^2)].               (5.1)

Thus q>=0, with equality only when a=b=c=0. Together with the repeated-index
squares this shows R>=0, and, under m<=lambda_i<=L,
  R>=c(m,L,n)|DA|_F^2                                     (5.2)
for some c>0 on tensors satisfying closure. For example one may bound a,b
from (5.1) and then c from (4.1); compactness of the finite-dimensional
normalized coefficient/tensor set gives the same uniform conclusion.
All derivatives of a skew A occur in this decomposition. Therefore R=0
forces DA=0; there are no omitted repeated-index directions.

6. GLOBAL H^1 AND THE ENERGY IDENTITY VIA CUTOFFS

Formally, Gaussian integration by parts and
  partial_j D_l^*=D_l^* partial_j+delta_jl
give
  E v^T Mv=J(A)+E R.                                      (6.1)
The two possible derivative-of-M terms vanish separately by Hessian symmetry:
  sum_{i,j} A_ij partial_j M_ik=0,
  sum_{k,l} partial_l M_ik partial_j A_kl=0.

For a smooth compact scalar cutoff chi the rigorous localized identity is

  E chi^2 v^T Mv = E chi^2 tr(A^TMA)+E chi^2 R
    +2 E chi (partial_j chi) A_ij M_ik v_k
    +2 E chi (partial_l chi) M_ik A_ij partial_j A_kl,       (6.2)

with repeated indices summed. H^1_loc suffices: justify first by local
mollification, then pass to the H^1_loc/L^2 limit in these products.
Use (5.2), Cauchy-Schwarz, and Young's inequality on the last term to obtain
  E chi^2 |DA|^2 <= C E chi^2 |v|^2+C E |grad chi|^2 |A|^2.
For standard radial chi_R, equal to one on radius R, vanishing beyond 2R,
and |grad chi_R|<=C/R, the right side is uniformly bounded. Thus DA is
globally L^2(gamma). Both boundary terms in (6.2) then vanish as R tends to
infinity. This proves (6.1), hence
  J(A)<=E v^T Mv.                                         (6.3)

This cutoff argument is the needed global integration/regularity closure.
There is no appeal to an unproved decay property of the minimizing A.

7. LOCAL NUCLEAR-TV SECOND VARIATION IN EVERY DIMENSION

For a compact smooth divergence-free v and its gamma-preserving flow S_t,
let V(t)=E ||D(T composed with S_t)||_*. In an eigenbasis of M at each point,
write
  K_ij=partial_j(Mv)_i-partial_i(Mv)_j.
The exact second-variation formula is
  V'(0)=0,
  V''(0)=E sum_{i<j} K_ij^2/(lambda_i+lambda_j)-E v^T Mv.  (7.1)
The trace contribution is -E v^T Mv; the nuclear Hessian contributes the
displayed sum with no additional factor of two.

Integration by parts using delta A=v gives
  E v^T Mv=E sum_{i<j} A_ij K_ij.
Meanwhile
  J(A)=E sum_{i<j}(lambda_i+lambda_j)A_ij^2.
Weighted Cauchy-Schwarz and (6.3) yield
  (E v^T Mv)^2
     <=J(A) E sum_{i<j}K_ij^2/(lambda_i+lambda_j)
     <=(E v^T Mv) E sum_{i<j}K_ij^2/(lambda_i+lambda_j).
This proves V''(0)>=0. The zero-velocity case is immediate separately.

Equality implies E R=0 and hence A constant, so v=A x is an infinitesimal
rotation. Conversely such rotations preserve V exactly. Within the compact
velocity class equality is possible only for v=0. This establishes local
second-order stability; it is not a finite-perturbation global N inequality.

8. A DIRECT BREGMAN TRANSFER, WITHOUT DIFFERENTIABLE POLAR FACTORIZATION

The parent supplied the following route after the local audit began. It is
valid under the same uniform Hessian bounds. Let nu=T#gamma and
  F_nu(Sigma)=MCov(N(0,Sigma),nu).
For symmetric positive A, coupling G,S with both marginals gamma gives the
exact identity

  F_nu(A^2)=E T(G) dot A G
    +sup_{G,S~gamma} { E [(A-I)T(G) dot (S-G)]
                       -E D_phi(S,G) },                  (8.1)

where D_phi(s,g)=phi(s)-phi(g)-T(g) dot (s-g). The identity follows because
the marginals make E[phi(S)-phi(G)]=0. Uniform Hessian bounds imply quadratic
growth, so this cancellation is integrable.

Take A_t=(I+tH)^(1/2), H symmetric. Since D_phi(S,G)>=m|S-G|^2/2 and the
diagonal coupling gives zero remainder, optimizing couplings (or sufficiently
accurate approximate optimizers) satisfy ||S-G||_2=O(|t|).
The conditional velocities
  v_t(G)=E[(S-G)/t | G]
are L^2 bounded. Any weak subsequential limit v satisfies div_gamma(v)=0:
use equality of marginals, Taylor expand a compact smooth test function, and
bound its remainder by O(E|S-G|^2/|t|)=O(|t|).

The Bregman energy lower bound does not require a uniform pointwise Taylor
remainder along these couplings. For any square-integrable test vector r,
Fenchel duality gives
  D_phi(S,G)/t^2
    >=r(G) dot (S-G)/t
      -D_{phi*}(T(G)+t r(G),T(G))/t^2.
The second term converges in expectation to
  one-half E r^T M^(-1) r,
by the uniform inverse-Hessian bound and dominated convergence. Consequently
  liminf E D_phi(S,G)/t^2
    >=sup_r {E r dot v-one-half E r^T M^(-1)r}
    =one-half E v^T Mv.                                  (8.2)

Set
  a=E tr(H^2 M),
  c*=E sum_{i<j} [(lambda_i-lambda_j)^2/(lambda_i+lambda_j)] H_ij^2.
Here H_ij denotes H in an orthonormal eigenbasis of M at that point.
For any weak divergence-free v use its minimizing A from Sections 2-6. Then
  b:=E HT dot v
    =E sum_{i<j} A_ij (lambda_j-lambda_i) H_ij,
so
  b^2<=c*J(A)<=c*E v^T Mv.                                (8.3)
This pairing only uses the bounded derivative HM of HT, so it is justified
for L^2 v and A by cutoffs even when the limiting velocity has no classical
flow or classical derivative.

Since A_t=I+tH/2-t^2H^2/8+o(t^2), (8.1)-(8.3) imply

  limsup_{t->0} [F_nu(I+tH)-F_nu(I)
                 -(t/2)tr(H E M)]/t^2
    <=-(a-c*)/8.                                         (8.4)

The constants check: maximizing b/2-e/2 with b^2<=c*e gives c*/8.
The baseline term contributes -a/8. If a classical second derivative exists,
its upper bound is -(a-c*)/4, twice the t^2 coefficient in (8.4).
Finally
  a-c*=E [sum_i lambda_i H_ii^2
            +sum_{i<j} 4lambda_i lambda_j/(lambda_i+lambda_j) H_ij^2]
       >=m ||H||_F^2.

Thus the local covariance upper second quotient follows directly and even
has strict curvature under uniform positive Hessian bounds. Differentiable
polar factorization is unnecessary and should not be silently invoked.

9. GLOBAL LIMIT: WHAT THIS AUDIT DOES AND DOES NOT CLOSE

At another covariance base Sigma_0, one must apply the local result to the
transformed target Sigma_0^(1/2)#nu, using the actual Brenier map from gamma
to that target. Composing the old Brenier map with Sigma_0^(1/2) does not
generally produce that Brenier map. Uniform Hessian bounds for the original
map do not automatically establish them for every such transformed target.

A global covariance-concavity theorem therefore still needs a rigorously
specified target class whose Brenier maps have the requisite bounds at every
positive covariance base, followed by a valid density/continuity passage to
the desired arbitrary laws. This audit validates the local algebra, minimizer
regularity, integration, and Bregman transfer. It does not independently
validate an external all-base regularity theorem or the final global passage.
