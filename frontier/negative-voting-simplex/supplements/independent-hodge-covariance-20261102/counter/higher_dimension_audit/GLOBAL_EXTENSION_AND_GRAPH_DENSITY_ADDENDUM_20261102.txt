1. EXACT IMPORT AND ITS APPLICATION

Source inspected directly:
  Maja Gwozdz, Caffarelli Estimates under Lipschitz Perturbations,
  arXiv:2609.04052v1, dated 3 September 2026,
  https://arxiv.org/html/2609.04052v1
Relevant statements: Theorem 3.2, Corollary 3.6, Proposition 3.8.

The first two provide an upper Brenier-Hessian bound for positive quadratic
reference curvatures with globally Lipschitz perturbations, assuming finite
normalizations and second moments. Proposition 3.8 supplies smooth invertible
transport for the smooth positive full-space densities used here.

For rho=Z^(-1)e^(-q)g_Sigma, q smooth compactly supported, use these parameters:

  Forward: V=|x|^2/2, a=0; W=y^T Sigma^(-1)y/2, b=q;
           Lambda=1, kappa=lambda_min(Sigma^(-1)).
  Reverse: V=y^T Sigma^(-1)y/2, a=q; W=|x|^2/2, b=0;
           Lambda=lambda_max(Sigma^(-1)), kappa=1.

All stated hypotheses hold: q is globally Lipschitz and bounded; both
densities are smooth, strictly positive, and have finite moments. The reverse
map is the Brenier inverse. Its finite derivative upper bound K gives
D^2 phi(x)>=K^(-1)I by differentiating the inverse identity. Smoothness makes
the bounds pointwise. Thus 0<mI<=D^2 phi<=LI with finite constants.

These are imported statements. Their full proofs were not independently
audited, and this addendum does not present them as newly proved facts.

2. THE DENSITY CLASS REALLY IS CLOSED AT EVERY COVARIANCE BASE

Let L be any invertible linear map. Direct density transformation gives

  L#(Z^(-1)e^(-q)g_Sigma)
    = Z^(-1)e^(-q_L)g_{Sigma_L},
  q_L(y)=q(L^(-1)y),       Sigma_L=L Sigma L^T.

The support of q_L is the compact set L(supp q); q_L is smooth; and
  Lip(q_L)<=||L^(-1)||_op Lip(q).
The normalizer is unchanged by the same change of variables. Therefore
the target class is invariant under the covariance congruences needed here.

In particular, at C0>0 set L=C0^(1/2). For a covariance direction H, put
K=C0^(-1/2) H C0^(-1/2). The exact cost transformation is
  F_nu(C0+tH)=F_{L#nu}(I+tK).
This is a change of Gaussian source coordinates and target labels in the
bilinear cost. It does not assert that L composed with an existing Brenier
map is itself a Brenier map.

The imported theorem applies to the actual Brenier map for every transformed
target. Its constants can depend on the base. The pointwise calculus below
does not require a single bound uniform over the entire positive cone.
The Hodge argument uses bounded positive Hessians, not constant tail Hessians,
so the transformed potentials meet exactly the needed domain.

3. DIFFERENTIABILITY AND THE ONE-DIMENSIONAL CONCAVITY STEP

For completeness, differentiability does not require differentiating any
optimal map. Fix nu in P2 and consider the set of cross-moment matrices
  K_nu={E[X Y^T] : X~gamma, Y~nu}.
It is compact in finite-dimensional matrix space. Couplings are weakly
compact, and the cross-products are uniformly integrable: split a large
product event into large-|X| and large-|Y| events and use Cauchy-Schwarz with
the fixed marginal second-moment tails.

The function A -> MCov(A#gamma,nu) is the support function of this compact
set, up to the fixed transpose convention. At invertible A its maximizing
coupling is unique by Gaussian-source quadratic OT uniqueness. Hence the
maximizing cross-moment is unique and the support function is differentiable.
Composition with the smooth positive-definite square root gives
differentiability of C -> F_nu(C) on the positive-definite cone.

Now take any covariance segment inside that cone and write f(s)=F(C0+sH).
The local transfer gives at every interior s
  limsup_{h->0} [f(s+h)-f(s)-h f'(s)]/h^2<=0.
Suppose f failed concavity. Subtract a chord on some [alpha,beta], obtaining
g with zero endpoints and a negative interior value. For sufficiently small
epsilon>0, g(s)-epsilon s^2 has a global minimum at an interior s_*: its
endpoint perturbations are arbitrarily small while the negative interior
gap persists. At that minimum g'(s_*)=2epsilon s_*, and
  g(s_*+h)-g(s_*)-h g'(s_*)>=epsilon h^2.
This contradicts the local upper quotient. Section 8's calculus is correct.
There is no assumption of second differentiability, nor an illicit uniform
little-oh bound across base points.

4. EXPLICIT P2 DENSITY APPROXIMATION

Given X with law nu in P2, first replace X outside radius R by zero, then
convolve its law with a smooth compactly supported mollifier whose scale
tends to zero. This gives smooth compactly supported probability densities
f_n with f_n dx ->nu in W2. Indeed the truncation error is bounded by
(E |X|^2 1_{|X|>R})^(1/2), and the mollification coupling error tends to zero.
In particular the second moments of f_n are uniformly bounded.

For 0<epsilon_n<1 tending to zero, define
  rho_n=(1-epsilon_n)f_n+epsilon_n g,
where g is the standard Gaussian density. Then exactly
  q_n=-log[1+((1-epsilon_n)/epsilon_n) f_n/g],
  Z_n=1/epsilon_n,
  rho_n=Z_n^(-1)e^(-q_n)g.

Because g is positive and smooth and f_n is smooth with compact support,
q_n is smooth and identically zero outside that compact support. The log
argument is at least one. Thus q_n really belongs to C_c^infinity; there is
no boundary-smoothness or positivity gap in this representation.

Couple an f_n variable X_n to itself with probability 1-epsilon_n, and to
an independent standard Gaussian G with probability epsilon_n. The second
marginal is rho_n and
  W2(rho_n dx,f_n dx)^2
     <=epsilon_n E|X_n-G|^2
     <=2epsilon_n(E|X_n|^2+d) ->0.
Therefore this exact regularity class is W2 dense in all P2 laws. No uniform
Lipschitz bound on q_n, or uniform transport Hessian bound in n, is required
to pass a finite concavity inequality to the limit.

5. TARGET CONTINUITY AND THE SEMIDEFINITE BOUNDARY

For C>=0, gluing an optimal covariance coupling with a W2 target coupling
and then applying Cauchy-Schwarz gives
  |F_nu(C)-F_eta(C)|<=sqrt(tr C) W2(nu,eta).
Symmetrizing the comparison gives the absolute value. Thus the three terms
of every fixed concavity inequality pass from rho_n to nu. This first gives
concavity for all P2 targets at positive-definite covariance matrices.

For the source variable, the same gluing argument gives
  |MCov(mu,nu)-MCov(mu',nu)|
    <=sqrt(E_nu|Y|^2) W2(mu,mu').
Coupling C^(1/2)G and D^(1/2)G therefore yields the explicit bound
  |F_nu(C)-F_nu(D)|
    <=sqrt(E_nu|Y|^2) ||C^(1/2)-D^(1/2)||_F.
The positive-semidefinite square-root map is continuous in finite dimension.
Apply the positive-definite concavity inequality to C+epsilon I and
D+epsilon I and let epsilon decrease to zero. Its middle covariance is
(1-t)C+tD+epsilon I, so every term converges correctly. This proves the
extension to the entire closed positive-semidefinite cone, including zero
and singular matrices. No uniqueness of a singular-source transport is used.

6. GRAPH-DENSITY ALTERNATIVE: VALID AND PREFERABLE FOR THE CORE HODGE BOUND

The primary note proves for every smooth compact skew A the augmented bound
  I(delta A)+(1/2) E<d(S_2(M)A),S_3(M)^(-1)d(S_2(M)A)> >=J(A).
I independently checked the three-index correction. If alpha,beta,gamma>0,
  q=-2(beta ab+gamma bc+alpha ca),
  k=(alpha+beta)a+(beta+gamma)b+(gamma+alpha)c,
then q+k^2/[2(alpha+beta+gamma)] is positive semidefinite. Its symmetric
matrix annihilates (1,1,1); its diagonal entries are positive; and each
principal two-by-two minor is alpha beta gamma/(alpha+beta+gamma)>0.
The displayed factor 1/2 and the bound 1/(6m) are correct.

For the minimizing L2 field A, the graph data are
  delta A=v in L2,       d(S_2(M)A)=0 in L2.
Take radial cutoffs chi_R with |grad chi_R|<=C/R. Distributionally,
  delta(chi_R A)=chi_R delta A-(grad chi_R) contracted with A,
  d(S_2(M)chi_R A)=chi_R d(S_2(M)A)+dchi_R wedge S_2(M)A.
Both commutators tend to zero in L2 since M is bounded and A is L2. There
are no derivative-of-M terms in these cutoff commutators. The remaining
cutoff errors in A and v vanish by ordinary L2 tail convergence.

For each fixed cutoff, compact Friedrichs mollification converges in the
graph norm of the first-order operator
  P A=(delta A,d(S_2(M)A)).
Its coefficients, including the relevant derivatives of M, are smooth and
bounded on that compact neighborhood. Gaussian and Lebesgue L2 norms are
equivalent there. The usual first-order Friedrichs commutator lemma therefore
applies. Componentwise mollification preserves skew symmetry. No global
bound on derivatives of M is required.

A diagonal cutoff/mollification sequence A_n consequently satisfies
  A_n->A, delta A_n->v, d(S_2(M)A_n)->0 in L2.
Since mI<=M<=LI, J(A_n)->J(A) and I(delta A_n)->I(v). The correction term
is bounded by ||d(S_2(M)A_n)||_2^2/(6m) and tends to zero. Passing to the
limit in the augmented bound gives J(A)<=I(v).

This method genuinely avoids assuming smoothness of the minimizer or proving
its global H1 regularity. It supplies exactly the weak-L2 Hodge estimate
needed by the transfer. My earlier ellipticity/cutoff proof remains a second
route, useful for the stronger equality analysis, but is not necessary here.
No substantive gap was found in the primary graph-density argument.
