1. THE SIGN CORRECTION IS NECESSARY

Before nonnegativity of the full noise excess E has been established,
the expression

  D<=C[E+A],

with an unspecified common constant C multiplying an unknown-sign E,
does not follow in general from

  (1-r ell)D<=E+C0 A,

even if D>=0 and A>=0. Enlarging C can make the first right-hand side
more negative when E+A<0. Thus the original shorthand must not serve
as a literal proved inequality on the class that allows E<0.

For example, purely as an algebraic warning, D=1, E=-2, A=1, C0=3,
and 1-r ell=1 satisfy D<=E+C0 A, whereas C(E+A)<0 for every C>0.
The root proof's intended positive final budget is recoverable without
this invalid intermediate manipulation.

2. V2 PERFORMS THE VALID OPERATIONS IN THE CORRECT ORDER

Let
  A=r^2/m+r^3L^3/m+r^4L^4/m >=0.
The actual retained-degree-two comparison gives

  (1-r ell)D<=E+C0 A,
  1-r ell>=1/2.

Division is legitimate and yields exactly

  D <= (E+C0 A)/(1-r ell).

Now use the authorized small-excess hypothesis E<=r/m^2 BEFORE
enlarging constants. Since the new upper numerator is nonnegative,

  D <= (r/m^2+C0 A)/(1-r ell)
    <=2r/m^2+2C0 A
    <=C[r/m^2+r^2/m].

The last step uses rL^3=o(1) and r^2L^4=o(1), valid uniformly over
0<r<=m^(-7/8)/L^2. This is exactly the operation made explicit in V2.
No assumption E>=0 is introduced, and the later comparison can still
prove E>=0 rather than assuming the desired global inequality.

The earlier coarse and preliminary localization budgets already
substitute the positive upper bound for E before displaying their
common-C estimates. They do not require another correction.
