EXACT EQUAL-ROW PROXY FROM AGGREGATE RADIAL ERROR AND ANGULAR SPREAD
1 November 2026. New elementary geometric proof; prior sources unchanged.

THEOREM — A FINITE STATEMENT WITH EXPLICIT SUFFICIENT CONSTANTS

Let B=(b_i)_(i=1)^m be a centered real m-by-N matrix and let beta>0.
Assume

  sum_i b_i=0, ||B||_F^2=w=m beta^2,
  E=sum_i (|b_i|-beta)^2,
  kappa=||B||op^2/w <=1/128,
  eta=E/w <=1/1024.                                      (1)

Then there is a matrix Z in exactly the same row span S=span{b_i}
such that

  sum_i Z_i=0, |Z_i|=beta for every i,
  ||Z-B||_F^2<=64 E.                                     (2)

No individual positive lower bound on |b_i| is assumed. Zero rows and
arbitrarily short rows are permitted. The constants do not depend on
m, N, rank, the number of zero rows, or separation between rows.
The construction needs no unused ambient coordinate. It gives a test
matrix; it does not claim centroid realizability or a noise comparison.

1. NORMALIZE DIRECTIONS, INCLUDING ZERO ROWS

Write r_i=|b_i|. Since ||B||_F^2=w>0, its row span S is nonzero.
For r_i>0 set u_i=b_i/r_i. For r_i=0 choose any unit vector u_i in S.
The choices for zero rows may coincide and may be adversarial.
In every case b_i=r_i u_i and |u_i|=1.

Call an index bad if r_i<beta/2. Then

  #bad/m <=4E/(m beta^2)=4eta.                            (3)

On good indices, u_i u_i^T <=4 beta^-2 b_i b_i^T in PSD order.
On every bad index, u_i u_i^T<=I_S. Consequently the full frame obeys

  ||m^-1 sum_i u_i u_i^T||op
   <=4||B||op^2/(m beta^2)+#bad/m
   <=4kappa+4eta <=9/256 <1/16.                           (4)

No estimate of a reciprocal zero-row norm has been used. The arbitrary
zero-row directions are charged only through their number.

Centering gives the exact identity

  beta sum_i u_i=sum_i(beta-r_i)u_i.

By Cauchy-Schwarz,

  |m^-1 sum_i u_i| <=(m beta)^-1 sum_i|beta-r_i|
                    <=sqrt(E/(m beta^2))=sqrt(eta).       (5)

This estimate is valid for every choice of the zero-row directions.

2. A MEDIAN OF THE UNIT DIRECTIONS

On S define Phi(a)=m^-1 sum_i |u_i-a|. Work in the closed ball |a|<=1/8.
All distances lie in [7/8,9/8], so Phi is smooth there. Write
v_i(a)=(u_i-a)/|u_i-a|. In PSD order,

  (u_i-a)(u_i-a)^T <=2u_i u_i^T+2aa^T.

Thus, using (4),

  ||m^-1 sum_i v_i(a)v_i(a)^T||op
   <=[2/(7/8)^2](1/16+1/64)=10/49.                      (6)

Each I_S-v_i(a)v_i(a)^T is PSD, so its positive denominator can be
bounded separately without changing the inequality direction. Hence

  Hess Phi(a)
   =m^-1 sum_i [I_S-v_i(a)v_i(a)^T]/|u_i-a|
   >=(8/9)(I_S-m^-1 sum_i v_i(a)v_i(a)^T)
   >=(104/147)I_S >=(1/2)I_S.                            (7)

Also grad Phi(0)=-m^-1 sum_i u_i, whose norm is at most sqrt(eta)<=1/32.
For a unit direction e and a=e/8, integrating (7) along the radius gives

  e.grad Phi(e/8) >=-sqrt(eta)+1/16 >=1/32>0.             (8)

A minimum of the continuous function Phi on the compact ball therefore
lies in its interior. Call it a_*. It has gradient zero and, by convexity,
is a global median of the unit rows. Taking the inner product of the
gradient increment with a_* and using (7) gives

  |a_*|<=2|grad Phi(0)|<=2sqrt(eta).                       (9)

The construction does not require the median of the original unscaled
rows b_i, which can lie at a zero row. It uses the unit rows, whose
distances from the small ball are uniformly positive.

3. CENTER, RESCALE, AND CONTROL THE COST

Define

  Z_i=beta (u_i-a_*)/|u_i-a_*|.                           (10)

The zero-gradient equation gives sum_i Z_i=0, and every row has norm
beta. All rows lie in S. Let Z0_i=beta u_i. Its discrepancy from B is
exactly

  ||Z0-B||_F^2=sum_i(beta-r_i)^2=E.                       (11)

For unit u and |a|<=1/8,

  |(u-a)/|u-a|-u| <=2|a|/(1-|a|) <=(16/7)|a|.

Equations (9)-(11) therefore give

  ||Z-Z0||_F <=(16/7)sqrt(w)|a_*|
              <=(32/7)sqrt(E),
  ||Z-B||_F <=(39/7)sqrt(E).

In particular ||Z-B||_F^2<=1521E/49<32E; the theorem's constant 64
is a harmless rounded value. There is no 1/min_i r_i factor.

4. THE ROW SPAN IS PRESERVED EXACTLY

It remains to exclude a loss of row span. Suppose v in S is orthogonal
to every Z_i. By (10), v.u_i=v.a_* for every i. Multiply these equalities
by r_i and sum. Centering and sum_i r_i>0 give

  0=v.sum_i b_i=(sum_i r_i)(v.a_*),

so v.a_*=0 and v.u_i=0 for all i. In particular v.b_i=0 for every i.
Since v belongs to S, v=0. Hence span{Z_i}=S. Zero rows cause no problem:
their weights r_i are simply zero in this argument.

5. GAUSSIAN-SCORE COROLLARY AT THE REQUIRED SCALE

Let L=1+log m and w=alpha^2(m-1) be the regular first-chaos scale, so
w is comparable to L/m. Suppose the normalized centered matrix Bhat
satisfies

  ||Bhat||^2=w, ||Bhat||op^2<=C0/m,
  E_rad=sum_i(|Bhat_i|-beta)^2<=C1 L^2 R,
  R<=C2 r/m.                                             (12)

Then kappa<=C/L and eta<=C rL. Thus the finite conditions (1) hold
for all sufficiently large m uniformly over any range with rL=o(1).
In particular they hold uniformly for

  0<r<=r_max(m):=m^(-7/8)/(1+log m)^2,                    (13)

because rL<=m^(-7/8)/L tends to zero. The theorem gives an exactly
centered equal-row matrix Z in the same span, with

  ||Z-Bhat||^2<=C E_rad<=C L^2 R.                         (14)

For an unnormalized matrix B=s Bhat with s bounded above and below,
the exact score remainder identity

  R_U(B)=w(s-1)^2/2+s R_U(Bhat)

implies ||B-Bhat||^2<=2R_U(B). If R_U(Bhat)<=C R_U(B), the same
construction therefore gives

  ||Z-B||^2<=C L^2 R_U(B).                               (15)

The fixed normalized row cap is still required when invoking the global
summed-row covariance theorem to obtain (12). The new geometric theorem
does not require such a cap, but it does not remove that separate analytic
hypothesis. Actual centroid entropy in a fixed mass window supplies it.

OPTIONAL OPERATOR BOUND. Equations (4), (9), and (10) also give
||Z||op^2<=C w(kappa+eta)<=C(||Bhat||op^2+E_rad).
In (12)-(13), E_rad<=C rL^2/m<=C/m, so ||Z||op^2<=C/m as well.
The exterior tensor argument needs only equal rows, centering and trace;
it does not require this additional conclusion.

6. ADVERSARIAL NECESSITY CHECK: SMALL RADIAL ERROR ALONE IS INSUFFICIENT

The angular/operator assumption cannot be dropped. For odd m=2k+1,
take a centered rank-one matrix with k rows
  +beta sqrt((k+1)/k)
and k+1 rows
  -beta sqrt(k/(k+1)).
It has ||B||^2=m beta^2 and

  E/w=2/[m(m+sqrt(m^2-1))] ->0.

Nevertheless an odd number of one-dimensional rows of norm beta cannot
sum to zero: each is +beta or -beta. Thus no centered equal-row proxy
exists in the same span, regardless of approximation cost. For this
example kappa=1, violating (1). This exact obstruction explains why
the proved angular spread, rather than radial smallness alone, is needed.

This is a new proof, not a numerical search. It removes individual row
lower bounds from the geometric proxy route while explicitly retaining
the hypotheses that make same-span centering possible.
