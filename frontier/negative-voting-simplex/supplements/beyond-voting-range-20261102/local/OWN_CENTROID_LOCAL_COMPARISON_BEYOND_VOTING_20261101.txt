THEOREM
Fix A>0. Set L=1+log m and R_m=m^(-7/8)L^(-2). For sufficiently large
m depending on A, every finite N>=m-1, and every 0<r<=R_m, let
f:R^N->Delta_m be measurable. Suppose its masses satisfy

 1/(2m)<=p_i<=3/(2m),

and its ACTUAL centroid matrix B_i=E[X f_i(X)] has Procrustes distance
d(B)<=A/m from the regular centroid orbit. Let g be the regular simplex
assignment, U_i=1/m, w=||B0||_F^2, and
R_U(B)=(w+||B||_F^2)/2-F_U(B). Then

 S_{-r}(f)-S_{-r}(g)>=||p-U||^2/2+r R_U(B).            (T)

Equality or competitiveness forces f to be a regular simplex assignment,
up to orthogonal transformations, label permutations, and Gaussian null
sets. The theorem permits fractional assignments and arbitrary dependence
on extra coordinates. It assumes no delta<=Cr^2/m localization rate.

The numerical exponent 7/8 and logarithmic power 2 are the requested
target range, not an assertion that this is the sharp local range.

depends on A,m, not r. The affine maximizer f0=g_(B,p), its prescribed-

   A2(f0)-A2(g)>=-C_A sqrt(m)L^13 R_U(B)-C_A mL^7||p-U||^2.

For completeness, polynomial facet probability can be proved without an

let x* be its closest point to zero, with |x*|<=C sqrt L, and let Y be

 z=T P(e_i+e_j), v=C^+z, T=C_* sqrt L.

Then v is tangent to that plane, |v|<=CT, and it raises the tied i,j

2. THE REGULAR NEGATIVE-NOISE LAW DOES NOT NEED rm<=1

On the regular score span H=1-perp, let X,Z be independent standard
Gaussians and Y=-rX+sqrt(1-r^2)Z. The joint covariance on H+H has
eigenvalues 1-r and1+r. It is positive definite for every 0<r<1.
The voting cutoff 1/(m-1) is not a condition for this two-copy law.
The within-copy label covariance remains P; it is not an m-by-m matrix
whose off-diagonal entries are being set equal to -r.

Write sigma=sqrt(1-r^2). Adding a common independent Gaussian coordinate
does not change the winning label. Hence T_{-r}g_i(x) is the utility
winning probability P_i(a), a_i=-rX_i/sigma. The exact integral is

 w_ij(a)=int phi(t-a_i)phi(t-a_j) product_{k!=i,j}Phi(t-a_k)dt.

Let w0=mu_m/[m(m-1)] and T have density phi(t)^2Phi(t)^(m-2)/w0.
Its mean M0 is nonnegative and O(sqrt L), and every linear tilt has
variance <=1/2. For U=range(a)/2,

 e^{-(4UM0+4U^2)}w0<=w_ij(a)<=e^{4UM0+4U^2}w0.

Integrating an ij utility swap yields a positive divided difference
k_ij(x), for ANY pair on either side of its tie, satisfying

 (T_{-r}g)_j-(T_{-r}g)_i=r k_ij(x)(B0_i-B0_j).x,
 sigma^{-1}e^{-E(U)}<=k_ij<=sigma^{-1}e^{E(U)},
 E(U)=4UM0+4U^2, U=r range(X)/(2sigma).                 (2)

No step in these exact formulas uses rm<=1. For r<=R_m, eventually
r<=1/2, so all appearances of sigma^{-1} are uniformly bounded.

With P0 the rank-(m-1) projection on the regular span, put

 eta=4(M0^2+1)r^2/(1-r^2),
 kappa(x)=exp(-1-eta|P0x|^2).

The Euclidean range bound and Young's inequality in (2) give
k_ij>=kappa. For

 W=log(sum_i exp X_i)+log(sum_i exp(-X_i)),
 e=C[r^2+r sqrt(L)W+r^2W^2] exp(C[r sqrt(L)W+r^2W^2]),

we also have e>=max_ij|k_ij-1|. These are the same explicit functions as
in [L], now justified on the larger range by (2), not by a voting-range
corollary. W is 2-Lipschitz and has first two derivative bounds independent
of ambient dimension.

3. DAMPING MOMENTS AT THE LARGER ENDPOINT

Fix q=C1L and every fixed multiple of q used in Holder. On an entire
pair hyperplane, on a normalized convex facet, and on bounded normal
shifts, Gaussian concentration and polynomial facet probability give

 ||W||_q<=C(L+sqrt q),
 ||e/r||_q<=CL^(3/2).                                  (3)

To check the exponential explicitly, its linear coefficient is
O(r sqrt L), its quadratic coefficient is O(r^2), and W has mean O(L)
and subgaussian fluctuations. The required exponent bounds follow from
rL^(3/2)=o(1) and r^2L^2=o(1), with q=O(L).

For the radial factor, convex-Gaussian concentration and a Gaussian
auxiliary integral give

 E exp(b|P0Y|^2)
 <=(1-2b)^(-(m-1)/2)
   exp[b|P0 EY|^2/(1-2b)], b<1/2.

Here |EY|^2=O(L). Taking b=q eta shows that ||kappa^{-1}||_q is uniformly
bounded provided r^2mL^2=O(1), and tends to its harmless constant limit
when r^2mL^2=o(1). At r=R_m,

 rL^(3/2)=m^(-7/8)L^(-1/2),
 r^2mL^2=m^(-3/4)L^(-2).

Both tend to zero. All first two normal derivatives of the analytic
factors have fixed L32 norms bounded by m^K, with K independent of r.
Differentiating kappa^{-1} introduces only eta-polynomials in P0x;
differentiating e/r introduces no inverse power of r. The relevant
projection has rank m-1, so no N-dimensional determinant is present.

4. EXTEND THE FULL SIGNED AFFINE TAIL UNDER rL<=c_*

We derive a slightly more general usable tail statement. For a small
absolute c_*>0, the proof of [H] gives, whenever 0<r<=c_*/L,

 |H_r(g_(B,p))-H_r(g)|
 <=C_A r^3L^10[d(B)^2+||p-U||^2],                     (4)
 H_r(f)=sum_{k>=3}(-r)^k A_k(f).

Here is the complete list of correlation-dependent checks in that proof.
Choose t=c/sqrt L, reducing c once to allow all fixed-multiple Holder
exponents. The dimension-free OU restriction lemma at time t is static:
normal Mehler-row Cauchy, tangential hypercontractivity, and polynomial
facet probability yield fixed conditional norms for T_t and its first
two derivatives. The exact derivative identity is

 nabla^j T_t v=(2t)^j T_{2t}(nabla^j T_{1/2}v), j=1,2.

It has no noise correlation parameter. Its normal contraction variant
only enlarges the normal calculation to a space of dimension at most2.

For the regular tail potential, the exact factorization

 q_i^0=T_t v_i^0,
 v_i^0=T_{-r/t^2}Pi_{>=3}T_tg_i

is valid when r/t^2<=1. Choose c_* so r/t^2<=1/2. Hypercontractivity
gives ||T_tg_i||_2<=C/m, hence ||v_i^0||_2<=C(rL)^3/m. Permutation
oddness then gives the exact quotient

 q_i^0-q_j^0=(X_i-X_j)P_e,
 ||P_e||_{Lq(facet)}<=Cr^3L^(5/2)/m,
 ||grad P_e||_{Lq(facet)}<=Cr^3L^2/m.                   (5)

For the current-potential difference q-q0=T_t v, integrate the affine
boundary derivative. The general-time trace [T] requires

 (r/t)^2 Lambda<=1, Lambda=O(L),

which follows from rL<=c_*. It gives
||v||<=Cr^3L^(7/2)d, and the same with d+||p-U|| on a mass path.
The signed boundary quadratic in the second derivative is bounded using
[T] at time sqrt(r), whose only requirement is r Lambda<=1. Thus

 |<f',T_{-r}Pi_{>=3}f'>|<=C(rL)^3 N_l.

Choose c_* small enough for all these inequalities simultaneously.
No condition involving rm has appeared.

The adjoint c solving L_graph c=div(w E(q_i-q_j)) cancels the complete
offset acceleration, as in the exact algebra of [H]. Its bound is
||c||<=Cr^3L^4d. For the coherent field V=-B^+(ZX+k), the full second
derivative is

 2<f',T_{-r}Pi_{>=3}f'>
 +2sum_e w_e E[l_e grad(q-c)_e.V+(q-c)_e Z_e.V
                              +(q-c)_e l_e div_gamma V].

The current-facet identity alpha(X_i-X_j)=-delta_l_e is used only after
differentiating the original quotient in (5). This gives

 grad(q_i^0-q_j^0).V
 =-[P_e l_e+P_e delta_B_e.V+delta_l_e grad P_e.V]/alpha.

Every subsequent transport bound uses (5), the displayed norm of v,c,
and correlation-free conditional polynomial moments. Therefore the
balanced Hessian bound Cr^3L^10||Z||^2 has unchanged constants.
Integration from the regular fan, whose first derivative is zero, and
the mass-path adjoint gradient 2c prove (4).

For the infinite series, cutoff k gains respectively (r/t^2)^k,
[(r/t)^2 Lambda]^(k/2), and (r Lambda)^k. All bases are bounded strictly
below1 by the same fixed choice of c_*. Thus the full signed potential,
its two parameter derivatives, and the adjoint converge uniformly; the
argument does not replace the tail by a finite chaos truncation.

Since R_m L=m^(-7/8)/L tends to zero, the proposed range lies inside
this newly derived affine-tail range. Combining (4) with the static A2

 S_{-r}(f0)-S_{-r}(g)

5. EXACT OWN-CENTROID CANCELLATION AT EVERY r<1

Let M be the centroid matrix of f0, v=f-f0, z=p-U, Dloc=d+||z||,
and Lroute=F_p(B)-||B||^2=<B,M-B>>=0. The inequality uses feasibility
of the actual assignment, not stationarity. Let

 u=T_{sqrt(r)}Pi_{odd>=3}v,
 qB=T_{-r}Pi_{>=2}f0, q0=T_{-r}Pi_{>=2}g,
 a=qB-q0-T_{sqrt(r)}u.

Hermite orthogonality gives, dropping only a nonnegative even square,

 S(f)-S(f0)>=r||M-B||^2+||u||^2
                +2[rLroute+<v,q0+a>].                     (7)

On a current winning cell i, put c_ij=(B_i-B_j).x+h_i-h_j>=0,
delta_l_i=(B_i-B0_i).x+h_i. The exact regular relation (2) yields

 r c_ij+q0_j-q0_i
 =r k_ij c_ij+r(1-k_ij)(delta_l_i-delta_l_j).

For psi_i=kappa^{-1}[e|delta_l_i|+|a_i|/r], preserve the j=i term as
zero, then sum against the arbitrary new probabilities f_j. This gives

 rLroute+<v,q0+a>>=-r Bcap(psi),
 Bcap=E max(0,max_{j!=i}(psi_i+psi_j-c_ij)).              (8)

These are algebraic identities and pointwise inequalities, valid for
arbitrary measurable fractional assignments and for every 0<r<1.

6. SMOOTHING AND THE CAP: DO NOT MISAPPLY [S]

The theorem printed in [S] assumes s^2<=1/(m-1). That theorem cannot
be cited with s^2=r in the new range. Instead we use the actual
moment-based normal-fiber argument below, whose hypotheses have no such
cutoff. The correlation-dependent inputs are explicitly checked here.

Using the general-time trace with cutoff2 as in Section 4 gives

 qB-q0=T_t vD, ||vD||<=Cr^2L^(5/2)Dloc.

Because u starts in degree3, r/t^2<=1 implies

 T_{sqrt(r)}u=T_t vu,
 ||vu||<=(sqrt(r)/t)^3||u||<=Cr^(3/2)L^(3/2)||u||.

Together with (3), Holder at q=C1L, and static offset estimates these
give exactly

 (sum_i||psi_i||_q^2)^(1/2)
 <=C[rL^(5/2)Dloc+sqrt(r)L^(3/2)||u||],
 sum_{e=ij}w_e E_e(psi_i^2+psi_j^2)
 <=C[r^2L^5Dloc^2+rL^3||u||^2].                       (9)

The second estimate uses bounded graph degrees and conditional OU norms;
it does not sum m unweighted facet probabilities. Put

 theta=C2L^4[rDloc+sqrt(r)||u||].

If theta=0 the forcing vanishes. Otherwise v_i=psi_i/theta has vector
Lq norm <=1 and bounded weighted facet square sum. Its analytic factors
and first two normal derivatives have fixed L32 norms bounded by m^K,
uniformly for all r in the new range. Indeed

 rDloc/theta<=CL^(-4),
 ||vD||/(r theta)<=CL^(-3/2),
 ||vu||/(r theta)<=CL^(-5/2).

The only remaining analytic factors are the damping functions controlled
in Section 3 and T_t at time determined by m. Absolute values are locally
Lipschitz and their a.e. derivatives are bounded by derivatives of those
analytic factors. No inverse r remains in the normalized estimates.

Here is the cap proof from precisely these hypotheses. For moderate
amplitudes m^{-B}<=theta<=m^{-5/4}, separate source and target rewards
using (a+b-c)_+<=[(2a-c)_++(2b-c)_+]/2. The correlation-free one-label
entry/exit margin has sublevel measure <=Cs for s<=c/m. Its Lq bathtub
bound and the vector Lq norm give

 Bcap<=C m^(1/(q+1))theta^(2q/(q+1))
       +(c/m)(C theta m/c)^q<=C_B theta^2.               (10)

For theta<=m^{-B}, set M=theta^{-1/8}. At projected points whose normal
supremum of |v_i|+|partial_n v_i| is <=M, positive pair rewards have width
at most C theta M/alpha. Fiber Taylor error and Gaussian-density error
are <=m^K theta^3M^3. Projections outside the actual facet belong to
third-label strips of this width; conditioned tangent altitudes >=c alpha
give the same error after summing the finitely many pairs and labels.
Bad projected points cost <=m^K theta M^{-31} by L32. Normal distance
>1 costs <=m^K theta^32 by full-space L32 and cost >=c alpha.
The main term is bounded by the weighted facet square sum in (9).
Therefore

 Bcap<=C theta^2+C m^K theta^(21/8), theta<=m^{-B0}.

Choose fixed B>max(B0,8K/5). The error is absorbed for theta<=m^{-B},
and (10) covers the remaining amplitudes. This proves Bcap<=C theta^2
at every allowed amplitude, with constants independent of r,N.

To verify the amplitude ceiling, use only the full mass box:
||z||<=C/sqrt m, d<=A/m, ||u||<=sqrt2 r^(3/2). At r<=R_m,

 theta<=C_A L^4[r/sqrt m+r^2]
       <=C_A[m^(-11/8)L^2+m^(-7/4)]
       =o(m^(-5/4)).                                    (11)

Thus even arbitrary masses in the full box are allowed. The stronger
post-localization premise delta<=Cr^2/m is unnecessary; if available,
it improves the ceiling to CL^4(r/m+r^2).

7. FINAL COMBINATION AND EXPLICIT ENDPOINT CHECKS

Substituting the cap into (7)--(8), the odd square is absorbed once
Cr^2L^8<=1/2. This yields

 S(f)-S(f0)>=r||M-B||^2-Cr^3L^8[d^2+||z||^2].          (12)

Combine (12) with (6) and use d^2<=C_A m sqrt(L)R_U(B). The first-chaos
terms cancel EXACTLY:

 r[w-||M||^2]+r||M-B||^2
 =r[w-||B||^2-2Lroute]=2rR_p(B).

Use R_p(B)>=R_U(B)+h_U(B).z and ||h_U||^2<=CL^2R_U(B). Young's
inequality gives the same final lower bound as in the preserved proof:

 E(f)>=[1-C_A(rL^2+r^2mL^7+r^3L^10)]||z||^2
       +[3/2-C_A(r sqrt(m)L^13+r^2mL^11)]rR_U(B).        (13)

For clarity, at the NEW endpoint r=R_m the relevant quantities are:

 condition                     exact endpoint monomial

8. EQUALITY, AMBIENT DIMENSION, AND PRECISE LIMITS

If f is competitive, (T) forces p=U and R_U(B)=0. For its balanced
affine allocation with centroid M,

 2R_U(B)=w-||M||^2+||M-B||^2.

The balanced endpoint equality theorem forces M=B to be a regular
centroid matrix. Since B is also the actual centroid of f, the regular
linear assignment objective is attained by f. Its winning label is
unique almost surely, which forces f to be that same regular assignment,
even if f was initially fractional.

Every use of a determinant or normal evaluation above is either on the
rank-(m-1) regular score span or on at most two normal coordinates.
Extra coordinates are integrated using Hilbert-valued hypercontractivity.
Full row rank holds throughout the local score ball; dimension-opening
score variations are covered by the coherent affine transport and
Procrustes formulation. No smoothness or boundedness assumption on the
exceptional sets of the original assignment is introduced.

No condition rm<=1 has been found or used. The only genuinely changed
inputs were the correlation range of the affine-tail lemma and the
all-amplitude cap justification; both were re-derived above. The voting-
range statement of [S] was not silently extended by citation.

This establishes the LOCAL extension within the same foundational
correlation-free proof chain. It does not establish the proposed global
theorem, does not verify every foundational input externally, and does
not provide a numerical alphabet threshold.
