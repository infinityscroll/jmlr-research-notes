INDEPENDENT WHOLE-BOX HAZARD HESSIAN CERTIFICATE
2 November 2026. Foundation challenge; new source only.

VERDICT AND THE PRECISE NEW EVIDENCE
The structured hazard remainder closes from the one-dimensional Gaussian
integrals. No unaccounted offset-acceleration or other-label term was found.
This certificate proves a slightly stronger statement than the requested
one: a Hessian estimate on an open variance box, for every direction and
every mixed pair of directions. It does not assume a Frobenius bound or
infer a uniform theorem from derivatives at the regular point.

The actual uniform evidence is (A) the finite-order whole-threshold moment
estimate proved below by a beta-tail integral, and (B) the pointwise ledger
in Sections 5-6. That ledger bounds the complete differentiated density,
including S'=sum_k (a_k b_k)', and solves for the actual balanced h''.
Finite identities alone would not establish the result. The global
covariance-concavity theorem and any later noise-stability theorem are
outside the conclusion of this certificate.

1. STATEMENT
Let m>=3, L=1+log m, mu=E max_{k<=m} Z_k for independent standard
normal Z_k, alpha=mu/(m-1). There are absolute c0,C>0 and m0 such that
for m>=m0 the following holds. Let

                 max_k |v_k-1| <= c0/L.                    (1.1)

Let h(v) be the unique mean-zero offsets for which the independent scores
sqrt(v_k) Z_k+h_k give each label winning probability 1/m. Set

 q_k=x-h_k, F_k=Phi(q_k/sqrt(v_k)), f_k=partial_x F_k,
 a_k=f_k/F_k, W=product_k F_k,
 rho_ij=W a_i a_j, omega_ij=integral_R rho_ij dx,
 ell_i=sum_j omega_ij.                                    (1.2)

The analytic diagonal j=i is included. For every real vector xi, put

 M1=mean_k |xi_k|, M2=mean_k xi_k^2,
 r_i=|xi_i|+M1, d_i=xi_i^2+M2.

Then, at the base point v,

 |D_xi h_i| <= C sqrt(L) r_i,
 |D_xi^2 h_i| <= C L^(3/2) d_i,
 |D_xi^2 ell_i| <= C alpha L^2 d_i.                       (1.3)

In particular this is homogeneous in xi, so no directional amplitude
restriction is required. For any two real directions xi,zeta,

 |D_xi D_zeta ell_i|
 <= C alpha L^2 sqrt(xi_i^2+mean xi^2)
                    sqrt(zeta_i^2+mean zeta^2).            (1.4)

The same polarized bound, with prefactor C L^(3/2), holds for h_i.
For a centered xi with max|xi_i|<=A, the target path v=1+t xi stays
inside (1.1) whenever |t|<=c0/((1+A)L), hence in particular whenever
|t|<=c_A/L^2. Along that path (1.3) gives the claimed ell_i'' bound.
Centering is used for the regular linear coefficient, not for (1.3).

2. ELEMENTARY WHOLE-THRESHOLD MOMENT CERTIFICATE
First allow arbitrary v,h with

 max|v_k-1|=epsilon <= delta/L,
 max|h_k|=H <= delta/sqrt(L),                              (2.1)

where delta>0 is a sufficiently small fixed number. Balance is not used.
Put X=1+|x| and Y=m max_k a_k. For every fixed pair of nonnegative
integers p,q, and all sufficiently large m depending only on p,q,

 c sqrt(L)/m^2 <= omega_ij <= C sqrt(L)/m^2,
 integral rho_ij Y^p X^q dx
            <= C_(p,q) [sqrt(L)/m^2] L^((p+q)/2).          (2.2)

All labels i,j, including equal labels, are covered. Constants are uniform
over (2.1). For (1.3)-(1.4), only the orders p+q<=4 are needed. Thus one
fixed maximum of their constants and m thresholds serves the whole proof.

Proof on x>=2H. Let sigma_-=min sqrt(v_k),
z=(x+H)/sigma_-. Every y_k=q_k/sqrt(v_k) is nonnegative and lies in
[z-C epsilon z-C H,z]. The change x->z has a bounded Jacobian.
Gaussian density ratios therefore give, with
E(z)=C(epsilon z^2+Hz+H^2),

 rho_ij dx <= C phi(z)^2 Phi(z)^(m-2) exp(E(z)) dz,
 max_k a_k <= C [phi(z)/Phi(z)] exp(E(z)).                 (2.3)

For i!=j, use rho_ij=f_i f_j product_{k!=i,j} F_k. For i=j,
rho_ii=f_i^2 product_{k!=i} F_k/F_i and F_i>=1/2, giving the same
upper bound with a constant. Also X<=C(1+z).

Set s=1-Phi(z). Elementary Mills estimates on z>=0 imply
phi(z)<=C s sqrt(log(e/s)) and z^2<=2log(1/s). Use
Hz <= H sqrt(L)/2 + H z^2/(2sqrt(L)).
After the substitution ds=-phi(z)dz, (2.3) yields

 integral_{x>=2H} rho_ij Y^p X^q dx
 <= C_(p,q) m^p integral_0^(1/2)
       (1-s)^(m-p-2) s^(p+1-beta)
             [log(e/s)]^((p+q+1)/2) ds,                  (2.4)

where 0<=beta<=C_(p,q)(epsilon+H/sqrt(L))<=C_(p,q)delta/L.
The factor exp(C_(p,q)H sqrt(L)) is bounded and absorbed in C_(p,q).
Take m>=2(p+2), so (1-s)^(m-p-2)<=exp(-ms/2).
With y=ms and R=(p+q+1)/2, the right side of (2.4) is bounded by

 C m^(-2+beta) integral_0^(m/2)
       exp(-y/2)y^(p+1-beta)[log(em/y)]^R dy
 <= C_(p,q) m^(-2+beta) L^R
 <= C_(p,q) m^-2 L^R.                                   (2.5)

For the middle inequality split y<=1 and y>=1. On y<=1, bound
log(em/y)<=L+log(1/y) and use beta<=1/2; the fixed power of
log(1/y) is integrable against y^(p+1-beta). On y>=1, the logarithm
is <=L and the exponential controls every fixed power. Finally
m^beta<=exp(C_(p,q)delta) is independent of m. This last observation
is the dimension-critical point; replacing it by an uncontrolled
exp(O(epsilon m)) would not suffice.

For the lower weight bound let sigma_+=max sqrt(v_k),
y=(x-H)/sigma_+. On x>=2H, y_k lies in [y,y+C epsilon y+C H].
Thus f_k>=c phi(y) exp[-C(epsilon y^2+Hy+H^2)], and F_k>=Phi(y).
For i!=j the resulting lower rho bound has Phi(y)^(m-2); for i=j
it has Phi(y)^(m-1), which is at least half that value when y>=0.
Restrict to 1-Phi(y) in [1/m,2/m]. For large m this interval has
y comparable to sqrt(L), x>=2H, and the exponential factor bounded
above and below by constants. Mills estimates also give
phi(y) comparable to (1-Phi(y))sqrt(L). Therefore

 omega_ij >= c integral_{1/m}^{2/m} phi(Phi^-1(1-s))ds
           >= c sqrt(L)/m^2.                             (2.6)

The omitted factor Phi(y)^(m-2) is bounded below by a positive constant
on this interval. This lower bound uses no concentration theorem.

Left and central thresholds. If x<=-2H, then y_k<=-c|x| and
Phi(y_k)<=2^-1 exp(-c x^2). Also a_k<=C(1+|x|), by the left-tail
Mills estimate. Hence
rho_ij Y^p X^q <= C_(p,q) m^p 2^-m exp(-cmx^2)
                                      (1+|x|)^(p+q+2).
Its integral is exponentially small relative to sqrt(L)/m^2.
On |x|<=2H all F_k<=q0<1 uniformly, while all a_k are bounded;
that interval contributes at most C_(p,q)m^p q0^m. This completes
(2.2), with no omitted negative-threshold region.

To identify its normalization exactly, set
Z0=integral phi(x)^2 Phi(x)^(m-2)dx. Since the maximum density is
m phi(x)Phi(x)^(m-1), integration by parts gives

 mu=m integral x phi(x)Phi(x)^(m-1)dx=m(m-1)Z0,
                         Z0=alpha/m.                     (2.7)

Applying (2.2) at v=1,h=0 gives Z0 comparable to sqrt(L)/m^2.
Thus (2.2), normalized by omega_ij, is equivalently

 omega_ij comparable to alpha/m,
 E_ij[Y^p X^q] <= C_(p,q)L^((p+q)/2).                     (2.8)

Here E_ij is expectation for rho_ij/omega_ij. Constants in (2.8)
also cover any fixed polynomial in X and Y of the indicated degree.

3. BALANCED OFFSETS AND THEIR COMPONENTWISE INVERSE
For fixed positive v, the function G_v(h)=E max_k(sqrt(v_k)Z_k+h_k)
is smooth in h. Restrict to mean-zero h. It is coercive: G_v(h)>=max h,
and on this finite-dimensional subspace max h tends to infinity with
the norm. Its h-gradient is the vector of winning probabilities p_i.
Direct differentiation of the one-dimensional winning integral gives
its Hessian as the complete graph Laplacian L_v with positive edge
weights omega_ij, i!=j. It is positive definite on 1-perp. Thus G_v
has a unique mean-zero minimizer, with p_i=1/m. The implicit function
theorem gives smooth h(v) throughout the positive variance orthant.
These conclusions need no external offset gap or rank-degeneracy theorem.

Whenever (2.1) holds, c alpha/m<=omega_ij<=C alpha/m. For a centered
vector g and mean-zero y=L_v^-1 g, one has

 |y_i| <= (C/alpha)(|g_i|+mean_j |g_j|).                   (3.1)

Here is a proof retaining the needed coordinate information. Subtract
the complete graph with edge weight c alpha/m, choosing c below the
lower bound. The remainder E is again a graph Laplacian, and on 1-perp
L_v=c alpha I+E. Its heat semigroup exp(-sE) is doubly stochastic and
contracts the l1 norm. Integrating exp(-sL_v)g gives
||y||1<=||g||1/(c alpha). The i-th row equation, its degree>=c'alpha,
and its edge upper bound C alpha/m give (3.1). A spectral l2 estimate
alone would not prove this componentwise statement.

4. FIRST OFFSET DERIVATIVE AND A UNIFORM OPEN BOX
Fix a base variance vector, and differentiate along v(t)=v+t xi.
Dots below mean these total derivatives, including h(v(t)). Put

 u_i=xi_i/(2v_i), b_i=dot h_i+u_i q_i.

Direct differentiation gives
dot F_i=-f_i b_i and dot f_i=-partial_x(f_i b_i).
Since p_i=integral f_i product_{k!=i}F_k, integration by parts gives

 0=dot p_i=sum_{j!=i} integral rho_ij(b_i-b_j)dx.           (4.1)

The boundary terms vanish in both tails. Moving dot h to the left,
(4.1) is L_v dot h=-g, where

 |g_i| <= C alpha sqrt(L)(|xi_i|+M1),
 mean |g_i| <= C alpha sqrt(L)M1.                         (4.2)

Indeed (2.8) gives E_ij|q_k|<=C sqrt(L) for every k; the pair
formula, omega_ij<=C alpha/m, and summation give (4.2).
The source g is centered by pairwise antisymmetry. Equation (3.1)
proves |dot h_i|<=C sqrt(L)r_i while (2.1) holds.

This estimate closes the entire box (1.1), not just one prescribed
direction. Given v satisfying (1.1), join 1 to v by 1+s(v-1), 0<=s<=1.
At s=0 the balanced h is zero. As long as max|h_i|<=delta/sqrt(L),
the preceding estimate with xi=v-1 gives

 max_i |d h_i/ds| <= 2C sqrt(L)||v-1||infinity
                  <=2C c0/sqrt(L).

Choose c0<=delta and 2Cc0<=delta/2. Integration gives the strict
improvement max|h_i|<=delta/(2sqrt(L)); by continuity there can be
no first exit from the moment box. Thus (2.8) and (4.2) hold uniformly
for every v in (1.1). The constants are fixed before m is increased.
No sum of squared coordinate errors is used in this continuation.

5. FULL SECOND OFFSET DERIVATIVE
The exact log-density velocity is

 lambda_i=dot f_i/f_i
   =-xi_i/(2v_i)+xi_i q_i^2/(2v_i^2)+dot h_i q_i/v_i.

Define A_i=a_i b_i and S=sum_k A_k. Then

 dot a_i=a_i(lambda_i+A_i),
 dot W=-WS,
 P_ij=dot rho_ij/rho_ij=lambda_i+lambda_j+A_i+A_j-S.       (5.1)

These formulas apply without modification to i=j. They retain all
other-label contributions. In particular S cannot be replaced by the
two displayed pair terms.

For a compact pointwise ledger let U=X+sqrt(L), Y=m max a_k. By (2.8),
every fixed mixed moment E_ij[U^p Y^q] is <=C_(p,q)L^((p+q)/2).
Using (4.2), bounded v, and small h, one has pointwise

 |b_i|<=C r_i U,
 |lambda_i|<=C r_i U^2,
 |S|<=C M1 YU,
 |P_ij|<=C(r_i+r_j)(U^2+YU).                            (5.2)

The S estimate uses sum r_k=2mM1 before applying the max hazard.
Consequently

 E_ij P_ij^2<=C L^2(d_i+d_j),
 E_ij |P_ij(b_i-b_j)|<=C L^(3/2)(d_i+d_j).                (5.3)

Here r_i^2<=2d_i and M1^2<=M2; all products between different
coordinates are covered by these inequalities.

Differentiate b_i exactly:

 dot b_i=ddot h_i+c_i,
 c_i=-xi_i^2 q_i/(2v_i^2)-xi_i dot h_i/(2v_i),
 |c_i|<=C d_i U.                                        (5.4)

Differentiating (4.1) now yields the actual acceleration equation

 L_v ddot h=-g^(2),
 g_i^(2)=sum_{j!=i} integral rho_ij
                  [P_ij(b_i-b_j)+c_i-c_j]dx.             (5.5)

This is not a fixed-offset derivative. Its right side is centered by
pairwise antisymmetry, since P_ij=P_ji. Equations (2.8), (5.3),
and (5.4), followed by the sum over j, give

 |g_i^(2)|<=C alpha L^(3/2)d_i,
 mean_i |g_i^(2)|<=C alpha L^(3/2)M2.

Apply (3.1):

                    |ddot h_i|<=C L^(3/2)d_i.             (5.6)

6. COMPLETE HAZARD SECOND-DERIVATIVE LEDGER
Here are every term and its summation cost. The exact derivative of
lambda_i is

 dot lambda_i=xi_i^2/(2v_i^2)-xi_i^2 q_i^2/v_i^3
       -2xi_i dot h_i q_i/v_i^2-(dot h_i)^2/v_i
       +ddot h_i q_i/v_i.                               (6.1)

Define the nonnegative threshold polynomials

 J=U^2+L^(3/2)U,
 K=YU^3+(Y^2/m)U^2+Y(L^(3/2)+U).

Equations (4.2),(5.4),(5.6) imply, pointwise,

 |dot lambda_i|<=C d_i J,
 |dot b_i|<=C d_i(L^(3/2)+U),
 dot A_i=a_i[lambda_i b_i+a_i b_i^2+dot b_i],
 |dot A_i|<=C(d_i/m)K,
 |dot S|<=C M2 K.                                      (6.2)

For clarity, the three sums behind the last estimate are

 sum a_k|lambda_k b_k| <= C YU^3 M2,
 sum a_k^2 b_k^2      <= C (Y^2/m)U^2 M2,
 sum a_k|dot b_k|     <= C Y(L^(3/2)+U)M2.                (6.3)

They use sum r_k^2<=4mM2 and sum d_k=2mM2. This is where summing
all moving offsets remains dimension independent. Bounding each label
first and then paying m without its hazard factor would lose the proof.

The moment certificate gives

 E_ij J<=C L^2, E_ij K<=C L^2.

Therefore the exact derivative
dot P_ij=dot lambda_i+dot lambda_j+dot A_i+dot A_j-dot S
satisfies

 E_ij |dot P_ij|<=C L^2(d_i+d_j).                        (6.4)

Finally ddot rho_ij=rho_ij(P_ij^2+dot P_ij), so (5.3),(6.4), and
omega_ij<=C alpha/m imply

 |D_xi^2 ell_i|
 <=sum_j omega_ij E_ij[P_ij^2+|dot P_ij|]
 <=C(alpha/m)L^2 sum_j(d_i+d_j)
 <=C alpha L^2(xi_i^2+M2).                               (6.5)

The sum explicitly includes j=i. No physical-facet interpretation of
rho_ii was used. Its positive-threshold extra denominator and its
negative-threshold tails were already covered in Section 2.

Differentiation under the integral is justified locally for every fixed
m by Gaussian tail domination of the differentiated expressions; on
negative thresholds the hazards have polynomial growth and W decays
as exp(-cmx^2), while on positive thresholds the density factors supply
Gaussian decay. The uniform values of those integrals are exactly the
finite-order estimates in Section 2. This separates differentiability
from the dimension-uniform bound instead of assuming the latter.

7. ALL MIXED DIRECTIONS
At each fixed v in (1.1), the preceding proof applies to any xi; a short
line segment through v suffices to define its derivatives. At boundary
points one may use the larger moment box, or pass by continuity from
the interior. Hence Q_i(xi)=xi_i^2+mean xi^2 is a positive definite
quadratic form for which
|D^2 ell_i[xi,xi]|<=C alpha L^2 Q_i(xi) for all real xi.
For any lambda>0, polarization gives

 |D^2 ell_i[xi,zeta]|
 <=(C alpha L^2/2)[lambda^2 Q_i(xi)+lambda^-2 Q_i(zeta)].

Optimize lambda to obtain (1.4), including zero directions by continuity.
This proves mixed bounds uniformly over all coordinate patterns, without
separate finite-support or two-block assumptions. The same argument
applied to (5.6) gives the stated mixed offset-acceleration bound.

One can also see the mixed offsets before polarization. Write
b_{i,xi}=D_xi h_i+xi_i q_i/(2v_i), and define P_{ij,zeta} by (5.1)
in direction zeta. Differentiating the xi-flux in direction zeta gives

 L_v D_zeta D_xi h=-g^(xi,zeta),
 g_i^(xi,zeta)=sum_{j!=i} integral rho_ij
       [P_{ij,zeta}(b_{i,xi}-b_{j,xi})
                       +c_{i,xi,zeta}-c_{j,xi,zeta}]dx,
 c_{i,xi,zeta}=-xi_i zeta_i q_i/(2v_i^2)
                              -xi_i D_zeta h_i/(2v_i).   (7.1)

Although the displayed forcing is not manifestly symmetric, it is the
derivative of a smooth balanced flux, so its solution is symmetric in
xi,zeta. The polarization argument bounds exactly this full derivative,
including all the asymmetric-looking transport terms in (7.1).

8. REGULAR COEFFICIENT, WITH THE NORMALIZATION RECHECKED
This section is supplementary: the uniform certificate is Sections 2-7.
At v=1,h=0, let T have normalized density phi(x)^2 Phi(x)^(m-2),
M=E T and V=Var T. Then omega_ij=Z0=alpha/m and ell_i=alpha.
For centered xi, (4.1) and sum dot h=0 give dot h_i=-M xi_i/2.
Writing a=phi/Phi, direct differentiation gives

 (dot a_i/a_i)|_(v=1)=k(x)xi_i,
 k(x)=[(x+a(x))(x-M)-1]/2.

Both dot W and sum dot a_i vanish at this point. Thus
dot ell_i=alpha E[k(T)]xi_i. The log derivative of T's density is
-2x+(m-2)a(x). Integrating its product with x-M gives

 -2V+(m-2)E[(T-M)a(T)]=-1,
 E k(T)=[mV-m+1]/[2(m-2)].

Therefore dot ell_i=-alpha eta_m xi_i with

                    eta_m=[m-1-mV]/[2(m-2)].             (8.1)

For completeness, V<=1/2 has an elementary proof. If the T-density is
proportional to exp(-Vpot), then
Vpot''=2-(m-2)a'>=2, since a'=-a(x+a)<0. Independent T,T' obey
E[(T-T')(Vpot'(T)-Vpot'(T'))]=2 by integration by parts. Strong
monotonicity makes the left side >=2E(T-T')^2=4Var(T).

For V>=1/m, realize T as the common coordinate of Z_1=Z_2 conditional
on these two coordinates being maximal. Conditioning on Z_1-Z_2=0
and the maximality inequalities depends only on the centered Gaussian
vector Z-bar Z. The independent common mean bar Z retains variance
1/m; thus T=bar Z+T_centered with independent summands. These two
variance bounds give exactly 1/4<=eta_m<=1/2.

Combining (6.5) with (8.1) proves, for centered bounded xi and
|t|<=c_A/L^2,

 |ell_i(t)-alpha+alpha eta_m t xi_i|
       <=C alpha L^2 t^2(xi_i^2+mean xi^2).

The sign of t does not affect any step of the continuation or the
remainder proof. This supplies the requested two-sided path statement.

9. SCOPE, SOURCE HANDLING, AND WHAT IS STILL SEPARATE
The requested hazard input is established by this derivation. There is
no smaller missing analytic statement for this input after Sections 2-7.
The proof yields an absolute variance box of radius c0/L, larger than
the path radius c_A/L^2 needed by the supporting-covariance application.
No claim is made that the L^2 loss is optimal.
