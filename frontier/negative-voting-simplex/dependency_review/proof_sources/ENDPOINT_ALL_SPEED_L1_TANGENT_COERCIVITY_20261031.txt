functional W(A)=sum_i |int_Ai x dgamma|^2 has, at the regular simplex,
an explicit positive spectral gap on balanced interface velocities modulo
ambient rotations. Its conversion to the first-order partition L1 metric
has order m^2/log m. This verifies the proposed GLOBAL coercivity scale
at the full tangent space. It does not provide a nonlinear neighborhood
uniform in m or a global deficit barrier.

The decomposition below is independently reconstructed from the exact

1. Reference form
Put H=1-perp in R^m, v_i=P e_i, P=I-11^T/m, and
 g_i={x in H:x_i=max_j x_j}, alpha=mu_m/(m-1), b_i=alpha v_i,
 ell=sqrt2 alpha, s=ell/m,
 lambda_e=gamma_H dH^(m-2)|Sigma_e.
Unused ambient coordinates carry independent standard Gaussian measure.
Let f_e be consistently oriented normal velocities with finite
 sum_e int f_e^2 dlambda_e. Set
 N(f)=ell sum_e int f_e^2 dlambda_e,
 A_0(f)=sum_i |sum_e B_ie int f_e dlambda_e|^2,
 A_1(f)=sum_i |sum_e B_ie int x f_e dlambda_e|^2,
 Q(f)=N(f)-A_1(f).
Here B is the complete-graph incidence matrix, and x includes all ambient
coordinates. Balanced first variations are exactly A_0(f)=0. The exact
endpoint half-Hessian is
                         -W''(0)/2=Q(f).                         (1)
This follows by differentiating the first moments: the quadratic term is
A_1, and the reference normal derivative of (b_i-b_j).x is ell. The
reference first variation vanishes on every interface, so acceleration
contributes no missing term. For actual balanced paths, second-order mass
repair also leaves(1) unchanged.

2. Complete intrinsic spectrum relevant to the gap
Let T have density proportional to phi(t)^2 Phi(t)^(m-2), and write
 M=ET, v=Var(T)-1/m,
 V=E[1-T phi(T)/Phi(T)-(phi(T)/Phi(T))^2].
In the first two moment representer space, the intrinsic velocities split
orthogonally in N into rotations, a two-copy standard space, and the
symmetric shape space. Explicitly the standard velocities are
 (u_i-u_j)+(z_i-z_j)(x_i-M), u,z in H, on Sigma_ij;
the shapes are (T_i-T_j).x/sqrt2 with T=T^T,T1=0,T_ii=0.
To verify completeness, split a centered score matrix into skew and
symmetric parts, discard its scalar P part (zero speed), remove the
diagonal by P diag(z)P, and leave the symmetric zero-diagonal matrix.
Constant speeds supply the other standard copy. The N-orthogonal
complement has A_0=A_1=0, so Q=N there.

Mass tangency kills u in the standard block. Exact facet moments then give
 N_std=m^2s^2 v|z|^2,
 A_1,std=m^2s^2 [m/(m-2)]v^2|z|^2,
 Q_std/N_std=1-mv/(m-2)>=1/2.                                    (2)
The inequality uses Var(T)<=1/2, a consequence of the tie density's
potential having second derivative at least2. Degenerate zero vectors
are simply omitted from the quotient.

The exact shape contractions in[S] give
 N_sh=m(m-1)s^2 V ||T||_F^2/2,
 A_1,sh=(m-1)^2s^2 V^2 ||T||_F^2/2,
 Q_sh/N_sh=kappa_m:=[m-(m-1)V]/m.                                (3)
Rotations have Q=0 and annihilate the bilinear form. Orthogonality of
standard and symmetric zero-diagonal shapes follows either by expanding
the index contractions or by the permutation-invariant tensor argument
in[A, Section4]. It is not inferred merely from quadratic vanishing.

For m>3 the tie integration-by-parts identity is
 1-V=m(2ET^2-1)/[(m-2)(m-3)].
Since ET^2~2log m, this yields
                  kappa_m~4log(m)/m.                            (4)
Consequently kappa_m<1/2 for all sufficiently large m, and the exact
smallest positive intrinsic eigenvalue is kappa_m. The shape space is
nonzero for m>=4.

3. Unused ambient coordinates
Expand velocities in Hermites of the unused coordinates. In external
degree0 the preceding calculation applies. In degree1, only the constant
intrinsic moment contributes to A_1. The constant cut space has Q=0:
these are precisely rotations mixing H and that extra coordinate.
On its N-orthogonal complement the complete-graph identity BB^T=mP and
s=ell/m give A_1=0, hence Q=N. All external degrees>=2 have A_1=0 as well.
This is the direct endpoint argument; it does not take a nonuniform
weighted-space limit as negative correlation tends0.

If R is the finite-dimensional space of all nonzero ambient rotation
velocities and f_perp is the N-orthogonal projection off R, then
              Q(f)>=kappa_m N(f_perp), A_0(f)=0.                 (5)
The nullspace is exactly R. Smooth compact regular-patch velocities are
dense in this L2 form domain, with the finite mass constraints repaired
by compact cut-space tests. Thus the form statement extends to all such
L2 velocities.

4. Direct first-order L1 estimate
The sum of the unoriented facet Gaussian masses is binom(m,2)s. Hence
for any velocity h, Cauchy gives
 [2 sum_e int |h_e| dlambda_e]^2 <=2(m-1)N(h).
Choosing h=f_perp, and then allowing the better L1 choice of rotation,
 D_1(f):=inf_(r in R) 2 sum_e int |f_e-r_e| dlambda_e
 satisfies
 D_1(f)^2 <=[2(m-1)/kappa_m]Q(f)
            ~[m^2/(2log m)]Q(f).                                (6)
For an actual smooth balanced path with this initial velocity, rotating
the reference at the selected first-order rotation gives
 limsup_(t->0) dist_L1(A(t),regular)^2/t^2 <=D_1(f)^2.
Together with w-W(A(t))=Q(f)t^2+o(t^2), this proves the desired order of
coercivity along every nonrotation tangent direction. For a pure rotation
velocity, both displayed quadratic terms vanish and no claim about their
higher-order ratio is made.

5. Exact global transport split and what is still missing
For an arbitrary balanced assignment f, let B be its centroid matrix,
W=||B||_F^2, and F_B the maximal linear score E sum_i g_i B_i.X among
balanced assignments. Let g_B be an optimizer with centroid matrix M.
Define R_B=F_B-W and G_B=w-[2F_B-W]. Then
 R_B>=0, G_B>=0,
                  w-W=G_B+2R_B,
                  ||M-B||_F^2<=G_B.                            (7)
Indeed W(M)=W+2R_B+||M-B||^2<=w. The global endpoint theorem gives the
last bound; existence of the linear transport optimizer is the standard
finite-target Gaussian transport problem. Coincident score sites may
have nonunique allocations; no unique fan is assumed in that case.

Equation(7) separates routing loss and score-shape loss without a factor
of log m. The unresolved step is a finite, coupled control of partition
distance by these two losses at scale m^2/log m. Applying a worst-case
centroid estimate and a separate regular-score margin bound loses that
logarithm. Equation(6) does not repair this globally: its neighborhood may
shrink with m, and first-moment localization does not place a partition
inside any proved uniform nonlinear neighborhood.

A sufficient weaker GLOBAL gate for BALANCED competitors at the improved
actual-noise deficit scale is a fixed-distance barrier: for every eta>0,
 liminf_(m->infinity) [m^2/log m]
 inf{w-W(f): f balanced, dist_L1(f,regular)>=eta}>0.                (8)
Then a balanced deficit O(m^-2) would imply L1 convergence. The actual
negative-noise competitors are only near balanced; applying this route
to them also requires a compatible near-balanced extension or a mass
repair whose deficit cost is controlled at this scale. Neither that
extension, (8), nor its quantitative eta^2 strengthening is proved here.
The cross-polytope source[C] is consistent with both and shows why the
older O(log m/m^2) localization alone cannot establish convergence.
