CONDITIONED SCORE OFFSETS AND THE NEAR-BALANCED FINITE DUAL
31 October 2026. Branch derivation. Independent full audit pending.

SCOPE
This supplies a deficit-relative offset bound for any centered score
matrix whose nonzero singular values are comparable to the regular
centroid scale alpha. It extends the new BALANCED finite score payment
to prescribed near-uniform masses. It does not assume the score matrix
is a covariance optimizer, and does not assert that actual competitive
centroids are well conditioned. No full-noise comparison is inferred.

The elementary Gaussian centroid, isoperimetric, and weighted Cheeger
inputs below are the same ones proved or invoked precisely in[R]. Here
no edge pruning or covariance-optimizer KKT hypothesis is required.

1. Statement and conventions
Let b=Phi^(-1)(1-1/m), alpha=E max_i Z_i/(m-1), U_i=1/m,
w=alpha^2(m-1), and m sufficiently large. Fix positive constants c0,C0.
Let B be a centered m-by-N score matrix, N>=m-1, with all its m-1
nonzero singular values in[c0 alpha,C0 alpha]. Define F_p(B), R_U(B)
as in[D], and take mean-zero optimal balancing offsets h=h_U(B).
There is K_m<=C_(c0,C0)(1+log m)^2 such that
                 ||h||^2<=2K_m R_U(B),                           (1)
and for every probability vector p,
 F_p(B)<= (w+||B||^2)/2-R_U(B)/2+K_m||p-U||^2.                  (2)
The final inequality is valid even outside the mass box, because it
uses one feasible dual offset. Only the proof of(1) needs a small mass
perturbation inside the accepted positive box.

2. An offset-Laplacian spectral gap for conditioned score matrices
Consider any masses p_i in[1/(2m),3/(2m)] and their unique Gaussian score
fan with sites B_i and optimal offsets. Let M_i=int_(cell i) x dgamma be
its unnormalized centroid. For physical interfaces put
 ell_ij=|B_i-B_j|,
 w_ij=(Gaussian facet area)/ell_ij,
 L=the offset Laplacian with weights w_ij.
The exact Gaussian integration-by-parts identity is
                              M=L B.                            (3)
All rows of L are in H. The Gaussian conditional-mean bound gives
 ||M_i||<=p_i sqrt(2log(1/p_i))<=C b/m.
Applying the pseudoinverse of B to(3),
 ||L_i||<=C b/(m alpha)<=C,
so every weighted degree L_ii is at most an absolute constant depending
only on c0,C0. This is NOT inferred from a stationary centroid equation
or from KKT: B need not be an optimizer of any centroid functional.

For a label subset S of cardinality k, the Gaussian perimeter of the
union of its cells is sum_(i in S,j outside S) ell_ij w_ij. Since
ell_ij<=sqrt2 C0 alpha and Gaussian isoperimetry gives perimeter at
least I(p(S))=phi(Phi^-1(p(S))),
 sum_(cross S) w_ij>=c I(p(S))/alpha
                  >=c min(k,m-k)/(m alpha)
                  >=c min(k,m-k)/b.                             (4)
The middle step uses I(t)>=2phi(0)min(t,1-t), plus the lower bound on
each mass and its complement. Weighted graph Cheeger with counting
measure and bounded maximum degree now gives
                           lambda_2(L)>=c/b^2.                  (5)
This estimate is uniform throughout the mass box. The score sites are
affinely independent because B has full rank on H, so the offset dual
is smooth and its Laplacian is positive definite on H.

3. A lower mass Hessian for the score value
The envelope derivative is grad_p F_p(B)=-h_p(B) in mean-zero gauge.
Differentiating the mass equations gives dh/dp=L^-1. Hence(5) implies
                         F_pp(B)=-L^-1>=-C b^2 I.                (6)
In particular, for any mean-zero z whose segment U+t z stays in the
mass box,
 F_(U+z)(B)>=F_U(B)-h.z-(C b^2/2)||z||^2.                       (7)
This is a lower Taylor estimate in the mass variable for a fixed score
matrix. It is not a claim of joint concavity in score covariance and mass.

4. Combine with the accepted global optimized-moment envelope
The first-moment identity in[D] gives, for every p,
 F_p(B)<= (||B||^2+V(p))/2.
Input[M,G] supplies V(U+z)<=w+A_m||z||^2 on the accepted mass box, with
A_m=O((1+log m)^2). Combining this upper bound with(7),
 -h.z <= R_U(B)+(A_m+C b^2)||z||^2/2.                           (8)
Choose K_m>=A_m+C b^2 and K_m>=b^4, with K_m<=C(1+log m)^2.

The increment z=-h/K_m is admissible for large m. To see this without
circularly using(1), each winning cell of mass1/m is contained in the
pairwise comparison halfspace against every other label. Using this for
both labels yields
 |h_i-h_j|<= b ell_ij<=C alpha b.
The mean-zero gauge then gives ||h||infinity<=C alpha b<=C b^2/m.
Consequently ||h/K_m||infinity<=C/(m b^2)<1/(2m), and the full segment
U-t h/K_m lies in[1/(2m),3/(2m)]. Substituting z=-h/K_m into(8) gives
                              ||h||^2<=2K_m R_U(B),
which is(1). Thus the certificate is deficit-relative, not just an
absolute offset-size estimate.

5. The finite score payment survives a mass displacement
The balanced optimal offsets are a valid competitor in the dual for
ANY p. Hence
 F_p(B)<=F_U(B)-h.(p-U)
 <=(w+||B||^2)/2-R_U(B)+sqrt(2K_m R_U(B))||p-U||.
Young's inequality bounds the last term by R_U(B)/2+K_m||p-U||^2,
proving(2).

Equivalently, for the regular centroid matrix B0 and B=B0+Z,
 sup_(E f=p){<M_f-B0,Z>-[w-<B0,M_f>]}
 <=||Z||^2/2-R_U(B)/2+K_m||p-U||^2.                            (9)
If the Procrustes score distance obeys d(B)<=A/m for fixed A, then the
conditioning hypothesis holds for large m and[F] supplies
 R_U(B)>=c_A d(B)^2/[m sqrt(log m)].
Thus(9) retains half of that actual finite payment, at a mass cost of
order log^2(m)||p-U||^2. It does not rely on the false uniform full-local-
Hessian approximation diagnosed in[D].

its higher-degree couplings. This note neither proves that comparison
