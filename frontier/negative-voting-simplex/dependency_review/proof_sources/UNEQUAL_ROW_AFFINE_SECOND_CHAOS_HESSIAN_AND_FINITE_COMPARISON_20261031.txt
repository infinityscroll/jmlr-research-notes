THEOREM AND SCOPE
Write L=1+log m, alpha=E max_(i<=m)Z_i/(m-1), P=I-J/m, and let B0 be
any regular centroid score matrix, B0 B0^T=alpha^2 P. For each fixed
A>0 and all sufficiently large m depending on A, let B be centered,
N>=m-1, and have Procrustes distance d(B)<=A/m from the regular orbit.
Let g_(B,p) be its affine Gaussian winning allocation with prescribed
masses p. Put U_i=1/m and
 R(B)=(w+||B||_F^2)/2-F_U(B), w=alpha^2(m-1).
There are constants C_A, independent of N,m and B, such that

 A2(g_(B,U))-A2(g)>=-C_A sqrt(m)L^13 R(B),                     (1)

 A2(g_(B,p))-A2(g)
 >=-C_A sqrt(m)L^13 R(B)-C_A m L^7||p-U||^2                  (2)

whenever every p_i lies in[1/(2m),3/(2m)]. The logarithmic exponents are
deliberately loose. In particular the coefficient of R in(1) is o(m).
The proof allows unequal row norms and NONZERO balancing offsets.

The key analytic lemma proved below is an honest balanced-affine
Hessian estimate in SCORE units. In a fixed aligned ball ||B-B0||<=A/m,
for every centered score velocity Z,

 |D_B^2 A2(g_(B,U))[Z,Z]|<=C_A L^10||Z||_F^2.                 (3)

Offset acceleration is canceled by a mass-Laplacian adjoint, not omitted.
The estimate(3) alone would be too weak in the shape directions; it is
combined with the accepted equal-row estimate[E] and the extra factor m
on diagonal inhomogeneity in covariance normal energy.

This is a finite SECOND-CHAOS comparison on an explicit score ball. It
does not place arbitrary actual centroids in that ball, prove full-noise
monotonicity, or resolve the global Standard Simplex/Plurality statement.
At negative voting r<=1/(m-1), r*sqrt(m)L^13 and r^2*mL^7 tend to0;
thus the coefficients have the required affordable orders if the other
full-noise and entry estimates are supplied. They are not supplied here.

1. Uniform geometry and polynomial trace bounds
After alignment, ||B-B0||<=A/m. Since alpha is comparable to sqrt(L)/m,
all singular values of B on1-perp lie in[alpha/2,2alpha] for large m.
The same holds on every fixed enlargement of this ball. Throughout this
section masses may range over[1/(2m),3/(2m)]. Define
 ell_e=|B_i-B_j|, mu_e=Gaussian surface mass on facet e=ij,
 w_e=mu_e/ell_e, mathcal L=the weighted offset Laplacian,
 nu_e=normalized Gaussian law on the facet.
Constants below are uniform in m,N and in this conditioned family.

The accepted identity M=mathcal L B and Gaussian centroid bound imply
max_i sum_j w_ij<=C. Union Gaussian isoperimetry and weighted Cheeger,
exactly as proved in[O, Section2], give

 ||mathcal L||op<=C,             mathcal L|_(1-perp)>=c/L.     (4)

We also need conditional polynomial moments, not only mean estimates.
Every intrinsic facet probability is >=m^(-C), and its normal coordinate
has absolute value <=C sqrt(L). Here is a direct justification. Normalize
scores and offsets by alpha. Pairwise winning halfspaces and the lower
mass1/(2m) bound give normalized offset differences <=C sqrt(L). In the
mean-zero gauge every normalized offset is bounded by C sqrt(L).
For a fixed pair prescribe equal winning utilities of height C1 sqrt(L),
with the other centered utilities equal and lower by at least C2 sqrt(L).
The minimum-norm inverse score map has norm bounded by an absolute
constant, so the prescribed point has squared norm <=C L. On its tangent
hyperplane, the event that all m normalized scores have absolute value
<=C3 sqrt(L) has probability >=1/2 by a union bound. A Cameron--Martin
shift, with gamma(E+v)>=gamma(E)^2 exp(-|v|^2), puts this event inside
the facet. This proves the claimed polynomial probability bound. This
is the construction in[E]'s predecessor28c75 with bounded row norms
in place of unit row norms; neither ambient dimension nor a small ball
probability appears.

Consequently, for any fixed polynomial degree d and fixed finite q,

 ||Q||_(L^q(nu_e))<=C_(d,q) L^(d/2)||Q||_(L^2(gamma_N)).       (5)

To verify(5), decompose Q into normal Hermite polynomials and tangential
coefficients. A normal Hermite polynomial of degree j, evaluated at a
normal coordinate of size O(sqrt L), costs at most C L^(j/2).
Tangential hypercontractivity at exponent C4 L costs L^((d-j)/2).
Holder restriction to a set of probability >=m^(-C) costs a bounded
factor at that exponent. Orthogonality and the finitely many degrees
give(5). In particular a conditional linear form has norm at most
C(sqrt(L)|z|+|k|), with the same assertion for each fixed q.

The accepted affine polynomial trace[T] supplies another useful estimate:
for any vector of polynomials of degree at most d,

 sum_e w_e E_(nu_e)(Q_i-Q_j)^2
                      <=C_d L^d sum_i||Q_i||_2^2.             (6)

Indeed use its OU trace at t^2=1/Lambda, Lambda=O(L), and apply T_t^(-1)
to the finite polynomial vector. Its L2 norm grows at most t^(-d).
This proves(6), including constants and mixed Hermite degrees. The
explicit full-rank hypothesis of[T, V2] holds here.

2. Balanced first derivatives, including offsets
At B choose a centered velocity Z, and let k=h'_B[Z] be the derivative
of its mean-zero balancing offsets. On a facet put
 l_e=(Z_i-Z_j).X+k_i-k_j.
Differentiated balance says div(w E_nu l)=0. Thus k minimizes the
nonnegative quadratic sum of l_e^2 over all constant offset velocities.
Using(4), the conditional linear moments from(5), and comparing with k=0,

 N_l:=sum_e w_e E l_e^2<=C L||Z||_F^2,
 k^T mathcal L k<=C L||Z||_F^2,
 ||k||^2<=C L^2||Z||_F^2.                                    (7)

The same upper bound C L||Z||^2 holds for
sum_e w_e ||l_e||_(L^q(nu_e))^2 for each fixed finite q. Indeed the
nonconstant terms are bounded by C L sum_e w_e|Z_i-Z_j|^2, while the
constant terms have precisely the offset energy bounded in(7).

Let Q_i(B)=E[(XX^T-I)g_(B,U),i] and write ||Q|| for the product
Frobenius norm. The facet first derivative, Cauchy with N_l, and(6)
with degree2 prove

 ||Q'_B[Z]||^2<=C L^3||Z||_F^2.                              (8)

Integrating on the aligned segment from B0 to B, and similarly using(7),
gives, with d=||B-B0||,

 ||Delta Q||<=C L^(3/2)d,       ||h_B||<=C L d,                (9)
 Delta Q=Q(B)-Q(B0).

All these are fixed-mass derivatives, not derivatives with offsets held
fixed. They are dimension free. For future use put
 delta l_e=(B_i-B0_i-B_j+B0_j).X+h_i-h_j.
At the current fan, (4),(5),(9) imply for every fixed q

 sum_e w_e||delta l_e||_(L^q(nu_e))^2<=C L^2 d^2.             (10)

3. The adjoint eliminates the whole offset acceleration
At the fixed base point B set q_i(X)=Q_i(B):(XX^T-I). Let c in1-perp
solve

 mathcal L c=div(w r),       r_e=E_(nu_e)(q_i-q_j).            (11)

This is solvable by(4). Set u_i=q_i-c_i. Then
div(w E u_e)=0, with u_e=u_i-u_j. Along the actual twice differentiated
balanced path, the coefficient of h'' in sum_i E[u_i f_i''] is
sum_e w_e E u_e (h_i''-h_j'')=0. Also subtracting c does not change
sum_i E[q_i f_i''], because every mass has zero second derivative.
Thus h'' is removed EXACTLY; no bound for it is asserted or needed.

The adjoint has a stronger bound than the crude inverse spectral norm
applied to arbitrary quadratic tensors. Write r_i=B0_i/alpha and
X_i=r_i.X. At the regular fan
 Q_i(B0)=gamma(r_i r_i^T-P_span/m),
 gamma=m[E(max_i Z_i)^2-1]/[(m-1)(m-2)]=O(L/m).
Hence q_i^0-q_j^0=gamma(X_i-X_j)(X_i+X_j). On every CURRENT facet,

 alpha(X_i-X_j)=-delta l_e,
 q_e^0=-(gamma/alpha)delta l_e(X_i+X_j),
 gamma/alpha<=C sqrt(L).                                    (12)

Conditional Holder, (5), and(10) bound sum_e w_e E(q_e^0)^2 by C L^4 d^2.
For the remainder Delta q use(6),(9), obtaining C L^5 d^2. Therefore
sum_e w_e r_e^2<=C L^5 d^2. Multiplying(11) by c and applying Cauchy
gives c^T mathcal L c<=sum_e w_e r_e^2. By(4),

 ||c||^2<=C L^6 d^2,
 sum_i||Delta q_i-c_i||_2^2<=C L^6 d^2.                      (13)

The regular quadratic's facet zero is the essential extra factor in
(12). Without it, (13) would contain a term of order A2(g), and the
generic transport estimate would lose a power of m.

4. Exact coherent affine-transport formula
For the linear score and offset variation Z,k, define the GLOBAL affine
velocity field

 V(X)=-B^+(ZX+k)=H X+v,
 K:=||H||_F+|v|<=C L||Z||_F/alpha.                           (14)

Here B^+ is the Moore--Penrose inverse. Since B B^+=P and the scores,
offsets and their velocities are centered, B V=-(ZX+k). It follows
that this single field moves all interfaces and all their intersections
coherently. Its normal velocity on e is -l_e/ell_e in the inward-normal
convention n_e=(B_i-B_j)/ell_e.

For fixed polynomial label tests u_i, differentiate their assigned
Gaussian integral twice along LINEAR B+sZ,h+sk. With div_gamma V=
tr H-X.(H X+v), the result is

 I''=sum_e w_e E_nu[ l_e grad u_e.V
                   +u_e (Z_i-Z_j).V
                   +u_e l_e div_gamma V ].                  (15)

One verification starts from I'=sum_e int u_e l_e/ell_e dgamma_facet.
Its material derivative has div_Sigma V-X.V and the derivative of
1/ell_e. These combine to div_gamma V, since
n_e.DV.n_e=-(ell_e'/ell_e). The derivative of l_e contributes Z_e.V.
Junction boundary terms cancel when summed: the global field is the
same on every incident face and the cyclic sum of label differences
u_i-u_j is zero. Equivalently apply the bulk Gaussian transport formula
to every cell under this global affine flow, then integrate by parts
once and sum the cell boundaries; this gives the same expression with
no independent junction term. Gaussian polynomial tails justify all
derivatives and boundary limits for fixed m,N.

For the ACTUAL balanced path the only additional term is its offset
acceleration, canceled in Section3. Since A2=||Q||^2/2, the exact formula
to estimate is therefore

 D_B^2 A2[Z,Z]=||Q'_B[Z]||^2+I'',                            (16)

with u_i=q_i-c_i in(15). The dependence of q and c on B produces no
further term: differentiating ||Q||^2/2 already gives the displayed
||Q'||^2, and c is frozen at the evaluation point to remove the mass
second derivative. No derivative of c is taken in this identity.

5. Every term in the Hessian is paid
We give the bounds to make explicit that(15) is not estimated by an
uncontrolled affine-flow dimension factor. The Gaussian L2 norm of
div_gamma V is <=C K: tr H cancels the mean of X.HX. Products of fixed
degree polynomials obey dimension-free Gaussian hypercontractive
L2 product estimates. Thus for z_i=Delta q_i-c_i,
 sum_i||z_i div_gamma V||_2^2<=C K^2 sum_i||z_i||_2^2,
 sum_i||grad z_i.V||_2^2<=C K^2||Delta Q||^2.
Also sum_i||Z_i.V||_2^2<=C K^2||Z||^2.

Apply(6) at degrees4,2,1 respectively, and Cauchy on the weighted facets,
using(7),(9),(13). The three remainder contributions in(15) are bounded
in absolute value by, respectively,
 C L^3 K d||Z||,  C L^(9/2)K d||Z||,  C L^(11/2)K d||Z||.
Enlarging powers, their sum is at most

                 C L^7 (d/alpha)||Z||^2.                    (17)

For the REGULAR part q^0, use(12) before taking absolute values. Put
S_e=X_i+X_j, delta B=B-B0. Its material derivative satisfies exactly

 grad q_e^0.V
 =-(gamma/alpha)[ S_e l_e+S_e delta B_e.V
                         +delta l_e(r_i+r_j).V ].            (18)

This follows from B_e.V=-l_e and
alpha(r_i-r_j).V=-l_e-delta B_e.V. Every term except S_e l_e
in(18) carries delta B or delta l. For clarity the bounds needed after
conditional Holder are
 sum_e w_e||Z_e||^2<=C||Z||^2,
 sum_e w_e||delta B_e||^2<=C d^2,
 ||S_e||_(L^q(nu_e))<=C sqrt(L),
 ||div_gamma V||_(L^q(nu_e))<=C L K,
 ||z.V||_(L^q(nu_e))<=C sqrt(L)|z|K.
The last two follow from(5); z is any fixed vector.

Consequently the term S_e l_e^2 from(18) costs at most C L^2||Z||^2.
The other terms of l_e grad q_e^0.V cost at most C L^3 Kd||Z||.
The term q_e^0 Z_e.V costs at most C L^(5/2)Kd||Z||, and
q_e^0 l_e div_gamma V costs at most C L^(7/2)Kd||Z||.
Here the two edge-local linear factors are summed using(7),(10), so
there is no spurious factor sqrt(sum_e w_e), which would be sqrt(m).
Using(14), all regular contributions are bounded by

                 C L^5(1+d/alpha)||Z||^2.                   (19)

Equations(8),(16)-(19) prove
 |D_B^2 A2[Z,Z]|<=C L^8(1+d/alpha)||Z||^2.
For d<=A/m and alpha comparable to sqrt(L)/m this implies(3), with the
stated looser exponent10. This proves the generic Hessian estimate,
including its negative signed transport term and offset acceleration.

6. Covariance derivatives and an honest upper bound for the mean gap
It is convenient to work with normalized covariance K near P and score
B(K)=alpha K^(1/2), embedded in the fixed H=1-perp. Rotation invariance
makes both A2 and R functions of K. Put J(K)=A2(g_(B(K),U)).

The square-root differential U solves K^(1/2)U+U K^(1/2)=H0. The second
derivative W in the same direction solves
 K^(1/2)W+W K^(1/2)=-2U^2.
Since all eigenvalues on H stay in a fixed positive interval,
 ||U||_F<=C||H0||_F and ||W||_*<=C||H0||_F^2,                 (20)
where the second norm is nuclear. These follow also by the positive
integral formula for the inverse Lyapunov operator; it is bounded on
both Frobenius and nuclear norms.

Let mathcal F(K)=F_U(alpha K^(1/2)). The score Hessian is N_l, so(7)
bounds its quadratic part by C alpha^2 L||H0||^2. For its acceleration,
use M=mathcal L B and (4):
 |<M,alpha W>|=alpha^2|tr(K^(1/2)mathcal L W)|
                              <=C alpha^2||W||_*.
Thus covariance concavity and(20) give

       0<=D_K^2 R[H0,H0]<=C alpha^2 L||H0||^2.              (21)

This step uses the nuclear norm and the operator bound on mathcal L;
bounding ||M||_F||W||_F instead would unnecessarily lose sqrt(m).
At K=P the gradient of R vanishes, since B0 is its own centroid matrix.
The gradient of J also vanishes there: every regular q_i^0-q_j^0 is
zero on its facet. Using(3), integration from P, and(20) therefore gives
on every fixed enlargement of the score ball

              |D_K^2 J[H0,H0]|<=C_A alpha^2 L^10||H0||^2.   (22)

Indeed the score-gradient acceleration is bounded by
C_A L^10||B(K)-B0|| alpha||W||_F, which is absorbed into(22).
No differentiability across a rank boundary is used: these balls have
full rank for sufficiently large m.

7. Strong diagonal coercivity and the equal-diagonal projection
First normalize the scale so ||B||^2=w. If B=t Btilde, with this
normalization, then the affine allocation is unchanged after scaling
its offsets, and

 R(B)=w(t-1)^2/2+t R(Btilde).                                (23)

In our ball t is bounded away from0 and Btilde remains in a fixed
enlargement of it. Thus R(Btilde)<=2R(B). Work with Btilde below.
Write K=P+E, tr E=0, E1=0. Let z=diag E and decompose orthogonally

 E_d=m/(m-2) P diag(z)P,          E_s=E-E_d.

Then diag E_s=0, tr E_s=0, and
 ||E_d||_F^2=m/(m-2)||z||^2. Denote a=||E_d||_F, b=||E_s||_F.
All K=P+s E_s+t E_d in the unit rectangle remain positive definite and
in a score ball C_A/m, because ||E||<=C_A/sqrt(L).

Retain the stronger diagonal term in[F]'s normal-energy proof. The
EXACT centered identity is
 sum_(i<j)(H_ii+H_jj-2H_ij)^2
            =m sum_i H_ii^2+2||H||_F^2+(tr H)^2.
On the initial segment of length c_A/sqrt(L), the facet bootstrap[F]
has weights comparable to their regular values alpha/m. Integrating
the nonnegative covariance Hessian remainder, with its linear weight,
therefore yields

 R(Btilde)>=c_A alpha^2/(m sqrt(L))*(m a^2+b^2).              (24)

This is precisely[F, Sections4-5] with the diagonal summand retained.
Its proof uses only the initial segment; it does not assert that the
larger endpoint has comparable individual facet weights.

Put R_s=R(alpha(P+E_s)^(1/2)). By(21) and grad R(P)=0,

 R_s<=R(Btilde)+C alpha^2 L*a(a+b).                          (25)

Similarly(22) and grad J(P)=0 give
 J(P+E)>=J(P+E_s)-C_A alpha^2 L^10*a(a+b).                   (26)
These are integral rectangle estimates, not a claim that the orthogonal
projection decreases a general convex function.

The matrix P+E_s has constant diagonal(m-1)/m and a uniform lower
spectral bound. Its balanced affine fan therefore falls exactly under
the audited equal-row theorem[E], after the common normalization:

 J(P+E_s)>=J(P)-C L R_s.                                    (27)

Finally,
 a^2<= (m a^2+b^2)/m,
 ab<= (m a^2+b^2)/(2sqrt(m)).
Combining(24)-(27) pays every projection term by
C_A sqrt(m)L^(21/2)R(Btilde). Enlarge to L^13 and use(23).
This proves(1). In particular the factor m on diagonal inhomogeneity is
essential: without it, the same rectangle estimates would cost m times
a logarithmic factor and would not be affordable at voting correlation.

8. A compatible near-balanced extension
Fix B and let p_s=U+s z, z=p-U, with its segment in the stated mass box.
The mass derivative of the offsets is k=mathcal L^(-1)z, so
 ||k||<=C L||z||,
 N_l=z^T mathcal L^(-1)z<=C L||z||^2.
The trace proof of(8) therefore gives ||Q'_s||<=C L^(3/2)||z||.
Together with(9), throughout this mass path
 ||Q(B,p_s)-Q(B0,U)||<=C L^(3/2)(d+||z||),
 ||h(B,p_s)||<=C L(d+||z||).

Repeat the exact adjoint construction(11)-(13), using these bounds.
Its conclusion is ||c(B,p_s)||<=C L^3(d+||z||). The mass gradient of A2
is EXACTLY c: for an offset velocity k, dA2=c.mathcal L k=c.p'. Hence

 |A2(g_(B,p))-A2(g_(B,U))|
                <=C L^3[d||z||+||z||^2].                    (28)

The already accepted finite gap[F] gives
d^2<=C_A m sqrt(L)R(B). Young's inequality in(28) pays the mixed term
by R(B)+C_A m L^7||z||^2. Combining with(1) gives(2).
Pair masses are not silently held uniform in this step; the path and
its mass derivative have been accounted for explicitly.

TESTS AND LIMITS
The sparse negatively correlated calibrated pair and the all-pairs
examples have A2 loss of order log(m) times R. They are consistent with
(1)-(2); the weaker sqrt(m)log^13 coefficient is chosen for robustness,
not sharpness. The remote balanced corrugation obstruction[b638] does
not lie in the affine-score family and does not satisfy the coherent
transport/factorization used in(12),(15),(18).

The theorem still assumes the explicit regular-score ball d<=A/m.
Unconditional actual-to-affine routing does not by itself prove this
assumption. Singular covariances are not covered by this affine theorem;
the separate rank-free CONICAL equal-row result has a different scope.
All prior sources remain unchanged.
