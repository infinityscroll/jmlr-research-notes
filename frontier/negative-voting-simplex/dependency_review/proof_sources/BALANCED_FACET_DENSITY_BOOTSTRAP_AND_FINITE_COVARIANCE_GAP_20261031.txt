THEOREM
There are absolute positive constants c,c0 and m0 with the following
property. Put b=Phi^(-1)(1-1/m), alpha=E max_(i<=m) Z_i/(m-1),
P=I-11^T/m and w=alpha^2(m-1). For a centered m-label score matrix B in
any finite ambient dimension N>=m-1, let
 d(B)=inf_R ||B-alpha Q R||_F,
where Q Q^T=P and R ranges over orthonormal embeddings of R^(m-1) into
R^N. Define
 F_U(B)=sup_(balanced assignments f) E sum_i f_i B_i.X,
 R_U(B)=(w+||B||_F^2)/2-F_U(B).
For m>=m0 and d(B)<=c0 alpha,
 R_U(B)>= (c/m) d(B)^2 min(1,c alpha/[b^2 d(B)]).                  (1)
The right side is interpreted as zero if d(B)=0. In particular, for each
fixed A>0, for all sufficiently large m depending on A,
 d(B)<=A/m  implies  R_U(B)>=c_A d(B)^2/[m sqrt(log m)].           (2)
The constants are independent of N. The conclusion is a genuine finite
Gaussian score-dual gap, not just a Hessian statement. It does not compare
the full noise functional, identify D_linear with D_trunc, or put every
competitive assignment in the required score neighborhood.

1. Fixed score coordinates and reference facet moments
Work on H=1-perp and let nu_e be the normalized standard Gaussian law on
the fixed facet Sigma_ij={y_i=y_j=max_k y_k}. Its mean mu_e and covariance
S_e, embedded as an operator on H, obey
 |mu_e|<=C b,  ||S_e-P||_F<=C.                                    (3)
Indeed[H](6) gives eigenvalue0 on the facet normal, eigenvalue
2m v/(m-2) in the remaining distinguished direction, and eigenvalue V on
the m-3 coordinate-difference directions. Here 0<=2m v/(m-2)<=1,
1-V=O(b^2/m), and |mu_e|=O(b). These statements follow from[H] and the
exact identity for1-V in[E]. Thus the Frobenius covariance deviation is
bounded uniformly, since b^4/m tends zero. The law nu_e is a standard
Gaussian restricted to a convex cone in its facet hyperplane, hence has
Brascamp--Lieb/Poincare constant at most1.

Let K=P+E be positive definite on H, epsilon=||E||_F<=1/8, and let h in H.
Denote by gamma_(K,h) the Gaussian density with covariance K and mean h.
The fixed-score-coordinate offset weights are
 w_e(K,h)=(1/sqrt2) int_(Sigma_e) gamma_(K,h)(y) dH^(m-2)(y).
At K=P,h=0 every weight equals
 w0=mu/[m(m-1)]=alpha/m.                                        (4)
The mass Jacobian for the shifted utilities Y_i+h_i is exactly the
complete-graph Laplacian L with weights w_e(K,h). This follows either
from differentiation of the Gaussian density or ordinary coarea. Notice
that the facets in these coordinates stay fixed as K and h vary.

2. A dimension-free quadratic-tilt estimate on each facet
The density ratio on Sigma_e is exp(c+q(y)), where
 D=P-K^-1, ell=K^-1 h,
 q(y)=(1/2)y^T D y+ell.y,
 c=-(1/2)log det_H K-(1/2)h^T K^-1 h.
One has ||D||_F<=2epsilon and ||ell||<=2|h|.
Using(3) and |tr D-log det K|<=C epsilon^2 gives
 |c+E_nu q|<=C[epsilon b^2+b|h|+|h|^2+epsilon^2].                (5)
Here the potentially large trace terms cancel. More explicitly,
tr(D S_e)-tr_H D is bounded by Cepsilon, the mean quadratic term by
Cepsilon b^2, and the linear mean term by Cb|h|. No ambient trace factor
or factor proportional to m is left in(5).

For clarity, the needed exponential moment estimate can be proved
directly rather than importing a quadratic concentration theorem. Tilt
nu_e by exp(t q), 0<=t<=1, and write mu_t,S_t for its mean and covariance.
Its potential has Hessian at least(1-||D||op)I>=3I/4 on the facet, so
 S_t<=2I,
 Var_t(q)<=2 E_t |P_e(DY+ell)|^2
          <=C[epsilon^2+|D mu_t+ell|^2].
Also, by covariance Cauchy and S_t<=2I,
 |mu_t'|<=sqrt(2 Var_t(q)).
Consequently v_t=D mu_t+ell satisfies
 |v_t|'<=Cepsilon(epsilon+|v_t|)
in the upper-derivative sense. Gronwall, (3), and the bound on ell give
 |v_t|<=C(epsilon b+|h|),
 Var_t(q)<=C(epsilon^2 b^2+|h|^2),
 |mu_1-mu_0|<=C(epsilon b+|h|).                                 (6)
All differentiations are justified by Gaussian domination, because the
tilted quadratic form remains uniformly positive definite. Convex-cone

now proves
 exp(-A)<= w_e(K,h)/w0 <=exp(A),
 A=C[epsilon b^2+b|h|+|h|^2+epsilon^2 b^2].                      (7)
The conditional mean on the perturbed facet is at most
C[b+epsilon b+|h|], by(6). The constants are uniform over every pair e.

3. Balancing offsets close the bootstrap
For the segment K_t=P+tE, let h_t in H be its unique offsets giving all
cell masses1/m, with h_0=0. Existence, uniqueness, and smooth dependence
hold for positive definite Gaussian score covariances: all simplex cones
have positive mass, and the complete offset Laplacian is positive on H.
These are the finite-target offset facts used in[C].

We claim that for epsilon<=c1/b^2, with an absolute sufficiently small c1,
 |h_t|<=C0 b epsilon t,
 w0/2<=w_e(K_t,h_t)<=2w0,
 |m_e(K_t,h_t)-h_t|<=C b,    0<=t<=1.                           (8)
Here m_e is the conditional score-coordinate mean on the facet.

The exact balancing ODE supplies the claim. Let a_e=e_i-e_j, orient each
edge once, and let B_inc be the incidence matrix. Differentiation of the
Gaussian density in K and h, followed by divergence on each fixed cone,
gives
 L_t h_t'=-B_inc W_t c_t,
 (c_t)_e=(1/2)a_e^T E K_t^-1(m_e-h_t).                           (9)
For example the covariance derivative is(1/2)div(E grad gamma); the
outward normal of cell i on ij is -a_e/sqrt2. This produces the plus
B_inc W c covariance contribution before imposing zero mass derivative.
Thus there is no missing factor or unnormalized facet mean in(9).

Suppose provisionally that |h_t|<=K0 b epsilon and that(7) puts the weights
in[w0/2,2w0]. Then m w0/2<=lambda_min(L_t|H) and lambda_max(L_t)<=2m w0.
The facet means in(6) and sum_e a_e a_e^T=mP give
 sum_e w_e(c_t)_e^2<=C m w0 b^2 epsilon^2.
Since ||B_inc W c||^2<=lambda_max(L) sum_e w_e c_e^2, (9) implies
                              |h_t'|<=C0 b epsilon.             (10)
The constant C0 can be fixed using just weights in[w0/2,2w0] and the
absolute mean bound; it has no dependence on K0 or m once |h_t|<=1.

Choose K0=2C0, then choose c1 so small that the right side of(7) has
A<log2 whenever epsilon<=c1/b^2 and |h|<=K0 b epsilon. This is possible
because each term in A is bounded by a fixed multiple of c1, with K0
already fixed. It also ensures |h|<=1. Continuation from t=0 and(10)
strictly improve the bootstrap bound, proving(8). In particular, balanced
offset size has been PROVED here; it is not inferred merely from positive
cell masses or from a uniform bound on an arbitrary covariance optimizer.

4. Normal-strain covariance curvature on the small ball
Write mathcal F(K)=F_U(K^(1/2)), the covariance functional for target labels
e_i with masses1/m. The accepted all-simplex normal-strain inequality[C](18),
transported from covariance I to K, is
 -4 mathcal F''(K)[H,H]
 >=sum_e w_e(K,h_K) (a_e^T H a_e)^2/(a_e^T K a_e).              (11)
The physical facet weights mu_e/|K^(1/2)a_e| equal the score-coordinate
weights in(4) by coarea. Thus the weights in(11) are exactly those bounded
in(8), not a new unproved collection.

For ||K-P||_F<=c1/b^2, every denominator in(11) is at most3 and every
weight is at least w0/2. The exact edge-dyad identity on H is
 sum_(i<j) (a_ij^T H a_ij)^2
 =m sum_i H_ii^2+(tr H)^2+2||H||_F^2>=2||H||_F^2.
Therefore
                  -mathcal F''(K)[H,H]>=w0||H||^2/12.           (12)
This uses only the normal part of the audited Hessian. No assertion is
made that its stronger local log(m)/m shape gain persists on this ball.

5. Concavity propagates a finite remainder beyond that ball
Let K=P+E with R=||E||_F<=1/4. By covariance concavity, the function
 phi(t)=alpha mathcal F(P+tE) is concave on[0,1]. Its tangent remainder is
 exactly R_U(alpha K^(1/2)), because
 phi(0)=w and phi'(0)=(alpha^2/2)tr E.
For
 s=min(1/2,c1/[b^2 R]),
equation(12) applies throughout0<=t<=s and gives
 -phi''(t)>=alpha w0 R^2/12=alpha^2 R^2/(12m).
Elsewhere concavity still gives -phi''>=0. Integrating with weight1-t,
 R_U(alpha K^(1/2))
 =int_0^1(1-t)(-phi''(t))dt
 >=c alpha^2 R^2/m *min(1,c1/[b^2 R]).                          (13)
The linear weight is essential: the loss from leaving the small ball is
only its relative segment length, not the square of that length.

6. Rotation gauge, arbitrary ambient dimension, and the final bound
For any centered full-row-rank score matrix B, orthogonal Procrustes gives
 d(B)^2=|| (BB^T)^(1/2)-alpha P ||_F^2.
Both F_U and ||B|| depend only on BB^T, so the same is true of R_U. If
d(B)<=c0 alpha with c0 sufficiently small, every singular value is close
to alpha; in particular B is full rank on H. Set K=BB^T/alpha^2. Spectral
calculus then gives
 c d(B)/alpha<=||K-P||_F<=C d(B)/alpha<=1/4.
Apply(13) to obtain(1), with absolute constants adjusted. At d(B)<=A/m,
alpha~b/m and b~sqrt(2log m), the minimum in(1) is at least c_A/b for
large m. This proves(2). No unused-coordinate OU evaluation or ambient
dimensional determinant enters this argument.
