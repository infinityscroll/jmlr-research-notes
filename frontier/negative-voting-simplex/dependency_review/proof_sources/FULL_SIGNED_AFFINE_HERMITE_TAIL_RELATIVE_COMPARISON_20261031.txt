THEOREM
Use the notation of[U]: L=1+log m, alpha=E max Z_i/(m-1), a regular
centroid matrix B0, and R(B)=(w+||B||^2)/2-F_U(B). Fix A>0. For all
sufficiently large m depending on A, N>=m-1, d(B)<=A/m, and masses
p_i in[1/(2m),3/(2m)], put f=g_(B,p), z=p-U. Define the ENTIRE signed
tail at rho=-r by

 H_r(f)=sum_(k>=3)(-r)^k A_k(f)=<f,T_-r Pi_(>=3)f>.

Uniformly for0<r<=1/(m-1),

 |H_r(f)-H_r(g)|<=C_A r^3 L^10[d(B)^2+||z||^2]               (1)
 <=C_A r^3 m L^11 R(B)+C_A r^3 L^10||z||^2.                 (2)

This is a relative finite tail estimate, not an absolute rare-cell
remainder. Every higher degree and its sign are retained in H_r.
The proof supplies the balanced score Hessian bound

 |D_B^2 H_r(g_(B,U))[Z,Z]|<=C_A r^3 L^10||Z||^2.             (3)

Combining(2) with the audited unequal-row A2 comparison[U] gives the
following exact-scope full-reference inequality:

 S_-r(f)-S_-r(g)
 >=[1-C_A(r^2 m L^7+r^3 L^10)]||p-U||^2
   +r[w-W(f)]-C_A[r^2 sqrt(m)L^13+r^3 m L^11]R(B).           (4)

At voting correlations this is
 (1-o(1))||p-U||^2+r[w-W(f)]-o(1)r R(B), uniformly in r.
No inequality replacing w-W(f) by a positive multiple of R(B) is
assumed. In particular, for a BALANCED affine fan with centroid matrix
M, the exact identity is 2R(B)=w-W(f)+||M-B||^2. Its direction must
be retained. Formula(4) does not by itself assert positivity for every
affine fan or actual optimizer, nor place centroids in the ball A/m.

2. A dimension-free OU restriction lemma, including a normal contraction
Here is the extra analytic ingredient needed for the infinite tail.
Let nu be any one of the facet laws above. Let n0 be ANY unit vector,
and let D_theta=I-(1-theta)n0 n0^T, 0<=theta<=1. There is an absolute
small c>0, depending only on the fixed geometric constants in Section1,
such that, with t=c/sqrt(L), for j=0,1,2 and each fixed finite q used
below,

 ||(nabla^j T_t v)(D_theta X)||_(L^q(nu;HS))
                                      <=C_j t^j||v||_2.       (6)

The same estimate holds for Hilbert-valued v and without D_theta.
The tensor norm is Hilbert--Schmidt; the constants do not depend on N.

Proof. Let n be the current facet normal and let E=span(n,n0), of
dimension at most2. The contraction D_theta is the identity on E^perp
and a contraction on E. On the containing affine hyperplane, X has
one E-coordinate fixed at a, |a|<=C sqrt L, at most one other standard
Gaussian E-coordinate, and independent standard Gaussian E^perp
coordinates. For a fixed y in E, the exact squared L2 norm of a normal
OU row is
 (1-t^4)^(-dim(E)/2) exp[t^2|y|^2/(1+t^2)].
Normal Cauchy and tangential hypercontractivity therefore bound the
L^Q norm on E^perp by this row norm times ||v||_2 whenever
t^2(Q-1)<=1. Integrate the remaining at most one Gaussian coordinate
in E. Since |D_theta y|<=|y|, this costs only
 exp(C t^2 a^2)*(E exp(C Q t^2|Z|^2))^(1/Q),
which is bounded if Q=C1 L and c is sufficiently small. Restriction
to the actual facet subset costs at most pi^(-1/Q), also bounded since
pi>=m^(-C). Choose Q larger than every fixed q needed for Holder.
This proves j=0, uniformly in theta, including theta=0.

For j=1,2, write
 nabla^j T_t v=(2t)^j T_(2t)(nabla^j T_(1/2)v).
Hermite orthogonality gives
 ||nabla^j T_(1/2)v||_(L2;HS)<=C_j||v||_2,
because the supremum of (k)_j 4^(-k) is finite. Apply the j=0 bound
at2t, reducing c once. Hilbert-valued normal Cauchy and Gaussian
hypercontractivity give the same proof for tensor-valued inputs. This
proves(6) without a pointwise OU bound carrying an ambient determinant.

3. The regular reference quotient keeps the rare-label factor1/m
Let P3=Pi_(>=3), rho=-r, and q_i^0=T_rho P3 g_i. Choose the same small
t as in(6). For large m, |rho|/t^2<=1/2. Factor exactly

 q_i^0=T_t v_i^0,
 v_i^0=T_(rho/t^2) P3 T_t g_i.

Hypercontractivity and gamma(g_i)=1/m give
 ||T_t g_i||_2<=||g_i||_(1+t^2)=m^(-1/(1+t^2))<=C/m.
The projection P3 and Hermite contraction therefore imply

 ||v_i^0||_2<=C(rL)^3/m.                                    (7)

All operators commute; negative OU times simply insert Hermite signs.
This factorization retains the full tail and the rare-label1/m, rather
than bounding it by the cruder unsmoothed1/sqrt(m) norm.

Let r_i=B0_i/alpha, X_i=r_i.X, and n0=(r_i-r_j)/sqrt2. Permutation
symmetry and OU commutation imply q_i^0-q_j^0 is odd in the coordinate
xi=n0.X and is zero when xi=0. Thus, exactly,

 q_i^0-q_j^0=(X_i-X_j)P_e(X),
 P_e(X)=1/sqrt2 int_0^1 partial_(n0)(q_i^0-q_j^0)(D_theta X)dtheta.

Equation(6), followed by(7), proves uniformly on ANY current facet

 ||P_e||_(L^q(nu_e))<=C r^3 L^(5/2)/m=:K0,
 ||nabla P_e||_(L^q(nu_e))<=C r^3 L^2/m=:K1.                 (8)

For the gradient differentiate under the integral; ||D_theta||op<=1,
so j=2 in(6) applies. On a current facet alpha(X_i-X_j)=-delta l_e;
hence

 q_e^0=-delta l_e P_e/alpha.                                (9)

Equations(8)-(9) are the full-OU replacement of the regular quadratic
factorization in[U]. No expansion in a remote spatial coordinate is used.

4. The change of the smoothed potential is small relative to score change
Put q_i=T_rho P3 f_i for the CURRENT balanced affine fan. Then
q-q^0=T_t v, v=T_(rho/t)P3(f-g). Integrate the boundary first derivative
on the balanced score segment from B0 to B. The affine boundary trace[T]
at s=|rho|/t, together with(5), gives

 ||v||_(L2(vector))<=C r^3 L^(7/2)d.                          (10)

Explicitly its squared operator factor is (s^2 Lambda)^3, Lambda=O(L),
so its square root is O(r^3 L^3); the square root of the velocity energy
contributes sqrt(L)d. The condition s^2 Lambda<=1 holds for voting r
and large m. The same estimate holds for a negative s by Hermite norm
orthogonality, or by reflection after positive smoothing.

On a mass path the same proof uses N_l=z^T mathcal L^(-1)z<=C L||z||^2.
Consequently(10) remains valid with d replaced by d+||z|| throughout
the path from the regular balanced fan to g_(B,p).

Set u_i=q_i-c_i. Then div(w E u_e)=0, so precisely as in[U, Section3]

6. Bounds for every transport term
Split u=q^0+T_t(v-c); constants are fixed by T_t. By(10),(12),
 ||v-c||<=C r^3 L^4d=:U0.
Conditional OU restriction(6) gives, for each edge and fixed q,
 ||(T_t(v-c))_e||_(L^q(nu_e))<=C||(v-c)_i-(v-c)_j||_2,
 ||nabla(T_t(v-c))_e||_(L^q(nu_e);HS)
                          <=C t||(v-c)_i-(v-c)_j||_2.
Conditional polynomial moments from[U] give
 ||V||_(L^q(nu_e);Euclidean)<=C sqrt(L)K,
 ||div_gamma V||_(L^q(nu_e))<=C L K,
 ||Z_e.V||_(L^q(nu_e))<=C sqrt(L)|Z_e|K.
Weighted graph Cauchy and(5) bound the three remainder terms of(13) by
 C sqrt(L)K U0||Z||, C sqrt(L)K U0||Z||,
 and C L^(3/2)K U0||Z||, respectively. Their sum is at most

                   C r^3 L^7(d/alpha)||Z||^2.                (15)

For the reference term differentiate(9) through its ORIGINAL quotient:

 grad q_e^0.V
 =-[P_e l_e+P_e delta B_e.V+delta l_e grad P_e.V]/alpha.       (16)

This uses alpha(r_i-r_j).V=-l_e-delta B_e.V. Bounds(5),(8) and conditional
Holder then give the following sufficient estimates. The leading
P_e l_e^2 term costs at most (C K0/alpha)L||Z||^2<=C r^3 L^3||Z||^2.
The remaining derivative terms cost at most
 C r^3 L^(7/2) Kd||Z||.
The term q_e^0 Z_e.V costs at most C r^3 L^(7/2)Kd||Z||, and
q_e^0 l_e div_gamma V costs at most C r^3 L^(9/2)Kd||Z||.
These are direct weighted Cauchy bounds using
sum_e w_e|delta B_e|^2<=C d^2,
sum_e w_e|Z_e|^2<=C||Z||^2, and the two edge-local linear norms in(5).
In particular no factor sqrt(sum_e w_e)=O(sqrt(m)) is introduced.
Together with K<=C L||Z||/alpha they are bounded by

                   C r^3 L^6(1+d/alpha)||Z||^2.              (17)

Equations(13)-(17) prove(3), even with the slightly smaller exponent8.
The stated exponent10 absorbs all fixed choices of Holder exponents.

7. Justification of the infinite series and finite comparison
For a fully direct regularization, first replace P3 by Pi_(3<=k<=n).
These projections commute with OU and all reference permutations; the
quotient construction and every estimate above hold with constants
independent of n. The high-degree version of[T] makes the boundary
quadratic tails uniformly small. In(7),(10) replacing the lower cutoff3
by any cutoff k multiplies by the corresponding powers (r/t^2)^k or
((r/t)^2 Lambda)^(k/2), both strictly below1 for large m. Thus the
potential, adjoint and transport derivative tails converge uniformly
on the relevant finite-dimensional parameter neighborhoods. Equivalently
one may pass using these bounds in the Gaussian transport identities.
This proves the entire signed series and its two derivatives, not only
formal termwise identities. Gaussian tails also justify the coherent
flow formulas for every finite cutoff before this limit.

At the regular balanced fan the first score derivative of H_r vanishes:
q_i^0-q_j^0 is zero on each regular facet. Integrate(3) twice on the
aligned score segment to obtain
 |H_r(g_(B,U))-H_r(g)|<=C_A r^3 L^10d^2.                     (18)

Along the prescribed-mass path of Section4 the adjoint proof gives
||c||<=C r^3 L^4(d+||z||). The mass gradient of H_r is EXACTLY 2c:
dH_r=2c.mathcal L k=2c.p'. Hence the mass-path difference is at most
C r^3 L^4(d||z||+||z||^2). Combining with(18) and elementary Young
proves(1). The finite gap[F] gives d^2<=C_A m sqrt(L)R(B), proving(2).

8. Full signed bookkeeping and the remaining gap
Hermite orthogonality gives EXACTLY
 S_-r(f)-S_-r(g)=||p-U||^2+r[w-W(f)]
                      +r^2[A2(f)-A2(g)]+H_r(f)-H_r(g).
Insert[U, Theorem2] and(2) to obtain(4). All coefficients and signs of
degrees0,1,2 and the full higher tail remain present.

For balanced f with centroid M, F_U(B)=<B,M>, so
 2R(B)=w-W(f)+||M-B||^2.
Thus a lower bound involving w-W(f) cannot be replaced by rR(B)
without an additional argument controlling the centroid residual. For
unequal masses F_U and F_p differ as well. Formula(4) explicitly leaves
those exact distinctions intact. It closes the RELATIVE higher-tail
estimate on the score ball, not the global entry or selection theorem.
