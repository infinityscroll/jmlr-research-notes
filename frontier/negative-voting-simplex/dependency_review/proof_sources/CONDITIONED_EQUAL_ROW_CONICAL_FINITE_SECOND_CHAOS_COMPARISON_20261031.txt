CONDITIONED EQUAL-ROW CONICAL FANS: FINITE SECOND-CHAOS COMPARISON
31 October 2026. strong_signal_prior_art. New proof for independent audit.

THEOREM AND SCOPE
Fix kappa>0. Let m>=3, N>=m-1, and let B be a centered m-by-N score
matrix whose rows have norm1. Assume C=BB^T>=kappa P on H=1-perp,
and assume the conical argmax cells of B have equal mass1/m. Put
 C_*=mP/(m-1), M_B=max_i B_i.X, M_*=max_i (B_*)_i.X,
 B_*=sqrt(m/(m-1))P, mu=E max_i Z_i,
 beta=mu/sqrt(m(m-1)), w=mu^2/(m-1), L=1+log m.
There is a constant K_kappa, independent of m,N and B, such that

 (E M_*^2-E M_B^2)_+
          <=K_kappa sqrt(L)(E M_*-E M_B),                     (1)

 A2(f_B)-A2(g)>=-K_kappa L R_U(beta B),                        (2)
 R_U(beta B)=(w+||beta B||_F^2)/2-F_U(beta B)
            =beta(E M_*-E M_B).

Here F_U is the fixed-equal-mass maximal Gaussian covariance functional.
No upper spectral bound is required: unit row norms and the lower bound
are enough. This is a finite comparison, not a local Hessian statement.
It includes the all-pairs family in[P]. It does NOT include arbitrary
balanced measurable assignments, unequal row norms, or nonzero affine
offsets at the endpoint. In particular the calibrated sparse-pair affine
example is not silently included. No full-noise conclusion is asserted.

The primary external input is Mulgund's equal-threshold stochastic
comparison[W]. All quantitative estimates below are derived here. The
other analytic input is the accepted covariance-concavity normal term[C].

1. Polynomial lower bound on every balanced facet
Write k0=min(kappa,1) and consider the covariance segment
 C_s=C_*+sD, D=C-C_*, 0<=s<=1.
Every C_s has diagonal1, annihilates1, and is >=k0 P. Realize it in H
by score rows b_i(s). Choose mean-zero offsets h_i(s) giving masses1/m.
These exist uniquely and smoothly on this full-rank segment by fixed-
mass Gaussian transport. Full rank makes the score sites affinely
independent. The argument below also proves every pair facet has
positive intrinsic measure, so the mass Jacobian has no extra nullspace.

Let ell_ij=|b_i-b_j|. Since a cell of mass1/m is contained in its
pairwise winning halfspace, applying this to both labels gives

 |h_i-h_j|/ell_ij<=b_m:=Phi^-1(1-1/m)<=sqrt(2 log m).

Since ell_ij<=2 and sum h_i=0, |h_i|<=2sqrt(2L).
Fix an ij facet, orient n=(b_i-b_j)/ell_ij, and put
 a=-(h_i-h_j)/ell_ij. Its Gaussian normal coordinate is a.
Let H0=max_k |h_k|, T=H0+8sqrt(L), and choose a centered score vector q:
 q_i=T-h_i, q_j=T-h_j,
 q_k=-(q_i+q_j)/(m-2) for k outside{i,j}.
Then ||q||^2<=1200 L. The minimum-norm x_* with B_s x_*=q satisfies
 ||x_*||^2<=1200 L/k0, and n.x_*=a.
At x_*, labels i,j tie, and beat every other label by at least8sqrt(L).

Write v=x_*-a n in the tangent hyperplane. If Z is standard Gaussian
on that hyperplane, the event
 A={max_k |b_k.Z|<=2sqrt(L)}
has probability at least1-2m exp(-2L)>1/2, by the union bound. On v+A,
the point a n+Z belongs to the ij facet, since the perturbations of a
pairwise score gap have absolute value at most4sqrt(L).

The elementary Gaussian shift estimate
 gamma(A+v)>=gamma(A)^2 exp(-||v||^2)
follows from Cauchy-Schwarz applied to the Cameron-Martin density.
Consequently the intrinsic facet probability and Gaussian surface mass
obey

 p_ij>=one-quarter exp(-1200L/k0),
 mu_ij=phi(a)p_ij>=c0 exp(-B0 L),
 c0=phi(0)/4, B0=1+1200/k0.                                  (3)

This lower bound is intentionally crude. Its polynomial dependence on
m, with an exponent depending only on kappa, is all that is needed.
There is no dimension-dependent small-ball estimate in this argument.

2. A mean gap with the exact covariance-deficit amplitude
Let F(C_s) denote fixed-mass maximal covariance. At the two endpoints,
the zero offsets produce the balanced conical cells, so
 F(C_*)=E M_*, F(C)=E M_B.
At C_* permutation invariance makes F_C a multiple of P. Since
tr D=0, F'(0)=0.

The whitened covariance Hessian normal term from[C] is
 -4F''(s)>=sum_(i<j) w_ij ell_ij^2 q_ij^2,
 w_ij=mu_ij/ell_ij,
 q_ij=[D_ii+D_jj-2D_ij]/ell_ij^2=-2D_ij/ell_ij^2.
The last equality uses the zero diagonal of D. Thus

 -F''(s)>=sum_(i<j) mu_ij D_ij^2/ell_ij^3
          >=c0 exp(-B0 L)||D||_F^2/16.

Integrating with weight1-s proves

 epsilon:=E M_*-E M_B
          >=c0 exp(-B0 L)||D||_F^2/32.                        (4)

Only the normal-energy part was used. No cycle energy was discarded
with the wrong sign; the accepted Hessian formula leaves its other
part nonnegative. Formula(4) is dimension free and uniform on the
entire stated class, although not a sharp quantitative mean-width bound.

3. A covariance-amplitude bound on the CDF tails
Set a0=(m-1)/m and R_s=a0 C_s+J/m. These are correlation matrices,
R_0=I, and R_s>=k1 I with k1=min(1,2k0/3). Their derivative is
E=a0 D, which has diagonal0 and E1=0.

Let p_s be their m-dimensional Gaussian densities. At a fixed s put
A=R_s^(-1/2) E R_s^(-1/2), and let Z be standard Gaussian. The exact
second logarithmic derivative gives

 p_s''/p_s = one-quarter(Z^T A Z-tr A)^2
            -(Z^T A^2 Z-tr A^2)-one-half tr A^2.              (5)

Its expectation is0. Also
 E(Z^T A Z-tr A)^4
      =12(tr A^2)^2+48tr A^4<=60||A||_F^4,
so the L2(p_s) norm of(5) is at most4||A||_F^2, hence at most
4k1^(-2)||E||_F^2. This estimate has no ambient-dimension factor.

Let G_s(t)=P_{R_s}{max X_i<=t}. At s=0,
 G'_0(t)=phi(t)^2 Phi(t)^(m-2) sum_(i<j) E_ij=0,              (6)
because the diagonal and row sums of E vanish. For t>=0 use the
complement event in the second derivative, whose total integral is0;
its probability is at most m exp(-t^2/2). For t<0 the orthant event
is contained in {X_1<=t}, with probability at most exp(-t^2/2).
Cauchy-Schwarz and(5), followed by Taylor's formula in s, give

 |G_1(t)-G_0(t)|
   <=2k1^(-2)||D||_F^2 sqrt(m) exp(-t^2/4)                  (7)

for all real t. We used ||E||<=||D||. One may replace the exponential
bound by1 near0, but this is unnecessary for the tail argument.
The path has strictly positive covariance, so all differentiations
follow directly from integrable Gaussian density derivatives.

4. Nonnegative CDF gap and a logarithmic threshold cutoff
The exact primary theorem[W, Theorem2.1] says that a correlation matrix
R with R>=J/m satisfies
 P_R{max X_i<=t}>=Phi(t)^m for every real t.
It applies to R_1. Therefore H(t):=G_1(t)-G_0(t)>=0 everywhere.
Represent the corresponding maximum as
 M_R=sqrt(a0) M_B+Z_0/sqrt(m),
with Z_0 independent standard Gaussian; the reference uses M_*.
Consequently

 I0:=int H(t)dt=E M_Z-E M_R=sqrt(a0)epsilon,
 2int tH(t)dt=E M_Z^2-E M_R^2
             =a0(E M_*^2-E M_B^2).                           (8)

If D=0 the theorem is immediate. Otherwise(4) makes I0 positive.
For any threshold T>=1, positivity and(7) yield

 int |t|H(t)dt
 <=T I0+8k1^(-2)||D||_F^2 sqrt(m) exp(-T^2/4).               (9)

Choose T=K0 sqrt(L), where K0 depends only on kappa and is large
enough that K0^2/4>B0+2 and that the fixed constants in(4),(9) are
absorbed. Then the last term in(9) is at most I0. Thus
 int |t|H<=K1 sqrt(L) I0.
Combining with(8), and using a0>=2/3, proves(1).

The same covariance-deficit amplitude ||D||^2 appears in both(4)
and(7). This is the point that prevents a residual error floor as
the fan approaches the regular one. Unquantified stochastic dominance
or a tail bound without this amplitude would not prove(1).

5. Conversion to the finite second-chaos comparison
For completeness, define
 Q_i=E[(XX^T-I)f_i], T_i=b_i b_i^T-(B^TB)/m.
Then sum Q_i=0, and
 sum <Q_i,T_i>=E M_B^2-1,
 sum ||T_i||^2=m-tr(C^2)/m<=m(m-2)/(m-1)=D_*.
Hence A2(f_B)>=((E M_B^2-1)_+)^2/(2D_*).
At the regular fan equality holds: permutation symmetry and the
conical identity tr Q_i=0 make all Q_i a common multiple of T_i.
It follows that

 A2(f_B)-A2(g)
 >=-[E(max_i Z_i)^2-1](E M_*^2-E M_B^2)_+/(m-2).             (10)

Since R_U(beta B)=beta epsilon, (1),(10) imply(2):
E(max_i Z_i)^2=O(L), beta is of order sqrt(L)/m, and m/(m-2)
is bounded for m>=3. Constants for the finitely many small m are
absorbed into K_kappa.

6. Tests and exclusions
After normalizing its common row variance, the all-pairs family[P]
satisfies the hypotheses with a fixed positive kappa. At t=1/mu,
its second-moment/mean-gap ratio is asymptotic to2mu. Its actual
A2 loss divided by R_U is asymptotic to4log m[Q]. Thus the logarithmic
factor in(2) has the correct order on this class.

The arbitrary remote-facet perturbations in[A] violate the conical
linear-score assumption, and do not contradict this result. The
mass-calibrated sparse-pair family has nonzero offsets and unequal
centered row norms, so it remains a mandatory test for the larger
affine statement, not a case claimed here. Neither convexity of the
cells alone nor L1 proximity supplies the hypotheses of this theorem.
