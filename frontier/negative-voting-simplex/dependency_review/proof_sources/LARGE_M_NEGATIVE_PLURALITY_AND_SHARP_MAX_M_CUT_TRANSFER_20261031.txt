1. Precise negative Plurality is Stablest statement
Fix ANY m>=m0, rho in[-1/(m-1),0), and epsilon>0. There exist an integer
d>=1 and tau>0, depending on(m,rho,epsilon) but NOT on voter count n,
such that the following holds for EVERY n>=1 and every

                 f:[m]^n -> Delta_m.

The input measure is uniform product measure. Assume every input
coordinate k and output label i satisfies

                      Inf_k^(<=d)(f_i)<=tau.                 (1)

Here the low-degree influence is the sum of squared product-Fourier
coefficients whose support contains k and has size at most d. Then

 S_rho(f)>=s_m(rho)-epsilon
         =lim_(l->infinity)S_rho(Plurality_(l,m))-epsilon.     (2)

No output-balance, permutation-equivariance, unanimity, deterministic
output, or finite-n optimality assumption is imposed. In particular(2)
holds under the stronger ordinary-influence condition
Inf_k(f_i)=E Var_(X_k)(f_i|the other coordinates)<=tau.
If vector influence is defined as sum_i Inf_k(f_i), its bound by tau
also suffices. The distinctions between these conventions are explicit.

The correlation channel is
 P(Y_k=a|X_k=b)=rho*1_(a=b)+(1-rho)/m,
independently across coordinates. The negative endpoint is included:
at rho=-1/(m-1), a coordinate is changed uniformly to a DIFFERENT label.
No channel exists with these probabilities below that endpoint. At rho=0,
(2) is elementary without any influence restriction, since S_0(f)=|Ef|^2
>=1/m. No assertion for positive rho follows here.

Proof of(2). IM Theorem7.1, at exactly the fixed(m,rho,epsilon), gives a
pure Gaussian assignment h in dimension n(m-1), with the SAME masses
as f and |S_rho(h)-S_rho(f)|<=epsilon. This dimension is finite and at
least m-1, so[G] applies. Its unrestricted masses are essential. IM
Lemma7.3 identifies s_m(rho) with the limiting plurality value by a
joint central limit theorem. Gaussian ties have measure zero, so the
finite plurality tie convention does not affect the limit. This proves
(2), including low-degree rather than just full influences.
There is no uniform-in-m influence threshold or explicit tau claimed.

2. Exact approximation constant and why negative correlations suffice
Define the fixed-m Frieze--Jerrum ratio

 a_m=min_(-1/(m-1)<=rho<=0)
                  [m/(m-1)] [1-s_m(rho)]/(1-rho).            (3)

The minimum exists by continuity on this compact interval. Formula(3)
is exact, not a large-m expansion, and it makes NO claim that the
minimizer is the left endpoint. The ratio at rho=0 is1.

For any pure Gaussian partition h with masses p and0<=rho<1, its
nonnegative Hermite coefficients give
 S_rho(h)<=|p|^2+rho(1-|p|^2).
In particular the regular partition has ratio at least1 for positive
rho. Thus its usual Frieze--Jerrum infimum over the entire admissible
interval through1 is exactly(3). Positive-noise simplex optimality is
not needed for this restriction. This is also the mechanism of IM
LemmaA.4; the harmless rho=1 endpoint may be interpreted by a limit.

For rho<=0, [G] says that the SAME regular partition maximizes the
Gaussian disagreement probability simultaneously for every rho. It
follows directly that both IM's sup_partition inf_rho constant and its
inf_rho sup_partition constant equal a_m. For the latter, positive rho
cannot lower the value: testing just the balanced regular partition
already gives at least1, while the negative interval includes value1
at zero. This explicitly verifies the use of the negative part alone
in the proof of IM TheoremA.5, whose headline assumes the full SSC.
Finite dimensions below m-1 cause no issue: embed any such assignment
cylindrically; the noise value is unchanged. Thus IM's dimension limit
has the exact same value from dimension m-1 onward on the negative side.

3. Sharp fixed-m MAX-m-CUT consequence under UGC
For EVERY fixed integer m>=m0 and every epsilon>0:

(a) There is a polynomial-time Frieze--Jerrum SDP rounding algorithm
    with approximation ratio a_m-epsilon.
(b) Assuming the Unique Games Conjecture, it is NP-hard to approximate
    MAX-m-CUT within a_m+epsilon.

The direct primary formulation is the nonnegative rational edge-weighted
problem of IM Definition1.12. The algorithmic statement is unconditional;
UGC is required for the hardness statement only. The constants and the
algorithm/reduction may depend on the fixed m and epsilon. This is not
a running-time claim uniform in an input alphabet m tending to infinity.

For(a), a feasible SDP edge has vector correlation rho>=-1/(m-1),
SDP contribution (m-1)(1-rho)/m, and regular-simplex rounding cuts it
with probability1-s_m(rho). Equation(3), together with the positive-rho
bound in Section2, gives the edgewise guarantee. Polynomial-precision
SDP solution/rounding gives the stated arbitrarily small slack, as in
IM AppendixA.3/TheoremA.6.

For(b), IM AppendixA.4 gives the following promise for every admissible
fixed rho and arbitrarily small eta,epsilon'>0: UGC-hardness of
distinguishing cut value at least
 (1-2eta)(m-1)(1-rho)/m
from value at most
 1-Lambda_m^-(rho)+epsilon',
where Lambda_m^- is the unrestricted Gaussian minimum. On the negative
interval[G] gives Lambda_m^-=s_m. Choose rho at, or arbitrarily near,
the minimum in(3), and then eta,epsilon' small. Rational rho can be
chosen by continuity, so the finite weighted verifier needs no irrational
probabilities. The resulting hardness ratio tends to a_m.

Crucially this soundness argument does NOT need a two-rule theorem.
The verifier queries functions at independently sampled neighbors w,w'
of a vertex v. Conditional on v, their average is one function
 g_v(x)=E_w[e_(f_w(sigma_vw x))] in Delta_m.
Its rejection probability is EXACTLY E_v S_rho(g_v). The single-rule
low-degree statement(2) therefore supplies the soundness/label-decoding
step. No inequality for arbitrary distinct functions f and h is inserted.
This is the concrete reason why unrestricted masses and fractional outputs
in the transfer are useful rather than an optional strengthening.

4. Optional simple-unweighted formulation: fixed-m reduction
This paragraph records a short verification rather than assuming the
binary statement of CST Theorem14 already has every alphabet size.
Its Theorem13 constructs simple bipartite mixing graphs on two N-vertex
parts, with prescribed edge scale W, and multiplicative rectangle error
epsilon' whenever both subsets have at least delta' N vertices, for fixed
epsilon',delta' and cN<=W<=N^2. Construction time is polynomial in N.

First normalize the input's nonzero nonnegative rational edge weights
to total1. Round them down in units1/K with K>=|E|/zeta. All cut values
change by at most zeta. Since a random m-cut has expected value1-1/m,
this creates only O(zeta) ratio error. The remaining integer weights
k_e are polynomially bounded. Ignore loops, which contribute zero.

Choose polynomial N and a common integer scale S so that every retained
W_e=S k_e lies in[cN,N^2]. Replace each vertex by N clones and each edge
by its mixing graph between the corresponding clone sets. Distinct
original edges use distinct clone-pair blocks, so the output is simple.
For any coloring of the clones let a_(v,i) be its color fractions. On
each original edge, mixing implies

 |cut_edges-W_e(1-sum_i a_(v,i)a_(w,i))|
                         <=[epsilon'+C m^2 delta'] W_e.     (4)

Indeed sum the rectangle estimates over the at most m(m-1) unequal
color pairs. If one color class is smaller than delta'N, enlarge it
(and the other class if necessary) to delta'N. The upper mixing bound
then gives O(delta' W_e) for that rectangle, while its predicted count
is at most delta' W_e. Take N large enough to absorb integer rounding.
Large color pairs together cost at most epsilon'W_e.

Assigning every clone the original optimal color proves completeness
at least(1-epsilon')S OPT. Conversely independently drawing each original
vertex's color from its clone fractions has expected original weighted
cut equal to the prediction in(4), summed and divided by S. Conditional
expectation derandomizes this choice in polynomial time. Since the
original optimum is at least(1-1/m) times total edge weight, the additive
error in(4) is an O(epsilon'+m^2delta') multiplicative error. For fixed m,
choose these constants arbitrarily small. Hence an improved approximation
on simple-unweighted graphs would improve the weighted ratio as well.
This proves the same sharp threshold for simple-unweighted MAX-m-CUT,
using only CST's explicit mixing construction plus the argument above.
No randomized reduction or stronger complexity conjecture is needed.

5. Historical normalization and exact limits of the consequence
The formal negative statement used here is IM Conjecture1.9/Eq19 and
its proved transfer, not an unqualified reading of KKMO's older informal
page9 display. That display writes an upper inequality over a range
which includes negative rho; the sign-separated IM statement is the
reliable locator. KKMO's ACTUAL negative Proposition11.4 on p32 is a
lower bound, with the large-m error C log log(m)/m^2. Its Theorem11
therefore supplied asymptotic hardness, not the exact fixed-m simplex
constant in(3). This note neither changes nor relies on the informal
negative-sign display. All noise quantities here use agreement
probability/Euclidean simplex inner product, with no Boolean-spin factor.

Heilman's1.3 is an informal m=3 application of IM1.10; its1.4 gives
sharp MAX-3-CUT using additional m=3 information. We do not import its
endpoint-minimization fact into general m. The present consequence is:
negative Plurality is Stablest and the exact UGC/FJ threshold for EVERY
SUFFICIENTLY LARGE fixed m, rather than merely an asymptotic error as
m grows. It does not supply the intervening finite m, positive Plurality,
Gaussian correlations below-1/(m-1), two-rule inequalities, an explicit
m0, unconditional hardness at a_m, or quantum-CUT consequences.
