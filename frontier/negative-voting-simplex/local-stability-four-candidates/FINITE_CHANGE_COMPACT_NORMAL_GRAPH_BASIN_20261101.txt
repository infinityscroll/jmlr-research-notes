FINITE-CHANGE BASIN FOR PAIRED TETRAHEDRAL NORMAL GRAPHS
1 November 2026. Successor to the certified all-boundary-speed local bound.

RESULT AND LIMIT

This note proves a quantified finite-change inequality, including an
explicit nonlinear remainder, for normal-graph modifications of the
tetrahedral partition in compact facet tubes away from junctions. Actual
matching masses are imposed, not just their linearization. No smoothness
of the graph heights is required beyond measurability and boundedness.

The proof uses the previously certified operator gap and the exact
Gaussian mass coordinate of a swept slab. Its key improvement is that
the routing losses dominate their quadratic model exactly. The only
adverse remainder comes from the displaced cross kernel.

This is a genuine basin result for the specified graph class. It does
not reduce arbitrary measurable competitors, or all possible global
minimizers, to that class. Section 10 isolates that remaining gap.

1. NOTATION AND TUBES

Work first in R3 at rho=-1/3. Let Theta be the regular tetrahedral
partition with sites y_i=(+/-1,+/-1,+/-1)/2, sign product positive.
For e=(i,j), i<j, put b_e=e_i-e_j and n_e=(y_j-y_i)/sqrt(2), the
outward unit normal from cell i towards j. Let Sigma_e be its facet.
Set lambda_e=(2pi)^(-3/2)exp(-|x|^2/2)dH^2(x) on Sigma_e.

Fix R>=1 and a>0. Choose measurable facet patches K_e such that

  |x|<=R,
  (y_i-y_k).x/sqrt(2)>=a for every k outside {i,j}.

Choose measurable heights u_e,v_e supported in K_e, with
max(|u_e|,|v_e|)<=epsilon<=a/3. The corresponding normal tubes are
disjoint and meet only the two adjacent reference cells. Indeed, other
normalized score gaps decrease by at most epsilon under a displacement
epsilon, while overlap with an adjacent facet tube would force a second
such gap to have absolute value at most epsilon. Since a>2epsilon this
is impossible. Tubes of opposite facets cannot overlap while each has
only its own two labels as possible maximizers.

For positive u_e, change label j to i on 0<t<u_e(x), where the point
is x+t n_e. For negative u_e, change label i to j on u_e(x)<t<0.
Do the same with v to form a second partition. Denote them f_u,h_v.
These are genuine partitions because the tubes do not overlap.
The requested positive-noise pair is (f_u,reflection(h_v)); reflection
preserves masses and turns its agreement into

  J(f_u,h_v)=sum_i <(f_u)_i,T_-1/3(h_v)_i>.

2. EXACT SLAB MASS COORDINATE

Put

  psi(t)=integral_0^t exp(-s^2/2)ds,
  p_e(x)=psi(u_e(x)), q_e(x)=psi(v_e(x)).                   (2.1)

Because the reference facet passes through the origin,
gamma3(x+t n_e)=gamma3(x)exp(-t^2/2). Therefore

  E(f_u-Theta)=sum_e b_e integral p_e d lambda_e=M p,
  E(h_v-Theta)=M q.                                       (2.2)

Actual common masses are exactly equivalent to M(p-q)=0. No Taylor
remainder in the constraint, and no correction multiplier, is needed.
The variables p,q belong to the full weighted boundary Hilbert space.

Write D(p)=sum_e integral d_e p_e^2 d lambda_e, with the ACTUAL positive
normal derivative d_e of G_i-G_j, G_i=T_-1/3 1_Theta_i. Let K be the
signed boundary bilinear form with kernel

  k(x,y)=(9/8)^(3/2)
          exp[-(|x|^2+|y|^2)/16-(3/8)x.y].                 (2.3)

The previously certified facts used below are

  H:=||D^(-1/2)K D^(-1/2)||_HS^2<30+3/8<36,
  D+K >=(3/8)D on the complement of common rotations,
  D-K >=(8/25)D on ker M.                                 (2.4)

The rotation space and projections here use the D inner product. Pure
translations have not been incorrectly deleted from ker M; their actual
constraint angle was included in the certificate leading to (2.4).

3. EXACT ROUTING LOWER BOUND

The routing loss of one graph is exactly

  L(u)=sum_e integral d lambda_e(x)
       integral_0^{u_e(x)} exp(-t^2/2)g_e(x,t)dt,
  g_e(x,t)=(G_i-G_j)(x+t n_e).                            (3.1)

Reflection in the facet exchanges cells i,j, so g_e is odd in t. For
t>=0, the reflected Gaussian formula and sinh(z)>=z give

  g_e(x,t)>=d_e(x)t exp(-t^2/16).

Consequently, with sigma^2=8/9,

  L_e(x,u)>=d_e(x)sigma^2[1-exp(-u^2/(2sigma^2))].          (3.2)

The following elementary comparison upgrades this to all heights.
The integral psi(u)^2 is the integral of exp(-(s^2+t^2)/2) over the
square [0,u]^2. Among sets of the same area in the positive quadrant,
the quarter disk centered at the origin maximizes this decreasing
radial integral. Its radius is 2u/sqrt(pi), hence

  psi(u)^2<=(pi/2)[1-exp(-2u^2/pi)].

For fixed u, c -> c[1-exp(-u^2/(2c))] is increasing. Since sigma^2=8/9
is greater than pi/4, (3.2) implies

  L(u)>=D(p)/2,       L(v)>=D(q)/2.                       (3.3)

Negative heights follow by oddness. This bound has NO routing-error
term and no small-height restriction. The independent routing audit
first established it for |u|<=1; the all-height quarter-disk comparison
was supplied and checked by root. It does not require an unproved
global Gaussian partition inequality. The standalone root source is
ALL_HEIGHT_GAUSSIAN_SLAB_ROUTING_20261101.txt.

4. EXACT DISPLACED CROSS TERM AND ITS REMAINDER

The exact finite deficit is

  J(f_u,h_v)-J(Theta,Theta)=L(u)+L(v)+B(f_u-Theta,h_v-Theta).

In the cross term, change each normal variable from t to z=psi(t).
Its density factor exp(-t^2/2)dt becomes dz exactly. If tau=psi^(-1),
the e,f kernel contribution is

  (b_e.b_f) integral d lambda_e(x)d lambda_f(y)
     integral_0^{p_e(x)} integral_0^{q_f(y)}
       k(x+tau(z)n_e,y+tau(w)n_f) dw dz.                  (4.1)

For every integration point |tau(z)|,|tau(w)|<=epsilon. The exact
logarithmic kernel ratio is

  -(t^2+s^2)/16
  -(3/8)[t n_e.y+s n_f.x+ts n_e.n_f].

Since |x|,|y|<=R, its absolute value is at most

  eta_R(epsilon)=(3/4)R epsilon+epsilon^2/2.                (4.2)

Define A# to mean the operator obtained by taking the POINTWISE absolute
value of the incidence-weighted normalized kernel. This is not a spectral
modulus. Its Hilbert-Schmidt square is also H, so ||A#||<=sqrt(H)<6.
Taking absolute values in (4.1) and applying its operator norm gives

  |cross-K(p,q)|
    <=(exp(eta_R)-1)sqrt(H) sqrt(D(p)D(q))
    <=[sqrt(H)/2][exp(eta_R)-1][D(p)+D(q)].                (4.3)

This controls the actual kernel remainder by the same energy appearing
in the certified Hessian. It is not an inference from positivity of the
Hessian alone. All orientations of positive and negative slabs are
included by the signed integrals in (4.1).

5. MASTER FINITE-CHANGE INEQUALITY

Use half-sum variables

  p_+=(p+q)/2, p_-=(p-q)/2.

Then M p_-=0, D(p)+D(q)=2[D(p_+)+D(p_-)], and

  D(p)/2+D(q)/2+K(p,q)
       =(D+K)(p_+)+(D-K)(p_-).

Combining (3.3), (4.3), and (2.4) proves

  J(f_u,h_v)-J(Theta,Theta)
   >=(3/8)D((I-P_rot)p_+)+(8/25)D(p_-)
       -E_R(epsilon)[D(p)+D(q)],                         (5.1)

  E_R(epsilon)=[sqrt(H)/2]
                    [exp((3/4)R epsilon+epsilon^2/2)-1].

Equation (5.1) is a quantified finite-change statement under ACTUAL
matching masses. It holds for bounded measurable graph heights, not
only differentiable one-parameter families.

6. REMOVING THE COMMON ROTATION DEGENERACY

There are two valid options. They must not be conflated.

Option A: impose the exact graph-coordinate gauge P_rot p_+=0. Then the
positive part of (5.1) is at least c[D(p)+D(q)] with c=4/25. This gauge
is an explicit additional condition; it is not silently inferred from
some informal choice of orientation.

Option B: use compact support, requiring no rotation gauge. All p_+
vanish outside intrinsic radius R, whereas every nonzero rotation field
has positive D energy outside that radius. This yields an explicit
angle gap as follows.

Let L=acos(-1/3),
  d0=L/(2pi^(3/2)), kappa=sqrt(2pi/3)/(4L).
At the facet origin d=d0 and grad log d=(-kappa,0) in the bisector frame.
The proved lower curvature bound Hess log d>=-I/8 gives

  d_e(x)>=d0 exp[-kappa|x|-|x|^2/16].                     (6.1)

Also d_e<=1/(2sqrt(pi)) by the elementary first derivative bound for the
Mehler smoothing of a function with absolute value at most one.

Rotations have speeds omega.(x cross n_e). Tetrahedral symmetry makes
their total and outside-radius-R Gram matrices scalar. Taking traces,
their outside fraction is the ratio

  integral_{Sigma_e,|x|>R} d_e|x|^2d lambda_e
       /integral_{Sigma_e}d_e|x|^2d lambda_e.

Use (6.1) on the radial annulus [R,R+1]. The numerator is at least
s d0 R^3 exp[-9(R+1)^2/16-kappa(R+1)], where
s=L/(2pi)^(3/2). The denominator is at most s/sqrt(pi).
Therefore a valid lower bound on that fraction is

  beta_R=d0 sqrt(pi) R^3
                   exp[-9(R+1)^2/16-kappa(R+1)]>0.        (6.2)

For any speed supported in the radius-R facet patches,

  D((I-P_rot)p_+)>=beta_R D(p_+).

This is the standard projection-angle inequality obtained by restricting
the rotation Gram to the complement of the support. Hence the positive
part of (5.1) is at least c_R[D(p)+D(q)], where

  c_R=min(3 beta_R/16,4/25)>0.                            (6.3)

The compact-support argument proves a basin without an extra gauge.
Its constants deliberately deteriorate with R; this deterioration cannot
be erased when attempting a global conclusion.

7. AN EXPLICIT BASIN THEOREM

Choose c=c_R for the gauge-free compact class, or c=4/25 under Option A.
If actual masses match and

  epsilon <= min{a/3, 1, c/[9(R+1)]},                    (7.1)

then

  J(f_u,h_v)-J(Theta,Theta)
                  >=(c/2)[D(p)+D(q)].                    (7.2)

Proof of the numerical absorption. Since c<=4/25, condition (7.1)
implies epsilon<=1/[2(R+1)]. Thus eta_R<=3(R+1)epsilon/4<1/2 and
exp(eta_R)-1<=2eta_R. Since sqrt(H)<6,

  E_R(epsilon)<= (9/2)(R+1)epsilon<=c/2.

Insert this into (5.1). This supplies the missing finite-change
remainder control explicitly.

Because |psi(u)|>=exp(-epsilon^2/2)|u|,
the right side of (7.2) is also at least
(c/2)exp(-epsilon^2)[D(u)+D(v)]. Equality in this basin requires u=v=0
as weighted speed functions, hence the partitions coincide with the
reference up to null sets.

There is also a weaker disagreement-measure consequence. If d_A,d_H
are the Gaussian measures on which the two labels differ from Theta,
then d_A=sum_e integral |p_e|d lambda_e and similarly for q. Put
d_R=d0 exp[-kappa R-R^2/16]. Since the total facet measure is 6s,

  J(f_u,h_v)-J(Theta,Theta)
                   >= c d_R(d_A^2+d_H^2)/(12s).            (7.3)

9. RELATION TO THE EXACT COMMON-MASS BOX

COMMON_MASS_BOX_RATIONAL_CERTIFICATE_20261101.txt proves that a pair
with agreement no greater than the tetrahedral value must have
1/8<p_i<3/8. The graph basin is compatible with that exact localization:
each label has three incident facets, so

  |E(f_u)_i-1/4|<=3s epsilon,

and likewise for h_v. Condition (7.1) is much smaller than the height
needed to leave the certified mass box. No balance assumption was added;
the common masses may vary inside it.

The mass box by itself does not imply that interfaces are graphs, that
the pair is close to a tetrahedral orbit, or that changes avoid junctions
and tails. Thus it is a valid global restriction, not a replacement for
the geometric hypotheses of this basin theorem.

10. PRECISE REMAINING OBSTRUCTION TO A GLOBAL PROOF

To settle arbitrary measurable competitors, one must still prove a
reduction that pays for changes outside this graph class. Three concrete
issues remain:

  * Near triple lines and the central vertex, legitimate cell deformations
    move junctions together. Independent facet slabs overlap there; the
    exact partition construction used above no longer applies. A compatible
    ambient deformation or a quantified junction-cutoff argument is needed.

  * In Gaussian tails, |x| is unbounded and the uniform displaced-kernel
    ratio in (4.2) has no fixed R. The gauge-free rotation angle beta_R
    also tends to zero. Truncation creates cross terms and matching-mass
    corrections; their cost cannot be discarded just because tail volume
    is small.

  * Arbitrary measurable partitions can have multiple normal crossings or
    fine interlacing, and the mass box supplies no closeness to the reference
    orbit. Stationarity/regularity of a putative minimizing pair may yield
    local graphs, but a proof must establish that reduction and control all
    excluded regions. The local Hessian and this basin do not do so alone.

This successor therefore advances an actual finite-change step while
leaving the global implication gap explicit. No publication, outreach,
or master-state edit was made.

11. A STRONGER NONCOMPACT GRAPH RESULT IN THE EXACT GAUGE

The independent successor
GLOBAL_NORMAL_SLAB_REMAINDER_AND_GAUGED_NONLINEAR_LEMMA_20261101.txt
removes the radial cutoff when the exact rotation gauge is assumed.
Its constants and proof were independently checked in this task.

Let a_e(x) be the normalized other-label clearance at each facet point.
Suppose the measurable heights satisfy

  |u_e(x)|,|v_e(x)|<=min{epsilon,a_e(x)/3}

everywhere, actual matching masses, and P_rot(p+q)=0. Pointwise clearance
taper still makes the tubes disjoint. For an alleged overlap of adjacent
tubes, the two projected distances t,s would have to satisfy both
|s|>=2|t| and |t|>=2|s|; the only exceptional boundary point has zero
height and is irrelevant to the slab measures.

For epsilon<=1, the logarithmic kernel remainder is at most
epsilon W(r,s), W=3(r+s)/8+1/2. The existing global HS envelope absorbs
W^2 exp(2W). The exact rational bounds J0<128, J2<2048 for the resulting
Gaussian radial integrals give a positive remainder-kernel norm below
2^18. Consequently

  |cross-K(p,q)|<=2^17 epsilon[D(p)+D(q)].

This proves the explicit all-radius graph inequality

  deficit>=[4/25-2^17 epsilon][D(p)+D(q)].

In particular epsilon<=1/(25*2^16)=1/1638400 gives the lower bound
(2/25)[D(p)+D(q)]. No unproved inference from an infinitesimal Hessian
is used; the remainder is integrated globally with a Gaussian envelope.

The exact gauge and the junction taper remain hypotheses. Neither this
note nor the cited successor asserts that arbitrary competitive pairs
can be put into this class while preserving the hypotheses. In
particular a general ambient diffeomorphism moves triple junctions;
the fixed-junction normal-slab representation does not cover it.

12. DIMENSION-FREE CYLINDRICAL EXTENSION

The compact theorem also extends to a cylindrical tetrahedron in every
finite N>=3. Heights may depend measurably on the extra coordinates,
while their intrinsic three-dimensional base point remains in the
radius-R patches and satisfies the same intrinsic clearance. Use the
actual all-N rotation projection and common-mass map.

The normalized boundary operator factors as A_N=A_3 tensor T_-1/3 on
the extra Gaussian coordinates. The proved all-N local gaps retain
3/8 in the common channel off rotations and 8/25 in the mass-zero
contrast channel. Equivalently this follows by Hermite degree: extra
degree0 is the proved 3D operator; degree1 turns the translation
eigenvalue3 into the mixed-rotation eigenvalue-1; every remaining new
channel lies strictly inside the already used gap bounds.

For the finite-change remainder, use the POINTWISE nonnegative kernel
operator A#, not a spectral modulus. It factors as
A#_N=A#_3 tensor T_-1/3, and its operator norm is at most sqrt(H3)<6.
Thus the compact cross estimate (4.3) has no dimension factor. The
all-radius remainder operator in Section 11 factors in the same way.
Using an N-dimensional Hilbert-Schmidt norm instead would introduce
an unnecessary and harmful dimension dependence.

Without an imposed rotation gauge, the all-N rotation space includes
both intrinsic rotations and mixed rotations proportional to a
translation speed times an extra Gaussian coordinate. The latter have
radial D energy proportional to integral d_e d lambda_e instead of
integral d_e|x|^2d lambda_e. The same annulus argument gives their tail
fraction at least

  2d0 sqrt(pi) R exp[-9(R+1)^2/16-kappa(R+1)].

The intrinsic and mixed sectors are orthogonal in the extra Gaussian
variables, including after a cutoff on intrinsic radius. Hence a valid
uniform tail-angle bound for ALL ambient rotation modes is

  beta_R,N=d0 sqrt(pi) min(R^3,2R)
                     exp[-9(R+1)^2/16-kappa(R+1)].

Replace beta_R in (6.3) by beta_R,N. The same explicit height condition
(7.1) and finite-change inequality (7.2) then hold, with constants
independent of N. This is still a theorem about the specified normal
graphs; it does not supply a global dimension-reduction or graph-
reduction theorem for arbitrary measurable competitors.
