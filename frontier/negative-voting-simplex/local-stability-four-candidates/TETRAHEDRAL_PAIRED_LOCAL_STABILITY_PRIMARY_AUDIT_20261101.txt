TETRAHEDRAL PAIRED ENDPOINT HESSIAN: PRIMARY-LITERATURE SCOPE AUDIT
Date: 2026-11-01

FINDING
I did not locate a primary theorem proving the precise requested result:
at (C,-C) in R^3 and r=1/3, the second variation of
Q_r(A,B)=sum_i <1_{A_i},T_r 1_{B_i}> is positive for every admissible pair
of boundary speeds with matched Gaussian masses, apart from the common
rotation directions. The closest checked results give variational identities,
dimension reduction, or a classification conditional on hyperstability.
None of those results supplies the proposed all-speed spectral certificate.
This is a bounded literature finding, not an exhaustive priority claim.

The relevant constraint allows the common mass vector to vary: along the
variation gamma(A_i(s))=gamma(B_i(s)), but these quantities need not stay 1/4.
Restricting to self-pairs B=-A, to fixed individual masses, to simplicial
deformations, or to positive-correlation self-maximizers would change the
question. In particular, a self-rule result would test fewer directions.

1. HEILMAN, HYPERSTABLE SETS WITH VOTING AND ALGORITHMIC HARDNESS APPLICATIONS
https://arxiv.org/html/2209.11216v1
https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.11216

Problem 7.1 is precisely positive-correlation paired minimization subject to
gamma(Omega_i)=gamma(Omega'_i), with the common masses free. Lemma 7.6,
equations (47)-(48), gives the paired second-variation expression in the
optimizer setting: cross-boundary integral terms plus positive weighted
squares of normal speeds. Lemma 7.7, equation (50), identifies translation
speeds as almost eigenfunctions with factor -1/rho before reflection and
normalization. These identities do not bound the operator on all other modes.

Theorem 7.9 reduces paired minimizers to a common (m-1)-dimensional space.
Theorem 8.5: if m<=4 and partitions minimize Problem 7.1 AND are hyperstable,
then they are congruent regular simplicial cones. This is conditional
classification of optimizers; it is not a converse stability theorem for
the displayed tetrahedral pair. Definition 1.3 includes a second-variation
sign condition and an extra mixed variation/correlation derivative condition.
Using hyperstability as evidence that the candidate Hessian is positive would
assume part of the desired conclusion. No rotations-only kernel or positive
gap for all matched-mass boundary speeds is established by these statements.

2. HEILMAN--TARTER, THREE CANDIDATE PLURALITY IS STABLEST FOR SMALL CORRELATIONS
https://arxiv.org/html/2011.05583v2
https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/forum-of-mathematics-sigma/article/three-candidate-plurality-is-stablest-for-small-correlations/2C2143844EB4F92A49B36C33002F4631

Theorem 1.9 concerns dimension reduction for positive-correlation maximizers.
Section 7 develops a two-partition minimization problem with prescribed mass
vectors; Lemma 7.6 gives its second variation, Lemma 7.7 its translation
identity, and Theorem 7.9 a common dimension bound 2m-2. The later 2022 paper
above treats the equal-common-mass constraint and obtains m-1 instead.
Neither dimension statement proves a spectral gap at a specific candidate.
Nor do the general formulas identify every null vector of its Hessian.

3. HEILMAN, STABLE GAUSSIAN MINIMAL BUBBLES
https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00526-026-03408-z
Author manuscript: https://www.stevenheilman.org/~heilman/papers/stable.pdf

Theorem 1.5 classifies minimizers of a Gaussian-perimeter functional with a
centroid penalty. Its m<=4 case has an explicit permitted penalty. Theorem
1.6 is a perimeter stability inequality and, as printed, assumes Conjecture
1.16 for m>3. Corollary 1.11 is a weak self-noise comparison for 1/2<rho<1,
with an o(sqrt(1-rho^2)) term as rho tends to 1. These are not finite negative
correlation or independent-pair Hessian results. The small-noise comparison
cannot simply be differentiated in a perturbation to obtain the requested
endpoint Hessian: it supplies neither that parameter nor a suitable uniform
remainder estimate. Theorem 9.1 is a general second-variation identity, not
the required tetrahedral spectral estimate.

4. HEILMAN--MOSSEL--NEEMAN, STANDARD SIMPLICES AND PLURALITIES ARE NOT THE
   MOST NOISE STABLE
https://arxiv.org/html/1403.0885v3

Theorem 2.6: a shifted flat partition into n+1 parts with nonuniform prescribed
Gaussian volumes is not optimal, for either sign of nonzero correlation;
positive correlations admit a larger self-stability, negative ones a smaller
self-stability, with the same volumes. The proof uses first variations.
This excludes an unbalanced shifted-simplex extension. It neither proves nor
disproves the full Hessian statement at the centered balanced tetrahedral
pair. In particular, its unbalanced competitors cannot be read as a negative
second-variation direction at the balanced candidate.

5. HEILMAN, EUCLIDEAN PARTITIONS OPTIMIZING NOISE STABILITY
https://arxiv.org/html/1211.7138v2

Theorem 1.2 proves the standard simplex statement for three sets at sufficiently
small positive correlation, with the permitted range depending on dimension.
It does not give four-label negative-endpoint local stability. The discussion
immediately following that theorem explicitly distinguishes the negative
correlation case and refers to Theorem 7.4 for failure of that proof strategy.

6. HEILMAN, SHARP HARDNESS FOR MAX-3-CUT AND QUANTUM MAX-CUT (2026)
https://arxiv.org/html/2608.00333v1

Theorem 1.2 addresses three labels. Section 1.4 says that the corresponding
four-or-more-set and four-or-more-candidate problems remain largely open and
identifies spherical triangle rearrangement as an obstruction. It points to
the hyperstable approach as an alternative. The same section says that stronger
quantitative stability estimates were not pursued. None of this is an
assertion that no local theorem exists, but it supplies no four-label,
all-speed Hessian theorem or rotations-only nullspace result.

CONDITIONAL ARITHMETIC CHECK OF THE CURRENT CERTIFICATE TARGET
Assume, separately from the literature, that the operator normalization,
constraint compression, translation/rotation eigenspaces, and residual
Hilbert--Schmidt argument in the working reduction have all been justified.
If H<30.6 and delta<.05, then h=sqrt(H-30)<sqrt(.6)<.8 and
  h+(3-h)delta = (1-delta)h+3delta < .8+2.2*.05 = .91 < 1.
Thus these targets give a comfortable strict margin in the stated reduction.
This arithmetic check is not certification of H, delta, the operator model,
the treatment of boundary junctions, or density of the allowed speeds.

Once the full reduction and numerical enclosures are certified, the justified
claim is coercivity of the constrained second variation modulo common
rotations in its stated weighted boundary space. A nonlinear local-minimizer
claim still needs a topology, admissible deformation theory, and uniform
remainder control. A global paired inequality, the full negative simplex
conjecture, and MAX-4-CUT sharp hardness do not follow from this Hessian result.

SEARCH SCOPE
The papers above and their directly relevant variation/structure statements
were checked. Searches combined regular simplex, tetrahedral, negative
correlation, Gaussian noise stability, local stability/minimum/maximum, and
second variation. Only primary papers were used for the finding. No private
correspondence is included in this note.
