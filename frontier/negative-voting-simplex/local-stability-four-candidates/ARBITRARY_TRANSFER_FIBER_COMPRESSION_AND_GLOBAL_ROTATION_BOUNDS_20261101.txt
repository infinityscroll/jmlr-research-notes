RESULT AND SCOPE

At rho=-1/3 in R3, arbitrary measurable partition changes can be split
into good transfers, which project to a reference facet and have normal
distance below delta, and bad transfers. No boundary regularity is used.
The good transfers compress to signed facet masses p and absolute masses
a with

                 D(p)<=2L_good, D(a)<=4L_good.           (A)

The good-good interaction differs from K(p,q) by at most

                    2^19 delta L_total.                 (B)

For each unit rotation direction, its actual good-transfer bulk moment
differs from the boundary moment -D(p,r_Omega) by at most

                    2^20 delta sqrt(L_good).            (C)

The estimate(C) is global in the facet radius. It does not require
delta|x| to be small. Bad-transfer mass and rotation moments are
o(sqrt(L_total)) along any sequence converging to C in Gaussian L1,
for each FIXED delta>0. A concrete rate O_delta(epsilon^(1/6)) is proved
below after division by sqrt(L_total).

This source does not assert that bad cross interactions are o(L_total).
That is the separate reciprocal-cost Hilbert-Schmidt compactness step.
It also does not infer a global optimum from a local result.

1. ROUTING COST AND GOOD/BAD TRANSFERS

Use the accepted sites Y_i, cells C_i, facet normals n_(ij), measures
d lambda_e=(2pi)^(-3/2)exp(-|y|^2/2)dH2(y), diagonal d_e, and boundary
forms D,K,M. Put G_i=T_-1/3 1_Ci.

For an arbitrary measurable partition P, let a_ij=1_(Ci intersect Pj)
for i!=j. Its routing cost is

 L(P)=sum_(i!=j) int a_ij(x)[G_j(x)-G_i(x)]d gamma(x)
     =<1_P-1_C,T 1_C>.                                  (1.1)

Every summand is nonnegative and is strictly positive off the reference
tie plane whenever a_ij=1. Also L(P)<=.5 sum_i gamma(P_i symmetric C_i),
since each cost is at most1. Thus L(P)->0 under L1 convergence, and a
nonzero change has positive routing cost.

Fix 0<delta<=1. For the pair{i,j}, write x=y+t n_e with y=P_e x on the
entire tie plane. A transfer is good if y belongs to the ORIGINAL facet
Sigma_e and |t|<delta. Every other transfer is bad. These definitions
refer to the source and destination labels of the actual transfer.
They give an exact vector decomposition

           1_P-1_C=f_good+f_bad.                         (1.2)

The full good/bad supports can be arbitrary measurable sets.

2. STRICT MONOTONICITY ON EVERY NORMAL FIBER

Fix y on a tie plane. Set g_e(t)=(G_i-G_j)(y+t n_e), with n_e pointing
from C_i to C_j. Reflection gives oddness. Moreover g_e is strictly
increasing, even when y lies outside the actual facet.

One direct proof uses independent Gaussian utilities. The vector Y_i.Z
has the law of N_i-mean(N), with independent standard normal N_i; the
common mean does not change the winning label. At y+t n_e, utility i has
mean slope +1/(3sqrt2), utility j slope -1/(3sqrt2), and the other two
means are fixed. The winning event for i increases pointwise as t grows;
the winning event for j decreases. Nondegenerate Gaussian differences
give strict probability changes on every nonzero interval. Hence
g_e(t)>0 for t>0 and g_e(t)<0 for t<0.

For y in Sigma_e, every positive normal point belongs to reference C_j
and every negative normal point belongs to reference C_i: the two tied
scores at the base dominate all other scores, and the maximum of these
two increases under either normal displacement.

3. EXACT NORMAL-FIBER COMPRESSION

For each y in Sigma_e, let E_+(y) subset(0,delta) be the good transfer
set C_j->P_i, and E_-(y) subset(-delta,0) the good transfer set C_i->P_j.
Define

 q_+(y)=int_E+ exp(-t^2/2)dt,
 q_-(y)=int_E- exp(-t^2/2)dt,
 p_e=q_+-q_-,  a_e=q_++q_-.                              (3.1)

Both q values are nonnegative and at most psi(delta), where
psi(t)=int_0^t exp(-s^2/2)ds. These are exact Gaussian normal masses;
there is no hidden factor of sqrt(2pi), because lambda_e already uses
the three-dimensional Gaussian density.

Pass to z=psi(t). The increasing integrand g_e(psi^(-1)(z)) has smallest
integral over a set of z-measure q when that set is[0,q]. This follows
directly by moving any mass above q into any missing portion below q.
Apply it separately to E_+ and the reflection of E_-. The all-height
Gaussian slab routing theorem gives

 L_e(y)>=int_0^(psi^-1(q+)) g_e(t)exp(-t^2/2)dt
         +int_0^(psi^-1(q-)) g_e(t)exp(-t^2/2)dt
        >=.5d_e(y)[q_+^2+q_-^2].                         (3.2)

The cited all-height statement is
ALL_HEIGHT_GAUSSIAN_SLAB_ROUTING_20261101.txt. It is a one-dimensional
reflection/rearrangement inequality, not a global partition theorem.

Since(q_+-q_-)^2<=q_+^2+q_-^2 and(q_++q_-)^2<=2(q_+^2+q_-^2),
integration proves exactly

                       D(p)<=2L_good,
                       D(a)<=4L_good.                   (3.3)

The good transfer mass vector is exactly Mp. In particular this
compression preserves the good signed mass; it does not discard the
opposite-direction transfer cancellation on a fiber.

4. GLOBAL GOOD-GOOD KERNEL REMAINDER

For two partitions let p,q be their signed compressed masses and a,b
their absolute compressed masses. The exact good-good interaction is
the signed fourfold integral of the shifted Mehler kernel. Replacing
that kernel by its value at the two facet bases gives EXACTLY K(p,q).

For |t|,|s|<=delta<=1,
 |log[k(x+tn_e,y+sn_f)/k(x,y)]|
 <=delta W(|x|,|y|), W(r,s)=3(r+s)/8+1/2,
 |ratio-1|<=delta W exp(W).                              (4.1)

The global positive remainder operator with kernel
|b_e.b_f|k W exp(W)/sqrt(d_e d_f) has norm below2^18, as proved in
GLOBAL_NORMAL_SLAB_REMAINDER_AND_GAUGED_NONLINEAR_LEMMA_20261101.txt.
That proof is a pointwise kernel bound and applies to arbitrary signed
normal measures, not only intervals or graph slabs. Taking absolute
values of the normal integrals produces exactly a_e and b_f.

Thus
 |<f_good,T h_good>-K(p,q)|
 <=2^18 delta sqrt(D(a)D(b))
 <=2^20 delta sqrt(L_good(P)L_good(Q))
 <=2^19 delta[L(P)+L(Q)].                               (4.2)

This controls the actual nonlinear interaction without a radial cutoff.

5. GLOBAL RELATIVE DERIVATIVE ENVELOPE

For a pair e=(i,j), define the positive extended diagonal

 D_e(z)=(3/4)int_Ci(-n_e.u)phi_sigma(u+z/3)du,
 sigma^2=8/9.                                          (5.1)

It equals d_e on the tie plane. The independently proved positive-
extension source gives

                    -I/8<=Hess log D_e<=0.               (5.2)

At the origin the probability density proportional to(-n_e.u)phi_sigma
on C_i has radial degree1. Its second radial moment is4sigma^2=32/9.
Since grad log D_e(0)=-(3/8)E u, its norm is at most sqrt2/2<1.
Therefore for r=|y| and |t|<=delta<=1, global concavity gives

 D_e(y+ut n_e)<=d_e(y) exp[delta(1+r/8)], |u|<=1.         (5.3)

Also, on these segments,
 |grad D_e|<=2(1+r)D_e,
 ||Hess D_e||op<=5(1+r)^2D_e.                            (5.4)

Use the exact positive representation

 g_e(t)=(t/2)int_-1^1 exp[(u^2-1)t^2/16]D_e(y+ut n_e)du.(5.5)

Differentiate(5.5) in orthonormal tangent/normal coordinates. The
exponential factor E satisfies |E'|<=E/8 and |E''|<=9E/64 for |t|<=1.
Equations(5.3)-(5.4), with deliberately generous constants, give

 |grad(G_i-G_j)(y+tn_e)|
 <=8(1+r) exp[delta(1+r/8)]d_e(y),

 ||Hess(G_i-G_j)(y+tn_e)||op
 <=64(1+r)^2 exp[delta(1+r/8)]d_e(y).                    (5.6)

For audit: before these generous norm bounds, the normal derivative is
at most4(1+r), each tangent derivative at most2delta(1+r); the normal
second derivative is at most11(1+r)^2, mixed entries at most8(1+r)^2,
and tangent entries at most5delta(1+r)^2, times the common factor in(5.6).

Let g_Omega,i(z)=-(Omega z).grad G_i(z), ||Omega||op<=1.
At the facet its label difference is exactly -d_e r_Omega,e. Normal
differentiation yields

 partial_n(g_Omega,i-g_Omega,j)
 =-(Omega n).grad(G_i-G_j)
   -(Omega z).Hess(G_i-G_j)n.

Consequently, for every facet radius r without restriction,

 |partial_n(g_Omega,i-g_Omega,j)(y+tn_e)|
 <=128(1+r)^3 exp[delta(1+r/8)]d_e(y).                   (5.7)

The exponential exp(delta r/8) is retained. Dropping it by assuming
delta r small would be an invalid tail argument.

6. EXPLICIT GOOD-TRANSFER ROTATION ERROR

By(5.7), the difference between a good-transfer bulk moment and its
compressed boundary moment -D(p,r_Omega) is at most

 128delta sum_e int a_e d_e(1+r)^3 exp[delta(1+r/8)]d lambda_e.

Weighted Cauchy bounds this by128delta sqrt(D(a)) B, where

 B^2=sum_e int d_e(1+r)^6 exp[2delta(1+r/8)]d lambda_e.

There is a uniform explicit bound B<2^12 for all delta<=1. Indeed use
d_e<1/2, sector opening<4, (2pi)^(-3/2)<1, e^2<9, and
r/4<=r^2/4+1/16. Then

 B^2<216 int_0^infinity r(1+r)^6 exp(-r^2/4)dr
    <=216*32[ int r exp(-r^2/4)dr+int r^7 exp(-r^2/4)dr]
     =216*32*(2+768)=5,322,240<2^24.                    (6.1)

Together with D(a)<=4L_good, this proves

 |good bulk rotation moment+D(p,r_Omega)|
 <=2^20delta sqrt(L_good).                              (6.2)

For a pair, the combined error is at most2^21delta sqrt(L_total).
The constants are independent of support, smoothness, and radial size.
For general Omega, multiply by ||Omega||op.

7. BAD MASS AND GAUGE MOMENTS: A RECIPROCAL-COST LEMMA

This section concerns only mass/gauge moments, not bad cross interactions.
For a source C_i and destination j, write x=y+t n_e; the source condition
fixes the sign of t. In this paragraph orient n_e from the source C_i
toward C_j; the resulting absolute-value bounds do not depend on this
orientation choice. Let c_ij(x)=G_j-G_i>0 on the source interior.
Reflection and sinh(a)>=a give, on the ENTIRE tie plane,

 c_ij(x)>=d_e(y)|t|exp(-t^2/16).

The lower curvature bound in(5.2), d_e(0)>1/8, and
|grad log D_e(0)|<1 give

 c_ij(x)>=(1/8)|t|exp[-|x|-|x|^2/16],
 1/c_ij(x)<=8|t|^(-1)exp[|x|+|x|^2/16].                (7.1)

Fix delta>0 and restrict to the bad set B_delta,ij. For every fixed
polynomial weight (1+|x|)^m,

 1_(B_delta,ij)(1+|x|)^m/c_ij(x) belongs to L^(3/2)(gamma). (7.2)

To prove this, the part |t|>=delta has no local denominator singularity;
the Gaussian exponent after taking power3/2 is -13|x|^2/32+O(|x|).
For an ineligible projection with |t|<1, some other label k satisfies

 0<(Y_k-Y_i).y<=|t|/sqrt2.

Its gradient within the tie plane has length sqrt(3/2). Thus y lies in
one of two strips of width |t|/sqrt3. After integration in y, any fixed
polynomial times the damped Gaussian is O(|t|), uniformly for |t|<1.
The remaining singular integral is int_0^1 t^(1-3/2)dt<infinity.
This proves(7.2). It also proves ordinary L1 integrability.

The rotation test has the crude global bound
 |g_Omega,j-g_Omega,i|<=C||Omega||op |x|,
obtained by differentiating the Gaussian convolution of 1_Cj-1_Ci.
Thus both 1/c_ij and |g_Omega,j-g_Omega,i|^2/c_ij have the integrability
needed below on every fixed bad set.

Let a sequence of partitions approach C with summed-label distance at
most epsilon. Its changed support has Gaussian measure at most epsilon/2.
Cauchy with routing cost, followed by Holder with exponent3/2, gives

 int_bad a_ij d gamma
 <=sqrt(L_bad,ij)[int_(bad changed support)1/c_ij d gamma]^(1/2)
 <=C_delta epsilon^(1/6) sqrt(L_total),

 |int_bad a_ij(g_Omega,j-g_Omega,i)d gamma|
 <=C_delta ||Omega||op epsilon^(1/6) sqrt(L_total).       (7.3)

Summing the finitely many transfers changes only C_delta. The constant
may deteriorate as delta decreases; no uniform-in-delta little-o claim
is made. First take epsilon->0 with delta FIXED.

8. CONSTRAINT CONSEQUENCES AND THE TWO-LIMIT ORDER

Suppose a pair P,Q has exact common masses and exact bulk common-rotation
gauge, and converges to(C,C). Let L_total=L(P)+L(Q)>0. The compressed good
masses satisfy

 D(p)+D(q)<=2L_total,
 |M(p-q)|<=C_delta epsilon^(1/6)sqrt(L_total),
 |D(p+q,r_Omega)|
 <=[2^21delta+C_delta epsilon^(1/6)]sqrt(L_total).        (8.1)

The second line follows from exact actual matching and bad mass(7.3).
The third follows from the exact bulk gauge, the good estimate(6.2), and
the bad gauge estimate(7.3). Projection onto the finite-dimensional mass
representer and rotation spaces uses their fixed positive Gram matrices.

Thus, after normalization by sqrt(L_total), the contrast mass defect
vanishes for fixed delta, and the common rotation defect is O(delta).
The accepted boundary spectral theorem implies

 K(p,q)>=-[17/25+C delta^2+o_delta(1)]L_total,            (8.2)

where o_delta(1)->0 as epsilon->0 for fixed delta. One way to see the
coefficient is that, with exact boundary constraints,
.5[D(p)+D(q)]+K(p,q)>=(4/25)[D(p)+D(q)], so
K>=-(17/50)[D(p)+D(q)]>=-(17/25)L_total.
Perturbing the mass projection costs o_delta(L_total); the rotation
eigenvalue is exactly-1, so its O(delta) projection contributes O(delta^2).

Combined with(B), this leaves a strict margin against a competitive
cross term at most -L_total, PROVIDED all interactions involving bad
transfers are o_delta(L_total). That last statement is intentionally a
separate input. If it holds, choose delta sufficiently small first, then
epsilon sufficiently small depending on delta. Interchanging these two
choices or silently asserting uniform bad compactness is unnecessary.

This note proves compression, the global kernel-shift and rotation
bounds, and bad mass/gauge integrability. It preserves the exact common-
mass setting; no balanced-mass assumption has been inserted.
