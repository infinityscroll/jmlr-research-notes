CONDITIONAL THEOREM
Let C be the regular four-way conical partition in R^3. At the pair (C,-C)
with positive correlation1/3 and freely varying matched Gaussian masses,
suppose the two scalar bounds above hold for the actual boundary operator
defined below. Then the continuous quadratic form extending the paired
second variation is nonnegative for every finite-energy boundary-speed
pair satisfying the linearized common-mass constraint. Its kernel consists
exactly of simultaneous rotation speeds.

This conclusion holds at (C x R^(N-3), -C x R^(N-3)) for EVERY finite N>=3.
The neutral boundary-speed space has dimension3N-6. In the normalized
product Hilbert norm, the Hessian is at least9/100 times the squared
distance from that neutral space. No global minimizer dimension-reduction
theorem is used in this extension.

1. DEFINITIONS AND THE REFLECTION CONVENTION
Use rows Y_1=(1,1,1)/2, Y_2=(1,-1,-1)/2,
Y_3=(-1,1,-1)/2, Y_4=(-1,-1,1)/2; Y^T Y=I_3.
Set C_i={x:Y_i.x=max_j Y_j.x}. For each edge e=(i,j), i<j,
 b_e=e_i-e_j, n_e=(Y_j-Y_i)/sqrt2,
 Sigma_e=the common planar facet of C_i,C_j,
 d lambda_e=(2pi)^(-3/2)exp(-|x|^2/2)dH^2 on Sigma_e.

For the original positive-correlation pair (A,B), reflect its second rule:
H=-B. Then Q_(1/3)(A,B)=Q_rho(A,H), rho=-1/3, and the reference becomes
(C,C). Reflection preserves each label mass. A boundary scalar speed of B
on -Sigma becomes the same scalar speed of H at the reflected point.

Let G_i=T_rho1_Ci. Reflection symmetry exchanging i,j gives G_i-G_j=0
on the whole tie plane and hence on Sigma_e. Set
 d_e=partial_n_e(G_i-G_j)>0,
 D(u,v)=sum_e int d_e u_e v_e d lambda_e.
Positivity follows, independently of minimization, from the actual formula
 d_e=(1/3)[2I_e+(1/2)sum_four_adjacent I_f],
 I_f=int_Sigma_f K_rho(x,y)d lambda_f(y)>0,
where
 K_rho=(9/8)^(3/2)exp[-(|x|^2+|y|^2)/16-(3/8)x.y].
No fixed-damping approximation is substituted for d_e.

Define the signed first boundary variation h_i[u]=sum_e(b_e)_i u_e lambda_e
and the symmetric bilinear form
 K(u,v)=sum_(e,f)(b_e.b_f)int int u_e(x)v_f(y)K_rho(x,y)
                                         d lambda_e(x)d lambda_f(y).
Write the normalized space H_3=direct_sum_e L^2(lambda_e), U_e=sqrt(d_e)u_e.
The normalized operator A has kernel
 A_ef(x,y)=(b_e.b_f)K_rho(x,y)/sqrt(d_e(x)d_f(y)).
Thus D(u,v)=<U,V> and K(u,v)=<U,A V>. The previously proved analytic
tail estimate makes A Hilbert--Schmidt and self-adjoint, so these forms
extend continuously to all of H_3.

2. THE HESSIAN FACTORS AND CONSTRAINT
For two smooth variations with boundary velocities u,v at (C,C),
 J''(0)=D(u,u)+D(v,v)+2K(u,v).                            (1)
To verify the diagonal factor, vary only the first argument. Its first
variation is sum_e int_(Sigma_e(s))(G_i-G_j)u_e d lambda_e.
At s=0 the potential difference vanishes. Differentiating leaves exactly
partial_n(G_i-G_j)u_e^2. Curvature, acceleration, and variation of the
surface density multiply the vanishing potential difference. Differentiating
the two first boundary measures gives the mixed term2K. In particular the
self variation u=v gives2(D+K), an independent factor check.

Put u_+=(u+v)/2, u_-=(u-v)/2, and U_+,U_- likewise. Then
 J''(0)=2[<U_+,(I+A)U_+>+<U_-,(I-A)U_->].              (2)
The derivative of the label mass vector is
 M u=sum_e b_e int u_e d lambda_e.
Equal common masses impose M(u-v)=0, equivalently M u_-=0. There is NO
condition M u_+=0: the common mass vector itself is allowed to move.

For c in label space1-perp, the normalized mass representer is
 G_c,e=(b_e.c)/sqrt(d_e), so M u=0 iff U is orthogonal to
G=span{G_c:c perpendicular to1}. The lower bound on d_e makes these
representers square-integrable. Hence M is continuous on the weighted
speed space and G has dimension3.

3. EXACT EIGENMODES, INCLUDING SIGNS
For a translation w in R^3, t_e(w)=n_e.w and T_w=sqrt(d_e)t_e(w).
The indicator derivative is -w.grad1_Ci, while
grad T_rho=rho T_rho grad. Therefore on Sigma_e,
 T_rho(h_i[t]-h_j[t])=-(1/rho)w.grad(G_i-G_j)
                    =3d_e(n_e.w),
and A T_w=3T_w. The translations span a three-dimensional eigenspace T.
More explicitly, M t(w)=-2sqrt2*s0*Yw, so no nonzero translation obeys
the contrast-channel mass constraint.

For a skew-symmetric matrix Q, let r_Q,e=n_e.(Qx) and R_Q=sqrt(d_e)r_Q,e.
The angular derivative (Qx).grad commutes with the isotropic OU operator.
The boundary derivative is -(Qx).grad1_Ci, so on Sigma_e its transformed
potential difference is -d_e n_e.(Qx). Hence A R_Q=-R_Q. These speeds
span a three-dimensional eigenspace R. This proves the eigenvalue, rather
than inferring a kernel solely from constancy along a rotation curve.

The two eigenspaces are orthogonal because A is self-adjoint and their
eigenvalues differ. Their dimensions are exactly3: the normals span R^3;
if n_e.Qx vanishes on each open facet, skew symmetry forces Qn_e=0 for
every e and therefore Q=0. Both eigenspaces belong to H_3. Indeed the
one-dimensional Gaussian derivative formula bounds d_e by
(|rho|/sqrt(1-rho^2))*E|Z|=1/(2sqrt(pi)), and the Gaussian facet measure
integrates constants and quadratic polynomials. Rotation speeds lie in
G-perp, since rotations preserve every label mass.

4. THE EXACT MASS/TRANSLATION ANGLE
By tetrahedral symmetry, the following scalars do not depend on the facet:
 s0=int d lambda_e=L/(2pi)^(3/2), L=acos(-1/3),
 a0=int d_e d lambda_e, b0=int d_e^(-1)d lambda_e.
All are finite and positive. Since sum_e n_e n_e^T=2I_3 and
b_e.Yw=-sqrt2 n_e.w, direct integration gives
 <T_v,T_w>=2a0 v.w,
 <G_Yv,G_Yw>=4b0 v.w,
 <G_Yv,T_w>=-2sqrt2 s0 v.w.
Consequently all three principal-angle squared cosines equal s0^2/(a0b0).
For delta=1-s0^2/(a0b0), Cauchy--Schwarz gives0<=delta<1 and
              ||P_T restricted_to G-perp||^2=delta.      (3)
For example, normalize the two three-column frames: their cross-Gram
matrix is -s0/sqrt(a0b0) timesI_3. Then
||P_(G-perp) P_T||^2=delta; taking adjoints gives (3).
The mass constraint therefore is NOT orthogonality to translations.

5. HILBERT--SCHMIDT REMAINDER AND EXPLICIT RATIONAL GAPS
Let H=||A||_HS^2. Define E=A-3P_T+P_R. Exact eigenmode orthogonality gives
 E P_T=E P_R=0,
 ||E||_HS^2=H-3*3^2-3*1^2=H-30.
In particular H>=30, a useful consistency check on numerical enclosures.
If H<153/5, then h=sqrt(H-30)<sqrt(3/5)<4/5 and ||E||op<=h.

For every U in H_3,
 <U,(I+A)U>
   >=4||P_T U||^2+(1-h)||P_(T+R)-perp U||^2
   >=(1/5)||P_(R-perp)U||^2.                            (4)
The common channel has kernel exactly R; the positive gap on its
orthogonal complement rules out any other null direction.

For unit U in G-perp, use (3) and the residual decomposition:
 <U,A U>
   <=h+(3-h)||P_TU||^2-(1+h)||P_RU||^2
   <=h+(3-h)delta <4/5+(11/5)(1/20)=91/100.
Thus, on the entire constrained space,
              <U,(I-A)U> >=(9/100)||U||^2.              (5)
The strict hypotheses give strict inequalities for nonzero vectors at the
corresponding endpoints; the weak rational inequalities are convenient
uniform coercivity statements.

Combining (2),(4),(5),
 J''(0)>=(2/5)||P_(R-perp)U_+||^2+(9/50)||U_-||^2.       (6)
The product-space distance from N_3={(R,R):R in R} is
 dist((U,V),N_3)^2=2||P_(R-perp)U_+||^2+2||U_-||^2.
Hence J''(0)>=(9/100)dist((U,V),N_3)^2. Its kernel is precisely N_3.

6. CYLINDRICAL EXTENSION TO EVERY FINITE N>=3
Let d=N-3 and use C_i^N=C_i x R^d. The facet measure factorizes as
lambda_e,N=lambda_e,3 tensor gamma_d, while the normal derivative d_e
depends only on the first three coordinates. Therefore, exactly,
 H_N=H_3 tensor L^2(gamma_d), A_N=A_3 tensor T_rho^(d).    (7)
The mass representers are G_c tensor1. Expand the extra-coordinate factor
in orthogonal Hermite chaoses of degree k. On degree k, A_N is rho^k A_3.
Only k=0 sees the mass constraint; every degree k>=1 is automatically
mass-zero. This is an operator identity at the reference, not a claim that
unknown global minimizers are cylindrical.

The accepted scalar hypotheses would give spectrum(A_3) in[-1,3], with
eigen3 only on T, eigen-1 only on R, and all remaining eigenvalues of
absolute value below4/5. Therefore:
 * k=0 has exactly the bounds (4)-(5).
 * k=1 multiplies eigenvalues by-1/3. Its only common-channel null modes
   are T tensor degree1; all other common-channel eigenvalues are at
   least1-(4/5)/3>1/5. Its contrast-channel eigenvalues are at least2/3.
 * k>=2 has |rho^k A_3|_op<=3*(1/3)^k<=1/3, so BOTH channels have
   gap at least2/3 there.
Thus the same rational gaps1/5 and9/100 hold in every finite N.

The common nullspace is R tensor1 plus T tensor linear extra-coordinate
functions. A full ambient skew matrix has block form
 [[Q,B],[-B^T,Q_extra]] and normal speed
 n_e.(Qx+Bz). These are exactly those modes. Q_extra contributes zero
boundary speed because it acts inside the cylindrical factors. The actual
neutral space has dimension3+3d=3N-6, not dim SO(N). In the original
positive-correlation variables these are simultaneous rotations of both
partitions. The product-distance bound following (6) holds with this
larger neutral space N_N.

7. ALL HILBERT SPEEDS VERSUS REALIZABLE SMOOTH VARIATIONS
The inequalities above hold on the full weighted L^2 spaces, once the
scalar certificate is accepted. They therefore include every smooth
admissible speed for which the second-variation formula is defined.
They do not assert that every individual L^2 vector integrates to a
classical smooth deformation of a partition.

There is nevertheless a concrete dense admissible core. On each facet take
smooth compactly supported normal speeds away from the multiple-junction
strata. The facet boundary has surface measure zero; since d_e is smooth
and positive, truncation and mollification make this core dense in H_N.
Each finite collection is realizable by smooth ambient vector fields in
disjoint tubular neighborhoods of the chosen facet patches.

For the mass-zero core, fix three such bump speeds on edges of a spanning
tree of the four labels, each normalized to have integral1 against lambda.
Their mass images are the three independent incidence vectors b_e.
Approximate any ker M vector by core vectors and correct their residual
mass images using these three fixed bumps. Continuity of M makes the
corrections tend to zero, proving density in ker M.

The same rank-three correction gives exact matched-mass smooth pair
families by the implicit-function theorem. If the initial velocities obey
M(u-v)=0, the required correction parameters begin at order s^2. Such
corrections do not change (1): the reference first variation vanishes for
every speed, without a nonzero mass multiplier. Thus the tangent condition
used in the theorem is not an artificial relaxation of the smooth local
problem. Junction contributions vanish in this core; more general paths
require their own justified shape differentiation, not an unsupported
extension of a smooth-boundary formula through singular strata.

8. WHAT THE THEOREM WOULD AND WOULD NOT ESTABLISH
An accepted certificate proves a strict constrained SECOND-VARIATION gap
modulo rotations. For each fixed C^2 admissible path with a non-rotation
first velocity and a valid second-order expansion, its cost is strictly
larger for sufficiently small nonzero parameter. The neighborhood may
depend on that path. A fixed finite-dimensional smooth family with an
immersed tangent space transverse to rotations also admits the ordinary
finite-dimensional local-minimum conclusion.

A uniform nonlinear local-minimum theorem over arbitrary nearby partitions
requires additional work: a deformation topology and chart, treatment of
junctions and tails, removal of the rotation freedom, and a uniform
Taylor remainder smaller than the coercive quadratic term in that chart.
None follows merely from Hilbert--Schmidt control of the Hessian. Paths
with zero first velocity and arbitrary nonsmooth finite changes are not
settled by the first-velocity statement.

Even a nonlinear local-minimum theorem would not prove the global paired
inequality, the unrestricted four-label negative simplex conjecture,
negative Plurality is Stablest, or sharp MAX-4-CUT hardness. The user's
original recognized-problem objective remains unresolved by this work.
