  gamma(A_i)=gamma(B_i)=p_i, sum_i p_i=1.

1. Borell applies to the independent pair

Write J_r(p,q)=P(X<=Phi^{-1}(p),Y<=Phi^{-1}(q)), with scalar standard Gaussians of correlation r, and J(p)=J_{-1/3}(p,p).

Mossel–Neeman, Robust Optimality of Gaussian Noise Stability, Theorem1.1, explicitly states Borell's inequality for TWO arbitrary measurable sets of prescribed masses. Theorem1.2 supplies the functional version. This is not a one-set-only statement.

Apply its positive-correlation upper bound to A_i and B_i^c:
  P_{1/3}(X in A_i,Y in B_i)
    =p_i-P_{1/3}(X in A_i,Y in B_i^c)
    >=p_i-J_{1/3}(p_i,1-p_i)
    =J_{-1/3}(p_i,p_i).
The last identity reflects the second scalar Gaussian variable. Consequently
                         Q(A,B)>=sum_i J(p_i).             (1)

For bounded coordinates f_i,g_i, the same conclusion follows from the functional theorem: J_r(u,v)>=uv for r>0, so E_r[f_i(1-g_i)]<=J_r(p_i,1-p_i). This proves (1) for simplex-valued assignments as well.

2. Convexity reduces the mass exclusion to two scalar tests

Put z=Phi^{-1}(p). Differentiating the diagonal bivariate CDF gives
  J'(p)=2 Phi(sqrt(2) z),
  J''(p)=2sqrt(2) exp(-z^2/2)>0, 0<p<1.                     (2)
The endpoint values extend continuously.

Fix one mass p. Jensen applied to the other three masses yields
  sum_i J(p_i)>=F(p):=J(p)+3J((1-p)/3).                     (3)
Moreover
  F'(p)=J'(p)-J'((1-p)/3).
Thus F is strictly decreasing on (0,1/4) and strictly increasing on (1/4,1). To exclude p<=1/8 and p>=3/8 it is enough to certify that both F(1/8) and F(3/8) exceed S_tet.
