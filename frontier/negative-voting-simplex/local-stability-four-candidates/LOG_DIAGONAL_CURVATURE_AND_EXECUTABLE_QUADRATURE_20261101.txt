ACTUAL TETRAHEDRAL DIAGONAL: LOG CURVATURE AND CERTIFIED QUADRATURE
2026-11-01. Independent derivation with radial-agent coordination.

RESULT
On the entire tie plane of a tetrahedral facet at rho=-1/3,
                 -I/8 <= Hess(log d) <= 0.                 (1)
Consequently, certified values of d at a modest grid of points suffice for
rigorous integration of its reciprocal. Interpolating log d gives a uniform
relative error. The interpolated reciprocal times the Gaussian density is
an exponential of a quadratic polynomial; a completely explicit tensor
Gauss error bound is given below and implemented in the companion Python
file. No convergence-only quadrature claim or zero-free conjecture is used.

1. NORMALIZATION AND THE REFLECTION IDENTITY
Use the parent's actual diagonal d_e and lambda_e from
COMPACT_SECTOR_DIAGONAL_AND_CERTIFIED_OPERATOR_TAIL_20261101.txt.
Let e=(i,j), n point from C_i into C_j, r=1/3, rho=-r, sigma^2=8/9.
The reflection R across n-perp swaps C_i,C_j. For x in that tie plane,
the Gaussian density phi_sigma(y-rho*x) is invariant under y->Ry. Thus

 d_e(x) = partial_n T_rho(1_Ci-1_Cj)(x)
        = (2r/sigma^2) int_Ci (-n.y) phi_sigma(y-rho*x) dy. (2)

Indeed differentiating the conditional density gives
rho*(n.y-rho*n.x)/sigma^2, n.x=0, and the reflected C_j integral doubles
the C_i contribution. On C_i, -n.y>=0.

The nonnegative integrand in (2) is jointly log-concave in (x,y): the cone
C_i is convex, log(-n.y) is concave on its positive half-space, and the
negative squared affine norm is concave. Prékopa's marginal theorem therefore
makes d_e log-concave on n-perp. This proves the upper half of (1).
Zeros of the linear weight on a boundary cause no problem; log-concavity
uses its continuous nonnegative extension. The integral is positive and
smooth for every finite real x in the plane.

Independently, the exact positive boundary formula is
 d_e(x)=r[2 I_e(x)+(1/2)sum_adjacent I_f(x)],
 I_f(x)=(9/8)^(3/2)(2pi)^(-3/2) exp(-|x|^2/16)
          *int_Sigma_f exp(-9|y|^2/16-(3/8)x.y) dH^2(y).
Therefore exp(|x|^2/16)d_e(x) is a positive Laplace transform.
The Hessian of its logarithm is a covariance matrix and is positive
semidefinite. Subtracting Hess(|x|^2/16)=I/8 proves the lower half of (1).

The reflection/log-concavity idea was supplied by radial_coercivity_cleanroom;
the direct Prékopa proof, interpolation mechanism, constants below, and
executable error bound were derived and checked here independently.

2. EXACT ORIGIN CONSTANTS AND A GLOBAL LOWER BOUND
Put L=acos(-1/3). In orthonormal facet coordinates
 x=(q,z/sqrt(2),-z/sqrt(2)), q along the facet bisector, one obtains
 d(0)=L/(2*pi^(3/2)),
 grad(log d)(0)=(-kappa,0), kappa=sqrt(2*pi/3)/(4L).       (3)

For verification, the sector Gaussian integral at zero is 8L/9, and the
sum of the five positive weights in the bracket defining d is 4. A sector's
mean radial coordinate under density exp(-9t^2/16)t dt is 2sqrt(pi)/3;
its mean angular unit vector is (2sin(L/2)/L) times its bisector. The four
adjacent facet bisectors cancel. The same facet receives weight2 out of4.
Multiplication by the Laplace coefficient -3/8 yields (3).

Integrating (1) along the segment from zero gives, globally on the tie plane,
 log d(0)-kappa*q-|x|^2/16 <= log d(x) <= log d(0)-kappa*q. (4)
In particular, the lower bound suggested by the root's log-mgf approach is
explicit. It is stronger for this purpose than the compact-sector bound.

3. BILINEAR INTERPOLATION: NONORTHOGONAL RAY COORDINATES INCLUDED
Let a,b be the two unit boundary rays of a facet; a.b=-1/3. Write
 x=u*a+v*b, u,v>=0,
 q=(u+v)/sqrt(3), z=sqrt(2/3)(v-u),
 |x|^2=u^2+v^2-(2/3)uv, dH^2=(2sqrt(2)/3)du dv.
Although these coordinates are not orthogonal, each coordinate direction
has unit Euclidean length. Hence (1) implies separately
 -1/8<=g_uu<=0 and -1/8<=g_vv<=0, where g=log d.

On a rectangle [u0,u1]x[v0,v1], let g_I be its bilinear corner interpolant.
Successive one-dimensional interpolation, whose error for -1/8<=f''<=0
lies between 0 and (t-a)(b-t)/16, proves
 0<=g-g_I<=[(u-u0)(u1-u)+(v-v0)(v1-v)]/16
          <=epsilon=(h_u^2+h_v^2)/64.                    (5)
No bound on the mixed derivative is needed for (5). This explicitly checks
the nonorthogonal-coordinate issue.
Thus, pointwise,
 exp(-epsilon)*exp(-g_I)<=1/d<=exp(-g_I).                 (6)
It follows for EVERY nonnegative moment integrand that its exact integral
is between exp(-epsilon) and 1 times the interpolated model integral.
Arb corner-log balls can be propagated through the same bilinear formula;
their uncertainty is separate from, and additional to, (5).

4. EXPLICIT CERTIFIED TENSOR GAUSS ERROR FOR THE MODEL
The desired even-z moment has the model density
 F(u,v)=J*(2pi)^(-3/2) q^p z^k
           exp[-5|x|^2/8-g_I(u,v)], k even, p>=0, J=2sqrt(2)/3.
This function extends to an entire function of two complex coordinates.
At the cell center write its exponent as
 E0+Eu*xi+Ev*eta+Euu*xi^2+Euv*xi*eta+Evv*eta^2.
Take complex polydisc radii R_u=2h_u, R_v=2h_v. A valid supremum M is

 J*(2pi)^(-3/2)
 *[|q_center|+(R_u+R_v)/sqrt(3)]^p
 *[|z_center|+sqrt(2/3)*(R_u+R_v)]^k
 *exp[E0+|Eu|R_u+|Ev|R_v+|Euu|R_u^2
                  +|Euv|R_u R_v+|Evv|R_v^2].             (7)

Use upper bounds for every factor when coefficients are interval-valued.
The n-node tensor Gauss-Legendre rule Q then satisfies
 |int_cell F-Q| <= (16/3)*h_u*h_v*M*16^(-n).              (8)

Proof: in one variable the radius ratio is tau=(h/2)/(2h)=1/4.
Truncating its Taylor series at degree2n-1 leaves a uniform remainder at
most M*tau^(2n)/(1-tau). Both the exact integral and the positive Gauss
rule have norm h and agree on that polynomial. The error is thus at most
2h times this remainder. Telescope the two tensor factors; positivity of
the other rule gives (8). This is an a priori remainder bound, not a
comparison between consecutive quadrature orders.

Combining (6),(8), if the evaluated model quadrature is the ball [Qlo,Qhi]
and E is the upward-rounded right side of (8), then a valid actual moment
enclosure is
 [max(0,exp(-epsilon)*(Qlo-E)), Qhi+E].                   (9)

The companion certified_log_diagonal_quadrature_20261101.py implements
(7)-(9), obtaining Gauss nodes AND weights as Arb enclosures from
arb.legendre_p_root. Its executable demo integrates four actual moments on
the cell [0,3/20]^2, using (4) to enclose the genuine corner logs. It does
not pretend that those coarse corner bounds suffice on the whole window.
The radial agent supplies sharper certified corner values there.
