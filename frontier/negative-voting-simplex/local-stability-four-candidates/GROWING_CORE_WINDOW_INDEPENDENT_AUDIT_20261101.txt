1. EXACT SMOOTHING CONSTANT AND FACET NORMALIZATION

Completing the square in k_rho(x,y)^2 gamma(dy) gives covariance
(1-rho^2)/(1+rho^2) I and mean2rho x/(1+rho^2). The normalizing integral is
(1-rho^4)^(-3/2) exp[rho^2|x|^2/(1+rho^2)]. At rho=-1/3, its square root
is(81/80)^(3/4) exp(|x|^2/20). Derivative multipliers are polynomials of
y and x, so C1 smoothing costs a polynomial in R with the same exponential.
Using L2 indicator difference<=sqrt(epsilon) is legitimate; the constant
does not come from an L1-to-Linfinity kernel supremum.

The utility weight normalization in source lines95-103 can be checked
without relying on a tie-density convention. Distributionally,
 grad 1_Ci=-sum_j n_ij delta_Sigma_ij, so
 D_x G_i=-rho sum_j n_ij I_ij.
Since n_ij=(Y_j-Y_i)/sqrt2, this equals
 (rho/sqrt2) sum_j I_ij(Y_i-Y_j).
The independent Gaussian utility representation gives
 D_x G_i=(rho/sigma)sum_j W_ij(Y_i-Y_j).
Thus W_ij=(sigma/sqrt2)I_ij exactly. Consequently
 DF0=(|rho|/sigma)Y^T L(W)Y and the complete graph yields
 lambda_min(DF0)>=2sqrt2|rho| c0(1+R)^(-2)exp(-R^2/16),
with c0 defined for I, as in the source. No factor of sigma or sqrt2 is
missing.

The small-sector integration giving the lower I bound works for every
x in B_R, not only points on the corresponding tie plane. Each I is a
Gaussian marginal of positive measure on a convex facet. Prékopa gives
Hess log I<=0. Removing exp(-|x|^2/16) leaves a positive Laplace transform,
so Hess log I>=-I/8. Hence |grad log I(x)|<=C+|x|/8 globally.

2. CONDITION-INDEPENDENT LAPLACIAN ESTIMATE

For z=L(w)^dagger b_e, the discrete maximum principle places the maximum
and minimum at the two endpoints of e. Their difference is R_eff(e),
and w_e R_eff(e)<=1 because the edge itself is an available conductance.
Mean zero implies ||z||_2<=sqrt(m)R_eff(e), which proves the source's
sqrt(2m)|E|theta bound for L(w)^dagger Delta L. This is valid without an
eigenvalue ratio. In the four-vertex case one can improve the intermediate
estimate to ||z||_2<=R_eff(e), using variance<=range^2/4; that improvement
is unnecessary for the exponent window.

Conjugation by Y is legitimate because Y is an isometry from R3 onto
1-perp. The pseudoinverse restricted to that subspace becomes the ordinary
inverse. No condition number is introduced by the conjugation.

3. INVERSE CHART: ORDER OF MATRIX FACTORS MATTERS

Set z=Psi(x), A=DF0(z), Delta=DF(z)-A. Then exactly

 D Psi(x)=(I+A^-1 Delta)^(-1)
                [I+A^-1(DF0(x)-A)].

The first perturbation is bounded by E_R/a_(R+1). The second is controlled
by the relative-weight estimate: each weight ratio changes by at most
exp[(C+(R+1)/8)|z-x|]-1. The value displacement is <=2E_R/a_(R+1).
Therefore ||Psi-id||_C1<=C(1+R)E_R/a_(R+1) when this quantity is small.
This confirms the source's crucial single inverse eigenvalue. A bound
through ||D^2F0|| followed by a second inverse would lose the window and
is not what this proof uses.

Existence and uniqueness follow from strong monotonicity and the stated
boundary-degree test. This remains valid when DF is nonsymmetric: its
symmetric part stays positive because the additive operator error is small.

4. PHYSICAL CORE AND EXPONENT BOOKKEEPING

The chart is parametrized on B_R, but its image need not contain that
identical physical ball. Its uniform displacement is o(1); a boundary-degree
argument shows it contains B_(R-1) once that displacement is sufficiently
small. Alternatively construct the chart on one slightly larger ball.
Thus an application to actual partition agreement should explicitly use
the physical core B_(R-1), or another fixed-width smaller core. This is a
bookkeeping clarification, not an exponent obstruction.

With R^2=a log(1/epsilon), the C1 chart bound is
epsilon^(1/2-9a/80-o(1)); shifts R->R+O(1) and all polynomial factors
contribute only epsilon^-o(1). Gaussian measure outside the physical
B_(R-1) is epsilon^(a/2-o(1)). Hence both required inequalities hold on
the open window4<a<40/9. At a=21/5 the chart exponent is11/400 and the
tail exponent is21/10. In particular the tail is o(epsilon^2).

Changing either assignment only on a Gaussian set of measure t changes
the bounded paired noise objective by at most2t; changing both costs at
most4t. This uses positivity and the Markov property of T_rho, which also
hold for negative rho. Thus the stated absolute objective-tail estimate
is justified.

5. EXACT LIMIT OF ACCEPTANCE

The source assumes |lambda|=O(sqrt(epsilon)) and a common-multiplier routing
representation of the minimizer. Its chart argument is valid conditional
on those premises. A separate source supplies the premises via the relaxed
first-order linear program and occupied thin prisms. The present audit
does not merge that input with an unproved moving-junction estimate.

The chart is C1-small on a growing core and the discarded objective is
o(epsilon^2). To conclude local isolation one still needs a nonlinear
energy estimate compatible with moving junctions, matching masses, and
rotation gauge, with constants that do not introduce an additional
exp(cR^2) loss. No such conclusion is inferred from this window alone.
