TETRAHEDRAL GAUSSIAN NOISE: LOCAL QUADRATIC GROWTH FOR MEASURABLE PAIRS
1 November 2026

RESULT

For every fixed finite N>=3, the cylindrical regular tetrahedral pair is
a strict local minimizer of paired Gaussian noise agreement at rho=-1/3,
among arbitrary measurable four-label partitions with matching label
masses. Their common mass vector is free. In some neighborhood of its
common orthogonal orbit,

       J(P,Q)-J(C^N,C^N) >= d_orb(P,Q)^2/1000.

The distance is the infimum, over one common orthogonal transformation,
of the maximum of the two summed-label Gaussian L1 distances. Reflecting
the second partition gives the equivalent positive-correlation1/3 result
near opposite tetrahedral partitions.

The coefficient1/1000 is universal. The neighborhood can depend on N,
and no numerical neighborhood radius is supplied. No smoothness,
stationarity, normal-graph representation, or minimizer hypothesis is
imposed on the competing partitions.

This does not prove global tetrahedral optimality, the full four-label
Standard Simplex conjecture, or sharp MAX-4-CUT hardness. Distant
competitors have not been excluded. External verification and priority
clearance remain outstanding. Internal reviews were performed by separate
AI-agent runs, with root review of the complete arguments.

WHY THE NONLINEAR STEP WORKS

Expand the objective exactly into two nonnegative reference-routing losses
and one signed interaction. Separate each label transfer according to
whether it lies in a thin normal neighborhood of its reference facet.
Compress the nearby transfers into signed normal masses. A one-dimensional
rearrangement and the exact all-height routing bound control their weighted
norms; the full boundary operator controls their interaction.

The remaining transfers have compact normalized interaction operators.
The apparently singular inverse routing cost is integrable near junctions:
the eligible transverse region shrinks in proportion to the singular
normal distance. Small total L1 changes therefore make these interactions,
mass errors, and rotation errors negligible compared with routing loss.

With normal cutoff2^-24, the explicitly bounded kernel-shift error is at
most1/32 of total routing loss. The certified spectral gap leaves the
margin8/25-1/32=231/800. This yields a gain of at least one quarter of
routing loss in a sufficiently small neighborhood, followed by the stated
L1 quadratic bound. Gaussian tensor factorization extends the argument to
each finite N; compactness constants may depend on N.

WHAT IS COMPUTED AND WHAT IS ANALYTIC

The computer-assisted input is the complete boundary-operator enclosure,
including interval arithmetic, exact polynomial cubature, rigorous spatial
and series tails, and an audited binary64 error budget. The portable
verifier recomputes it in a temporary directory. Additional exact checkers
verify finite arithmetic bounds used in the nonlinear arguments.

The nonlinear theorem additionally uses analytic proofs of reflection,
rearrangement, Hilbert--Schmidt compactness, weak convergence, and rotation
modulation. Running the checkers is not a machine formalization of these
proofs. No Lean formalization or external expert endorsement is claimed.
