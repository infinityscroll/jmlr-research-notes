WEIGHTED JUNCTION TRACE LEMMAS FOR THE TETRAHEDRAL FACETS
2026-11-01. Analytic estimates, separated from the geometric cap reduction.

PURPOSE
This note supplies explicit polynomial-in-radius estimates for cubic
edge and quartic vertex errors relative to facet L2 energy, and for the
lower-power errors needed to compare changed measure with facet L1 size.
It does not claim that every geometric defect has one of these forms;
that is the separate cap-decomposition task.

1. A HALF-STRIP IDENTITY
Write(s,t) for orthonormal facet coordinates with s>=0 along a triple ray
and t>=0 perpendicular inward. A regular tetrahedral facet has opening
L=acos(-1/3)>pi/2. Hence the entire quadrant s,t>=0 lies in that facet,
for either boundary ray. There is no need to stop the t integration at
the other ray. The two quadrants may overlap; summing estimates costs
at most a fixed multiplicity.

Let f be compactly supported and Lipschitz on the facet. Let w>0 be C1,
and suppose on the support of f that
 ||f||_infinity<=a, ||partial_t f||_infinity<=b,
 |partial_t log w|<=L_w.
For every k>=2, the fundamental theorem of calculus gives, for a.e.s,
 w(s,0)|f(s,0)|^k
 =-int_0^infinity partial_t[w(s,t)|f(s,t)|^k]dt
 <=(k b+L_w a)int_0^infinity w(s,t)|f(s,t)|^(k-1)dt.     (1)
The same follows by approximation for Lipschitz f. It is essential to
differentiate the weight as well; freezing the Gaussian weight along a
long strip would introduce an unnecessary exponential radius loss.

After integrating s, the cases k=3 and k=2 are
 int_edge w|f|^3 <=(3b+L_w a)int_facet w f^2,            (2)
 int_edge w|f|^2 <=(2b+L_w a)int_facet w |f|.            (3)

For w=d_e(x)(2pi)^(-3/2)exp(-|x|^2/2), the established curvature bound
 -I/8<=Hess log d_e<=0 on the tie plane yields
 |grad log w(x)|<=|grad log d_e(0)|+(9/8)|x|.
Thus L_w<=C+(9/8)R on a support of radius R. For Gaussian surface weight
alone, L_w<=R. Consequently if a,b<=eta,
 cubic weighted edge errors <=C(1+R)eta * facet D-energy,
 quadratic Gaussian edge errors <=C(1+R)eta * facet L1 size.             (4)
All constants here are independent of the support radius apart from the
displayed linear factor. With R=O(sqrt(log(1/epsilon))) and
eta=epsilon^(11/400-o(1)), the relative coefficients tend to0.

2. VERTEX ESTIMATES
Let W be a planar sector of opening L>0 with vertex0. Suppose f is
Lipschitz with |grad f|<=b and a=|f(0)|. If b=0 and f is compactly
supported then f=0, so assume b>0. On W intersect B_(a/(2b)),
                  |f(x)|>=a/2.
If w(x)>=c_w w(0) on that small sector, then for any p>=1,
 int_W w|f|^p
 >=c_w w(0)*(a/2)^p*(L/2)*(a/(2b))^2,
and hence
 w(0) a^(p+2)
 <=[2^(p+3)/(c_w L)] b^2 int_W w|f|^p.                 (5)
For p=2 this controls a quartic vertex defect by b^2 times facet energy;
for p=1 it controls a cubic vertex volume by b^2 times facet L1 size.

If a<=eta and b<=eta, use the Lipschitz upper bound eta in the disk
radius a/(2eta)<=1/2 instead of b. Both relevant weights have a uniform
positive comparison c_w on the fixed ball B_(1/2), independent of R.
Thus
 w(0)|f(0)|^4<=C eta^2 int_W w f^2,
 w(0)|f(0)|^3<=C eta^2 int_W w |f|.                     (6)
For six facets or finitely many height components, sum these estimates.
Finite-dimensional equivalence of their values costs only a fixed factor.

3. ENERGY-TO-MEASURE CONSEQUENCE ONCE GEOMETRY IS SUPPLIED
Suppose a cap/slab representation of an ambient deformation produces
facet heights p_e such that its changed Gaussian measure is bounded by
 C sum_e int|p_e|d lambda_e,
 plus quadratic edge and cubic vertex expressions controlled by(3),(6).
Then, for (1+R)eta sufficiently small, its changed measure is at most
                  C' sum_e int|p_e|d lambda_e.          (7)
The GLOBAL weighted Cauchy--Schwarz bound is
 sum_e int|p_e|d lambda_e
 <=[sum_e int d_e^(-1)d lambda_e]^(1/2)
   [sum_e int d_e p_e^2 d lambda_e]^(1/2).               (8)
The first factor is a fixed finite number, already enclosed in the
accepted translation/mass-angle certificate. It does not deteriorate
as R grows. Therefore(7) would give E>=c epsilon^2 with a fixed c>0,
avoiding any exponentially small minimum Gaussian density on B_R.

The hypotheses of this last consequence are deliberately explicit.
Showing that normal extensions and moving junctions actually admit the
stated cap/slab representation remains geometric work; equations(1)-(8)
are the analytic trace and measure estimates available once it does.
