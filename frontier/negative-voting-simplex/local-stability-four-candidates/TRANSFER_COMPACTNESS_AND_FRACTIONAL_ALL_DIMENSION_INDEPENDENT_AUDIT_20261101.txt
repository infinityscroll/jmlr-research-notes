10. DIRECT FRACTIONAL-ASSIGNMENT EXTENSION

THEOREM. Fix a finite N>=3. Let theta be the cylindrical tetrahedral
assignment and rho=-1/3. There exists epsilon_N>0 such that measurable
f,h:R^N->Delta_4 with E f=E h and

 d_orb(f,h)=inf_O max{sum_i ||f_i-1_(OC_i)||_1,
                     sum_i ||h_i-1_(OC_i)||_1}<epsilon_N

satisfy

       sum_i <f_i,T_rho h_i>-sum_i <theta_i,T_rho theta_i>
                         >= d_orb(f,h)^2/1000.

The common masses are arbitrary. This theorem has the same documented
certificate premises and local scope as Sections 1-8. Reflection of h
gives the correlation +1/3 version near the pair (theta,-theta).

PROOF. On the reference source cell C_i, define the fractional transfer
a_ij(x)=f_j(x) for j!=i, and zero outside C_i. Then 0<=a_ij<=1 and

 f-theta=sum_(i!=j) a_ij(e_j-e_i),
 sum_i |f_i-theta_i|=2 sum_(i!=j) a_ij,
 L_f=sum_(i!=j) integral a_ij w_ij dgamma.

The bilinear expansion, routing identity, good/bad split, and all mass
identities are therefore unchanged. The same holds for h. The rotation
gauge applies because ||f_i-theta_i||_2^2<=||f_i-theta_i||_1, so its C2
perturbation estimates require no purity assumption.

The only pure-support step needing replacement is weak convergence of
the bad normalized transfer. Set F=a sqrt(w)/sqrt(L), with 0<=a<=1.
Its L2 norm is at most one. For a fixed threshold tau>0, split into
a>tau and a<=tau. The first set has Gaussian measure at most
(integral a dgamma)/tau, which tends to zero. On the second set,

 ||F 1_(a<=tau)||_2^2
       <= tau (integral a w dgamma)/L <= tau.

For any fixed L2 test phi, Cauchy--Schwarz makes the first pairing tend
to zero, and bounds the second by sqrt(tau)||phi||_2. Sending tau to
zero proves F weakly tends to zero. Thus all compact-operator and bad
mass/gauge arguments survive even when the fractional supports do not
shrink. This does not use a false shrinking-support assertion.

On each good side the fiber is now a density q(t) in [0,1], supported
in (0,delta). Its normal mass m is at most psi(delta). Let u satisfy
psi(u)=m and let c(t) be the increasing reference cost. Then

 integral [q(t)-1_(0,u)(t)] [c(t)-c(u)] dnu(t) >=0.

The mass difference is zero, so this proves the same initial-interval
bathtub inequality. Consequently D(p)<=2L_f and D(p_abs)<=4L_f remain
exact. The summed first normal moment also retains both bounds in
Section 6, proving its normalized weak convergence and the good gauge
estimate. Positivity permits every kernel domination used above.

All remaining steps are linear identities, bounded-operator inequalities,
or the same sequential contradiction. The coefficient 1/1000 follows.
If L=0, positivity of each w off its null tie set forces every off-diagonal
a_ij to vanish, hence f=h=theta almost everywhere after alignment.

This fractional extension is direct. It neither purifies the pair nor
assumes that either rule is a reflection of the other.
