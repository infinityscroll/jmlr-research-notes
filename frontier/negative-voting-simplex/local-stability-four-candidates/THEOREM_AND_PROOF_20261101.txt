1. STATEMENT AND CONVENTIONS

Let gamma_N be standard Gaussian measure on R^N. For -1<rho<1, set
 T_rho f(x)=E[f(rho*x+sqrt(1-rho^2)Z)], Z~gamma_N,
and define paired agreement
 J_rho(P,Q)=sum_(i=1)^4 int 1_(P_i) T_rho 1_(Q_i) d gamma_N.

In R^3 take the four score vectors
 Y_1=(1,1,1)/2, Y_2=(1,-1,-1)/2,
 Y_3=(-1,1,-1)/2, Y_4=(-1,-1,1)/2,
and C_i={x:Y_i.x=max_j Y_j.x}. For finite N>=3 write C_i^N=C_i x R^(N-3).
We study J_(-1/3) at (C^N,C^N). Equivalently, reflecting the second
partition studies J_(1/3) at (C^N,-C^N).

For e=(i,j), i<j, let Sigma_e^N be the common facet, n_e=(Y_j-Y_i)/sqrt2
(padded with zero coordinates), and b_e=e_i-e_j in R^4. Thus n_e points
from cell i into cell j. Put
 d lambda_e,N=gamma_N(x)dH^(N-1)(x) on Sigma_e^N,
 d_e(x)=partial_n_e T_(-1/3)(1_(C_i^N)-1_(C_j^N))(x)>0.
The function d_e depends only on the first three coordinates.

The boundary-speed Hilbert space and its norm are
 H_N=direct_sum_e L^2(Sigma_e^N,d_e d lambda_e,N),
 ||u||_D^2=sum_e int d_e u_e^2 d lambda_e,N.
Speeds are scalar outward normal velocities; tangential reparametrizations
have no separate representation in this space. Define the mass derivative
 M_N u=sum_e b_e int u_e d lambda_e,N.

Consider two partition variations whose masses match label by label:
 gamma_N(P_i(s))=gamma_N(Q_i(s)) for all i and s. Their COMMON mass vector
may vary with s; it is not required to stay (1/4,1/4,1/4,1/4). If u,v are
their initial normal speeds at (C^N,C^N), the tangent constraint is
                         M_N(u-v)=0.                    (1)
For the positive-correlation formulation, v denotes the speed after
reflecting the second partition back to C^N.

Let R_N consist of the rotation speeds
 r_Omega,e(x)=n_e.(Omega x), Omega^T=-Omega,
and let N_N={(r,r):r in R_N} in H_N x H_N. Distances in the product use
||(u,v)||^2=||u||_D^2+||v||_D^2.

THEOREM (subject to the computational acceptance stated above).
For every finite N>=3, the continuous quadratic form B_N extending the
paired second variation at (C^N,C^N), rho=-1/3, satisfies

 B_N(u,v) >= (9/100) dist_(H_N x H_N)((u,v),N_N)^2        (2)

whenever (1) holds. Its kernel on that constrained space is exactly N_N.
The neutral speed space R_N has dimension3N-6. Consequently simultaneous
rotations are the only nonzero neutral normal-speed directions.

The same conclusion holds for positive correlation1/3 at (C^N,-C^N).
For every admissible smooth variation to which moving-boundary
differentiation applies, B_N is the actual second derivative of agreement.
The extension to H_N is a quadratic-form statement, not a claim that every
L^2 speed integrates to a classical smooth deformation.

2. EXACT HESSIAN AND OPERATOR

Write rho=-1/3. The Gaussian kernel relative to gamma_N(x)gamma_N(y) is
 k_N(x,y)=(1-rho^2)^(-N/2)
 exp[(2rho*x.y-rho^2(|x|^2+|y|^2))/(2(1-rho^2))].
Define
 K_N(u,v)=sum_(e,f)(b_e.b_f) int int u_e(x)v_f(y)k_N(x,y)
                                     d lambda_e,N(x)d lambda_f,N(y).
The self-adjoint boundary operator A_N is specified by
                  <u,A_N v>_D=K_N(u,v).                 (3)

Reflection interchanging vertices i,j fixes their tie plane. Isotropy of
T_rho therefore gives T_rho(1_Ci-1_Cj)=0 on that plane. The reference
first variation vanishes without mass multipliers. Moving-boundary
differentiation gives
 B_N(u,v)=||u||_D^2+||v||_D^2+2K_N(u,v).                 (4)
Indeed each pure second derivative is the normal derivative of the
vanishing potential difference times the square of its boundary speed;
curvature and acceleration multiply the zero potential difference. The
two first boundary derivatives produce the mixed term2K_N.

For u_+=(u+v)/2 and u_-=(u-v)/2, equations (1),(4) become
 M_N u_-=0,
 B_N(u,v)=2[<u_+,(I+A_N)u_+>_D+<u_-,(I-A_N)u_->_D].     (5)
There is no mass constraint on u_+.

Here are analytic bounds making the three-dimensional operator well-defined.
With L=acos(-1/3),
 k_3=(9/8)^(3/2)exp[-(|x|^2+|y|^2)/16-(3/8)x.y],
 I_f(x)=int_(Sigma_f) k_3(x,y)d lambda_f(y),
 d_e=(1/3)[2I_e+(1/2)sum_four_adjacent I_f].              (6)
All terms in (6) are positive. Restricting the same-facet integral to
|y|<=1/(1+|x|) gives
 d_e(x)>=c0 exp(-|x|^2/16)/(1+|x|)^2,
 c0=(L/3)(9/8)^(3/2)(2pi)^(-3/2)exp(-15/16).             (7)
Thus mass derivatives are continuous on H_3. The squared normalized kernel
is integrable: after (7), its exponential part is at most
exp[-3(|x|^2+|y|^2)/16], times a polynomial. Hence A_3 is Hilbert--Schmidt.
The proof and explicit spatial tails appear in
COMPACT_SECTOR_DIAGONAL_AND_CERTIFIED_OPERATOR_TAIL_20261101.txt.

3. THE THREE-DIMENSIONAL SPECTRAL REDUCTION

Let T be the span of translations t_w,e=n_e.w. The indicator's translation
derivative is -w.grad1_Ci, and grad T_rho=rho T_rho grad. Consequently
                         A_3 t_w=3t_w.                 (8)
For r_Q,e=n_e.(Qx), Q skew, angular differentiation commutes with T_rho;
the same potential calculation gives
                         A_3 r_Q=-r_Q.                 (9)
Both spaces have dimension3. The normals span R^3; a skew Q giving zero
speed on each open facet must annihilate all normals and hence vanish.
The spaces T,R are orthogonal because their eigenvalues differ.

Use the full-space scalar integrals, independent of e by symmetry,
 s0=int d lambda_e=L/(2pi)^(3/2),
 a0=int d_e d lambda_e, b0=int d_e^(-1)d lambda_e.
The mass representers in H_3 are q_c,e=(b_e.c)/d_e for c perpendicular to1.
Let G be their three-dimensional span; ker M_3=G-perp. Since
sum_e n_e n_e^T=2I_3 and b_e.Yw=-sqrt2 n_e.w,
 <t_v,t_w>_D=2a0 v.w,
 <q_Yv,q_Yw>_D=4b0 v.w,
 <q_Yv,t_w>_D=-2sqrt2 s0 v.w.
Thus, with
                    delta=1-s0^2/(a0b0),
every u in ker M_3 satisfies
                 ||P_Tu||_D^2<=delta||u||_D^2.           (10)
This angle correction is necessary: mass-zero speeds need not be
orthogonal to translations.

The computational inputs are exactly

 H=||A_3||_HS^2
  =sum_(e,f)(b_e.b_f)^2 int int k_3(x,y)^2/[d_e(x)d_f(y)]
                                         d lambda_e(x)d lambda_f(y)
                         <153/5,
                         delta<1/20.                   (11)

Let E=A_3-3P_T+P_R. Equations (8),(9) and self-adjointness imply
 E P_T=E P_R=0, ||E||_HS^2=H-30.
Put h=sqrt(H-30). From (11), h<sqrt(3/5)<4/5. For all u,
 <u,(I+A_3)u>_D>=4||P_Tu||_D^2+(1-h)||P_(T+R)-perp u||_D^2
                    >=(1/5)||P_(R-perp)u||_D^2.          (12)
For a unit u in ker M_3, (10) gives
 <u,A_3u>_D<=h+(3-h)delta-(1+h)||P_Ru||_D^2<91/100.
Therefore
 <u,(I-A_3)u>_D>=(9/100)||u||_D^2 on ker M_3.            (13)
The common channel's kernel is exactly R, and the contrast channel is
strictly positive.

4. ALL FINITE DIMENSIONS

Write d=N-3. At the cylindrical reference,
 lambda_e,N=lambda_e,3 tensor gamma_d and d_e,N=d_e,3.
Consequently
 H_N=H_3 tensor L^2(gamma_d), A_N=A_3 tensor T_(-1/3)^(d). (14)
Mass representers are q_c tensor1. On extra-coordinate Hermite degree k,
the operator in (14) equals (-1/3)^k A_3. Only degree0 has a mass
constraint; every higher degree automatically has zero mass derivative.

Equations (8)-(13) put the spectrum of A_3 in[-1,3], with only T at3,
only R at-1, and remaining spectrum of absolute value below4/5.
Degree0 therefore has the gaps (12),(13). At degree1, the only eigen-1
modes are T tensor linear functions; away from them the common-channel
gap exceeds1-(4/5)/3, and the contrast-channel gap is at least2/3.
At degree k>=2, the operator norm is at most3(1/3)^k<=1/3, so both
channel gaps are at least2/3. Thus (12),(13) hold in H_N with R replaced
by R_N.

A skew ambient matrix has blocks [[Q,B],[-B^T,Q_extra]]. Its normal
speed is n_e.(Qx+Bz), exactly R tensor1 plus T tensor linear functions.
The Q_extra block has zero normal speed. Hence dim R_N=3+3d=3N-6.
Finally (5) yields
 B_N>=(2/5)||P_(R_N-perp)u_+||_D^2+(9/50)||u_-||_D^2.
Since the squared distance in (2) is
2||P_(R_N-perp)u_+||_D^2+2||u_-||_D^2, this proves (2) and its exact
kernel. This argument concerns the reference operator; it uses no global
dimension-reduction statement about unknown minimizers.

6. ADMISSIBLE VARIATIONS AND LIMITS OF THE CONCLUSION

Smooth compactly supported facet speeds away from junctions form a dense
core in H_N and are realizable by ambient smooth vector fields supported
in disjoint facet neighborhoods. To approximate a mass-zero speed within
that core, correct the residual mass derivative using three fixed bump
speeds on a spanning tree of the four labels. Their mass images are three
independent incidence vectors, and continuity of M makes the corrections
tend to zero. Thus the constrained core is dense in ker M.

For smooth initial velocities satisfying (1), the same rank-three correction
and the implicit-function theorem restore exact matching masses along
the pair's paths. Corrections start at order s^2 and do not alter (4),
because the reference first variation vanishes without multipliers.
The all-Hilbert-space bounds therefore control realizable smooth tests
without asserting that every rough speed has a classical deformation.

For a fixed twice-differentiable admissible path with non-rotation first
velocity and a justified second-order expansion, the theorem gives a
strict increase for sufficiently small nonzero parameter. A uniform
nonlinear neighborhood theorem requires a deformation chart, treatment
of junctions and tails, and uniform remainder control. None is supplied
by the Hessian bound alone. No global paired inequality, global simplex
conjecture, or sharp MAX-4-CUT hardness conclusion is claimed here.
