POSITIVE EXTENDED DIAGONAL AND RELATIVE POTENTIAL DERIVATIVES
2026-11-01. Independent completion of the derivative estimates used by
Sections5 and7 of MOVING_JUNCTION_FORMAL_SLAB_CAP_DECOMPOSITION_20261101.txt.

Let e=(i,j), n=n_e, rho=-1/3, sigma^2=8/9, and
G=T_rho(1_Ci-1_Cj). The reference cell C_i lies in n.y<=0 and is exchanged
with C_j by reflection in n-perp. Write phi_sigma for the centered
three-dimensional Gaussian density with covariance sigma^2 I. Define
the POSITIVE EXTENSION of the facet diagonal to every z in R^3 by

 D_e(z)=(3/4)int_Ci(-n.y)phi_sigma(y+z/3)dy.              (1)

This extension is not asserted to equal partial_n G away from n-perp.
On the tie plane it does equal the actual positive diagonal d_e.

1. GLOBAL LOGARITHMIC CURVATURE
The factor(-n.y)1_Ci(y) is log-concave: on the convex cone interior it
has logarithm log(-n.y), a concave function; extension by0 preserves
log-concavity. Its convolution with a Gaussian is log-concave. Hence
Hess log D_e<=0. This is the same Prekopa argument already used for the
accepted diagonal-curvature lemma, now retaining all three coordinates.

Alternatively writing out the Gaussian exponent gives
 D_e(z)=constant*exp(-|z|^2/16)
     int_Ci(-n.y)exp(-9|y|^2/16-3z.y/8)dy.
The integral is a positive Laplace transform. Its log Hessian is a
covariance matrix and is positive semidefinite. Therefore exactly

                    -I/8<=Hess log D_e<=0.               (2)

There are only six edges. Thus a fixed finite constant C0 bounds all
|grad log D_e(0)|, and for |z|<=R+1,
 |grad log D_e(z)|<=C0+(R+1)/8,
 ||Hess D_e(z)||<=D_e(z)[1/8+(C0+(R+1)/8)^2].            (3)
All differentiations are justified by the Gaussian integrability of
polynomials times linear tilts. No lower minimum of d_e on B_R is used.

2. EXACT REPRESENTATION OF THE POTENTIAL DIFFERENCE
For x in n-perp, reflection gives
 G(x+tn)=2exp(-t^2/16)
              int_Ci sinh(alpha(y)t)phi_sigma(y+x/3)dy,
 alpha(y)=-(3/8)n.y>=0.
Use sinh(alpha t)=(alpha/2)int_(-t)^t exp(alpha s)ds and
phi_sigma(y+(x+sn)/3)=phi_sigma(y+x/3)exp(alpha s-s^2/16).
The result, valid also for negative t with oriented integration, is

 G(x+tn)=(1/2)exp(-t^2/16)
                   int_(-t)^t exp(s^2/16)D_e(x+sn)ds
        =(t/2)int_(-1)^1 exp[(u^2-1)t^2/16]
                                      D_e(x+utn)du.     (4)

At t=0 this gives partial_n G(x)=D_e(x)=d_e(x), checking the factor.
The representation uses a strictly positive integrand. In particular,
if |x|<=R, |t|<=1, and(R+1)|t|<=1, then(3)-(4) give
 |G(x+tn)|<=C|t|d_e(x),
 |grad_tan G(x+tn)|<=C|t|(R+1)d_e(x),
 |partial_n G(x+tn)|<=C d_e(x),
 ||Hess G(x+tn)||<=C(R+1)d_e(x).                         (5)
Here C is universal for the fixed tetrahedron. To verify the last bound,
differentiate(4) twice. Tangential second derivatives have an extra
factor |t|(R+1)^2; mixed derivatives have size O(R+1); and normal second
derivatives have size O(R+1)+O(|t|(R+1)^2). The hypothesis
|t|(R+1)<=1 absorbs the extra factors. Formula(3) controls every derivative
that occurs. This makes the Hessian estimate explicit without a separate
unproved tilted-moment assertion.

3. APPLICATION NEAR A TRIPLE RAY OR THE VERTEX
If z lies within distance a<=1 of a triple-ray point x0=s ell, and
(R+1)a<=c for a fixed constant c, write z=x+tn for any incident facet.
Then |x-x0|,|t|<=a. Equation(3) makes d_e(x) comparable to d_e(x0) by
a universal factor; the incident d_e(x0) are equal by label symmetry.
Equation(5) therefore gives
 |G_i(z)-G_j(z)|<=C a d_j(x0)
for each pair among the three incident labels. At the vertex the same
argument uses D_e(0), common to all six edges. This is exactly the extra
factor a needed to convert cap volume into the cubic/quartic routing
error controlled by the weighted traces.

4. ROTATION TESTS AND THEIR NORMAL DERIVATIVES
For ||Omega||<=1, let g_Omega,i=-(Omega z).grad G_i(z). Then
 g_Omega,i-g_Omega,j=-(Omega z).grad G(z).
At x in the tie plane, grad G(x)=d_e(x)n, so
        g_Omega,i(x)-g_Omega,j(x)=-d_e(x)r_Omega,e(x).
For |t|(R+1)<=1, (5) gives
 |g_Omega,i(x+tn)-g_Omega,j(x+tn)|<=C(R+1)d_e(x).
Differentiating this actual signed difference, rather than estimating
individual potentials, gives
 partial_n(g_Omega,i-g_Omega,j)
  =-(Omega n).grad G-(Omega z).Hess G n.
Thus its magnitude is at most C(R+1)^2 d_e(x), by(5).
This justifies both the formal-slab Taylor error and the cap rotation
test bound used in the moving-junction source.

All constants in these estimates grow at most polynomially with R.
Only small(R+1) times the displacement is required. The estimates are
therefore compatible with the growing core R=O(sqrt(log(1/epsilon))) and
the displacement epsilon^(11/400-o(1)).
