1. REFERENCE AND FULL KERNEL

Work in R3 with tetrahedral score rows
  y1=(1,1,1)/2, y2=(1,-1,-1)/2,
  y3=(-1,1,-1)/2, y4=(-1,-1,1)/2.
Their Gram matrix is I4-11^T/4. The regular cells are
  Theta_i={y_i.x=max_j y_j.x}.
For an edge e=(i,j), i<j, let
  Sigma_e={y_i.x=y_j.x=max_k y_k.x},
  n_e=(y_j-y_i)/sqrt2,
  d lambda_e=gamma_3 dH2 restricted to Sigma_e,
so n_e points out of cell i. Set rho=-1/3 and G_i=T_rho Theta_i.
The reference is stationary without a mass multiplier: G_i=G_j on Sigma_e.
Define its ACTUAL positive normal derivative
  d_e(x)=partial_{n_e}(G_i-G_j)(x)>0.
For scalar interface speeds u=(u_e), write
  D(u,v)=sum_e int d_e(x)u_e(x)v_e(x)d lambda_e(x).
This D is a boundary quadratic form; it is not the degree-0/1 deficit of
an assignment used in the earlier endpoint obstruction note.

With b_e=e_i-e_j in label space, define the signed boundary distribution
  h_i[u]=sum_e (b_e)_i u_e lambda_e.
The full Gaussian kernel relative to gamma_3(x)gamma_3(y) is
  K_rho(x,y)=(1-rho^2)^(-3/2)
       exp((2rho x.y-rho^2(|x|^2+|y|^2))/(2(1-rho^2))).
Then
  K(u,v)=sum_(e,f)(b_e.b_f)
     int_Sigma_e int_Sigma_f
          u_e(x)v_f(y)K_rho(x,y)d lambda_f(y)d lambda_e(x).    (1)
This is precisely sum_i<h_i[u],T_rho h_i[v]>. It includes every chaos.

2. THE TWO PAIRED CHANNELS AND THE CORRECT MASS CONSTRAINT

Reflect the second original rule, so paired cost is <f,T_rho h> with
rho=-1/3. At (Theta,Theta), let their two interface velocities be u,v.
The first variations vanish. Differentiating twice gives
  J''=D(u,u)+D(v,v)+2K(u,v).                       (2)
The diagonal terms come from normal differentiation of G_i-G_j, and the
mixed term comes from the two first boundary derivatives. Curvature and
acceleration terms multiply the zero reference potential difference.
For compact smooth speeds away from multiple junctions this is ordinary
moving-boundary differentiation. The reference's Gaussian smoothing makes
all potential derivatives smooth. The formula extends to any weighted
closure on which the displayed forms are finite and continuous; that
extension is not assumed without its analytic estimates.

Let u_+=(u+v)/2 and u_-=(u-v)/2. Then exactly
  J''=2[(D+K)(u_+,u_+)+(D-K)(u_-,u_-)].           (3)
The derivative of the label mass vector is
  M u=sum_e b_e int u_e d lambda_e.
Equal common masses for the two rules impose
  M u_-=0.                                       (4)
They do not impose M u_+=0, because the common mass vector itself is free.
This distinction is essential. The paired local problem therefore asks
for D+K on common speeds and D-K on zero-mass opposite speeds.

For realizable compact test speeds satisfying (4), second-order equality
of the two mass vectors can be restored by offset adjustments of order
O(t^2): the regular offset Laplacian has rank3. Such corrections do not
change (2), because reference potential differences are zero. This justifies
using (4) as the tangent constraint in a necessary local stability test.

3. EXACT NORMALIZATION CHECKS

Translations. Let t_e(w)=n_e.w. Translation of the whole partition has
boundary derivative h[t]=-w.grad Theta. The identity
  grad T_rho= rho T_rho grad
implies, on each interface,
  (T_rho h_i[t]-T_rho h_j[t])
    =-(1/rho) w.grad(G_i-G_j)
    =-(1/rho)d_e t_e(w).
Consequently
  K(t,v)=-(1/rho)D(t,v)=3D(t,v)                   (5)
for every admissible test v. Thus translations are exact generalized
eigenvectors of K relative to D with eigenvalue3. They would make D-K
negative. However
  M t(w)=-B0 w,
where B0 is the regular centroid matrix of spatial rank3. No nonzero
translation satisfies (4). Their exclusion is a common-mass fact, not a
single-rule dimension-reduction theorem. The constraint is M u=0; it
must not silently be replaced by D-orthogonality to translations.

Rotations. Every common rotation of the reference pair stays stationary
and has unchanged cost. Differentiating stationarity along a rotation
shows that its speed t_rot satisfies
  (D+K)(t_rot,v)=0,
so K=-D on the rotation subspace. These give exact eigenvalue-1 and are
null directions of the common channel, as expected.

Actual diagonal formula. Put sigma=sqrt(1-rho^2), a_i=rho y_i.x/sigma.
For Gaussian utilities Z_i+a_i, let
  w_ij(a)=int phi(t-a_i)phi(t-a_j)
                       product_(k!=i,j)Phi(t-a_k)dt.
At x in Sigma_ij, a_i=a_j and symmetry gives w_ik=w_jk. Differentiating
by swapping utility means yields
  d_e(x)=(-rho sqrt2/sigma)
                   [2w_ij(a)+sum_(k!=i,j)w_ik(a)].          (6)
Every term is positive. The code computes this full expression, not its
rho=0 value or an asymptotic estimate.

5. IMPLICATION GAP

A proof that D-K>=0 on every zero-mass speed would establish the opposite
paired LOCAL condition, not global minimality. Even the combination of
both local conditions cannot classify arbitrary pure pairs from Lemma A
without another global argument. Conversely, a certified negative value
on a realizable constrained speed would be a genuine local counterexample;
none was found in the tested space.

The numerical evidence supports investigating the actual diagonal-weighted
operator (1),(6), rather than a constant first-chaos replacement. It does
not license the false reflection-averaging step or scalar impurity bridge
refuted in the companion theory note.
