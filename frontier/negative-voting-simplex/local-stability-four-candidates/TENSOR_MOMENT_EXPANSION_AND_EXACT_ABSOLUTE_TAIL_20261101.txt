RESULT

The proposed tensor expansion is correct, with the constant
432 exp(15/8) INCLUDING the Gaussian-kernel normalization, angular factors,
and ordered label-incidence sum. An explicit two-parity ratio bound proves
an absolute tail below .008218 after degree60, below4.56e-6 after degree80,

The tetrahedral symmetries reduce every finite coefficient to positive

This genuinely replaces the finite four-dimensional double integral by

No local or global endpoint theorem is claimed here.

2. EXACT TENSOR SERIES, WITH JUSTIFIED INTERCHANGE

Let M_e^(k)=int x^tensor k d mu_e(x), and put
  A_k=||sum_e c_e tensor c_e tensor M_e^(k)||^2>=0. (4)
All tensor spaces carry their ordinary Euclidean inner products. The identity
  <x^tensor k,y^tensor k>=(x.y)^k
and the analogous label identity give

Therefore
  H=sigma^(-6) sum_(k>=0) (-b)^k A_k/k!.           (5)
The coefficient A_k is nonnegative even for odd k. Positivity follows from
the assembled tensor square, not from asserting (x.y)^k>=0 pointwise.

To justify termwise integration, bound the absolute exponential series by
exp(b|x.y|)<=exp(bst). The envelope in(3) then has exponent

       <=-(3/16)(s^2+t^2).
This is integrable with the polynomial radial factors. Tonelli and dominated
convergence prove absolute convergence and the interchange in(5).

4. EXACT RECURRENCE AND GEOMETRIC TAIL

Let

Then
  J0=(2/3)sqrt(pi), J1=8/9,
  J_(n+2)=(8/9)(n+1)J_n,
  I_k=J_(k+1)+2J_(k+2)+J_(k+3).                   (7)
The recurrence follows by one integration by parts, with vanishing boundary
terms. It gives
  I_(k+2)
   =(8/9)[(k+2)J_(k+1)+2(k+3)J_(k+2)+(k+4)J_(k+3)]
   <=(8/9)(k+4)I_k.
Consequently
  T_(k+2)/T_k <= q_k,
  q_k=(4/9)(k+4)^2/[(k+1)(k+2)].                 (8)
Here q4=128/135<1 and
  q_k-q_(k+1)=(4/9)(5k+23)/[(k+1)(k+2)(k+3)]>0.
Separating the two parities gives, for N>=3,
  sum_(k>N) T_k
      <=T_(N+1)/(1-q_(N+1))+T_(N+2)/(1-q_(N+2)). (9)
The limiting two-step ratio is4/9, corresponding to a one-step rate2/3.

There is also a simple one-step majorant. The exact positive moment sequence

6. ONE FACET, POSITIVE RATIONAL CONTRACTIONS

Use bisector coordinates (q,z) on the reference facet12:
  x=q(1,1,-1,-1)/2+z(0,0,1,-1)/sqrt2,
  q>=0, |z|<=sqrt2 q.
The measure mu is invariant under z -> -z. Define
  m_(p,j)=int q^p z^j d mu.
Odd-j moments vanish exactly; all even-j moments are positive.
The same formulas apply if every measure is restricted to the same radial
cutoff, which preserves tetrahedral symmetry and this reflection.

For two copies of the same facet, the frame Gram matrix is I2, giving
  S_k=sum_(j=0)^k binom(k,j)m_(k-j,j)^2,             (11)
with only even j surviving.
For an adjacent facet choose its bisector frame so the cross Gram is
  [[0,1/sqrt2],[1/sqrt2,1/2]].
Then
  Adj_k=sum_(ell+m+n=k) [k!/(ell!m!n!)]
       2^(-(ell+m)/2-n) m_(ell,m+n) m_(m,ell+n).   (12)
A term survives only when m+n and ell+n are both even, which is equivalent
to ell,m,n all having the same parity. In particular ell+m is even, so
EVERY surviving coefficient in(12) is rational. Its moments are positive.
There is no cancellation inside either(11) or(12).

All six same-facet contributions have incidence square4; the24 ordered
adjacent pairs have incidence square1; disjoint pairs have zero incidence.
Thus
  A_k=24[S_k+Adj_k].                               (13)
The alternating sum in(5) is the only remaining cancellation. No full
3^k tensor needs to be constructed, and no square-root interval enters
the contraction coefficients.

Through even degreeN=2m, the number of nonzero one-facet scalar moments
m_(p,2j) with p+2j<=N is exactly(m+1)^2. Thus degree60 uses961 moments;
degree80 uses1681; degree100 uses2601.

8. CONDITIONING AND WHAT STILL REQUIRES A REAL CERTIFICATE

The exact rational checker also proves the conservative aggregate bound
  sum_(k>=0) T_k < 1551114.                        (14)
Suppose the finitely many positive moments have approximate values with
relative error at most epsilon. Every product/square in(11)--(12) then has
relative error at most2epsilon+epsilon^2, and positivity propagates this
bound to each A_k. Hence the error in the alternating finite sum is at most
  (2epsilon+epsilon^2)*1551114.                    (15)
At epsilon=1e-8 this is rigorously less than .0311. Degree60's tail is less
than .008218. Those conservative budgets already fit comfortably inside
the exploratory gap between H approximately30.283777 and the sufficient
local target H<30.4. The exploratory value is not an input to the proofs
above, and a certified finite sum is still required.

One-sided interval evaluation is straightforward: positive contractions
are monotone in the positive moments. For an upper bound on H, use upper
coefficient bounds for even k and lower bounds for odd k, then add the
absolute tail. Do not substitute upper bounds for every alternating term.

What remains hard is rigorous enclosure of the2D moment integrals,
including their actual reciprocal diagonal d_e. Merely running high-order
quadrature twice or using high-precision floating arithmetic is not an
error proof. A bound on only the first derivative of log d_e makes naive
first-order interval meshes prohibitively fine at the1e-8 relative target.
A practical certificate needs high-order interval quadrature, an analytic
interpolation remainder, or another explicit integration error bound.

For reference, the proposed first-derivative bound was independently checked.
The diagonal is a positive combination of cone Gaussian integrals with
  grad log d_e(x)=-x/8-(3/8)E Y.
Intrinsic radial integration by parts on each two-dimensional cone gives
  (9/8)E|Y|^2=2-(3/8)x.EY.
The formula survives positive mixing of the cones. For m=E|Y|,
  9m^2-3|x|m-16<=0,
  m<=(|x|+sqrt(|x|^2+64))/6.
It follows that
  |grad log d_e(x)|<=(3|x|+sqrt(|x|^2+64))/16
                         <=|x|/4+1/2.             (16)
This is a correct local-enclosure ingredient, but is not by itself the
high-order quadrature certificate needed above.
