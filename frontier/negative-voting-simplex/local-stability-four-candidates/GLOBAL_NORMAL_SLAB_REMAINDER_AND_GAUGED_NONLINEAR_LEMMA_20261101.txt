RESULT

At rho=-1/3, consider the cylindrical tetrahedral partition in any finite
N>=3. There is a quantitative finite-change lower bound for arbitrary
measurable normal graphs with exact matching masses and exact rotation
gauge. No spatial cutoff is needed. A sufficient uniform height bound is

                        epsilon <=1/1638400.               (1)

The graph heights must also taper near other interfaces so their slabs are
valid and disjoint. Under the precise conditions below, the actual paired
noise deficit is at least (2/25)(D(p)+D(q)), where p,q are exact signed
slab-mass coordinates. This is a nonlinear inequality for the specified
graph class, not a reduction of arbitrary measurable partitions to graphs.

1. VALID MEASURABLE SLABS AND EXACT MASS COORDINATES

Use all definitions from THEOREM_AND_PROOF_ACCEPTED_20261101.txt. For the
facet e=(i,j), n_e points from C_i into C_j. Write a base point as x=(x0,z),
where x0 is on the three-dimensional facet and z is in R^(N-3). Define the
normalized gap to the other labels by

 a_e(x0)=min_{k notin{i,j}} (Y_i-Y_k).x0/sqrt(2).

On the facet Y_i.x0=Y_j.x0, so the same expression results with j. It is
positive on the relative interior and zero on the junctions. Let u_e,v_e
be arbitrary measurable heights satisfying, almost everywhere,

 |u_e(x)|,|v_e(x)| <=min(epsilon,a_e(x0)/3).                (2)

The normal map x+t n_e has Gaussian volume measure
d lambda_e,N(x) exp(-t^2/2)dt, because x.n_e=0. Moving a distance at most
a_e/3 preserves a strict gap between both labels i,j and every other label.
Every point in such a tube therefore has the unique unordered top-two
label pair{i,j}. Tubes associated with different facets are disjoint; the
normal projection within one tube is unique. Thus the signed slabs define
actual measurable partitions P,Q: for positive u, label i replaces j on
0<t<u, and for negative u, label j replaces i on u<t<0. Outside the slabs
the reference partition is unchanged. Apply the same construction with v.
No differentiability of the height functions is used.

Put psi(t)=int_0^t exp(-s^2/2)ds, p_e=psi(u_e), q_e=psi(v_e).
The mass changes are EXACTLY M_Np and M_Nq. Consequently label-by-label
matching masses of P and Q are equivalent to

                            M_N(p-q)=0.                  (3)

Their common masses can change; they need not stay equal to1/4. Bounded
heights imply p,q belong to H_N. Impose also the explicit rotation gauge

                       P_{R_N}(p+q)=0.                   (4)

This is an assumption of the lemma, not a claim that every nearby pair
admits such a gauge while remaining in the class(2).

2. EXACT FINITE-CHANGE IDENTITY AND ROUTING

Let G_e=T_-1/3(1_Ci-1_Cj). Expanding the bilinear objective exactly gives

 J_-1/3(P,Q)-J_-1/3(C,C)=L(u)+L(v)+C(u,v),                 (5)

where
 L(u)=sum_e int d lambda_e,N(x) int_0^{u_e(x)}

and C(u,v) is the signed double-slab interaction, with incidence factor
b_e.b_f and Mehler kernel k_N(x+t n_e,y+s n_f). Integrals from0 to a

cross term in(5), whereas the Hessian has twice the mixed boundary form.

The independently checked routing theorem in

Negative heights follow by reflection. This checks the routing sign and

3. GLOBAL INTERACTION REMAINDER

Let r=|x0|, s=|y0|. For any |t|,|w|<=epsilon, direct substitution into the
three-dimensional kernel gives

 log[k_3(x0+t n_e,y0+w n_f)/k_3(x0,y0)]
 =-(t^2+w^2)/16
   -(3/8)[t n_e.y0+w x0.n_f+tw n_e.n_f].                  (7)

The same ratio holds in dimension N, because the extra-coordinate kernel
is unchanged by these normal displacements. Therefore, if epsilon<=1,

 |log ratio| <=(3epsilon/8)(r+s)+epsilon^2/2
              <=epsilon W(r,s),
 W(r,s)=3(r+s)/8+1/2,
 |ratio-1| <=epsilon W(r,s) exp(W(r,s)).                   (8)

Define C_3^# on H_3 to be the operator whose unitarily normalized kernel
(after the map f_e -> sqrt(d_e)f_e into direct_sum L^2(d lambda_e)) is

This is a pointwise nonnegative kernel. The superscript# does NOT mean
spectral absolute value. Squaring the kernel uses the same incidence

4. NONLINEAR CONCLUSION IN THE EXACT GAUGE

Let p_+=(p+q)/2 and p_-=(p-q)/2. Conditions(3)-(4) give M_Np_-=0 and
P_{R_N}p_+=0. The accepted operator theorem supplies

 .5[D(p)+D(q)]+K_N(p,q)
 =<p_+,(I+A_N)p_+>_D+<p_-,(I-A_N)p_->_D
 >=(3/8)D(p_+)+(8/25)D(p_-)
 >=(4/25)[D(p)+D(q)].                                    (13)

Combining(5),(6),(12),(13), for every epsilon<=1,

 J_-1/3(P,Q)-J_-1/3(C,C)
 >=[4/25-2^17 epsilon][D(p)+D(q)].                        (14)

The threshold(1) makes the coefficient at least2/25. The deficit is
strictly positive unless both graphs vanish almost everywhere. Reflection
of the second partition gives the corresponding positive-correlation
statement at(C,-C), preserving matching masses.

This proves a genuine finite-change inequality for measurable graphs in
all finite dimensions. It assumes the exact rotation gauge and the
junction taper(2); it does not prove those properties for an arbitrary
nearby or globally competitive measurable pair. Establishing such a
reduction remains a separate task.
