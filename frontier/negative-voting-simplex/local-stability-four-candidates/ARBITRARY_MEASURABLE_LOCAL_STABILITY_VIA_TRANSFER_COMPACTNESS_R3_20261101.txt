THEOREM TO AUDIT
Let C be the regular tetrahedral partition in R3 and rho=-1/3. There
exists delta0>0 such that every pair of measurable four-way partitions
P,Q with exactly matching Gaussian label masses and orbit distance

 d_orb(P,Q)=inf_(O in O(3)) max{sum_i gamma(Pi triangle OCi),
                               sum_i gamma(Qi triangle OCi)}<delta0

satisfies
 J(P,Q)-J(C,C)>=d_orb(P,Q)^2/1000.                       (T)
Here J(P,Q)=sum_i<1_Pi,T_rho1_Qi>. The common masses are unrestricted.
Equality at the reference value therefore requires a common orthogonal
image of(C,C), up to null sets. Reflect Q for the correlation+1/3 version
near(C,-C).

This is local Gaussian-L1 stability for arbitrary measurable rules in R3.
It assumes the accepted computational all-speed Hessian certificate and
its documented arithmetic premises. It does not assert a numerical
radius delta0 or global optimality. The proof below does NOT assume
stationarity, normal-graph regularity, local minimality, or global
minimality of P,Q. The transfer compactness argument replaces that step.

1. FIXED REFERENCE DATA AND EXACT ROTATION GAUGE
Use the accepted notation: facet Sigma_e, unit normal n_e from C_i to
C_j, incidence b_e=e_i-e_j, Gaussian surface measure lambda_e,
 G_i=T_rho1_Ci,
 d_e=partial_n(G_i-G_j)>0 on its tie plane,
 D(p)=sum_e int d_e p_e^2 d lambda_e,
 K(p,q)=<p,Aq>_D.
The accepted operator has spectrum in[-1,3], common gap3/8 off rotations,
and contrast gap8/25 on ker M. Also
 V0=int_(Sigma_e)d_e^(-1)d lambda_e<1,
so sum_e int d_e^(-1)d lambda_e<6.

It is enough to prove the theorem along every sequence approaching the
common orthogonal orbit; uniform local constants then follow by the
sequential characterization of a neighborhood. First align to a closest
reference orientation. The finite-dimensional rotation-gauge lemma applies
to ANY sufficiently close measurable pair: minimize locally
 sum_i int(P_i+Q_i)T_rho1_(O C_i)dgamma over O near identity.
Its reference rotational Hessian is2D(r_Omega)>0, and its first two
derivatives are C2-close by Gaussian Cauchy--Schwarz. After the additional
common rotation, P_n,Q_n tend to C in Gaussian L1 and obey EXACTLY

 sum_i int(P_ni+Q_ni-2C_i)g_Omega,i dgamma=0,
 g_Omega,i=-(Omega x).grad G_i.                          (1)

The post-alignment distance epsilon_n tends to0 and is at least d_orb.
All subsequent quantities refer to this aligned sequence. There is no
cutoff of the partitions and therefore no discarded objective tail.

2. ROUTING LOSS AND A GLOBAL LOWER BOUND FOR TRANSFER COST
For an ordered transfer i->j, on C_i define
 w_ij(z)=G_j(z)-G_i(z)>0, except on a null tie set.
The strict inequality follows from the Gaussian utility ordering, or
the reflection formula below. Put
 L_P=sum_(i!=j)int_(Ci intersect Pj) w_ij dgamma,
 L_Q similarly, L=L_P+L_Q.                               (2)
These are exactly the two reference-routing terms in the bilinear
expansion. They are nonnegative, tend to0 under L1 convergence, and
L=0 precisely when P=Q=C almost everywhere.

For e=(i,j), write z=y+t n_e, y perpendicular to n_e. The positive
extension from the derivative addendum is
 D_e(z)=(3/4)int_Ci(-n_e.u)phi_sigma(u+z/3)du,
 sigma^2=8/9, D_e(y)=d_e(y) on the entire tie plane.
Restrict the integral to a fixed angular subcone in the interior of C_i
and radius(1+|z|)^(-1). The factor(-n.u) has degree1, so the volume
integral is bounded below by a fixed positive constant times
(1+|z|)^(-4). The other exponent terms are bounded on that subcone.
Consequently, for all z,
 D_e(z)>=c exp(-|z|^2/16)/(1+|z|)^4.                      (3)

Reflection and sinh(a)>=a for a>=0 give
 |G_i(y+tn)-G_j(y+tn)|>=D_e(y)|t|exp(-t^2/16).
Thus on the source cell C_i,
 w_ij(z)>=c |t| exp(-|z|^2/16)/(1+|z|)^4.                (4)
This bound also holds when the projection y is outside the actual facet.
That point is essential for the bad-transfer analysis.

3. GOOD AND BAD TRANSFERS
Fix once and for all
                         delta=2^(-24).                 (5)
A transfer between labels i,j is GOOD if |t|<delta and its orthogonal
projection y belongs to Sigma_ij. All other transfers are BAD. The
decomposition is made separately for P and Q. No disjoint-tube assumption
is required: an actual changed point has only one source and one target.

The bad source region B_ij is fixed independently of the sequence:
 B_ij={z in Ci: |t_ij|>=delta or P_ij z notin Sigma_ij}.
For every a>0 and finite exponent m,

 int_(B_ij) (1+|z|)^m exp(-a|z|^2)/|t_ij(z)| dz<infinity. (6)

Proof near the apparent singularity. The far part has1/|t|<=1/delta.
If projection is ineligible, some k outside{i,j} has
 r_k>(r_i+r_j)/2, where r_l=Y_l.z and r_i is maximal. In normalized
gap coordinates u=(r_i-r_j)/sqrt2=|t_ij| and
v=(r_i-r_k)/sqrt2, this gives0<=v<u/2. The two unit comparison normals
have inner product1/2. In orthonormal transverse coordinates the allowed
width is u/sqrt3. It cancels the1/u singularity. The remaining triple-line
coordinate has a Gaussian integral. A finite union over k proves(6).

4. BATHTUB COMPRESSION OF EACH GOOD NORMAL FIBER
On a fixed facet base y, let E_+(y) be the measurable set of t in(0,delta)
where the actual transfer is j->i, and E_-(y) the absolute negative
normal coordinates where the transfer is i->j. Define
 a_+=int_(E_+)exp(-t^2/2)dt,
 a_-=int_(E_-)exp(-t^2/2)dt,
 p=a_+-a_-, a=a_++a_-.                                  (7)
Use p,a for P and q,b for Q. These functions are measurable; the normal
Jacobian is exactly d lambda_e(y) exp(-t^2/2)dt.

For0<t<delta the reference cost G_i(y+tn)-G_j(y+tn) is increasing. One
direct proof differentiates its positive reflection integral:
 alpha cosh(alpha t)-(t/8)sinh(alpha t)
 >=alpha cosh(alpha t)(1-t^2/8)>0.
For a prescribed Gaussian normal mass a_+ or a_-, the least routing cost
is therefore the initial interval from0 having that mass. The exact
all-height routing inequality then gives cost at least d_e a_+^2/2 or
d_e a_-^2/2. This yields

 D(p)<=2L_P, D(q)<=2L_Q,
 D(a)<=4L_P, D(b)<=4L_Q.                                (8)

The first inequalities use(a_+-a_-)^2<=a_+^2+a_-^2; the second use
(a_++a_-)^2<=2(a_+^2+a_-^2). The exact good mass changes are Mp and Mq.
This compression permits arbitrary measurable subsets in each fiber.

5. COMPACT OPERATORS REMOVE ALL BAD INTERACTIONS
For each bad transfer write its indicator as A_ij. Its normalized function
 F_ij=A_ij sqrt(w_ij)/sqrt(L) belongs to L2(gamma), with squared norm
equal to its fraction of the total routing loss. The finite vector F is
bounded in L2. Its support has Gaussian measure tending to0, because the
partitions tend to C. Thus F converges WEAKLY to0: against any L2 test,
Cauchy--Schwarz on that shrinking support proves the assertion.

The bad-bad normalized kernel is, up to fixed incidence constants,
 k_rho(x,y)/sqrt(w_ij(x)w_kl(y)), on B_ij times B_kl.
Using(4), its square with the Gaussian volume densities is bounded by
 C(1+|x|)^4(1+|y|)^4 exp[-3(|x|^2+|y|^2)/16]
                     /(|t_ij(x)||t_kl(y)|).              (9)
Indeed the joint quadratic exponent before bounding x.y is
-9(|x|^2+|y|^2)/16-3x.y/4. Equation(6) makes(9) integrable.
The corresponding operator is Hilbert--Schmidt, hence compact.

The mixed bad-boundary kernel
 k_rho(x,y)/sqrt(w_ij(x)d_e(y)), y in Sigma_e,
is Hilbert--Schmidt by the same calculation and the accepted global
facet lower bound d_e(y)>=c exp(-|y|^2/16)/(1+|y|)^2.
Actual good points are y+s n_e with |s|<=delta. Dominate their kernels by
the positive shifted envelope exp[C(1+|x|+|y|)]k_rho(x,y).
Its normalized square remains integrable: linear exponential factors
do not defeat the Gaussian exponent in(9) or the integrable1/|t| factor.

The normalized absolute good masses a/sqrt(L),b/sqrt(L) are bounded in
the boundary D space by(8). Compactness applied to F weakly tending to0
therefore gives, for ACTUAL signed measures,
 bad-bad interaction=o(L),
 bad-good and good-bad interactions=o(L).                (10)
This argument uses a positive dominating kernel; it does not assume
incidence cancellation, normal-graph regularity, or convergence of the
compressed good masses.

7. MASS AND ROTATION DEFECTS ARE o(sqrt(L))
For mass,1_(B_ij)/sqrt(w_ij) belongs to L2(gamma), by(4),(6). Testing
the weakly zero F against it gives bad label masses=o(sqrt(L)). Exact
matching of the actual masses therefore implies
                         M(p-q)=o(sqrt(L)).              (12)

For rotation, the bad test(g_Omega,j-g_Omega,i)/sqrt(w_ij) is also L2
on B_ij. Gaussian convolution bounds |grad G_i| by a fixed constant,
so the numerator is at most C|x|. Equations(4),(6) again apply. Bad
rotation moments are consequently o(sqrt(L)).

The good rotation Taylor remainder needs a separate argument; a crude
uniform C delta bound is not substituted for convergence. Define the
nonnegative first normal moment on each good fiber by
 c_e(y)=int_(E_+) t exp(-t^2/2)dt
                 +int_(E_-) t exp(-t^2/2)dt.
The two integrals are ADDED: E_- uses absolute negative coordinates,
so the two positive-coordinate sets can overlap and must retain their
separate multiplicities.
Then c_e<=delta a_e and
 D(c)<=4delta^2 L,
 sum_e int d_e c_e d lambda_e<=exp(delta^2/16)L.          (13)
The second inequality is the reflection lower bound for the routing cost.
Thus c/sqrt(L) is bounded in the D Hilbert space and tends weakly to0:
its pairings against compact bounded tests tend to0 by the second bound,
and such tests are dense.

The extended-diagonal identity and its global log-curvature imply, for
all y on a facet and |t|<=1,
 |partial_n(g_Omega,i-g_Omega,j)(y+tn)|<=d_e(y)F(|y|),

To verify the global bound, compare D_e(y+utn) to d_e(y) using
|grad log D_e(z)|<=C+|z|/8 in the exact positive integral representation,
then differentiate twice. No condition r|t|<<1 is needed for(14).
The function F belongs to the boundary D space: d_e is bounded on its
facet and Gaussian surface measure integrates polynomial times linear
exponentials.

The absolute good Taylor remainder divided bysqrt(L) is bounded by
<c/sqrt(L),F>_D, which tends to0. On the facet the exact test difference

                     D(p+q,r_Omega)=o(sqrt(L)).          (15)
The three rotation representers have a fixed positive Gram matrix.

8. APPLY THE CERTIFIED QUADRATIC FORM WITH THESE DEFECTS
Set E=D(p)+D(q)<=2L, a0=(p+q)/2, b0=(p-q)/2. Let
r=P_rot a0 and g=P_G b0. The fixed mass Gram and(12) give
||g||_D=o(sqrt(L)); the rotation Gram and(15) give the same bound for r.
The robust quadratic estimate is

                   -2sqrt2||g||sqrt(E)-(58/25)||g||^2
             >=(4/25)E-o(L).                            (16)
Notice that the defects are measured relative tosqrt(L), notsqrt(E).
This is sufficient because E<=2L, even if cancellations make E very small.

The bilinear expansion around C is exact. Its routing term is L.
Using(10),(11),(16),
 J(P,Q)-S>=L-E/2+(4/25)E-L/32-o(L)
          >=(8/25-1/32)L-o(L).
Since8/25-1/32=.28875>1/4, along every aligned nontrivial sequence,
                         J(P,Q)-S>=L/4                  (17)
eventually. There is no unestimated residual routing loss; the use of
E<=2L retains it with its favorable sign.

9. ROUTING LOSS DOMINATES THE L1 DISTANCE
The actual good label-L1 distance for one rule is exactly

The bad label-L1 distance is o(sqrt(L)), by the mass-test argument in
