QUANTITATIVE COMPACT STRAIGHTENING OF NEAR-TETRAHEDRAL PAIRED MINIMIZERS
2026-11-01. Successor note; existing theorem and certificate files preserved.

RESULT AND LIMIT
This note proves a compact regularity lemma, including the triple junctions
and the four-way vertex. A sufficiently close matched-mass paired global
minimizer in R^3 is, on every fixed smaller ball, an analytic diffeomorphic
deformation of the entire regular tetrahedral partition. Explicit Gaussian
measure-to-C1 constants and a bound on the common-mass multipliers are given.
This is stronger than regularity only away from junctions. It does NOT yet
prove that every sufficiently close global minimizer is regular: a uniform
tail/nonlinear argument is still missing. The certified Hessian gap alone
does not supply that argument.

1. SETUP AND A SPECIFIC WEIGHTED MEASURE METRIC
Use the tetrahedral rows Y_i=(+/-1,+/-1,+/-1)/2 with positive sign product,
Y^T Y=I_3, and C_i={Y_i.x=max_j Y_j.x}. Put rho=-1/3.
Reflect the second positive-correlation rule, so the reference pair is(C,C)
and the objective is sum_i <1_Ai,T_rho1_Hi>. Assume gamma(A_i)=gamma(H_i).

For two labelled pairs define the Gaussian-weighted measure distance
 d_gamma((A,H),(C,C))
   =max{sum_i gamma(A_i symmetric_difference C_i),
         sum_i gamma(H_i symmetric_difference C_i)} = epsilon.          (1)
A common rotation may first be chosen to align the reference. The lemma
uses the resulting value of (1); it does not demand a preferred orientation.
All claims below concern R^3. A lift to arbitrary-dimensional minimizers
would separately require the applicable paired dimension-reduction theorem
and control of alignment of their active three-dimensional subspace.

2. ROUTING CONDITIONS WITH OPPOSITE MULTIPLIERS
A global paired minimizer admits a vector lambda in R^4, sum_i lambda_i=0,
such that, almost everywhere,
 A_i={x: U_i(x)+lambda_i=min_j(U_j(x)+lambda_j)},
 H_i={x: V_i(x)-lambda_i=min_j(V_j(x)-lambda_j)},           (2)
with possible measurable tie-breaking, where
 U_i=T_rho1_Hi, V_i=T_rho1_Ai.

Justification of the coupled sign and a single multiplier vector:
first regard assignments as simplex-valued. Pure and relaxed infima agree:
with one rule fixed, finite-dimensional nonatomic vector-measure splitting
purifies the other rule while preserving its label masses and the current
linear objective; then purify the second rule. A pure global minimizer is
therefore also a relaxed global minimizer. The derivative along every
feasible segment is nonnegative, so(A,H) minimizes the linearized objective
sum_i int f_i U_i+g_i V_i subject to Ef=Eg. The set of attainable mass
differences and linear objectives is convex and compact. Its mass-difference
projection contains a neighborhood of0 in1-perp, using constant assignments
near(1/4,...,1/4). A supporting hyperplane at the minimum therefore has a
finite mass multiplier lambda. Pointwise minimization gives (2), with
opposite signs because the constraint is Ef-Eg=0. This argument does not
incorrectly assume that the bilinear objective itself is convex.

The associated dual objective is
 int min_i(U_i+lambda_i)+int min_i(V_i-lambda_i).
Since0<=U_i,V_i<=1, it is at most2-(max lambda-min lambda), which also
shows coercivity in the normalized multiplier space. No arbitrary separate
constants for the two partitions are imported into (2).

For completeness, the purification step needs only a finite-constraint
extreme-point argument. The simplex-valued rules with specified label
masses and specified current linear objective form a nonempty weakly
compact convex subset of L2. If an extreme point had two coordinates
bounded away from0 on a positive-measure set, split that set into enough
positive-measure pieces. A nonzero linear combination of their signed
two-label transfers annihilates the finitely many constraints. Scaling
it gives two distinct feasible rules averaging to the original, a
contradiction. Hence an extreme point is pure. This avoids any claim
that a general bilinear minimum must itself occur at an extreme point.

3. EXPLICIT SMOOTHING BOUNDS
Let K_rho(x,y) denote the kernel relative to Gaussian measure. Direct
Gaussian integration gives, for |x|<=R,
 ||K_rho(x,.)||_L2(gamma)<=C_R,
 C_R=(81/80)^(3/4) exp(R^2/20).                           (3)
Under the probability measure proportional to K_rho(x,y)^2 gamma(dy),
y has mean-3x/5 and covariance(4/5)I. The first kernel derivative is
(-3y/8-x/8)K, whose multiplier has mean x/10 and covariance(9/80)I.
Consequently, for unit directions a,b,
 ||partial_a K||_2<=C_R B1_R,
 ||partial_a partial_b K||_2<=C_R B2_R,
 B1_R=sqrt(R^2/100+9/80),
 B2_R=(R/10+3^(1/4)*3/sqrt80)^2+1/8.                    (4)
The second inequality follows from the fourth Gaussian moment and
partial_ab K=[Z_a Z_b-(a.b)/8]K, with Z=-3y/8-x/8.

For F_P(x)=-Y^T(T_rho1_Pi(x))_i and F0=F_C, (1),(3),(4) imply
 sup_(B_R)|F_P-F0|<=C_R sqrt(epsilon),
 sup_(B_R)||D F_P-D F0||<=C_R B1_R sqrt(epsilon).          (5)
Indeed sum_i||1_Pi-1_Ci||_2^2<=epsilon and ||Y^T||op=1.
Also ||D^2 F0||<=L_R:=C_R B2_R on B_R. These estimates follow from
Gaussian kernel integrals; they do not presume any boundary regularity of P.
The smoothed potentials are real analytic.

4. A QUANTITATIVE MULTIPLIER BOUND FROM OCCUPIED THIN PRISMS
For every pair i,j, the point x_ij=Y_i+Y_j is the unit facet bisector.
Take the radius1/4 tangential disk about x_ij, and normal thickness
t in[-a,-a/2] for C_i or[a/2,a] for C_j, with0<a<=1/4.
These prisms lie respectively inside C_i,C_j, involve no other label,
and are contained in B_(3/2). Each has Gaussian measure at least c*a, where
                 c=exp(-9/8)/(64 sqrt(2pi)).              (6)
This uses tangential area pi/16, thickness a/2, and the minimum Gaussian
density on B_(3/2). The reference score gaps to the other two labels
remain at least1/2 throughout each prism.

Assume0<epsilon<c/8 and choose a=2epsilon/c<1/4. Each prism has measure
at least2epsilon, so it contains a positive-measure portion of the
corresponding A_i or A_j. Apply its routing inequality in (2).
The reference potential difference vanishes on the tie plane and has
gradient norm at most D_*=1/(2sqrt(pi)), by the Gaussian derivative
formula for a function taking values in[-1,1]. Its size in these prisms
is at most D_*a. The perturbed difference differs by at most
C_(3/2)sqrt(2epsilon), from (3). Using both sides gives, for every i,j,

 |lambda_i-lambda_j|<=Delta(epsilon),
 Delta(epsilon)=epsilon/(c sqrt(pi))+C_(3/2)sqrt(2epsilon). (7)

With sum lambda_i=0, this implies ||lambda||_2<=2Delta(epsilon).
The epsilon=0 case follows by a limit or directly from (2).
Thus the free common masses do not leave an uncontrolled order-one
multiplier in the compact perturbation problem.

5. A GLOBAL REFERENCE PROBABILITY MAP WITH POSITIVE DERIVATIVE
Let c_*=1/(2sqrt2) and a_i(x)=-c_*Y_i.x. The vector
G(x)=(T_rho1_Ci(x))_i is the choice-probability vector for independent
standard Gaussian utilities Z_i+a_i(x). The common Gaussian component
in the score representation cancels from all comparisons.

The derivative of the choice probabilities with respect to their means
is the Laplacian W(a) of the complete four-vertex graph. Its edge weights are
 w_ij(a)=int phi(t-a_i)phi(t-a_j) product_(k!=i,j)Phi(t-a_k)dt>0.
The diagonal entries are sums of incident weights and the off-diagonal
entries are -w_ij. Therefore
                  D F0(x)=c_* Y^T W(a(x))Y.              (8)
This matrix is symmetric positive definite at every finite x.

For an explicit lower bound on B_R, let A_R=sqrt(3/32)*R. On[-1,1],
each Gaussian product is at least(2pi)^(-1)exp[-(1+A_R)^2], and
Phi(-1-A_R)>=phi(2+A_R). Thus
 w_ij>=omega_R=(1/(2pi^2))exp[-(1+A_R)^2-(2+A_R)^2].
Since sum_(i<j)((Y_i-Y_j).v)^2=4|v|^2, (8) gives
                  D F0>=ell_R I,
                  ell_R=sqrt2*omega_R>0 on B_R.          (9)
The constants are intentionally coarse but fully explicit.

The map F0 preserves the labels of C. To see this, hold all other means
fixed and vary a_i-a_j symmetrically about its midpoint. The derivative
of G_i-G_j is one half of(e_i-e_j)^T W(e_i-e_j), strictly positive;
it vanishes when a_i=a_j by exchange symmetry. Hence G_i-G_j has the
sign of a_i-a_j, opposite to(Y_i-Y_j).x. Finally
 Y_i.F0=-G_i+1/4. Therefore F0 maps each C_i, and each of its labelled
faces and junctions, into the same labelled cone arrangement.

6. COMPACT STRAIGHTENING LEMMA, INCLUDING EVERY JUNCTION
Fix R>0 and0<r<R/2. Define
 eta0=C_R sqrt(epsilon)+2Delta(epsilon),
 eta1=C_R B1_R sqrt(epsilon).
Assume
 epsilon<c/8, eta1<ell_R/2, eta0<ell_R*r/2.               (10)
Set
 F_A=-Y^T(U+lambda), F_H=-Y^T(V-lambda).
Each map differs from F0 by at most eta0 in value and eta1 in derivative
on B_R. Its derivative's symmetric part is at least ell_R/2, so it is
strongly monotone and injective on that ball, with invertible derivative.

For x in B_(R-r), the equation F_A(z)=F0(x) has a unique solution z in B_R.
Existence follows by degree: on the boundary of B_R, for every homotopy
F_t=F0+t(F_A-F0),
 (F_t(z)-F0(x)).(z-x)>=ell_R|z-x|^2-eta0|z-x|>0.
The target is never met on the boundary, and the reference map has degree1
there. Strong monotonicity gives uniqueness. Define
             Psi_A=F_A^(-1) composed_with F0,
and similarly Psi_H. They are analytic diffeomorphisms onto their images.
Equations (2) and the label-preservation property of F0 give, up to null sets,
 Psi_A(C_i intersect B_(R-r))=A_i intersect Psi_A(B_(R-r)),
 Psi_H(C_i intersect B_(R-r))=H_i intersect Psi_H(B_(R-r)). (11)
One can take canonical closed routing cells in the images; the tie strata
have measure zero because the potential map is a local diffeomorphism.

The quantitative bounds are
 ||Psi_A-id||_infinity, ||Psi_H-id||_infinity <=eta0/ell_R,
 ||D Psi_A-I||, ||D Psi_H-I||
        <=(2/ell_R)[eta1+L_R*eta0/ell_R].                 (12)
For the displacement bound use strong monotonicity of F0 and
F_A(Psi_A(x))=F0(x). For the derivative bound differentiate this identity,
use ||D F_A^(-1)||<=2/ell_R, and use the Lipschitz bound L_R for D F0.
The images contain B_(R-2r); the same boundary-degree argument applied to
F0 on B_(R-r) proves this inclusion.

This is the promised compact lemma. It straightens the entire partition,
including all triple lines and its single four-way vertex in the smaller
ball. It does not merely describe regular points of individual interfaces.
If the second bound in (12) is less than1/2, every compact facet patch away
from a junction is, after its tangential reparametrization, a unique normal
graph. The height is bounded by eta0/ell_R and its slope by beta/(1-beta),
where beta is the derivative bound in (12), on the projected patch.
This supplies the input to a compact normal-graph remainder estimate.

An executable check of these deliberately conservative constants is in
certify_compact_straightening_constants_20261101.py. At R=2, r=1/2,
epsilon<=10^-30, Arb verifies (10), displacement<10^-8 and derivative
error<10^-3. More precise enclosed bounds are about7.654e-10 and1.750e-4,
respectively. The straightened images cover B_1. This finite example
certifies the stated constants, not the existence of a close minimizer.

7. WHAT PRIMARY REGULARITY ALREADY PROVIDES
Heilman, Hyperstable Sets with Voting and Algorithmic Hardness Applications,
https://arxiv.org/html/2209.11216v1, Lemmas7.4-7.5, gives local finite surface
area, locally finite smooth boundary decomposition, and potential level-set
conditions for paired minimizers. Theorem7.9 is a paired dimension reduction.
Those statements do not provide a quantitative near-reference normal-graph
radius, a global graph at infinity, or the uniform nonlinear remainder
needed here. Equations (5)-(12) give a direct quantitative compact result
under the stated Gaussian-measure closeness. No positive-maximizer or
hyperstability assumption is used in this derivation.

8. PRECISE REMAINING GAP TO ISOLATION OF GLOBAL MINIMIZERS
The certified Hessian gap applies to the full weighted boundary space.
The radial agent's finite-change estimate applies to bounded normal heights
supported in compact patches away from junctions. Lemma(11) now supplies
compatible analytic deformations across compact junctions for minimizers.
It does not yet give the global remainder estimate needed to combine these
facts. The constants C_R grow and ell_R tends to zero as R grows; no fixed
epsilon in (10) controls the whole space.

Two tasks remain genuinely open in this route:
 (i) a nonlinear estimate for the compatible moving-junction charts, or a
     controlled cutoff reduction to the existing compact patch estimate;
 (ii) a tail/no-concentration lemma for stationary global minimizers that
      makes the remainder negligible relative to the global D-energy.
Small Gaussian symmetric difference alone cannot supply (ii). For example,
swapping two labels only outside a distant ball gives matched pairs with
distance tending to zero and extra far-away interfaces. These examples are
NOT asserted stationary or minimizing; they identify where minimization
must supply additional information rather than being silently replaced
by a global normal-graph assumption.

Accordingly this note does not conclude that a sufficiently close global
minimizer is regular, nor that the global four-label endpoint is solved.
It isolates and proves the compact regularity/junction step needed for
that attempted implication, with explicit hypotheses and estimates.
