EXACT THEOREM
Let C=(C1,...,C4) be the regular tetrahedral conical partition of R3.
Let gamma denote standard Gaussian measure and T=T_(-1/3). For two
measurable partitions A,H with gamma(Ai)=gamma(Hi), write
 J(A,H)=sum_i<1_Ai,T1_Hi>, S=J(C,C),
 d(P,Q)=sum_i gamma(Pi symmetric_difference Qi),
 dist(A,H)=inf_(Q in O(3)) max{d(A,QC),d(H,QC)}.          (1)
The common mass vector is free, not fixed to(1/4,...,1/4).

There exists delta0>0 such that every Gaussian-L1 LOCAL MINIMIZER of J
under the matched-mass constraint, with dist(A,H)<delta0, satisfies

                 J(A,H)>=S+dist(A,H)^2/10000.            (2)

In particular, if such a local minimizer also has J(A,H)<=S, it is a
common orthogonal image of(C,C), up to Gaussian null sets. Every global
minimizer is a local minimizer and has value<=S, so the same isolation
conclusion applies to global minimizers in this neighborhood.

Here local minimizer means that J cannot be lowered by any sufficiently
small Gaussian-L1 change of the two pure rules preserving matching
masses. The size of that individual local neighborhood need not be
uniform. No explicit numerical delta0 is claimed. The coefficient1/10000
is deliberately conservative; the existence proof below supplies it
after decreasing delta0.

Reflecting the second partition gives the equivalent correlation+1/3
statement near(C,-C). This theorem is proved in R3. The accepted Hessian
extends to every finite ambient dimension, but this nonlinear isolation
argument is not silently given the same extension.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT SAY
Equation(2) is asserted for local minimizers, not for every nearby
measurable competitor. It does not prove that C is a Gaussian-L1 local
minimum among arbitrary partitions. It also does not prove that any
global minimizer lies in the stated neighborhood. Those are separate
selection/regularity and global-closeness problems.

A concrete extension toward arbitrary near competitors is a selection
principle: from a hypothetical sequence with J<S and dist->0, produce
competitive Gaussian-L1 local minimizers also approaching the orbit.
That implication has NOT been established. Minimizing inside a small L1
ball can put the minimizer on its boundary and introduce an additional
distance multiplier, so it cannot simply reuse(4).

Two fixed-mass best responses give a possible smoothing start: each
replacement lowers J and preserves common masses. Against a rule tending
to C, any such response also tends to C, by the strictly positive
reference routing gap away from the tie set. But two replacements do
not make the pair mutually optimal, and repeated responses need not stay
in a quantitatively controlled shrinking neighborhood. Nor do two steps
alone supply the growing-core/tail rate relative to their final distance.
Those are precise remaining requirements for this route, not assumed
consequences of the present theorem.
