STATUS AND PRECISE TARGET

This note supplies a concrete cap decomposition for compact C1-small
ambient deformations of the regular tetrahedral partition. It includes
moving triple junctions and the moving vertex. Its estimates use only
C1 bounds. In particular, no uncontrolled second derivative of a choice-
map straightening diffeomorphism is assumed.

Throughout C denotes a finite universal constant for the fixed regular
tetrahedron; it can increase between displayed estimates. No C depends
on R, on a deformation, or on its normal energy. The proof gives a
polynomial-in-R rate, rather than a numerical sharp basin radius. A
single numerical upper bound for all occurrences of C has not been
collected or certified in this source. That distinction is intentional.

The conclusion to audit is the following. Let P=Phi(C), Q=Psi(C), where
Phi=id+w and Psi=id+v are global C1 diffeomorphisms equal to identity
outside B_R, R>=1. Suppose

 max(||w||infinity,||Dw||op,infinity,
     ||v||infinity,||Dv||op,infinity)<=eta,
 eta(R+1)^2 sufficiently small.

Suppose the actual partitions have exactly matching label masses.
Let zeta be the norm of their reference-subtracted bulk rotation-gauge
defect, with an orthonormal basis of the three skew matrices. Then the
formal exact swept-mass heights p,q defined below have E=D(p)+D(q), and

 J(P,Q)-J(C,C)
 >=[4/25-C(R+1)^2 sqrt(eta)] E-C zeta^2.                  (T)

After decreasing the universal smallness threshold, each actual summed-
label Gaussian L1 distance from C is at most6 sqrt(E). Thus, when the
maximum of those distances is at least epsilon/2,

                         E>=epsilon^2/144.              (L)

The accepted all-speed Hessian is an input. Equation(T) is a finite-
change statement, proved by controlling actual caps and signed cross
terms. It is not obtained merely by asserting positivity of a Hessian.
The independent compact near-minimizer reduction and rotation-gauge
notes are separate inputs to any L1-isolation application.

1. ENTIRE TIE PLANES HAVE UNAMBIGUOUS NORMAL GRAPHS

Use sites Y_i=(+,+,+),(+,-,-),(-,+,-),(-,-,+), divided by2.
For e=(i,j), n_e=(Y_j-Y_i)/sqrt2 points from C_i to C_j;
Pi_e=n_e perpendicular, and Sigma_e is the actual reference facet.
Let P_e be orthogonal projection to Pi_e.

For a single deformation Phi=id+w, define on the ENTIRE plane Pi_e

 F_e(x)=x+P_e w(x),
 h_e(y)=n_e.w(F_e^(-1)(y)).                              (1.1)

If eta<1/2, F_e is globally bi-Lipschitz, with inverse Lipschitz constant
at most(1-eta)^(-1). Indeed F_e-id has Lipschitz constant eta; surjectivity
also follows by the contraction equation x=y-P_e w(x). Consequently

 ||h_e||infinity<=eta, Lip(h_e)<=eta/(1-eta)<=2eta.        (1.2)

The image of the entire tie plane is precisely
{y+h_e(y)n_e:y in Pi_e}. Orientation is preserved: for every z,
n_e.Phi^(-1)(z) and n_e.z-h_e(P_e z) have the same sign. To see this,
along each normal line the first quantity has positive derivative:
n_e.DPhi^(-1)n_e>=1-eta/(1-eta)>0 for eta<1/2 sufficiently small.
Its unique zero is the displayed graph. Therefore the actual image cells
are exactly intersections of these deformed comparison halfspaces.

Only h_e restricted to the ORIGINAL facet Sigma_e is used as a formal
slab height. The entire-plane construction supplies its extension and
its Lipschitz constant. The restriction is supported in B_R: Phi is the
identity off B_R, so its tangential plane map is also the identity there.
Set p_e=psi(h_e), psi(t)=int_0^t exp(-s^2/2)ds.

Let S_e(z) be the oriented signed indicator of the slab from t=0 to
t=h_e(y), y in Sigma_e. Define the R4-valued formal sum

               ftilde=1_C+sum_e b_e S_e, b_e=e_i-e_j.    (1.3)

This formal sum may overlap or take values outside{0,1}. It is used only
as a bounded vector function in exact bilinear identities. Let
f=1_P and c=f-ftilde be its cap defect. Its label sum is zero, pointwise.
There are at most three eligible slabs at any point, so a harmless bound
||c(z)||_1<=14 suffices. Define gtilde,q,k similarly for Q.

2. SCORE-GAP PROOF OF CAP LOCALIZATION

Write r_i=Y_i.z for the reference scores and a_i=Y_i.Phi^(-1)(z) for
the actual scores. Ignore tie sets, which have Gaussian measure zero.
Let i be the reference winner.

Projection P_(ij) averages r_i,r_j and leaves every other r_k unchanged,
because Y_k.(Y_i-Y_j)=0 for k outside{i,j}. Thus

 P_(ij)z in Sigma_(ij)
 iff (r_i+r_j)/2>=max_(k outside{i,j}) r_k.               (2.1)

Every eligible formal slab at z includes the reference winner i. Its
sign is a transfer i->j exactly when a_j>a_i. Hence

 ftilde(z)=e_i+sum_(j: (2.1) and a_j>a_i)(e_j-e_i).       (2.2)

If this differs from the actual winner e_l, one of two things occurs:

(a) The actual winner l has an ineligible projection in(2.1). Then for
some k, r_k>(r_i+r_l)/2, while a_l>a_i. Thus i,k,l have reference scores
within r_i-r_l of one another, and this difference is bounded by
sqrt2 |h_(il)(P_(il)z)| because the i,l comparison has crossed.

(b) There are at least two eligible flips i->j and i->k. Both crossed
comparisons bound the three reference score differences by sqrt2 times
the largest of their crossing heights.

This also covers the case where l is eligible but an extra formal flip
is present. If the actual winner remains i, no formal flip is possible.

For three labels, their two independent unit comparison normals have
Gram eigenvalues1/2 and3/2. Consequently a bound b on the two normalized
score gaps implies distance at most2b to their FULL triple line. Write
z=s ell_j+z_perp, |z_perp|=t, where ell_j is the unit triple-ray direction
on which those three labels beat the fourth. On that line, all relevant
tie planes contain s ell_j. Define

 H_j(s)=max_(e incident to ray j)|h_e(s ell_j)|, s>=0,
 h0=max_e |h_e(0)|.                                    (2.3)

For s>=0, the Lipschitz estimate gives b<=H_j(s)+2eta t.
Absorbing the second term for eta<=1/16 gives

                           t<=4 H_j(s).                 (2.4)

If s<0, the fourth label beats the tied triple along the line. Since i
was a reference winner, its transverse score correction must overcome
that gap. The unit-ray normalized gap slope is sqrt(2/3), so |s|<=2t.
Thus |z|<=3t<=6b. The entire-plane Lipschitz bound gives
b<=h0+2eta|z|, hence |z|<=12h0 for eta<=1/24.

In particular, using the fixed generous constant A=4096, the entire cap
defect lies in the union of

  the vertex ball |z|<=A h0,
  the four triple-ray tubes t<=A H_j(s), s>=0.           (2.5)

For the portions of a tube outside the vertex ball, the fourth-label
gap is separated quantitatively. Retain the narrow bound(2.4), so
t<=4h0+8eta s. If |z|>A h0, eta<=2^(-20), then s>=|z|/2; otherwise
t>sqrt(3)|z|/2 contradicts t<=4|z|/A+4eta|z|. Consequently
t/s<=8/A+8eta<1/100. The normalized reference gap from each tied-triple
label to the fourth is at least sqrt(2/3)s-t/sqrt3>4s/5.
On the other hand every comparison height, including those involving
the fourth label, is at most h0+2eta|z|<=2s/A+4eta s<s/100.
No fourth-label comparison can cross. Thus both the actual label and
all participating formal labels belong to this same triple outside
the vertex ball. Use the narrow tube for this inference; the wider
A H tube is only a convenient cover in the estimates.

3. COVER BY REFERENCE-FACET SHADOWS

In a transverse slice of a separated triple tube, the three actual
reference facet rays make angles120 degrees. Project a point to its
nearest ray; the angle is at most60 degrees, so its ray coordinate is
nonnegative, its normal distance is at most the tube radius, and its
projected ray coordinate is no greater than that radius. The coordinate
s along ell_j is unchanged because every incident n_e is perpendicular
to ell_j. The reference-facet base therefore lies in

 x=s ell_j+tau v_(e,j), s>=0, 0<=tau<=A H_j(s),           (3.1)

where v_(e,j) is the inward unit conormal to the edge within Sigma_e.
The whole quadrant(s,tau)>=0 is contained in Sigma_e, because its sector
opening acos(-1/3) exceeds pi/2. There is no short-collar or vertex-end
loss in this assertion.

For a point in the vertex ball, choose a closest supporting plane of
its containing reference cone. Its orthogonal projection belongs to
the corresponding actual facet: the other normalized gaps fall by
at most half the chosen minimal gap. Both projected radius and normal
distance are at most A h0. Thus vertex defects are also covered by
reference-facet shadows.

Define a nonnegative shadow height on each original facet by summing
the at most two incident edge-shadow heights

 A H_j(s) 1_{0<=tau<=A H_j(s)}

and the vertex-shadow height A h0 1_{|x|<=A h0}. Let this sum be r_e(x).
Every cap point lies in at least one signed-normal shadow |t|<=r_e(x).
The cover can overlap. Only an upper bound is needed.

With eta A(R+1) sufficiently small, all shadows have height below1 and
their Gaussian/diagonal weights differ from the weights at their edge
or vertex by at most a fixed factor. The established bound

 |grad log(d_e lambda-density)|<=C0+(9/8)|x|             (3.2)

justifies this comparison. Here C0=|grad log d_e(0)| is a fixed constant;
the established curvature bound -I/8<=Hess log d_e<=0 gives(3.2) directly.
Its explicit value is sqrt(2pi/3)/(4acos(-1/3))<1/2, but that numerical
comparison is not needed. On a triple ray the three incident d_e are
equal by permutation symmetry. At the vertex all six are equal.

Let D(h)=sum_e int d_e h_e^2 d lambda_e and
L(h)=sum_e int |h_e|d lambda_e. Direct integration across the shadow
width, followed by the weighted trace estimates, gives

 D(r)<=C sum_j int_ray d_j H_j^3 d lambda_ray
                +C d(0) lambda-density(0) h0^4
      <=C(R+1)eta D(h),                                 (3.3)

 ||c||_(L1(gamma;ell1))
 <=C sum_j int_ray H_j^2 d lambda_ray
                +C lambda-density(0) h0^3
 <=C(R+1)eta L(h).                                      (3.4)

Here lambda_ray means the density(2pi)^(-3/2)exp(-s^2/2)ds,
not normalized one-dimensional Gaussian measure. All factors are thus
consistent with the facet surface measure. H_j^k<=sum_incident |h_e|^k;
h0^k<=sum_e|h_e(0)|^k. Squaring the sum in r costs multiplicity at most3.

For clarity, the edge trace used in(3.3)-(3.4) is the exact half-strip
identity, with f compact and Lipschitz,

 int_edge w|f|^k
 <=[k Lip(f)+sup|grad log w| ||f||infinity]
                       int_facet w|f|^(k-1).            (3.5)

It holds along the full inward orthogonal quadrant and differentiates
the weight. Use k=3 with w=d_e lambda-density and k=2 with w=lambda-
density. At the vertex, |f|>=|f(0)|/2 on the sector disk of radius
|f(0)|/(4eta)<=1/4. Both weights have fixed positive comparison on this
disk, giving h0^4<=C eta^2 D(h) after the displayed weight normalization,
and h0^3<=C eta^2 L(h). These estimates use no D2Phi.

Since exp(-eta^2/2)|h_e|<=|p_e|<=|h_e|, D(h) and D(p) differ by at most
exp(eta^2), and likewise L(h),L(p). The global certified V0<1 gives

 L(p)<=sqrt(6D(p)),
 ||c||_1<=C(R+1)eta sqrt(D(p)).                          (3.6)

All these statements apply separately to the second partition.

4. A GLOBALLY BOUNDED ALL-FACET POSITIVE KERNEL

The cap defect may move labels not represented by the facet on which
its shadow is placed. Thus using the signed incidence matrix of the
Hessian for cap estimates would be incorrect. Instead use the kernel
with coefficient2 on EVERY ordered pair of the six facets.

The ordered coefficient-square sum is144, three times the value48 in
the accepted compact-sector HS envelope. If |t|,|s|<=1 are normal shifts,

 k(x+tn_e,y+sn_f)<=k(x,y) exp(W(|x|,|y|)),
 W(r,s)=3(r+s)/8+1/2.                                   (4.1)

After division by sqrt(d_e d_f), the square of this positive kernel has
finite HS norm bounded by

 3*432 exp(47/8) J0^2,
 J0=int_0^infinity r(1+r)^2 exp(-3r^2/32)dr<128.         (4.2)

The bound follows from the already proved global envelope and
(3/4)r<=3r^2/32+3/2. With exp(47/8)<404, it is a fixed finite numerical
constant (in particular the resulting norm is below2^18). Crucially it
does not depend on R or on the minimum of d_e in B_R.

The total variation of the cap is bounded by14 times the shadow cover.
The absolute normal mass on a symmetric shadow is2psi(r_e)<=2r_e.
Taking absolute values of label coefficients and using(4.2), therefore,

 |<c,T(gtilde-C)>|<=C sqrt(D(r)D(q)),
 |<k,T(ftilde-C)>|<=C sqrt(D(r_Q)D(p)),
 |<c,T k>|<=C sqrt(D(r)D(r_Q)).                          (4.3)

These are bounds for actual signed cap measures, not point evaluations
of a smoothed potential. By(3.3) their combined size is at most

                         C sqrt((R+1)eta) E.            (4.4)

This is the reason the weaker square-root remainder is sufficient. A
cap-volume estimate alone would not establish(4.4).

5. REFERENCE ROUTING ON THE CAPS

The remaining linear cap terms are <c,T C> and <k,T C>. Their label sums
are zero. In a triple tube only its three incident labels can contribute;
the fourth-label gap is separated outside the vertex ball. Reference
potential differences of those three labels vanish on the triple ray.
They obey, within distance a<=1 of that ray,

 |G_i(z)-G_j(z)|<=C d_j(s) a

when(R+1)a is bounded. The same assertion at the vertex uses d(0).

Here is a relative-derivative justification, avoiding a tiny lower
diagonal. On a tie plane, log d has gradient at most |x|/4+1/2.
Reflection writes the normal potential difference as
2exp(-t^2/16) int_Ci sinh(alpha(y)t)phi_sigma(y+x/3)dy,
alpha=(3/8)(-n.y)>=0, sigma^2=8/9. After division by td(x), the positive
integral is bounded by the tilted moment generating function of alpha.
For its density proportional to alpha phi_sigma on C_i, radial
integration gives 9m^2-3|x|m-32<=0 for m=sqrt(E|y|^2).
The same bound after a tilt uses |x|+|t|. Hence the logarithmic derivative
of that moment generating function is at most C(R+1). This proves the
displayed bound for |t|(R+1) small. It applies on the entire tie plane,
including the small portions outside the original facet.

Combining the extra potential factor a with the cap-volume cover gives
the cubic/quartic expressions in(3.3), rather than quadratic/cubic mass
expressions. Therefore

              |<c,T C>|+|<k,T C>|<=C(R+1)eta E.          (5.1)

No adverse reference-routing term has been omitted.

6. EXACT FORMAL ROUTING AND THE MASS DEFECT

For each formal slab, the all-height routing theorem applies algebraically:

 <ftilde-C,T C> >=D(p)/2,
 <gtilde-C,T C> >=D(q)/2.                                (6.1)

It does not require ftilde or gtilde to be a partition. Likewise the
cross interaction is K(p,q) plus the bounded kernel-shift remainder.
The compact estimate or the global remainder lemma gives

 |<ftilde-C,T(gtilde-C)>-K(p,q)|<=C(R+1)eta E.            (6.2)

The formal mass changes are EXACTLY Mp and Mq. Since the ACTUAL masses
match, their difference is the negative cap-mass difference. By(3.6),

                       |M(p-q)|<=C(R+1)eta sqrt(E).      (6.3)

The Gram of the mass representers is4V0 times identity on1-perpendicular,
with V0 bounded above and below by the accepted certificate. Projection
onto ker M therefore changes p_-=(p-q)/2 in D norm by at most the right
side of(6.3), up to a universal constant. The operator norm of A is3, so

 (D-K)(p_-)>=(8/25)D(p_-)-C(R+1)eta E.                  (6.4)

Thus the exact common-mass condition survives the cap decomposition with
a RELATIVE small error. It is not replaced by balance or by orthogonality
to translation speeds.

7. BULK ROTATION GAUGE VERSUS FORMAL BOUNDARY GAUGE

For a skew Omega, let
 g_Omega,i=-(Omega x).grad G_i,
 r_Omega,e=n_e.Omega x.
The reference facet difference is exactly -d_e r_Omega,e. Normalize Omega
in a fixed Euclidean basis of the three skew matrices, and let zeta be
the norm of the three actual reference-subtracted moments

             sum_i int(P_i+Q_i-2C_i)g_Omega,i dgamma.   (7.1)

Across a formal slab, expansion of this test gives
-D(p+q,r_Omega) plus an error at most C(R+1)^2 E.
Indeed its normal derivative is bounded by C(R+1)^2 d_e near the facet.
At t=0, the term(Omega n).grad(G_i-G_j) is zero, and the remaining
derivative is bounded using grad log d. For the off-plane estimate, the
tilted positive density in Section5 satisfies E|Y|<=C(R+1) and
E|Y|^2<=C(R+1)^2, uniformly for |t|(R+1) bounded. Differentiating its
Gaussian factor tangentially inserts -(3/8)Y_T-x_T/8; differentiating
sinh(alpha t) normally inserts alpha cosh(alpha t). The same tilted
moment bounds therefore give

 |partial_n(G_i-G_j)|<=Cd_e,
 |grad_T partial_n(G_i-G_j)|<=C(R+1)d_e,
 |partial_nn(G_i-G_j)|<=C(R+1)^2 |t|d_e.

For the last estimate use |sinh(alpha t)|<=alpha|t|exp(alpha|t|);
normal differentiation twice also has only lower-order Gaussian terms.
In partial_n[(Omega z).grad(G_i-G_j)], the leading normal gradient
annihilates Omega n, while |Omega z|<=C(R+1). The displayed bounds yield
C(R+1)^2 d_e when |t|(R+1) is small. This supplies the claimed normal
test derivative without any second derivative of the deformation.

8. ASSEMBLY AND THE ENERGY-TO-MEASURE BOUND

Expand J(f,g) exactly around the formal sums. Equations(4.3),(5.1),
(6.1),(6.2) give the formal leading quadratic form plus a relative
O(sqrt((R+1)eta)+(R+1)eta)E error. The leading form is exactly

 .5[D(p)+D(q)]+K(p,q)=(D+K)(p_+)+(D-K)(p_-).

Use(6.4),(7.3), and D(p)+D(q)=2D(p_+)+2D(p_-).
When eta(R+1)^2 is sufficiently small, all remaining terms are bounded
by C(R+1)^2 sqrt(eta)E. This proves the asserted inequality(T), subject
to independent audit of the geometric and relative-potential estimates.

The actual L1 distance is bounded by2L(p)+||c||_1. Equations(3.4)-(3.6)
and V0<1 imply, after shrinking the universal smallness threshold,

 ||P-C||_(L1(gamma;ell1))<=6 sqrt(D(p)),
 ||Q-C||_(L1(gamma;ell1))<=6 sqrt(D(q)).                  (8.1)

This proves(L). No minimum Gaussian density on B_R is used.

9. WHAT THIS WOULD AND WOULD NOT CLOSE

For the independently derived growing compact window,
R^2=(21/5)log(1/epsilon) and eta=epsilon^(11/400-o(1)).
Then every polynomial(R) times sqrt(eta) tends to zero. Compact cutoff
and mass repair cost o(epsilon^2), while(8.1) forces E>=epsilon^2/144
for a nontrivial aligned pair with distance at least epsilon/2. The bulk
rotation-gauge cutoff defect is also o(epsilon^2). Thus an independently
audited(T), combined with those separate reductions, would exclude a
sequence of distinct competitive matched-mass minimizers converging to
the common rotation orbit of(C,C).

This is a LOCAL isolation implication. It does not prove every globally
competitive measurable partition is close to that orbit. The certified
common-mass box alone does not supply such closeness or exclude distant
competitors. No global Standard Simplex conclusion follows here.

The present detailed proof is in R3. A fiber-preserving cylindrical
extension can reuse the geometric/trace argument fiberwise and tensor
the positive kernel with the extra-coordinate OU operator. A claim for
arbitrary ambient dimensions additionally needs the correct mixed-
rotation gauge bookkeeping; it is not silently asserted in this note.

10. AN UNGAUGED FIXED-SUPPORT COROLLARY

There is also a direct conclusion for arbitrary compact deformations,
without assuming a bulk rotation gauge or any minimizer regularity.
Fix finite R>=1. The formal heights are supported in B_R. The established
compact rotation-tail angle from
FINITE_CHANGE_COMPACT_NORMAL_GRAPH_BASIN_20261101.txt gives

 ||(I-P_rot)p_+||_D^2>=beta_R D(p_+),
 beta_R=d0 sqrt(pi) R^3
                  exp[-9(R+1)^2/16-kappa(R+1)]>0,
 d0=acos(-1/3)/(2pi^(3/2)),
 kappa=sqrt(2pi/3)/(4acos(-1/3)).

This concerns the fixed reference facets, so it applies to the formal
heights even though the ACTUAL junctions have moved. Replace Section7
by this angle estimate. With

                  c_R=min(3beta_R/16,4/25)>0,

the preceding cap and approximate-mass arguments give

 J(P,Q)-J(C,C)>=[c_R-C(R+1)^2 sqrt(eta)]E.                (10.1)

For eta small enough depending on R, the right side is at least
(c_R/2)E. By(8.1), E=0 implies P=Q=C almost everywhere. This is strict
local minimality among exactly matched-mass compact C1-small ambient
deformations supported in the fixed ball, including moved triple
junctions and a moved vertex. It requires no rotation alignment.

The dependence of beta_R on R prevents using this ungauged corollary
alone with the growing compact window. The bulk gauge is what removes
that loss in the separate proposed L1-isolation assembly. Neither
conclusion asserts global optimality among arbitrary measurable pairs.
