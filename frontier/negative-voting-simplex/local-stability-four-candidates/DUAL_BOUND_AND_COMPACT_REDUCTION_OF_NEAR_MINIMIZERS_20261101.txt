DUAL BOUND AND COMPACT REDUCTION OF NEAR-TETRAHEDRAL GLOBAL MINIMIZERS
2026-11-01. Successor to the compact straightening and growing-core notes.

PROVED OUTPUT
For a matched-mass paired global minimizer in R^3 at correlation-1/3,
Gaussian L1 distance epsilon from the regular pair implies a normalized
dual bound ||lambda||_2<=13sqrt(epsilon), for all sufficiently small epsilon.
This closes the explicit dual premise in
MEHLER_SMOOTHING_AND_GROWING_CORE_WINDOW_20261101.txt.

Combining that note's audited inverse-chart argument with the bound proves
a useful reduction: a sequence of such minimizers tending in Gaussian L1
to the regular pair can be replaced by EXACTLY matched-mass, compactly
supported C1-small ambient deformations of the regular pair. Their
objectives and Gaussian L1 distances change by o(epsilon^2). The maps
respect the entire junction arrangement. This note does not supply the
remaining energy-relative nonlinear estimate for moving junctions, nor
conclude local isolation or global tetrahedral optimality.

1. NOTATION AND ROUTING
Use rho=-1/3, tetrahedral rows Y_i=(+/-1,+/-1,+/-1)/2 with positive sign
product, and regular cones C_i. Let(A,H) be a pure paired global minimizer
of sum_i<1_Ai,T_rho1_Hi> with gamma(A_i)=gamma(H_i). Reflection of H
recovers the equivalent positive-correlation1/3 formulation.
After a common rotation and fixed common relabelling, define
 epsilon=max{sum_i gamma(A_i symmetric_difference C_i),
              sum_i gamma(H_i symmetric_difference C_i)}.              (1)
The conclusions apply along epsilon->0; epsilon=0 is already the reference.

There is a single vector lambda, normalized by sum lambda_i=0, such that
 A_i is selected among argmin_j(U_j+lambda_j),
 H_i is selected among argmin_j(V_j-lambda_j),             (2)
where U_i=T_rho1_Hi and V_i=T_rho1_Ai. The selections hold almost everywhere.
Their signs are coupled by the constraint E1_A-E1_H=0.

Here is a complete route to (2), rather than only pairwise level-set
stationarity. Any relaxed rule can be purified with the other rule fixed
while retaining its four label masses and its current linear objective.
Indeed these finitely constrained simplex-valued functions form a weakly
compact convex subset of L2. An extreme point must be pure: if two
coordinates are bounded below on a positive-measure set, split that set
into more pieces than constraints and take a nonzero linear combination
of their two-label transfers that annihilates all constraints. A small
positive and negative perturbation contradicts extremality. Purifying the
two rules successively shows that pure and relaxed global infima coincide.

At the relaxed global minimizer, differentiating along every feasible
segment says that(A,H) minimizes the LINEAR objective
 L(f,g)=sum_i int f_i U_i+g_i V_i, subject to Ef=Eg.
The attainable set of (Ef-Eg,L(f,g)) is convex and compact. Its mass
projection contains a neighborhood of0 in1-perp: use constant f,g near
the uniform vector. The convex value function therefore has a finite
subgradient at0. Equivalently, a supporting hyperplane gives a finite
lambda with L(f,g)+lambda.(Ef-Eg) minimized at(A,H). Pointwise minimization
now gives (2). No convexity of the original bilinear objective is assumed.

2. EXPLICIT SMALLNESS OF THE DUAL VECTOR
Put
 c=exp(-9/8)/(64sqrt(2pi)),
 C0=(81/80)^(3/4)exp(9/80).
For each i,j, center a tangential radius1/4 disk at x_ij=Y_i+Y_j on their
tie plane. Attach normal intervals[-a,-a/2] and[a/2,a], 0<a<=1/4.
The resulting prisms are respectively in C_i and C_j, are contained in
B_(3/2), and each has Gaussian measure at least c*a. Their score gaps to
the other labels are at least1/2.

If0<epsilon<c/8, choose a=2epsilon/c. A prism of measure at least2epsilon
cannot have lost all its reference label under a rule at distance at
most epsilon. Thus each contains positive-measure points in A_i or A_j
where the corresponding inequality (2) holds.

The reference difference T_rho(1_Ci-1_Cj) vanishes on the tie plane and
has global gradient norm at most1/(2sqrt(pi)). Its absolute value in the
prisms is at most a/(2sqrt(pi)). The exact Mehler L2 bound, on B_(3/2),
makes the perturbation of this difference at most C0sqrt(2epsilon).
Using both prisms gives
 |lambda_i-lambda_j|
   <=epsilon/(c sqrt(pi))+C0sqrt(2epsilon)=:Delta(epsilon). (3)
Since sum lambda_i=0, ||lambda||_2<=2Delta(epsilon). Moreover epsilon<c/8
implies
 ||lambda||_2
 <=2[(8pi c)^(-1/2)+sqrt2 C0]sqrt(epsilon)
 <13sqrt(epsilon).                                      (4)
The coefficient in square brackets times2 is about12.063046. Its strict
comparison with13 is enclosed by Arb in
certify_dual_multiplier_constant_20261101.py. Equation(3), not merely its
asymptotic order, is already proved in the predecessor compact note.

3. ROUTING MAPS AND THE GROWING CORE
Set
 F0=-Y^T(T_rho1_Ci)_i,
 F_A=-Y^T(U+lambda), F_H=-Y^T(V-lambda).
Independent Gaussian utilities give
 DF0=(1/(2sqrt2))Y^T L(W)Y,
 W_ij=(2/3)int_(Sigma_ij)k_rho(x,y)d lambda_ij(y)>0.
Thus F0 has positive definite derivative and preserves every reference
label, face and junction. These identities and the label-preservation
proof appear in COMPACT_MINIMIZER_STRAIGHTENING_WITH_JUNCTIONS_20261101.txt.
From(2), A and H are the preimages of the same labelled cones under F_A
and F_H. This provides the routing premise for the inverse-choice charts.

The root's growing-core note proves the improved derivative estimate
without a second inverse-eigenvalue loss. Briefly, all W_ij have a global
lower bound const*(1+R)^(-2)exp(-R^2/16). Their logarithmic derivatives
are O(1+R). A weighted-Laplacian maximum principle bounds
 ||L(W)^dagger[L(W')-L(W)]|| by a geometry constant times the maximum
relative edge-weight change, independently of conditioning. The exact
Mehler L2 norm grows as exp(R^2/20). Together with(4), these yield
 analytic maps Psi_A,Psi_H on B_R, with images containing B_(R-1),
 Psi_A(C_i)=A_i and Psi_H(C_i)=H_i locally in those images, and

 beta_epsilon:=max||Psi_A-id||_C1,||Psi_H-id||_C1
 <=const*sqrt(epsilon)*polynomial(R)*exp[9(R+1)^2/80].     (5)

The radius is R^2=(21/5)log(1/epsilon). Therefore
              beta_epsilon=epsilon^(11/400-o(1))->0.     (6)
To avoid ambiguity about chart images versus parameter balls, all physical
agreement assertions below use B_(R-3), leaving fixed-width margins.

An optional sharpening of the graph constant is6sqrt2 for the six edges
of K4. For u=W_e L(W)^dagger b_e, the maximum principle and effective
resistance give range(u)<=1. Since u has mean0 in four coordinates,
||u||_2<=range(u)<=1. Hence each rank-one term
W_e L(W)^dagger b_e b_e^T has norm at mostsqrt2. The root's larger constant
also suffices, so no revision of that note is needed.

4. SMOOTH CUTOFF TO GENUINE COMPACT AMBIENT DEFORMATIONS
Choose a smooth radial cutoff chi_R equal to1 on B_(R-2), equal to0
outside B_(R-1), with a derivative bound independent of R. Define
 Phi_A(x)=x+chi_R(x)(Psi_A(x)-x),
 Phi_H(x)=x+chi_R(x)(Psi_H(x)-x),                         (7)
and set both maps equal to identity outside B_R. The cutoff is supported
strictly inside the chart domain, so these are globally smooth. For small
epsilon their derivatives differ from identity by O(beta_epsilon)<1/2.
They are proper, globally injective diffeomorphisms, equal to identity
outside a compact set. One can verify injectivity directly from the
Lipschitz bound on Phi-id and surjectivity by properness/degree.

Put A*=Phi_A(C), H*=Phi_H(C). Since the displacement is eventually<1/2,
these pairs agree with A,H on the PHYSICAL ball B_(R-3), up to null sets.
Indeed chart points mapping there lie within B_(R-2), where the cutoff
is1; points outside B_(R-2) cannot move into B_(R-3). The same applies
to the inverse chart. All junctions remain compatible because a single
ambient diffeomorphism moves each entire partition.

Let t_R=gamma_3(R^3 minus B_(R-3)). Elementary Gaussian tail integration
gives, for R>4,
 t_R<=sqrt(2/pi)[R-3+1/(R-3)]exp[-(R-3)^2/2]
     =epsilon^(21/10-o(1))=o(epsilon^2).                 (8)
Thus each partition's summed label symmetric difference from its original
is at most2t_R. A bounded paired Gaussian objective is Lipschitz in this
metric: changing one partition on measure t changes agreement by at
most t, since it replaces one probability in[0,1] by another. Hence
                  |J(A*,H*)-J(A,H)|<=2t_R.               (9)

5. RESTORE THE EXACT COMMON MASSES
The truncated pair may no longer have matching masses. Its mass-difference
vector z=gamma(A*)-gamma(H*) has sum0 and norm O(t_R), because the original
pair had matching masses.

Fix three smooth compact vector fields supported near disjoint interior
patches of three reference facets forming a spanning tree of the labels.
Normalize their normal fluxes so their reference mass derivatives are the
three corresponding incidence vectors. This is an invertible map from
the three flow parameters to label space1-perp. The fields and supports
are fixed independently of epsilon and R.

Apply their flow family Xi_t to H*. The map
 t ->gamma(Xi_t(H*))-gamma(A*) is smooth. Its derivative at0 tends to the
invertible reference matrix as epsilon->0. For instance its entries can
be written as integrals of div(gamma X) over H*_i, so convergence follows
already from Gaussian L1 convergence on the fixed supports. Its parameter
derivatives are uniformly bounded, by differentiating the smooth compact
flow and density inside a bounded integral. The finite-dimensional
quantitative implicit-function theorem consequently provides a unique
small correction t=O(t_R) with exactly equal mass vectors.

Set H**=Xi_t(H*). The correction is compact, changes C1 size by O(t_R),
and changes H* on measure O(t_R). The latter bound follows by the swept
boundary formula: the relevant fixed facet patches have uniformly bounded
area and normals because H* is C1-close to C there. Therefore
 gamma(A*_i)=gamma(H**_i) for every i,
 |J(A*,H**)-J(A,H)|=O(t_R)=o(epsilon^2),                  (10)
 d_gamma((A*,H**),(A,H))=O(t_R)=o(epsilon^2).              (11)
Both new partitions are compactly supported C1-small ambient
deformations of C, with the SAME control O(beta_epsilon), and with
support radius O(sqrt(log(1/epsilon))). No arbitrary isolated islands
are introduced to repair mass.

The measure distance to the aligned reference changes by at most O(t_R),
by the triangle inequality. In particular an original distance epsilon
remains epsilon+o(epsilon^2). This is useful for comparing a later
quadratic energy lower bound with the error in(10).

6. THE REMAINING MATHEMATICAL REQUIREMENT
Equations(4)-(11) close the dual-vector and discarded-objective-tail
premises of the growing-core route. To contradict a nonregular global
minimizer, one still needs a nonlinear bound for the compact deformations
produced by(7), including moving triple lines and the vertex, after a
rotation gauge. Specifically, the required remainder must be o(D-energy),
with constants compatible with the radius O(sqrt(log(1/epsilon))), and
the energy must dominate a constant times epsilon^2 modulo rotations.
An absolute error bounded only by a power of ||Phi-id||_infinity is
insufficient: tangential transport must cancel and concentrated normal
displacements require a relative estimate.

One useful elementary bridge is a trace inequality. For a compactly
supported facet height f(s,t), t>=0 inward from a triple edge,
 |f(s,0)|^3<=3||partial_t f||_infinity int_0^infinity f(s,t)^2 dt.
It follows by differentiating |f|^3 and integrating. After integrating in
s, cubic junction defects can potentially be controlled by C1 size times
facet energy. This does not by itself prove that all geometric junction
defects have the required cubic form; that cap-decomposition argument is
being developed separately by the radial agent.

No claim of local isolation, a nonlinear basin theorem for all nearby
partitions, or a global four-label conjecture solution follows until
that final geometric estimate is proved and audited.
