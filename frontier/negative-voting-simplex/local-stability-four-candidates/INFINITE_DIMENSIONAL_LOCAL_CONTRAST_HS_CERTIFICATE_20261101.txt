TETRAHEDRAL ENDPOINT: AN ALL-BOUNDARY-SPEED LOCAL CONTRAST CERTIFICATE
1 November 2026

RESULT AND SCOPE

For the actual tetrahedral facet operator at Gaussian correlation -1/3,
the enclosed calculation gives

  total Hilbert-Schmidt square H < 30.374796,
  translation/constraint angle parameter delta < 0.027458,
  max_{M u=0} K(u,u)/D(u,u) < 0.677770 < 1.                (1)

The operator estimates concern the full weighted L2 space on all six
facets, not a finite polynomial subspace. The calculation uses Arb balls,
exact polynomial cubature with controlled exponential remainders, an
explicit binary64 forward-error allowance, exact rational series tails,
and an exact rational Gaussian spatial-tail check. Its implementation
and arithmetic model are exposed below for independent verification.

This is a second-variation result. It does NOT prove the global paired
inequality, the four-label Standard Simplex conjecture, or a nonlinear
neighborhood theorem without a separate justified remainder argument.
The moving-boundary Hessian convention is the one derived in
PAIRED_BOUNDARY_FORM_AND_NUMERICAL_DIAGNOSTIC_20261101.txt.

1. OPERATOR, MEASURE, AND NORMALIZATION

Use regular sites y_i=(+/-1,+/-1,+/-1)/2 with sign product positive.
For e=(i,j), i<j, put n_e=(y_j-y_i)/sqrt(2), and let Sigma_e be the
facet between cells i,j. Let b_e in R4 have entries +1 at i, -1 at j.
The surface Gaussian measure is

  d lambda_e(x)=(2pi)^(-3/2) exp(-|x|^2/2) dH^2(x).

Let d_e(x)>0 be the OUTWARD normal derivative of
T_{-1/3}(1_Theta_i-1_Theta_j) on this facet. Then

  D(u,u)=sum_e integral d_e u_e^2 d lambda_e,
  K(u,v)=sum_{e,f}(b_e.b_f) integral integral
             u_e(x)v_f(y) k(x,y)d lambda_e(x)d lambda_f(y),

  k(x,y)=(9/8)^(3/2)
           exp[-(|x|^2+|y|^2)/16-(3/8)x.y].

The normalized self-adjoint operator A on the Hilbert space with norm D
has Hilbert-Schmidt square

  H=sum_{e,f}(b_e.b_f)^2 integral integral
             k(x,y)^2/[d_e(x)d_f(y)]d lambda_e d lambda_f. (2)

Same-edge incidence square is 4, adjacent-edge incidence square is 1,
and opposite edges contribute zero. Their total ordered sum is 48.
Equivalently H=24 times a same-edge integral plus 24 times one adjacent
integral. No artificial facet diagonal, node self-loop, or empirical
mass constraint is introduced.

2. EXACT MODES AND THE MASS-CONSTRAINT DISTINCTION

Translations t_e(x)=n_e.v have exact eigenvalue 3, with multiplicity 3.
Rotations r_e(x)=n_e.(Omega x), Omega antisymmetric, have exact eigenvalue
-1, with multiplicity 3. These identities follow from the actual normal
derivative and Gaussian invariances; they are not fitted numerical modes.
Distinct eigenspaces are D-orthogonal.

Write their projections as P_T,P_R. With

  E=A-3P_T+P_R,

one has E P_T=E P_R=0 and

  ||E||_HS^2=H-27-3=H-30.                                (3)

But M u=0 does NOT mean P_T u=0. The mass derivative is

  (M u)_i=sum_e (b_e)_i integral u_e d lambda_e.

Its D-inner-product representers contain 1/d_e(x), whereas translation
speeds are constant on each facet. Merely deleting the three translation
eigenvalues from the constrained problem would be invalid.

The required correction reduces to three scalar facet quantities:

  s=integral d lambda_e=acos(-1/3)/(2pi)^(3/2),
  U=integral d_e d lambda_e,
  V=integral d_e^(-1) d lambda_e.

Tetrahedral symmetry and sum_e n_e n_e^T=2I give the translation Gram
2U I, the mass-representer Gram 2V I, and their cross Gram 2s I.
Thus all three principal-angle squared cosines equal s^2/(UV). Put

  delta=1-s^2/(UV).

For every u with M u=0,

  ||P_Tu||_D^2 <= delta ||u||_D^2.                         (4)

Let h=sqrt(H-30). If h<1, the exact spectral decomposition gives

  <u,Au>_D
   <=h||u||_D^2+(3-h)||P_Tu||_D^2-(1+h)||P_Ru||_D^2.

Combining with (4) yields max K/D<=h+(3-h)delta. This is the bound
certified in (1). It avoids certifying a triple integral for A^2 acting
on the mass representers.

The same decomposition gives A>=-I on the entire space when h<1.
Its -1 eigenspace is exactly the rotation space. Consequently the
common-direction form D+K is nonnegative modulo common rotations,
while the matched-mass contrast form D-K has the strict gap in (1).

3. DIAGONAL EVALUATION AND A UNIFORM CURVATURE LEMMA

On the prototype facet use x=(q,w,-w), q>=|w|. The independent utility
means are mu_i=-(x.y_i)/(2sqrt(2)). Put

  W_ij=integral phi(t-mu_i)phi(t-mu_j)
                   product_{k not i,j}Phi(t-mu_k)dt.

Then exactly d=(2W_01+W_02+W_03)/2. These integrals have a closed
bivariate-CDF expression. If delta_mu=mu_i-mu_j, avg=(mu_i+mu_j)/2,
u=sqrt(2/3)(avg-mu_k), v=sqrt(2/3)(avg-mu_l), then

  W_ij=exp(-delta_mu^2/4)/(2sqrt(pi)) Phi_{1/3}(u,v).

arb_facet_diagonal.py evaluates Phi_{1/3} by the exact Plackett integral
from correlation 0 to 1/3 using Arb's validated complex integration.
The square-root analytic flag is forwarded correctly. The derivatives
use the exact identities

  partial_u Phi_c(u,v)=phi(u)Phi((v-cu)/sqrt(1-c^2)),

and its v counterpart. Thus the cell constants and log gradients are
balls enclosing the ACTUAL d, not a numerical surrogate.

There is a particularly useful uniform curvature bound. Reflection in
the tie plane exchanges cells i,j. At a point of that plane,

  d(x)=(2r/(1-r^2)) integral_{Theta_i}(-n_e.y)
                      phi_{sqrt(1-r^2)}(y+r x)dy, r=1/3.

The positive factor (-n_e.y) is log-concave on its supporting halfspace,
and the cell is convex. The integrand is jointly log-concave in x,y,
so its integral is log-concave by Prekopa. Conversely, after removing
exp(-|x|^2/16), d is a positive Laplace transform. Therefore, in
orthonormal tangent coordinates,

  -I/8 <= Hess(log d) <= 0.                                (5)

This lemma was independently checked during the collaboration. It can
also be obtained from the covariance bound for the strongly log-concave
tilted measure. No strict covariance-deficit assumption is needed.

At a cell center c=(q_c,w_c), let g=log d and use its tangent plane.
For delta_q=q-q_c, delta_w=w-w_c, (5) gives

  g(c)+grad g(c).delta-(delta_q^2+2delta_w^2)/16
       <=g(q,w)<=g(c)+grad g(c).delta.                     (6)

4. POSITIVE MOMENTS AND THEIR EXACT SERIES

In orthonormal facet coordinates (q,z), z=sqrt(2)w, let

  M_{p,n}=integral q^p z^n exp(-|x|^2/8)d lambda_e(x)/d_e(x).

Odd n vanish by reflection symmetry. All required even-n moments are
nonnegative. The contraction formulas and a rational global absolute
tail are independently derived in
TENSOR_MOMENT_EXPANSION_AND_EXACT_ABSOLUTE_TAIL_20261101.txt.

For completeness, with mu denoting the above positive facet measure,
the same-edge degree-k contraction is

  S_k=sum_{j=0}^k binom(k,j) M_{k-j,j}^2.

For adjacent facets, a choice of bisector frames has cross Gram
[[0,1/sqrt(2)],[1/sqrt(2),1/2]]. The contraction is

  A_k=sum_{l+m+n=k} k!/(l!m!n!)
            2^(-(l+m)/2-n) M_{l,m+n} M_{m,l+n}.

Only l,m,n of the same parity survive. Their coefficients are rational
and every surviving term is positive. Consequently

  H=(9/8)^3 sum_{k>=0}(-3/4)^k/k! *24(S_k+A_k).             (7)

For an upper enclosure, even-k terms take upper moment products and
odd-k terms take lower moment products. This preserves the cancellation
without assuming uncertain numerical signs inside a coefficient.

The present calculation uses k<=60. The certified absolute series tail
is less than 0.008217977378. Its checker uses only rational recurrences:
J0<32/27, J1=8/9, J_{n+2}=(8/9)(n+1)J_n, and the two-step coefficient
ratio bound (4/9)(k+4)^2/[(k+1)(k+2)]. The positive partial coefficient
sum is itself enclosed above by 88.772835; no floated absolute-sum value
is used in the error certificate.

5. COMPACT CUBATURE AND ANALYTIC ERROR

The compact region is 0<=w<=q<=14, reflected to negative w. It contains
the entire radius-14 facet disk. Its half-facet measure prefactor is
2sqrt(2)/(2pi)^(3/2), accounting for both signs and dH^2=sqrt(2)dqdw.

Use h=1/8, 6,216 complete squares and 112 diagonal triangles. The
bounding-square center is used even for a triangle. The squared tangent
distance is at most 3h^2/4. Hence (6) puts the actual reciprocal density
between its tangent model and exp(epsilon) times that model, where

  epsilon=3h^2/64=0.000732421875.

After extracting its Arb-enclosed central constant, the model weight
factors as exp(f_q)exp(f_w), with

  f_q=-(5q_c/4+g_q)delta_q-(5/8)delta_q^2,
  f_w=-(5w_c/2+g_w)delta_w-(5/4)delta_w^2.

Every exact argument has absolute value less than 3. For example the
proved bound |grad log d|<=|x|/4+1/2, |x|<=14sqrt(3)<25, and
|delta_q|,|delta_w|<=1/16 give a bound below 2.88. The implementation
also checks every computed argument is below 2.9. The actual maximum
is recorded in the output.

Replace each exponential by its Taylor polynomial P18. The relative
remainder obeys

  eta=exp(6)3^19/19! < 0.000003854572.

Each polynomial remains positive on this range. On squares, Gauss49
in each coordinate integrates every required monomial times the two
Taylor polynomials exactly: coordinate degree is at most 60+36=96.
The separable square integrals are accumulated as positive outer products.

On a diagonal triangle, q=a+hu, w=a+huv, 0<=u,v<=1, with Jacobian h^2u.
The exact polynomial degrees are at most 133 in u and 96 in v. Thus
Gauss67 by Gauss49 is exact. All monomial evaluations are nonnegative.
No high-degree Gaussian quadrature derivative heuristic is used.

The same construction encloses U and V, with Gaussian exponent 1/2
instead of 5/8. For U the tangent density is an upper model and receives
the lower correction exp(-epsilon); V has the same one-sided correction
as the moments.

6. ARITHMETIC ACCOUNTING

The cell constants and log gradients are validated Arb values. Converting
them to binary64 is permitted only after code assertions check relative
constant error <1e-12 and absolute gradient error <1e-12. Gauss nodes
are first mapped to the positive unit interval in Arb; their binary64
relative errors and weight errors are asserted <2^-48. Positive
barycentric coordinate formulas avoid cancellation near the origin.
Taylor coefficients are independently asserted within relative 2^-48.
Powers of two use exact integer shifts, and powers of coordinates use
multiplication rather than a floating transcendental routine.

The arithmetic model is IEEE binary64 round-to-nearest, checked for the
Python/NumPy scalar environment. Fused multiplication/addition only
reduces the usual gamma_n error bounds. No floating exp, erf, sqrt, or
trigonometric routine occurs in the positive accumulation stage.

A deliberately loose 1e-6 relative forward-error allowance covers:

  * true versus evaluated Taylor arguments, bounded by 1e-11;
  * the condition number of P18 for |t|<3, less than 404;
  * Horner roundoff, with fewer than 40 operations per polynomial;
  * monomial power propagation through degree60 and unit-node conversion;
  * positive dot products, outer products, and sums over all cells.

One may use a gross operation-path bound of two million per moment;
gamma_2000000<2.23e-10. The Horner/argument sensitivities enter before
the positive sums, not exponentially once for every cell. Bounding
these contributions as in the independent implementation audit gives
404 gamma_2000000+1000*1e-11+1e-10 <9.981e-8. The code checks this
last budget inequality in exact Fraction arithmetic; 1e-6 leaves a
substantial margin. The exact operation structure is visible in code.

Underflow is treated as an absolute error, not silently ignored. Even
allowing flush-to-zero below 2^-1022, fewer than 2^34 local operations
and subsequent amplification below 2^300 give less than 2^-688 total
error in every needed raw moment. The code adds the larger absolute
radius 2^-600 before applying relative factors. A fixed subrectangle
q in[1,9/8], w in[1/2,5/8] gives every needed z-moment a positive lower
bound 2^-42; thus the absolute allowance is negligible. This also
addresses the very small w^60 values in the first Duffy triangle.

If F is the computed positive moment, the implemented enclosure is

  lower=(F-2^-600)/[(1+1e-6)(1+eta)^2],
  upper=(F+2^-600)exp(epsilon)/[(1-1e-6)(1-eta)^2].

Both exponential factors are accounted for. All signed series assembly
after these positive moment enclosures is performed in Arb.

7. GLOBAL TAILS AND CERTIFICATE OUTPUT

The compact-sector estimate gives, with L=acos(-1/3),

  d_e(x)>=c0 exp(-|x|^2/16)/(1+|x|)^2,
  c0=(L/3)(9/8)^(3/2)(2pi)^(-3/2)exp(-15/16).

It follows that the total HS tail outside radius R is bounded by

  432exp(15/8)[2I0 I_R-I_R^2],
  I_R=integral_R^infinity t(1+t)^2 exp(-3t^2/16)dt.

The implementation independently verifies a rational upper bound below
1e-7 at R=14, using exp(-147/4)<(3/8)^36. Since q>14 implies |x|>14,
this covers the omitted part of the compact region used in (7).

Similarly, d<=1/(2sqrt(pi)) gives the U tail below 1e-40, and the lower
bound on d gives the V tail below 1e-30. These two statements are checked
with exact Fraction inequalities using exponent bounds (3/8)^98 and
(3/8)^85 and elementary Gaussian integration-by-parts tail formulas.

8. LOCAL CONSEQUENCE, AND WHAT IS STILL NOT PROVED

For every finite-D-energy boundary speed u satisfying exact first-order
common-mass matching M u=0,

  (D-K)(u,u) > 0.322230 D(u,u)

unless u=0. On the entire speed space D+K>=0, with common rotations as
its kernel. Thus the full local paired Hessian split

  B''=2[(D+K)(u_+)+(D-K)(u_-)]

has no negative direction under M u_-=0. This controls arbitrary speed
functions in the Hilbert space; the degree3 spectral scan was merely an
initial diagnostic and is not used as evidence for unsampled modes.

In rational terms, D-K>8D/25 on the mass-zero contrast space, and
D+K>3D/8 on the D-orthogonal complement of common rotations. Thus
B'' is at least (3/4)D(u_+ minus its rotation projection)+(16/25)D(u_-).

The unrestricted, finite-change paired inequality remains open in this
work. Positive second variation, even with this contrast gap, does not
by itself justify nonlinear regularity, a uniform Taylor remainder,
compactness, or global optimality. No such implication is claimed.
