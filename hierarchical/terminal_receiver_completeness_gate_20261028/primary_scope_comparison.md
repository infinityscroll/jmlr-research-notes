# Primary scope and significance gate: separated terminal receivers

28 October 2026. Bounded primary comparison for `separated_receiver_completeness.md`, SHA256 `9b6d81b482c7fbf5b63e703d20e5e6af4a12bea865ac48341b3b4d7c7f15f2ce`. This is not priority clearance.

## Verdict

This is a defensible scoped completeness extension of the held private-switch result to actual unit receivers. It does not constitute a new causal identification principle. A direct reduction explains nearly all of it: identify each upstream input law by flat ID, transport it through an observed conditionally independent response kernel, and propagate a positive ambiguity when one input is unidentified. The original-policy support lemma makes this reduction valid without an additional, potentially false receiver-support assumption.

It is **not** a resolution of the original paper's difficult numerical-constraint problem with shared latent unit causes. The control in `exact_controls_and_boundary.md` shows that identified and supported input laws alone do not remove that problem. The next meaningful advance must handle shared upstream/receiver latent structure through an actual constraint that forces identification; attaching more separated response variables would repeat this closure.

## Exact comparisons

**Weinstein and Blei, Hierarchical Causal Models.** Reread [Definitions 1–2, Assumptions 1–3, Theorem 12 and Sections 8.1–8.2](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume27/25-0899/25-0899.pdf). Theorem 12 provides sufficient subunit-action conditions while excluding hidden subunit confounders. The present theorem permits those confounders and supplies a selected-unit-outcome iff for a separated terminal block. Its support and limiting-response semantics are inherited. Section 8.2's general completeness question concerns restrictions on collapsed receiver functions; this theorem covers a simple unconfounded response composition and leaves the shared-latent restriction problem open. It supplies no failure certificate for the authors' full augmentation language.

**Shpitser and Pearl, Complete Identification Methods for the Causal Hierarchy.** Reread [Theorem 17 and the countermodel propagation discussion](https://jmlr.org/papers/volume9/shpitser08a/shpitser08a.pdf). Ordinary ID is the upstream identification mechanism. Extending an equal-observation counterpair by a common response channel and transmitting its discrepancy along a directed path is classical. Those facts alone do not supply the stronger whole-policy-supported hierarchical counterpair; that input comes from the held private-switch theorem. This receiver extension is therefore an application of that existing portfolio ingredient, not a new flat completeness result.

**Direct conditional-kernel reduction.** Under the stated separator the exact natural experiment factors as P(C,Q)K(Y|C,S(Q)). The policy changes the first factor's received-input law and preserves K. Ordinary disintegration gives T4 as soon as Law(C,S^pi) is dominated by Law(C,S). T5 proves that domination from the original policy atom, including when the atom itself is unobserved. No new version of do-calculus or an unavailable experiment is required. This mathematical implication is more informative about significance than a claim that a literature search did not find the theorem's wording.

## Internal antecedents checked

The previous general bridge/reset and receiver-confounding criteria classify numerical resets with hidden unit causes but no hidden subunit confounders. Their primary comparison already reduces the positive half to CSI or district recovery. The generic-policy and receiver-quotient results respectively use receiver encodability or fixed natural supports, also without the present hidden subunit structure. The no-feedback private-switch theorem supplies the new converse used here. None of those files was changed.

The current additional architecture is real: nonlinear unit outcomes, latent receivers and arbitrary within-block confounding now coexist with hidden subunit confounding upstream. The additional *proof idea* is modest. Specialist assessment may reasonably regard the result as a useful closure corollary to a larger paper. Neither a passing proof audit nor the exact fixtures establish sufficient novelty or importance for a standalone newsworthy claim.
