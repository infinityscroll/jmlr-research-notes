**Exact increment.** The old portfolio already had positive binary-observed flat hedge witnesses and a supported lift through a universal hidden unit root. The candidate instead superposes nested inner/outer perturbations, turns only the outer contribution on and off through A's private unit disturbance, and swaps the two observed law atoms. This removes the need for any shared unit selector. Its converse works on every prescribed no-feedback unit graph, including an empty one, with observed unit covariates and strictly positive support of the actual whole policy. The flat ingredients and label ambiguity are not new principles.

**Relation to the requested paper.** [Weinstein–Blei, Definitions 1–2 and Assumptions 1–3](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume27/25-0899/25-0899.pdf) license precisely the private mechanism disturbance, soft intervention, full mechanism-law observation and policy support used here. Their Section 8.1 leaves hidden subunit confounding for further study. Theorems 12–13 exclude that confounding; Theorem 13 also concerns a unit treatment and a full observed target. Thus those statements do not directly imply this converse. Section 8.2's receiver/aggregation issue remains outside the candidate because all subunit-to-unit arrows are excluded. This is a restricted answer to part of the broader program, not resolution of the paper's full completeness question.

**Closest exchangeable-data antecedent.** [Guo et al., Causal de Finetti, Definition 7 and Theorem 5](https://arxiv.org/html/2203.15756v3) formalize independent causal mechanisms and identify graphical structure under Markov/faithfulness assumptions. Their ICM graph has a DAG within each sample and cross-sample links for the same variable; Appendix D.1 explicitly excludes unobserved variables. The passage after Theorem 5 explains why degenerate mechanism variation can violate the required faithfulness. Our fixed known graph has hidden within-subunit causes, and the converse intentionally permits constant non-A mechanisms. That theorem neither subsumes nor is refuted by this construction.

[Guo et al., Do Finetti, Theorem 1 and Theorem 2/Appendix J](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/e656933e4e9626f5ada42349bcadffb8-Paper-Conference.pdf) derive exchangeable truncated factorization and combine it with graphical identification. The inspected factorization concerns a Markovian observed-variable model; their identifiability proof imports the Markov/faithful ICM graph result. Applying it to a larger graph containing our hidden subunit roots would require their unobserved distributions. It does not provide an observed-law-only formula or a completeness converse for the hidden-subunit class here.

[Karlsson–Krijthe, Detecting Hidden Confounding Using Multiple Environments, Sections 4–4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.13935v3) study observable implications of independent mechanism variation for confounding detection, with explicit discussion of degenerate mechanisms and faithfulness violations. That is not an all-graph effect-identification completeness theorem. It is nevertheless a material warning against interpreting our worst-case pair as saying informative multi-environment variation never helps.

**Scientific limit.** The theorem is uniform over a graph-defined nonparametric class, not a statement that every compatible observed law is ambiguous. Its witnesses deliberately ignore allowed unit arrows and hold all non-A mechanisms constant across units. Such behavior is permitted in the source model, but excludes stronger conclusions under guaranteed informative variation, causal faithfulness, constrained hidden alphabets, conditional/multiple policies, or subunit-to-unit feedback. The selected-event effect can be arbitrarily small. A large state bound is sufficient, not minimal.
